**NARRATIVE — The Problem:**

This story began with a conversation, not a whiteboard.

The founder's sister is a wet-lab researcher. She is deeply trained in her domain — she designs experiments, runs cell cultures, generates large-scale datasets that take months and significant resources to collect. She knows what she is looking for. She has hypotheses worth testing. But between her data and her next insight sits a bottleneck she cannot remove on her own: the computational analysis that turns raw output into scientific meaning takes her more than two days. Every time. And when the result finally arrives, it raises three new questions — and the cycle starts again, from the beginning.

She is not an exception. She is the rule.

Scientific discovery is a cycle. Ideas emerge from observation and literature. Experiments test those ideas. Results generate new ideas. The speed of discovery is determined not by the quality of any single hypothesis, but by how many times a researcher can travel around that loop — from question to result to next question — in a given span of time.

Today, both halves of that loop are harder than they need to be. Forming a well-grounded hypothesis requires synthesizing a literature that doubles in volume every few years and spans dozens of databases, journals, and experimental modalities. Most researchers cannot keep up. Critical connections go unnoticed. Hypotheses are formed in partial information. And then, when an experiment is ready to run, the computational analysis required to interpret its results sits behind queues of days to months, costs of hundreds to thousands of dollars per project, and a specialist skillset that most domain researchers were never trained to acquire.

A bioinformatics consultant charges $150 to $300 per hour. Academic core facilities charge $500 to $5,000 per project with weeks-long queues. The analytical component of a CRO engagement can reach $10,000 to $50,000, often becoming the rate-limiting step before any downstream decision can be made. A dedicated in-house bioinformatician costs $120,000 to $180,000 per year — beyond the reach of most academic labs and small biotech teams.

In every one of those gaps, hypotheses sit unvalidated. Grants go unrenewed. Researchers abandon lines of inquiry not because the science was wrong, but because testing it was too slow or too expensive to sustain. The global investment in biomedical research exceeds $250 billion per year. The constraint is not funding. It is the speed of the discovery cycle itself. BioMate exists to accelerate it — at both ends.

**NARRATIVE — The Solution:**

BioMate is built around a simple but powerful insight: the scientific method is a loop, and every existing platform only covers part of it.

**Think.** BioMate reads the literature so you don't have to start from scratch. It surveys published evidence, generates hypotheses grounded in real data, and suggests experimental approaches backed by citations. This is not hallucination — it is literature-anchored reasoning. A researcher can arrive with a question and leave with a validated experimental plan in minutes.

**Execute.** BioMate then runs the analysis. Not suggests it. Not describes it. Runs it. BioMate integrates 8,354 validated scientific tools — spanning genomics, proteomics, metabolomics, structural biology, pharmacology, synthetic biology, and 20 scientific domains in total. It runs 967 workflow templates, from RNA-seq to Cryo-EM data processing, from ADMET prediction to metabolic pathway simulation. The platform handles the computation, the parallelization, the infrastructure — so the researcher handles the science.

**Collaborate.** BioMate inherits the full collaboration infrastructure of the Galaxy ecosystem — one of the most widely adopted open platforms in life science, with over 50,000 active researchers worldwide. Histories, workflows, datasets, and results can all be shared across teams and institutions. Every analysis is fully logged, every parameter tracked, every result reproducible. The lab without walls.

But the differentiator is not any single pillar. It is what happens when all three operate together. A researcher hypothesizes → BioMate designs the workflow → executes it → returns results → the researcher forms a new hypothesis → and the loop runs again. What used to take 2 days now takes minutes. What used to require three different specialists now requires one conversation. This is the discovery cycle, compressed.

**NARRATIVE — How It Works:**

The BioMate experience is designed around a principle that sounds obvious but has never been implemented at this scale: the researcher should never have to leave their scientific thinking to deal with computational mechanics.

A researcher opens BioMate and describes their problem in plain language — the same way they would describe it to a collaborator over coffee. "I have RNA-seq data from three treatment conditions. I want to understand which pathways are differentially regulated and whether there is overlap with known disease signatures." This is the try/error cycle that drives all scientific discovery, compressed from days or weeks into minutes. A researcher who previously could afford to test one hypothesis per month can now test ten. That is not an incremental improvement. That is a change in the nature of research itself.

And when the work is done, it does not disappear into a personal folder. The full history — every tool used, every parameter set, every intermediate result — is logged, shareable, and reproducible. A collaborator at another institution can take the exact workflow and run it on their own data with one click.

**NARRATIVE — What BioMate Is:**

There is a distinction between a tool and infrastructure. A tool solves a specific problem for a specific kind of user. Infrastructure is the layer everything else runs on — broad enough to support any use case, deep enough that it becomes indispensable, and designed from the ground up to be used at scale rather than patched into scale later.

BioMate is infrastructure. It is the computational foundation that any life science researcher — regardless of domain, institution, or technical background — can use to move from a scientific question to an executed, reproducible result. That scope is deliberate. Research does not confine itself to one domain, one toolset, or one pipeline standard. Neither does BioMate.

**Intelligent.** BioMate does not wait to be told which tool to run. It understands what a researcher is trying to learn — from natural language, imprecise and domain-specific as that language always is — and constructs an evidence-based plan. Literature is consulted. Hypotheses are formed. Every recommendation is grounded in published methodology, not in pattern-matching against previous queries. The intelligence layer is what separates BioMate from a workflow engine: it understands intent, not just instructions.

**Automated where it counts.** Automation in BioMate has a precise scope: the computational pipeline. Tool selection, parameter configuration, pipeline execution, infrastructure provisioning, result formatting — all of this runs without manual intervention. What is not automated, by design, is the scientific judgment. The researcher decides what question to ask, evaluates the results, and determines what to do next. BioMate does not presume to replace that expertise. It removes the technical barrier that currently stands between a researcher's question and the result they need to answer it — and it automates as much of that barrier as possible, so that the researcher's effort goes entirely into the science.

**Platform-scale.** 8,354 validated scientific tools. 967 workflow templates. 20 scientific domains. 97 distinct tool categories. 231 production-ready nf-core pipelines. These numbers are not a feature list — they are the expression of a design decision: to build broad enough that BioMate is the last platform a research organization needs to evaluate. A point solution serves one workflow in one domain. BioMate serves the full spectrum of life science computation, from genomics to structural biology to pharmacology to synthetic biology, within a single coherent interface.

**Accessible.** The 500,000 life science researchers who are not computational experts should not need to become computational experts to do their science. BioMate takes a natural language description of a research problem and returns a reproducible, shareable result. No code written. No infrastructure configured. No queue waited in. The platform absorbs the complexity so the researcher does not have to.

The outcome of these four properties working together is concrete: research that used to take weeks of specialist time now takes minutes. Analysis that used to cost thousands of dollars in consulting or core facility fees now costs a fraction of that. And the researcher who used to test one hypothesis per month — because each test required a specialist, a queue, and a budget — can now test ten. That compression does not change what science is. It changes how fast science moves.

That is the infrastructure BioMate is building. Not a smarter chatbot. Not a faster tool. The foundation that makes life science computation fast, accessible, and affordable — for every researcher who needs it.

The value capture potential is direct and measurable: every hour of analyst time saved, every week of core facility queue avoided, every CRO engagement that starts from a stronger analytical foundation.
