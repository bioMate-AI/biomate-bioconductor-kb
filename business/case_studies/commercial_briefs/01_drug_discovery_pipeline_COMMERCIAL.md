## CASE BRIEF
# From Structure to IND §2.6 in an Afternoon — Without Losing Regulatory-Grade Rigor

### THE PROBLEM
Preclinical safety and pharmacokinetics is a relay race across four specialists — a chemist triaging toxicity liabilities, a PK modeller, a cardiac-safety expert, and a regulatory writer — that takes days per compound and stalls at every hand-off. Worse, standard single-channel cardiac screening kills clinically safe candidates by over-flagging them, and the resulting IND nonclinical summary still takes a scientist 40–120 hours to write.

### THE BIOMATE AI WORKFLOW
BioMate runs the whole preclinical-to-IND chain as one automated pipeline: it profiles ADMET liabilities, runs a multi-channel cardiac-safety model that distinguishes truly dangerous compounds from safe multi-channel blockers, predicts human pharmacokinetics, automatically corrects quality-gate failures within a documented audit trail, designs the Phase I dose escalation, and drafts the IND §2.6 nonclinical overview as a filing-ready document — all from a plain-language request.

### OUTCOME
Teams can screen a full compound series, kill real liabilities, keep the falsely-flagged safe ones, and walk out with a Phase I dose plan and a filing-ready IND §2.6 draft in a single afternoon — work that previously consumed weeks of scarce specialist time. Every decision is captured in a 21 CFR Part 11 audit trail, so the speed does not cost regulatory defensibility.
