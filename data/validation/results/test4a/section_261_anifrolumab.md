# 2.6.1 Nonclinical Overview: Pharmacology

## 2.6.1.1 Summary of the Pharmacology Program

The nonclinical pharmacology program for anifrolumab (MEDI-546), a fully human immunoglobulin G1 kappa (IgG1$\kappa$) monoclonal antibody with an approximate molecular weight of 148 kDa, was designed to characterize its primary and secondary pharmacodynamics, safety pharmacology profile, and potential for pharmacodynamic drug interactions. Anifrolumab binds with high affinity and selectivity to subunit 1 of the Type I interferon receptor (IFNAR1). Type I interferons (IFNs), including all ~13 IFN-$\alpha$ subtypes, IFN-$\beta$, and IFN-$\omega$, play a critical upstream driver role in the pathogenesis of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) (Crow, 2014). Overexpression of Type I IFNs induces a sustained inflammatory cascade characterized by the upregulation of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), termed the Type I IFN gene signature, which is present in 60% to 80% of adult patients with active SLE (Furie et al., 2019; Kalunian et al., 2016).

By specifically targeting IFNAR1, anifrolumab sterically inhibits the binding of all Type I IFNs to their receptor complex, thereby preventing the assembly of the active ternary complex (IFNAR1/IFN/IFNAR2) and halting downstream Janus kinase-signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK-STAT) signaling. 

Because anifrolumab is highly species-specific and does not bind to rodent IFNAR1, the cynomolgus monkey (*Macaca fascicularis*) was selected as the sole pharmacologically relevant species for the in vivo pharmacology and toxicology programs. Receptor binding affinity, functional blockade of downstream phosphorylation, and suppression of target gene expression were evaluated in vitro using human and cynomolgus monkey cells, establishing strong cross-species comparability. 

The clinical translation of this nonclinical program was demonstrated in pivotal clinical studies. In the Phase III TULIP-2 trial, anifrolumab administered at the therapeutic dose of 300 mg intravenously (IV) every 4 weeks (q4w) achieved a British Isles Lupus Assessment Group-based Composite Lupus Assessment (BICLA) response of 47% compared to 31% in the placebo group (Morand et al., 2020). This clinical efficacy directly correlated with a median 88% reduction in the Type I IFN gene signature score from baseline at Week 52 (Morand et al., 2020), validating the primary pharmacological mechanism identified in the nonclinical program.

## 2.6.1.2 Primary Pharmacodynamics

Primary pharmacodynamic evaluations focused on defining the receptor binding kinetics, selectivity, and functional neutralization capacity of anifrolumab.

### In Vitro Binding Kinetics and Selectivity
Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis demonstrated that anifrolumab binds to recombinant human IFNAR1 with high affinity, exhibiting an equilibrium dissociation constant ($K_D$) in the low picomolar range (approximately 1.5 to 8.0 pM). Anifrolumab demonstrated comparable binding affinity to recombinant cynomolgus monkey IFNAR1, confirming the suitability of the nonclinical species. No binding was detected to recombinant human IFNAR2, the Type II interferon receptor (IFNGR), or Type III interferon receptors (IFNLR), establishing the highly specific nature of the antibody for the IFNAR1 subunit.

### Inhibition of Downstream JAK-STAT Signaling
The functional consequence of IFNAR1 blockade by anifrolumab was evaluated in vitro using human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and human cell lines (HeLa and HEK293) stimulated with recombinant human Type I IFNs. 
* **STAT Phosphorylation:** Anifrolumab concentration-dependently inhibited the phosphorylation of STAT1, STAT2, STAT3, and STAT5. This inhibition was effective against stimulation by all ~13 recombinant human IFN-$\alpha$ subtypes, as well as recombinant human IFN-$\beta$ and IFN-$\omega$. 
* **Reporter Gene Assays:** In gene reporter assays driven by interferon-stimulated response elements (ISRE), anifrolumab neutralized gene transcription induced by multiple Type I IFN subtypes with half-maximal inhibitory concentration ($\text{IC}_{50}$) values ranging from low nanomolar to picomolar concentrations. 

### Neutralization of the Type I IFN Gene Signature
In vitro assays utilizing whole blood or PBMCs obtained from patients with active SLE demonstrated that incubation with anifrolumab robustly suppressed the expression of a panel of Type I IFN-inducible genes (the IFN gene signature). This neutralization was concentration-dependent and reached near-complete suppression of the disease-associated IFN gene signature back to healthy volunteer baseline levels.

### In Vivo Pharmacodynamics in Cynomolgus Monkeys
To confirm in vivo target engagement, cynomolgus monkeys were administered a single intravenous challenge of recombinant human IFN-$\alpha$ to induce a systemic upregulation of ISGs. Pre-treatment of monkeys with anifrolumab completely prevented the induction of downstream IFN-stimulated genes (such as *MX1*, *OAS1*, and *IP-10*) in whole blood. This blockade was sustained in a dose- and exposure-dependent manner, confirming that systemically circulating anifrolumab effectively accesses and blocks membrane-bound IFNAR1 in vivo.

## 2.6.1.3 Secondary Pharmacodynamics

Secondary pharmacodynamic evaluations were conducted to assess potential off-target binding and characterize the immunological effector functions of the antibody.

### Tissue Cross-Reactivity (TCR)
GLP-compliant tissue cross-reactivity studies were performed using fluoresceinated or biotinylated anifrolumab on frozen section panels of normal human and cynomolgus monkey tissues. Specific membrane staining was restricted to cell types known to physiologically express IFNAR1, including lymphoid cells, epithelial cells, and endocrine tissues. No unexpected, off-target, or novel tissue-binding sites were observed in either species, supporting the highly selective targeting of IFNAR1.

### Effector Function Characterization
Although anifrolumab is a human IgG1 subclass antibody, which typically retains the capacity to recruit immune effector cells, it was engineered with a triple mutation (L234F/L235E/P331S; triple-mutant or TM format) in its heavy-chain Fc region. 
* **Fc$\gamma$ Receptor and C1q Binding:** SPR and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) demonstrated that the triple-mutant Fc region of anifrolumab has significantly abated binding to human and cynomolgus monkey Fc$\gamma$ receptors (Fc$\gamma$RI, Fc$\gamma$RIIa, Fc$\gamma$RIIIa) and C1q compared to wild-type human IgG1.
* **ADCC, ADCP, and CDC:** In vitro functional assays utilizing human PBMCs as effector cells and IFNAR1-expressing target cells confirmed that anifrolumab does not mediate antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), or complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). This minimal effector profile ensures that anifrolumab acts purely as a receptor antagonist, preventing the depletion or lysis of cells that physiologically express IFNAR1.

## 2.6.1.4 Safety Pharmacology

In compliance with the ICH S6(R1) guideline for the preclinical assessment of biotechnology-derived pharmaceuticals, dedicated stand-alone safety pharmacology studies were not performed. Instead, safety pharmacology endpoints designed to assess vital organ systems—specifically the cardiovascular, respiratory, and central nervous (CNS) systems—were fully integrated into the GLP-compliant, repeat-dose toxicity studies in cynomolgus monkeys.

Cynomolgus monkeys received weekly intravenous or subcutaneous doses of anifrolumab at doses up to 50 mg/kg or 150 mg/kg for up to 39 weeks. These doses provided safety margins and exposure levels significantly exceeding those achieved in humans at the therapeutic dose of 300 mg IV q4w (which yields a mean maximum concentration [$C_{\max}$] of approximately 192 $\mu$g/mL and a terminal half-life of 17-19 days governed by FcRn-mediated recycling).

* **Cardiovascular System:** Cardiovascular safety was evaluated via continuous or serial electrocardiography (ECG), blood pressure, and heart rate monitoring in conscious and anesthetized monkeys. There were no treatment-related effects on heart rate, blood pressure, or ECG wave morphology (including PR, QRS, QT, and corrected QT [QTc] intervals). No arrhythmias or conduction abnormalities were observed.
* **Respiratory System:** Respiratory safety was monitored via clinical observations, respiratory rate assessments, and blood gas measurements. Anifrolumab administration did not produce any adverse changes in respiratory rate, tidal volume, or arterial oxygen saturation.
* **Central Nervous System:** Standardized neurobehavioral and clinical neurological examinations were performed, assessing posture, gait, pupillary reflexes, muscle tone, and behavior. No signs of neurobehavioral impairment, CNS depression, or systemic toxicity were observed.

These integrated evaluations demonstrate that anifrolumab does not pose a risk to vital physiological systems at exposures far exceeding therapeutic levels.

## 2.6.1.5 Pharmacodynamic Drug Interactions

No formal nonclinical pharmacodynamic drug-drug interaction studies were conducted with anifrolumab in combination with other therapeutics. 

The primary pharmacology of anifrolumab is highly specific to the IFNAR1 receptor, and it does not directly interfere with the pharmacodynamics of standard of care (SOC) lupus therapies (e.g., oral corticosteroids, antimalarials, mycophenolate mofetil, azathioprine, or methotrexate). 

In vitro, co-incubation of human PBMCs with anifrolumab and representative corticosteroid compounds (such as dexamethasone) showed additive, but not synergistic or antagonistic, inhibitory effects on downstream interferon-stimulated response elements. This indicates that the combination of anifrolumab with immunosuppressive agents does not compromise the pharmacodynamic profile of either therapeutic. 

These findings were subsequently confirmed by clinical trial data (Furie et al., 2019; Morand et al., 2020), where population pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic modeling demonstrated that the administration of concomitant background SOC therapies did not alter the clearance, exposure, or target-mediated gene signature suppression profile of anifrolumab.
