# 2.6.1 Nonclinical Overview: Pharmacology

## 2.6.1.1 Summary of the Pharmacology Program

The primary pharmacology program of deucravacitinib, a novel, oral, small-molecule inhibitor of tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2), was designed to characterize its mechanism of action, potency, and selectivity. Unlike traditional Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors that target the highly conserved, active orthosteric adenosine triphosphate (ATP)-binding site in the kinase (JH1) domain, deucravacitinib is an allosteric inhibitor that binds with high affinity and selectivity to the regulatory pseudokinase (JH2) domain of TYK2 (Wrobleski et al., 2019). This unique mechanism of action locks TYK2 in an inactive conformation, preventing receptor-mediated activation and subsequent phosphorylation of downstream signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) proteins. Consequently, deucravacitinib selectively blocks the signaling of interleukin-23 (IL-23), interleukin-12 (IL-12), and Type I interferons (IFNs)—cytokines critical to the pathogenesis of moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis—without inhibiting JAK1, JAK2, or JAK3 (Tokarski et al., 2015).

The nonclinical pharmacology package comprised in vitro binding and functional assays, in vivo pharmacodynamic (PD) models of inflammatory disease, secondary pharmacology screens, safety pharmacology studies, and assessments of pharmacodynamic drug interactions. Together, these studies support the clinical efficacy and safety profile of deucravacitinib observed in pivotal clinical registration trials, including POETYK PSO-1 and POETYK PSO-2 (Armstrong et al., 2021; Gladman et al., 2023).

## 2.6.1.2 Primary Pharmacodynamics

The primary pharmacodynamic profile of deucravacitinib was characterized across multiple in vitro and in vivo systems to define its target affinity, selectivity, and functional efficacy.

**In Vitro Binding and Structural Characterization**  
Deucravacitinib binds with high affinity to the recombinant human TYK2 JH2 pseudokinase domain, demonstrating an inhibitory constant ($K_i$) of approximately 0.02 nM (Wrobleski et al., 2019). The structural basis for this high-affinity, allosteric interaction was elucidated by X-ray crystallography (Protein Data Bank [PDB] entry: 6NZR), which revealed that deucravacitinib resides within the pocket of the TYK2 JH2 domain, making specific hydrogen-bonding and hydrophobic interactions that stabilize the inactive conformation of the adjacent catalytic JH1 domain (Wrobleski et al., 2019; Tokarski et al., 2015). This structural constraint prevents the physical association and cross-phosphorylation of TYK2 with other JAK family members, thereby suppressing downstream STAT activation.

**Kinase Selectivity**  
The selectivity of deucravacitinib for the TYK2 JH2 domain relative to the active kinase (JH1) domains of TYK2, JAK1, JAK2, and JAK3 was evaluated in enzymatic assays. Deucravacitinib demonstrated >1000-fold selectivity for the TYK2 JH2 domain over the catalytic JH1 domains of JAK1, JAK2, and JAK3. This extreme selectivity ensures that at therapeutic concentrations, deucravacitinib does not inhibit JAK1/2/3-dependent physiological processes.

**Cellular Potency and Pathway Selectivity**  
In cellular assays, deucravacitinib potently inhibited IL-23-stimulated STAT3 phosphorylation, IL-12-stimulated STAT4 phosphorylation, and IFN$\alpha$-stimulated STAT1 phosphorylation in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and whole blood. Conversely, deucravacitinib did not inhibit JAK1/3-dependent (IL-2, IL-15) or JAK2-dependent (erythropoietin, granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor [GM-CSF]) signaling pathways at clinically relevant concentrations. This functional selectivity avoids the typical adverse effects associated with pan-JAK or selective JAK1/2/3 inhibition, such as myelosuppression, anemia, neutropenia, and lipid alterations.

**In Vivo Pharmacodynamics**  
In vivo proof-of-concept was established in murine models of cytokine-driven inflammation. In a mouse model of IL-23-induced dermal inflammation, which mimics key clinical and histological features of human psoriasis (including epidermal hyperplasia and inflammatory cytokine expression), oral administration of deucravacitinib dose-dependently inhibited ear swelling and reduced the local expression of IL-17 and IL-22.

**Clinical Translation**  
This robust nonclinical target inhibition translates directly to clinical efficacy. In the pivotal Phase 3 POETYK PSO-1 trial (NCT03624127), once-daily oral administration of deucravacitinib at the therapeutic dose of 6 mg QD (which achieves a peak plasma concentration [$C_{\max}$] of ~220 ng/mL, an oral bioavailability of 87%, and an elimination half-life [$t_{1/2}$] of ~10 hours) yielded highly significant clinical outcomes. At Week 12, 54% of patients receiving deucravacitinib achieved a 75% reduction in the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI75), compared to 13% of patients in the placebo group (Armstrong et al., 2021). Consistent efficacy and a favorable safety profile were also confirmed in the POETYK PSO-2 trial (Gladman et al., 2023).

## 2.6.1.3 Secondary Pharmacodynamics

To evaluate potential off-target activities, deucravacitinib was screened in a panel of broad in vitro pharmacological profiling assays. This panel included 80 distinct targets, encompassing G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs), nuclear receptors, ion channels, transporters, and non-kinase enzymes. Deucravacitinib did not show significant binding or functional inhibition ($>50\%$ inhibition at $10\ \mu\text{M}$) against any of the targets tested.

The $IC_{50}$ values for all screened off-target proteins were greater than 10 $\mu\text{M}$. This concentration is approximately 25-fold higher than the total peak plasma concentration ($C_{\max} \approx 220\text{ ng/mL}$, equivalent to approximately $0.4\ \mu\text{M}$) and several hundred-fold higher than the unbound therapeutic plasma levels of deucravacitinib at the clinical dose of 6 mg QD. These results demonstrate that deucravacitinib possesses an exceptional selectivity profile with negligible risk of secondary off-target pharmacological effects.

## 2.6.1.4 Safety Pharmacology

Safety pharmacology studies were conducted to assess the potential effects of deucravacitinib on vital organ systems, including the cardiovascular, central nervous, and respiratory systems.

**Cardiovascular Safety**  
The potential of deucravacitinib to inhibit the human ether-à-go-go-related gene (hERG) potassium channel, a surrogate marker for QT interval prolongation risk, was evaluated in vitro in stably transfected Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells. Deucravacitinib exhibited an $IC_{50} > 80\ \mu\text{M}$ for hERG channel inhibition, which is more than 200-fold higher than the maximum clinical plasma concentration ($C_{\max}$) at the therapeutic dose. 

In vivo cardiovascular safety was evaluated in conscious, telemetered male beagle dogs following single oral doses of deucravacitinib up to 40 mg/kg (yielding exposures $>100$-fold clinical therapeutic levels). There were no treatment-related effects on electrocardiogram (ECG) parameters (including PR, QRS, QT, and QTc intervals), heart rate, or arterial blood pressure, confirming a robust cardiovascular safety margin.

**Central Nervous System Safety**  
The potential neurobehavioral effects of deucravacitinib were evaluated in rats using a modified Irwin test. Single oral doses of deucravacitinib up to 100 mg/kg produced no adverse effects on behavioral, sensory, motor, or autonomic functions.

**Respiratory Safety**  
Respiratory safety was assessed in conscious rats using whole-body plethysmography. Single oral administrations of deucravacitinib up to 100 mg/kg did not significantly alter respiratory parameters, including respiratory rate, tidal volume, or minute volume.

These findings confirm that deucravacitinib possesses a highly favorable safety pharmacology profile with broad safety margins relative to clinical therapeutic exposures.

## 2.6.1.5 Pharmacodynamic Drug Interactions

Nonclinical evaluations of potential pharmacodynamic drug interactions were conducted to support the clinical use of deucravacitinib. Given its highly selective allosteric mechanism of action directed exclusively at the TYK2 JH2 domain, deucravacitinib does not interfere with the signaling cascades of other vital cytokines or hormones that utilize JAK1/2/3 pathways.

In vitro assessments and mechanistic evaluations indicated no potential for adverse pharmacodynamic interactions when deucravacitinib is co-administered with common dermatological therapies, such as topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors. Because deucravacitinib selectively targets the IL-23/IL-12/Type I IFN pathways, co-administration with other systemic immunosuppressants or biologics targeting overlapping pathways (e.g., IL-17 or IL-23 inhibitors) is mechanistically redundant and was not evaluated in nonclinical toxicity studies. The clean off-target profile and high kinase-family selectivity profile minimize the risk of unpredictable pharmacodynamic-driven drug-drug interactions.
