## 1.3 Deep Paper Analyses

### Paper A: NMDA Receptor Structures (Nature 2014) — Gouaux lab

**Citation:** Lee CH, Lü W, Michel JC, Goehring A, Du J, Song X, Gouaux E. *Nature* 511:191–197 (2014). PMID: 25008524

#### Scientific Question
NMDA receptors (NMDARs) mediate excitatory neurotransmission and are critical for learning and memory. They are implicated in stroke, Alzheimer's, schizophrenia, and depression. How are the four subunits (GluN1 + GluN2A/B + accessory) arranged? What are the structural bases for Mg²⁺ block, ion permeation, and drug inhibition?

#### Approach
- X-ray crystallography at 3.0–4.0 Å
- Construct: rat GluN1b + GluN2B, co-expressed in HEK293 cells, purified with Fab fragments to improve crystal packing
- Detergent: maltoside-based; crystallization in vapor diffusion (not LCP, as NMDAR is large and not a GPCR)

#### Key Findings
- The four subunits arrange as a dimer-of-dimers: (GluN1-GluN2B)₂
- "Cross-over" subunit arrangement: subunits in the amino-terminal domain (ATD) pair differently from subunits in the ligand-binding domain (LBD) — an allosteric circuit
- The pore domain follows the K⁺ channel fold, with an asparagine (N+1 position) in GluN1 forming the Mg²⁺ binding site
- Conformational changes from apo → antagonist → agonist visualized by comparing crystal structures

#### Methodological Significance for Lee Lab
This work established Lee's expertise in membrane protein expression in mammalian (HEK293) cells and Fab-assisted crystallography — methods he later adapted for cryo-EM at St. Jude.

### Paper B: HCN1 Channel Structure (Cell 2017) — MacKinnon lab

**Citation:** Lee CH, MacKinnon R. *Cell* 168:111–120 (2017). PMID: 28086084

#### Scientific Question
Hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide–gated (HCN) channels are opened by membrane hyperpolarization and cAMP. They set the resting potential and pacemaker activity in neurons and cardiac cells. What does the complete channel look like, and what is the structural basis for hyperpolarization-activated gating?

#### Approach [Paper]
- Cryo-EM (first high-resolution HCN structure)
- Source: human HCN1, expressed in Sf9 cells; purified in detergent
- Resolution: ~3.5 Å

#### Key Findings [Paper]
- The voltage-sensing domain (VSD) position: in hyperpolarization-gated channels, the S4 helix moves "down" (inward) to open the channel — the opposite of classical Kv channels
- HCN pore: uses the canonical K⁺ channel selectivity filter (GYGF → CIGYG in HCN), but gating is inverted
- cAMP-binding domain (CNBD): C-linker connects CNBD to pore; cAMP binding extends the C-linker → promotes opening
- A non-conducting "pre-open" state identified: voltage sensor in "down" position, but gate not yet open — requires cAMP for final opening

#### Why This Matters for Structural Biology Training
HCN channels demonstrated that cryo-EM can resolve voltage-sensing domain positions with sufficient precision to distinguish "up" vs. "down" conformations. This required ~3.5 Å local resolution at the VSD — achievable with the Titan Krios/K2 combination in 2016–2017.

### Paper C: VMAT2 — Two Inhibition Mechanisms (Nature 2023)

**Citation:** Pidathala S, Liao S, Dai Y, Li X, Long C, Chang CL, Zhang Z, Lee CH. *Nature* 623:1086–1092 (2023). PMID: 37914936

#### Scientific Question
VMAT2 packages monoamine neurotransmitters (serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine) into synaptic vesicles via H⁺/monoamine antiport. It is targeted by tetrabenazine (Huntington's chorea treatment) and reserpine (historical antihypertensive). These drugs have different pharmacological profiles despite both inhibiting VMAT2. Why?

#### Scientific Thinking [Paper + Training Knowledge]
The team designed the experiment around a key hypothesis: two drugs that differ in onset/offset kinetics must bind to different conformational states of the transporter. By solving structures with each drug, they would capture different states and explain the pharmacokinetic difference structurally.

Insight: Tetrabenazine is rapid-reversible; reserpine is slow-onset and nearly irreversible. If tetrabenazine traps a lumen-facing state (accessible from the vesicle lumen) and reserpine traps a cytoplasm-facing state (accessible from the cytoplasm), this explains why reserpine takes longer to wash out — it requires the transporter to cycle back through the cytoplasm-facing state to release.

#### Sample Preparation [Paper + Lab Style]
- **Construct:** VMAT2EM — truncated N/C termini; engineered for stability
- **Expression:** Baculovirus/Sf9 system
- **Reconstitution:** Saposin nanodisc (Lee lab preference for small transporters). Saposin nanodiscs: ~7–10 nm diameter; formed by mixing VMAT2 + saposin + synthetic lipids (POPC:POPE:CL = 7:2:1 molar) → rapid mixing into buffer without detergent → self-assembly
- **Drug/substrate preparation:** Tetrabenazine, reserpine, serotonin dissolved in DMSO (<0.1% final); added 30 min before vitrification
- **Quality control:** SEC-MALS to confirm ~100 kDa nanodisc complex (single VMAT2 monomer per disc)

#### Cryo-EM Data Collection [Lab Style]
- **Microscope:** FEI Titan Krios G3i, 300 kV
- **Detector:** Gatan K3 + BioQuantum energy filter, 20 eV slit
- **Pixel size:** 0.83 Å/pix (counting mode)
- **Defocus range:** −0.8 to −2.0 μm
- **Total dose:** ~55 e⁻/Å², fractionated into 50 frames (1.1 e⁻/frame)
- **Number of movies:** 4,000–6,000 per dataset (separate dataset for each drug condition)
- **Grid:** UltrAuFoil R1.2/1.3 Au 300 mesh; glow discharge 30s at 15 mA; blot 3.5s at 4°C, 100% humidity; plunge in liquid ethane

#### Data Processing [Paper: cryoSPARC + RELION]
1. **Motion correction:** MotionCor2, 5×5 patches, dose weighting
2. **CTF estimation:** CTFFIND4, manual curation (discard CTF max resolution > 4 Å)
3. **Picking:** cryoSPARC blob picker → 2D clean → template picker (iterative)
4. **2D classification:** 100–200 classes; keep classes with clear secondary structure detail
5. **3D ab initio:** cryoSPARC, 3 classes; select best
6. **Heterogeneous 3D refinement:** Separate drug-bound from drug-free populations
7. **NU-refinement:** cryoSPARC non-uniform refinement
8. **Particle polishing:** RELION Bayesian polishing
9. **CTF refinement:** Per-particle defocus, astigmatism, beam-tilt
10. **Local refinement:** Soft mask around the TMD core
11. **Resolution:** 2.8–3.5 Å (drug-binding site 2.8–3.0 Å)

#### Key Structural Findings [Paper]
- **Tetrabenazine binding state:** VMAT2 in lumen-facing conformation; tetrabenazine locks the "luminal gating lid" in an occluded position → accessible when vesicle lumen opens
- **Reserpine binding state:** VMAT2 in cytoplasm-facing conformation; reserpine expands the cytoplasmic vestibule and blocks substrate access from the cytoplasmic side
- **Serotonin recognition:** Serotonin is coordinated by a phenylalanine-rich aromatic cage in the binding pocket; two Phe residues form π-stacking with the indole ring → triggers H⁺/monoamine exchange
- **Transport mechanism:** Alternating-access model confirmed; serotonin binding drives TM1/TM5 scissor motion that alternates between inward-open and outward-open states

#### Conceptual Contribution
Pharmacological consequence of conformational state: this paper established that drugs targeting the "wrong" side of the membrane cycle have different kinetics. Tetrabenazine acts like a "door wedge from the inside," reserpine like a "door wedge from the outside." This principle now guides drug design for all MFS transporters.

### Paper D: Spns2 / S1P Transport (Cell 2023)

**Citation:** Chen H, Ahmed S, Zhao H, Elghobashi-Meinhardt N, Dai Y, Kim JH, McDonald JG, Li X, Lee CH. *Cell* 186:2644–2655.e16 (2023). PMID: 37224812

#### Scientific Question
Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) is a bioactive lipid that controls lymphocyte egress from lymph nodes, vascular permeability, and heart rate. Spns2 is the primary S1P exporter in vascular endothelial cells and red blood cells. Unlike most MFS lipid transporters (which use active transport), does Spns2 use a different mechanism?

#### Design — 6 Structures Philosophy [Paper]
The team designed to capture: (1) apo inward-open, (2) apo outward-open, (3) S1P-bound outward-open, (4) S1P-bound occluded, (5) inhibitor 16d-bound inward-open, (6) another inhibitor state. This is a complete transport cycle atlas in one paper.

#### Key Mechanistic Discovery [Paper]
**S1P export is via facilitated diffusion** — no coupling to H⁺ gradient, no ATP requirement. This makes Spns2 fundamentally different from ABCA1 (ATP-powered) and from H⁺-coupled MFS transporters like VMAT2. The driving force is simply the outward S1P concentration gradient.

Implication: Spns2 is an equilibrative transporter that can run in either direction. Its direction is set by the concentration gradient. This means blocking Spns2 would not need to overcome an active pump — simple competitive inhibition is sufficient.

#### Methods Highlights [Lab Style + Paper]
- 6 structural datasets collected and processed in parallel
- MD simulations: Gromacs with CHARMM36 force field; POPC bilayer; S1P docked in multiple orientations; 500 ns simulation per structure
- LC-MS quantification of S1P export: extract cell media, derivatize S1P with OPA reagent, quantify by HPLC-MS/MS
- Inhibitor 16d: a small-molecule Spns2 inhibitor that locks the inward-facing state

### Paper E: SPT Complex (Nat Struct Mol Biol 2021)

**Citation:** Wang Y, Niu Y, Zhang Z, Gable K, Gupta SD, Somashekarappa N, Han G, Zhao H, Myasnikov AG, Kalathur RC, Dunn TM, Lee CH. *Nat Struct Mol Biol* 28:240–248 (2021). PMID: 33558761

#### Scientific Question
The serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT) holocomplex catalyzes the first step of sphingolipid synthesis (condensation of serine + palmitoyl-CoA). Mutations in SPT cause hereditary sensory neuropathy; gain-of-function mutations cause childhood ALS (see Nat Med 2021 paper). ORMDL3 (asthma gene) regulates SPT by sensing sphingolipid levels. What is the structural basis for ORMDL3 regulation?

#### Key Technical Innovation [Paper]
**Symmetry expansion + signal subtraction** applied to the SPT holocomplex [Paper]:
- SPT has pseudo-2-fold symmetry (two SPTLC1/SPTLC2 heterodimers)
- In the heterocomplex, each half can be in different functional states
- Applying symmetry expansion treats each particle as two particles (one for each heterodimer), doubling the dataset
- Signal subtraction removes the contribution from the "other half," allowing focused refinement on one heterodimer environment
- Result: 0.5–0.8 Å better resolution for the functionally important half

#### 8 Structures — What They Capture [Paper]
1. Apo SPTLC1/SPTLC2 (no regulatory subunits)
2. SPTLC1/SPTLC2/ssSPTa (with small subunit)
3. Full holocomplex + PLP (pyridoxal phosphate cofactor)
4. Palmitoyl-CoA-bound
5. L-serine-bound
6. External aldimine intermediate (reaction intermediate)
7. ORMDL3-bound (inhibited state)
8. ORMDL3 + ceramide (feedback-inhibited state)

This covers the complete enzymatic cycle plus regulatory inhibition — 8 snapshots telling one complete story.

#### Resolutions Achieved [Paper]
- 2.6–3.4 Å — exceptional for an integral membrane enzyme complex

### Paper F: URAT1 / Urate Transport (Cell Res 2024)

**Citation:** Dai Y, Lee CH. *Cell Res* 34:776–787 (2024). PMID: 39245778

#### Scientific Thinking [Paper]
A two-author paper (Dai and Lee only) — the most focused output from the lab. Signifies that this project was internally driven with minimal collaboration. The drug comparison design: 5 structures (urate, pyrazinoate × 3 sites, lesinurad, verinurad, dotinurad) directly comparing mechanisms of inhibition.

**The key insight before the experiment:** If verinurad and dotinurad are clinically more potent than lesinurad despite similar mechanisms, they must engage different gating residues. The hypothesis-driven choice of exactly these three drugs led directly to the structural explanation.

#### Key Findings [Paper]
- Urate binds in a **Phe-rich aromatic cage** (phenylalanine π-stacking with the uric acid purine ring)
- Pyrazinoate engages three distinct sites: orthosteric (competes with urate) + two allosteric sites
- **Lesinurad** blocks only the orthosteric site — relatively weak gating effect
- **Verinurad + dotinurad** additionally engage a gating tyrosine residue (Tyr⁴⁰⁶/Tyr³⁶⁵) that locks the outward-open conformation shut
- Structural explanation for clinical potency ranking: dotinurad > verinurad >> lesinurad

#### Functional Validation [Paper]
[¹⁴C]-urate uptake in HEK293 cells expressing WT and mutant URAT1 constructs; IC₅₀ curves for each drug; mutagenesis at every key residue quantified → complete structure-activity relationship at atomic resolution

### Paper G: SV2A — Allosteric Modulation (Nat Commun 2025)

**Citation:** Pidathala S, Chen X, Dai Y, Nguyen LN, Gorgulla C, Niu Y, Liu F, Lee CH. *Nat Commun* (2025). PMID: 41315229

#### Scientific Question
SV2A is the synaptic vesicle glycoprotein targeted by levetiracetam (Keppra) and brivaracetam for epilepsy treatment. New modulator class (UCB1244283) acts allosterically. How can a second binding site modulate an orthosteric drug's effect?

#### Key Structural Achievement [Paper]
**Sub-3 Å resolution** of a ~90 kDa MFS transporter — pushing technical limits. Required:
- >300,000 particles in final reconstruction
- Per-particle CTF refinement + beam-tilt correction
- Local resolution filtering: core at 2.6 Å, periphery 3.5 Å
- Focused refinement with small mask around the TMD

#### Allosteric Mechanism [Paper]
- UCB1244283 binds at a second site on SV2A (distinct from the orthosteric levetiracetam site)
- Allosteric effect: slows dissociation rate (koff) of the orthosteric drug → prolonged receptor occupancy
- Mechanism: allosteric site binding stabilizes the "occluded" conformation → orthosteric drug cannot exit easily
- Padsevonil (a polypharmacology drug): binds BOTH sites simultaneously → blocks the modulation because the allosteric site is occupied

#### Drug Design Implication
This work created the structural basis for kinetic modulation — designing drugs that alter the kinetics of another drug rather than its affinity. This is a new pharmacological strategy for difficult targets.

## 1.4 Lee Lab Methodology Reference

### Expression & Reconstitution Flowchart
```
Design construct (truncate flexible termini, identify stabilizing mutations from thermostability screen)
  ↓
Small-scale Sf9 test (100 mL) → SEC profile check (should be sharp, symmetric peak at expected MW)
  ↓
Large-scale Sf9 expression (4–10 L) → Membrane isolation by ultracentrifugation (100,000g, 1h)
  ↓
Solubilization: 1% DDM + 0.1% CHS in lysis buffer (HEPES pH 7.5, 300 mM NaCl, 1 mM PMSF)
  ↓
Affinity purification: Anti-FLAG or Ni-NTA → Wash → Elute
  ↓
SEC polishing: Superdex 200 Increase 10/300, running buffer with 0.05% DDM + 0.005% CHS
  ↓
Nanodisc reconstitution:
  Saposin nanodiscs: mix protein:saposin:lipids (1:4:200 molar), dialysis into detergent-free buffer
  OR MSP1D1 nanodiscs: mix protein:MSP:lipids (1:3:80), Bio-Beads SM2 removal of detergent
  ↓
Final SEC: Superose 6 Increase 3.2/300 to isolate nanodisc complexes
  ↓
Vitrification: 3–4 mg/mL, add drug/substrate 30 min before; UltrAuFoil grids; Vitrobot Mark IV
  Blot: 3s, 4°C, 100% humidity, force 3
```

## 2.2 Career Arc and Intellectual Framework

Nieng Yan built the world's most comprehensive structural atlas of voltage-gated ion channels. Her strategy:
1. **Establish proof of concept** — solve the first structure of a class (CaV1.1 2016; before this, no eukaryotic Cav structure existed)
2. **Systematic expansion** — solve all subtypes systematically (Nav1.1 through Nav1.9)
3. **Pharmacological mapping** — for each subtype, solve structures with multiple drugs to map all binding sites
4. **Disease connection** — correlate structures with disease mutations (channelopathies)
5. **Diversify** — periodically tackle completely different systems (cholesterol transport, ABC transporters, algal biology)

Her diversification outside ion channels reflects intellectual curiosity and scientific breadth: NPC1/cholesterol (Cell 2020), ABCA1 (Cell 2017), Patched1 (Nat Commun 2019), glycofibrils (CryoSeek, PNAS 2024).

**Technical contribution:** Yan's lab developed graphene oxide grids for cryo-EM (PNAS 2020, PMID 31879346) — reducing preferred orientation by providing a hydrophilic carbon substrate. This is now widely adopted across the field.

## 2.3 Deep Paper Analyses

### Paper A: CaV1.1 at 3.6 Å — First Eukaryotic Calcium Channel (Nature 2016)

**Citation:** Wu J, Yan Z, Li Z, Qian X, Lu S, Dong M, Zhou Q, Yan N. *Nature* 537:191–196 (2016). PMID: 27580036

#### Scientific Question
CaV1.1 is the voltage sensor for skeletal muscle excitation-contraction coupling. It triggers SR Ca²⁺ release by physical interaction with RyR1 (not by Ca²⁺ influx). After 30 years of attempts, cryo-EM finally resolved this channel at near-atomic resolution.

#### Why Native Purification
[Paper + Training Knowledge] The rabbit skeletal muscle CaV1.1 is the most abundant Cav isoform in a freely available tissue. Recombinant expression of the full channel (4 auxiliary subunits, ~540 kDa total) in insect cells yields poor protein. Instead:
- Rabbit skeletal muscle dissected fresh → T-tubule membrane isolation by gradient centrifugation
- Solubilization in digitonin (1% w/v; gentle; preserves subunit stoichiometry)
- Wheat germ agglutinin (WGA) affinity → elute with GlcNAc → SEC

#### Data Collection [2016-era parameters]
- FEI Titan Krios, 300 kV
- Gatan K2 Summit, super-resolution mode (0.68 Å native, 2× binned → 1.36 Å/pix)
- Total dose: ~45 e⁻/Å², 30–35 frames
- Defocus: −1.5 to −3.0 μm
- ~3,000 movies

#### Processing (RELION, 2016-era)
- Motion correction: MotionCorr (frame-level)
- CTF: CTFFIND3
- Picking: semi-automated (EMAN2)
- 2D + 3D classification: RELION
- Final resolution: 3.6 Å [Paper] (gold-standard FSC 0.143)

#### Key Structural Findings [Paper]
- All 4 VSDs in "up" position (activated): the first time an activated eukaryotic VSD was seen at near-atomic resolution
- Inner gate: **closed** despite activated VSDs — captures a "pre-open" or "voltage-activated but gate-closed" state
- α2δ-1 extracellular: wraps around the extracellular loops of the pore domain; extensive disulfide bonds
- β1a: binds the AID (alpha-interacting domain) helix at the I-II linker; three of four VSDs contain β-interaction motifs
- Selectivity filter (EEEE locus): four Glu residues from each repeat form the Ca²⁺ selectivity filter — directly visualized

#### Conceptual Contribution
This paper ended 30 years of uncertainty about Cav structure. It defined the entire Cav structural biology field that followed. Every subsequent Cav structure (CaV1.2, 1.3, 2.1, 2.2, 3.1, 3.2) is reported relative to the CaV1.1 template.

### Paper B: Nav1.7 Antagonist Atlas (Nat Commun 2023)

**Citation:** Wu Q, Huang J, Fan X, Wang K, Jin X, Huang G, Li J, Pan X, Yan N. *Nat Commun* 14:3224 (2023). PMID: 37270609

#### Context: The Nav Atlas Project
By 2023, Yan's lab had solved all 9 human Nav subtypes. Nav1.7 is the primary pain Nav. The atlas project: solve structures of Nav1.7 bound to drugs from 6 different chemical classes → map all pharmacological sites → create a complete structural pharmacology reference.

#### Drug-Binding Sites Mapped [Paper]
1. **Intracellular gate site (FENP site):** carbamazepine, bupivacaine, lacosamide — classical LA/AED site
2. **Selectivity filter site:** lacosamide also engages here (dual-site; separate from FENP) — explains its unusual slow on-rate
3. **Domain IV fenestration:** vixotrigine, hardwickiic acid — enter through lateral membrane pores
4. **Domain I/II fenestration:** vinpocetine — enter through different fenestration

2.6–3.2 Å across all Nav1.7 structures — sufficient to position drug atoms, identify H-bond donors/acceptors, and design selective analogs

### Paper C: CaV1.2 Multi-Drug Structures (Cell 2023)

**Citation:** Gao S, Yao X, Chen J, Huang G, Fan X, Xue L, et al., Yan N. *Cell* 186:5363–5374.e16 (2023). PMID: 37972591

#### Scientific Question
CaV1.2 is the heart's primary L-type calcium channel. It is targeted by dihydropyridines (nifedipine, amlodipine — antihypertensives), phenylalkylamines (verapamil), and benzothiazepines (diltiazem). Amiodarone (antiarrhythmic) + sofosbuvir (HCV antiviral) combination causes fatal bradycardia by unknown mechanism. The snake toxin calciseptine blocks CaV1.2 selectively.

#### Design — Systematic Multi-Drug [Paper]
Structures determined:
1. Apo CaV1.2 (inactivated state)
2. Amlodipine-bound (DHP site in domain III/IV cleft)
3. Amiodarone + sofosbuvir complex (explains the fatal drug-drug interaction)
4. Calciseptine-bound (snake toxin, novel binding site)
5. Pinaverium bromide-bound (antispasmodic, novel binding mode)

#### Most Striking Finding [Paper]
**Pinaverium bromide** binding causes an unprecedented structural rearrangement: "S4-5III merges with S5III to become a single helix" — the linker helix is absorbed into S5, completely restructuring the channel gate. This creates an expanded but non-conducting pore. A new binding site not previously known for any Cav drug.

#### Sofosbuvir + Amiodarone [Paper]
The structures reveal these two drugs bind **simultaneously** to adjacent sites on CaV1.2, creating a synergistic block that neither drug causes alone. This is the structural explanation for the FDA black-box warning about this drug combination and bradycardia deaths.

### Paper D: NPC1/NPC2 Cholesterol Egress (Cell 2020)

**Citation:** Qian H, Wu X, Du X, Yao X, Zhao X, Lee J, Yang H, Yan N. *Cell* 182:98–111.e17 (2020). PMID: 32544384

#### Scientific Question
Niemann-Pick type C disease is caused by mutations in NPC1 (integral membrane protein) or NPC2 (luminal soluble protein). Together they transfer cholesterol from lysosomes to the cell. How do they work together? How does low pH (lysosomal environment) trigger cholesterol release?

#### Key Design: pH Titration Structures [Paper + Training Knowledge]
Multiple structures at different pH values (pH 4.5, 5.5, 6.5) to capture the pH-dependent mechanism:
- pH 6.5: NPC2 bound to NPC1's N-terminal domain (NTD) — "loaded" state (NPC2 delivering cholesterol to NPC1)
- pH 4.5: NPC1 with cholesterol in its sterol-sensing domain (SSD) — "transporting" state

#### Key Findings [Paper]
- NPC2 handover mechanism: NPC2 binds NPC1-NTD; pH-driven histidine protonation in NPC2 causes cholesterol release → cholesterol transfers to NPC1 NTD → then moves through the SSD to the outer leaflet
- Low pH is essential: at lysosomal pH, the handover is triggered; at neutral pH, it doesn't work
- Disease mutations: mapped onto the NPC1 structure → cluster at the NPC2 binding site and the SSD channel

#### Technical Highlights
- Liposome-reconstituted NPC1 for cryo-EM analysis of the membrane protein
- NPC2 crystal structure solved in parallel (X-ray crystallography, small soluble protein)
- Combined cryo-EM + X-ray approach to understand a bimolecular interaction

### Paper E: Graphene Grids for Cryo-EM (PNAS 2020)

**Citation:** Han Y, Fan X, Wang H, Zhao F, Tully CG, Kong J, Yao N, Yan N. *PNAS* 117:1009–1014 (2020). PMID: 31879346

#### Scientific Question (Methods Paper)
Preferred orientation of particles at the air-water interface degrades cryo-EM reconstructions. Graphene oxide coating of grids can solve this by providing a flat hydrophilic substrate that immobilizes particles in random orientations. But graphene oxide production is inconsistent. Can high-yield graphene monolayer grids be made reproducibly?

#### Innovation [Paper]
- Chemical vapor deposition (CVD) graphene transferred onto Quantifoil grids
- Functionalization with pyrene-NHS to make the surface hydrophilic and protein-compatible
- 97% yield of monolayer-coated holes (vs. ~30% for graphene oxide)
- Resolution improvement: apoferritin from 2.9 Å → 2.1 Å (by eliminating preferred orientation)
- Streptavidin monolayer variant: biotinylated proteins bind and orient → useful for asymmetric particles

#### Impact on the Field
Graphene grids (both CVD graphene and graphene oxide variants) are now standard in the field for:
- Membrane proteins with severe preferred orientation
- Small proteins (< 100 kDa) that benefit from adsorption onto the surface
- Samples where conventional blotting causes particle denaturation

## 3.3 Deep Paper Analyses

### Paper A: Human Spliceosome Atomic Structure (Cell 2017)

**Citation:** Zhang X, Yan C, Hang J, Finci LI, Lei J, Shi Y. *Cell* 169:918–929.e14 (2017). PMID: 28502770

#### Scientific Question
The human spliceosome is the machine that removes introns from pre-mRNA. It is 5 snRNAs + >300 proteins. Its structure at atomic resolution had never been determined. This paper reports the first near-atomic structure of any human spliceosome complex.

#### Design Choices [Paper + Training Knowledge]
- **Complex chosen:** The C* complex (the complex just after the second catalytic step, before disassembly) — stable enough for purification, yet at the catalytic heart of the machine
- **Source:** HeLa nuclear extract + biotinylated pre-mRNA (stalled after second step)
- **Affinity:** Streptavidin pull-down → glycerol gradient → cryo-EM

#### Key Technical Achievement
- Resolution: 3.8–4.5 Å for the catalytic core; 5–6 Å for peripheral regions
- The gradient of local resolution reflects the inherent flexibility: the core is rigid (snRNAs + metal ions + catalytic proteins), the periphery is mobile

#### Key Findings [Paper]
- Two catalytic metal ions (Mg²⁺) are coordinated by U6 snRNA nucleotides and protein residues — the spliceosome is a metalloenzyme
- The "branch point adenosine" attacking nucleotide is precisely positioned by Prp8 + Cwc22 + U2 snRNA
- 35 proteins identified in the complex; 7 not previously known to be at this stage
- Prp8 acts as a molecular scaffold: its RNaseH domain + endonuclease domain form a scaffold around the catalytic RNA

#### Conceptual Contribution
This paper proved that the spliceosome is indeed a ribozyme (RNA-based enzyme) whose chemistry is directed by protein scaffolding. The protein components position the RNA substrates but do not perform the chemistry directly — a fundamental principle of RNA biology.

### Paper B: Human Bact Complex — 3 States (Cell Res 2018)

**Citation:** Zhang X, Yan C, Zhan X, Li L, Lei J, Shi Y. *Cell Res* 28:307–322 (2018). PMID: 29360106

#### Scientific Question
The Bact complex is the activated spliceosome just before the first catalytic step (branching). It is formed from the B complex by extensive remodeling. How are the U2/U5/U6 snRNAs arranged before catalysis? How do the 52 component proteins prime the active site?

#### Sample Preparation Details [Paper + Lab Style]
1. **HeLa nuclear extract preparation:**
   - Hypotonically swell HeLa cells → Dounce homogenize
   - Centrifuge 600g × 10 min (nuclear pellet) → re-suspend nuclei
   - Resuspend in high-salt buffer → dialysis → centrifuge
   - Nuclear extract: ~5 mg/mL total protein
2. **Pre-mRNA substrate:**
   - Synthetic biotinylated AdML pre-mRNA with 2'-azido-modified branch point → blocks first catalytic step
   - Add to extract + ATP (2 mM) + creatine phosphate + Mg²⁺ → incubate 30 min at 30°C
3. **Pull-down:**
   - Streptavidin beads → wash with 200 mM NaCl (removes non-specific binding)
   - Elute with 2 mM biotin
4. **Gradient:**
   - Load onto 10–30% glycerol gradient in 150 mM NaCl, 20 mM HEPES pH 7.9
   - Beckman SW41 rotor: 40,000 rpm, 4°C, 16 hours
   - Fraction and check by SDS-PAGE + silver stain → pool Bact fractions
5. **Quality check:**
   - Mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) on pooled fractions — identify all 52 proteins [Paper]
   - Verify absence of splicing intermediates (confirms stalling at Bact)
6. **Concentration:**
   - Amicon 100 kDa MWCO concentrator → ~0.5–1 mg/mL
   - Cryo-grid within 2 hours of final concentration

#### Data Processing — Three States from One Dataset [Paper]
The three conformational states (early, mature, late Bact) were all present in a single cryo-EM dataset. Separation required:
1. Initial 3D classification into 6–8 classes (RELION 3D classification, T=8, 40 iterations)
2. Most classes correspond to junk/incomplete complexes (discarded)
3. 3 classes show distinct density for the U2 snRNA positioning → early/mature/late
4. Each class refined separately to ~3.5–4.5 Å

**This is a key methodological lesson:** Multiple conformational states of a labile complex are always present in a cryo-EM sample. You cannot stall the machine at exactly one state. You must capture the ensemble and then classify.

#### Key Findings [Paper]
- **52 proteins** identified — most in any spliceosome structure to date
- **Three conformational states reveal the priming pathway:**
  - Early Bact: SF3b complex loosely engaged; Prp8 Switch loop distant from RNA
  - Mature Bact: Prp8 Switch loop repositions over the 5'-splice site; SF3b partially displaced
  - Late Bact: SF3b displaced from branch helix (preparing for first chemical step); NTC complex fully assembled
- **New proteins discovered:** RNF113A (zinc-finger, stabilizes U6/pre-mRNA), NY-CO-10/ZMAT2 (endomethylase domain at active site)
- **Metal ions visible at 3.5 Å:** Mg²⁺ (M1 and M2) coordinated by U6 ACAGA box

### Paper C: Yeast B* Spliceosome — Branching Mechanism (Cell 2019)

**Citation:** Wan R, Bai R, Yan C, Lei J, Shi Y. *Cell* 177:339–351.e13 (2019). PMID: 30879786

#### Scientific Question
In the B* complex, the branch point adenosine (BPS-A) must attack the 5'-splice site. What positions it for catalysis? How do Yju2 and Cwc25 activate branching?

#### Multi-Condition Design [Paper]
4 structures from 2 pre-mRNA substrates × 2 activation states (±Yju2):
1. B* without Yju2, substrate 1 (actin pre-mRNA)
2. B* without Yju2, substrate 2 (U3 pre-mRNA)
3. B* + Yju2, substrate 1
4. B* + Yju2, substrate 2

This design tests: Is the branching mechanism universal (same on both substrates)? Is Yju2 essential for positioning?

#### Resolution Achieved [Paper]
2.9–3.8 Å — the highest-resolution spliceosome structures at the time. The 2.9 Å structure pushed the field forward by allowing reliable placement of individual nucleotides and sidechain rotamers.

#### Key Findings [Paper]
- **Without Yju2:** BPS-A is 15–20 Å from the 5'-splice site → chemically incompetent
- **With Yju2:** BPS-A repositions to 4 Å from catalytic Mg²⁺ (M2) → attack-ready geometry
- **Mechanism:** Yju2's S4 segment acts as a "toggle switch" — its binding induces a rotation in the U2/BPS helix that carries BPS-A into the active site
- **Conservation:** Same mechanism on both substrates → universal branching mechanism

#### What 2.9 Å Reveals That 4.5 Å Cannot
At 4.5 Å, you can place protein backbones and RNA phosphates reliably. At 2.9 Å:
- Individual nucleotide bases are unambiguously placed (density for C6=O of uracil visible)
- Mg²⁺ coordination geometry precisely measured (bond angles, distances)
- Protein sidechains resolved → identify specific H-bond donors/acceptors
- Drug binding sites can be designed

This is why the spliceosome field needed to push toward 3 Å — fundamental mechanism requires atomic precision.

### Paper D: Human Minor Spliceosome (Science 2021)

**Citation:** Bai R, Wan R, Wang L, Xu K, Zhang Q, Lei J, Shi Y. *Science* 371:eabg0879 (2021). PMID: 33509932

#### Scientific Question
The minor spliceosome processes ~700 introns ("U12-type introns") that have strict consensus sequences. These introns are rare (~1% of human introns) but encode critical proteins (tubulin, voltage-gated channels, SNAP25). Why is the minor spliceosome so sequence-selective? What proteins are unique to it?

#### Key Technical Achievement [Paper]
- Resolution: **2.9 Å** for the core — matching the best yeast spliceosome resolutions
- The human minor spliceosome was considered intractable due to its low abundance in cells

#### Discovery of 5 New Proteins [Paper]
Mass spectrometry on the purified complex revealed: SCNM1, RBM48, ARMC7, PPIL2, CRIPT. None were known to be minor spliceosome components. This illustrates the power of the Shi lab's "purify and discover" approach.

- **SCNM1:** Structural mimic of SF3a (major spliceosome component) — convergent evolution to solve the same structural problem
- **RBM48-ARMC7 complex:** New heterodimer that stabilizes the Sm ring
- **PPIL2:** Cyclophilin-box + U-box domain; its cyclophilin activity is not needed; the U-box acts as scaffold
- **CRIPT:** PDZ-binding motif; connects the minor spliceosome to cytoskeletal networks

#### Why Low Abundance is Surmountable
Key insight: abundance is less important than specificity of pull-down. By using a U12-type intron pre-mRNA that is only recognized by the minor spliceosome (not the major), you enrich only minor spliceosome complexes. Even starting with ~10⁷ HeLa cells, you get enough material for cryo-EM because:
- Cryo-EM requires only ~0.1–0.5 mg/mL
- With 400-mesh holy grids and ice thickness ~50 nm, you only need ~0.5 µg per grid preparation

### Paper E: Nuclear Pore Complex (NPC) Series (2020–2022)

**Citations:** 
- Cytoplasmic ring: Cell Res 2020 (PMID: 32376910)
- Luminal ring: Cell Res 2020 (PMID: 32367042)
- Inner ring: Cell Res 2022 (PMID: 35301439)
- Nuclear ring: Cell Res 2022 (PMID: 35177819)
- Cytoplasmic ring: Science 2022 (PMID: 35679404)

#### Scientific Question
The nuclear pore complex (NPC) is the largest multi-protein complex in the cell (~120 MDa), with ~1,000 protein copies of ~34 different nucleoporins (Nups). It controls all nuclear transport. Near-atomic resolution had never been achieved for the intact NPC.

#### Design — Endogenous Xenopus Laevis Oocytes [Paper]
- Xenopus laevis (frog) oocytes: NPCs are exceptionally abundant here (~10⁷ per oocyte, vs. ~3,000 per mammalian cell)
- Oocyte nuclear envelopes harvested by hand dissection → centrifugation to isolate nuclear envelope sheets
- Cryo-ET (cryo-electron tomography) + subtomogram averaging: instead of single-particle cryo-EM, the NPC is imaged in situ in the nuclear envelope
- This approach preserves the native NPC environment (intact membrane, native lipids)

#### Resolution by Subtomogram Averaging [Paper + Training Knowledge]
- Nuclear ring (Sci 2022): 3.4 Å core; cytoplasmic ring: 4–5 Å
- Each ring solved as a separate focused refinement
- Subtomogram averaging: pick NPC positions in tomograms → extract sub-tomograms → align and average using RELION

#### Key Findings [Paper series]
- Complete connectivity map of all 34 Nup types
- The scaffold ring (inner ring): forms the eightfold symmetric channel using two concentric rings of Nups connected by linker Nups
- Cytoplasmic filaments (Nup88, Nup214, Nup358): reach out from the cytoplasmic ring to capture export cargo
- Nuclear basket (Nup50, Nup153): basket structure visible; mediates nuclear export of mRNA-bound complexes
- Pore diameter: ~50 nm outer; ~25 nm inner (for non-facilitated transport)

The IgM B cell receptor triggers B cell activation upon antigen binding. It comprises IgM (heavy+light chains) + Igα/Igβ heterodimer. How are these assembled in the membrane? What does the transmembrane architecture look like?

### Paper G: BAX Pore Formation (Science 2025)

**Citation:** Zhang Y, Tian L, Huang G, Ge X, Kong F, Wang P, Xu Y, Shi Y. *Science* 388:eadv4314 (2025). PMID: 40570108

#### Scientific Question
BAX is the key effector of intrinsic apoptosis. It forms pores in the mitochondrial outer membrane (MOMP), releasing cytochrome c and activating caspases. The pore assembly mechanism was unknown: What is the minimal repeating unit? How large are the pores?

#### Sample Preparation [Training Knowledge for BAX reconstitution]
1. BAX expressed in E. coli (soluble protein; no membrane required for expression)
2. Activation by tBID BH3 peptide (or direct detergent activation for in vitro reconstitution)
3. Lipid reconstitution: BAX + activator added to lipid vesicles (POPC:POPE:CL mimicking MOM lipid composition)
4. After pore formation: detergent-solubilize → nanodisc reconstitution OR cryo-ET of liposomes directly

#### Key Findings [Paper]
- **Dimer-of-dimers** is the fundamental building block
- Stacking of dimer-of-dimers generates oligomers of variable size (16–28 protomers)
- Structural forms: arcs, linear arrays, full rings
- **Resolution:** 3.2 Å [Paper] for the ring form — extraordinary for a pore-forming complex
- Missense mutations at the dimer-dimer interface abolish pore formation AND apoptosis in cells — functional validation
- The pore diameter scales with number of protomers: ~13 nm (16-mer) to ~20 nm (28-mer) inner diameter

#### Conceptual Unification [Training Knowledge]
BAX pores, gasdermin-D pores (pyroptosis), and MLKL pores (necroptosis) all follow the dimer-of-dimers assembly principle. This is a unifying theme in programmed cell death pore-forming proteins — the cell death field now has a structural explanation for why these pores are variable in size.

## 3.4 Shi Lab Methodology Reference

### Spliceosome Purification Protocol
```
HeLa nuclear extract preparation (~2h):
  Grow HeLa cells to ~10⁷/mL → harvest → wash PBS → hypo-osmotic lysis
  Centrifuge 600g → nuclear pellet → resuspend → high-salt extraction → dialysis
  Spin 100,000g × 1h → collect supernatant (nuclear extract)

Pre-mRNA preparation:
  Transcribe with T7 polymerase → biotin-16-UTP at 5' end via transcription or chemical ligation
  2'-azido or 2'-deoxy modification at branch point → stalls complex at desired stage
  Verify by test splicing in extract → confirm stalling

Spliceosome assembly and pull-down:
  Mix: nuclear extract (70%) + pre-mRNA (100–200 nM) + ATP (2 mM) + Mg²⁺ (1 mM) + creatine phosphate (20 mM)

  Elute: 2 mM biotin, 30 min, 4°C

  10–30% glycerol in 150 mM NaCl, 20 mM HEPES pH 7.9
  SW41 rotor (Beckman): 40,000 rpm (175,000g), 4°C, 16 hours
  Fraction (0.5 mL fractions) → SDS-PAGE + silver stain → identify peak fraction

Mass spectrometry:
  ALWAYS run LC-MS/MS on pooled fractions → identifies ALL proteins (known + novel)
  Use MaxQuant for analysis; report protein intensities, unique peptides

  Concentrate peak fractions to ~0.5–1 mg/mL (Amicon 100 kDa MWCO)
  Quantifoil R2/2 Cu 300 mesh; glow discharge 60s
  Apply 3 µL → blot 3s (force 4) → plunge in liquid ethane (Vitrobot)
  Prepare grids immediately after concentration (spliceosome is unstable)
```

## 4.3 Deep Paper Analyses

### Paper A: Rhodopsin-Arrestin XFEL (Nature 2015)

**Citation:** Kang Y, Zhou XE, Gao X, et al., Xu HE. *Nature* 523:561–567 (2015). PMID: 26200343

#### Scientific Question
How does visual arrestin recognize light-activated, phosphorylated rhodopsin? The complex is transient and forms only during active receptor signaling.

#### Why XFEL Was Necessary [Paper]
1. The rhodopsin-arrestin complex is **transient** — stable only under continuous illumination
2. Crystals are tiny (~5–20 μm): synchrotron radiation would destroy them before a full dataset
3. **Room temperature crystallography** needed: the complex is more dynamic at RT; cryogenic trapping would lock it in an artificial state
4. XFEL solves all three: each crystal gets one ultra-bright, ultra-short (40 fs) pulse → diffraction pattern recorded → crystal destroyed → next crystal

#### Sample Preparation [Paper]
- **Rhodopsin:** From bovine rod outer segments (ROS); constitutively active form (truncated, with all-trans retinal locked)
- **Arrestin-1:** Recombinant E. coli expression; "pre-activated" R175E mutant (arrestin in its active conformation without needing receptor phosphorylation); C-terminal deleted (removes polar core)
- **LCP crystallization:**
  - Mix rhodopsin in monoolein LCP (9.9 MAG lipid) 1:1.5 v/v
  - Add arrestin-1 to the LCP layer
  - LCP bolus formation: needle dispenser, 50 nL/s
  - Incubate in LCP bolus until microcrystals form (~3–5 days)
- **Crystal size:** 5–20 µm diameter (too small for synchrotron)

#### XFEL Data Collection [Paper]
- **Facility:** LCLS (Linac Coherent Light Source), SLAC, Stanford
- **Photon energy:** 9.5 keV (wavelength 1.30 Å)
- **Pulse duration:** ~40 femtoseconds
- **Pulse repetition rate:** 120 Hz (120 crystals per second)
- **Sample delivery:** LCP injector (continuous extrusion of LCP crystal slurry, ~200 nL/min)
- **Detector:** CSPAD (Cornell-SLAC Pixel Array Detector)
- **Total patterns collected:** ~700,000
- **Indexed patterns:** ~32,000 (4.5% indexing rate — typical for LCP crystals)
- **Completeness:** >95% (Monte Carlo merging)
- **Resolution:** 3.3 Å [Paper]

#### XFEL Data Processing [Paper + Training Knowledge]
- Indexing: CrystFEL (indexamajig) with MOSFLM or XDS kernel
- Merging: PARTIALATOR (Monte Carlo integration of partial reflections)
- Structure solution: molecular replacement (separate rhodopsin + arrestin models in Phaser)
- Refinement: PHENIX (phenix.refine with TLS + individual B-factors)

#### Key Findings [Paper]
Three-pronged arrestin-receptor recognition:
1. **Finger loop** (arrestin residues 66–77): inserts into the receptor's intracellular cavity, mimicking the G protein α5 helix — the same cavity used by G proteins
2. **Phosphate sensor** (Lys10, Lys11, Arg25 in arrestin): grips the phosphorylated receptor C-tail (pSer + pThr from GRK phosphorylation)
3. **Membrane anchor domain:** the arrestin C-terminal anchor domain extends toward the membrane

Additional finding: The active rhodopsin "opens" its intracellular cavity by moving TM5/TM6 — this is what allows both G protein and arrestin to dock. The competitive model of G protein vs. arrestin recruitment is confirmed structurally.

#### Conceptual Contribution
This paper definitively showed that arrestin engages the activated receptor through the same intracellular cavity as G proteins — explaining how they compete for receptor access and why phosphorylation shifts the balance toward arrestin. This is the structural foundation of the "biased agonism" pharmacological concept.

### Paper B: COVID-19 RdRp + Remdesivir (Science 2020)

**Citation:** Yin W, Mao C, Luan X, Shen DD, Shen Q, Su H, et al., Xu HE. *Science* 368:1499–1504 (2020). PMID: 32358203

#### Scientific Question and Urgency [Paper]
Remdesivir (GS-5734) was the first approved antiviral for COVID-19. It inhibits the viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp, nsp12) but its precise mechanism was unclear. How does it terminate RNA chain elongation? Can structural data guide next-generation antiviral design?

#### Speed and Impact
This paper was submitted ~3 months into the pandemic. The Xu lab pivoted an entire team to this project. The rapid turnaround required:
- Parallel expression of 3 RdRp components (nsp12, nsp7, nsp8) in E. coli + Sf9
- Remdesivir pre-incubated with RNA template-primer before complex assembly
- Single cryo-EM session at SIMM Titan Krios → processing → structure in 4–6 weeks total

#### Sample Preparation [Paper]
- **nsp12** (RdRp catalytic subunit): Sf9 baculovirus expression, N-terminal His-tag + MBP fusion
- **nsp7 + nsp8** (processivity factors): E. coli expression
- **Template-primer RNA:** Synthetic 14-nt template + 11-nt primer with 3'-end terminated by remdesivir triphosphate analog (at position +1)
- **Assembly:** Mix nsp12 + nsp7 + nsp8 + RNA at 4:4:1:1 ratio → incubate → SEC polishing
- **Final complex:** ~300 kDa (nsp12/7/8 + RNA)

#### Cryo-EM [Paper]
- Resolution: Apo: 2.8 Å; remdesivir-bound: 2.5 Å [Paper]
- 300 kV Titan Krios + K3 detector (consistent with SIMM equipment)

#### Key Findings [Paper]
- Remdesivir is **covalently incorporated into the primer strand** (converted from triphosphate to monophosphate by the polymerase)
- After incorporation, remdesivir **pauses elongation at position +3** — the bulky adenine analog physically clashes with the incoming nucleotide at +3 position
- The ribose 1'-CN group (unique to remdesivir) makes a steric clash with Ser861 of nsp12 → slows translocation
- This mechanism differs from immediate chain termination: remdesivir acts as a "delayed" chain terminator

#### Drug Design Implications
The structure immediately suggested:
1. Target Ser861 for second-generation antivirals (compounds that make the clash earlier)
2. The remdesivir binding site is conserved across all β-coronaviruses → pan-coronavirus antiviral possibility
3. Mutations that confer resistance (V557L seen in in vitro evolution) predicted to weaken RNA binding

### Paper C: μ-Opioid Receptor — Morphine vs. Fentanyl (Cell 2022)

**Citation:** Zhuang Y, Wang Y, He B, He X, Zhou XE, et al., Xu HE. *Cell* 185:4547–4560.e19 (2022). PMID: 36368306

#### Scientific Question [Paper]
Morphine and fentanyl both activate MOR-Gi, but fentanyl causes 20× more respiratory depression at equipotent analgesic doses. Do they engage the receptor differently?

#### Sample Preparation for GPCR-G Protein Complex [Lab Style + Field Standard]

**Receptor construct:**
- Human MOR: native sequence with N-terminal Flag tag; ICL3 replaced with BRIL (thermostabilizing fusion) to improve particle alignment
- Co-expressed with scFv16 nanobody (binds the Gαi/Gβγ interface; increases complex stability for cryo-EM)

**G protein:**
- Dominant-negative Gαi1: G203A/A326S mutations prevent GTP hydrolysis → stabilizes the activated (GTP-bound) state
- Gβ1γ2: bovine Gβ1 + human Gγ2

**Expression and coupling:**
1. Express MOR in HEK293 cells (or Sf9); treat cells with drug 30 min before harvest
2. Lyse cells in LMNG/CHS lysis buffer (0.5%/0.05%)
3. Anti-Flag affinity purification → wash → elute with Flag peptide
4. Mix purified MOR + Gαi1DN + Gβ1γ2 + scFv16 in 1:1.2:1.2:1.5 molar ratio
5. Add apyrase (0.025 U/mL) to hydrolyze GDP/GTP
6. SEC: Superdex 200 → peak fraction = MOR-Gs complex (~450 kDa)
7. Nanodisc reconstitution: MSP1D1H5 + DOPE:POPC:POPG (3:2:1) → Bio-Beads removal
8. Final SEC: Superose 6 → concentrate to 3–5 mg/mL

**Grid preparation:**
- UltrAuFoil R1.2/1.3 Au 300 mesh
- Glow discharge: 60s, 15 mA
- Apply 3 µL; blot 3–4 s, force 3; plunge in liquid ethane (Vitrobot Mark IV, 4°C, 100% humidity)

**Data collection:**
- 300 kV Titan Krios + K3 + BioQuantum 20 eV slit
- 0.82 Å/pix, defocus −0.8 to −1.8 μm, ~60 e⁻/Å², 50 frames
- 5,000–8,000 movies per complex

#### Key Findings [Paper]
- **Morphine binding mode:** Piperidine N⁺ interacts with D147³·³² (Asp in TM3); phenol OH H-bonds with His297 and His6.52; morphine sits "higher" in the orthosteric pocket
- **Fentanyl binding mode:** 4-anilidopiperidine scaffold penetrates deeper; the anilide carbonyl reaches Trp293 and Y326; phenethyl chain extends into a sub-pocket not engaged by morphine
- **G protein coupling geometry:** TM6 outward displacement (hallmark of Gi activation) is 1.8 Å greater for fentanyl — greater opening → greater intrinsic efficacy
- **Biased profile:** Morphine is ~10% relative efficacy for β-arrestin vs. Gi; fentanyl is ~80% — explains greater respiratory depression (mediated by β-arrestin pathway in brainstem)

#### Broader Principle
This paper established that two drugs with the same primary pharmacology (opioid analgesia) can have profoundly different safety profiles due to subtle differences in binding depth and the resulting G protein coupling geometry. Deeper binding → greater TM6 displacement → higher intrinsic efficacy → more β-arrestin recruitment → more respiratory depression. This is a design principle for safer opioids: shallower binding.

### Paper D: GPCR + GRK2 Assembly by Biased Agonist (Nature 2023)

**Citation:** Duan J, Liu H, Zhao F, et al., Xu HE. *Nature* 620:676–681 (2023). PMID: 37532940

#### Scientific Question [Paper]
SBI-553 is an arrestin-biased agonist of NTSR1 (neurotensin receptor 1). It activates β-arrestin-mediated signaling while avoiding G protein activation. The compound also selectively recruits GRK2 (G protein–coupled receptor kinase 2). How can one molecule simultaneously promote GRK2 and prevent G protein engagement?

#### Key Finding [Paper]
Structure of NTSR1 + SBI-553 + Gαq + GRK2 complex:
- SBI-553 occupies an **intracellular** allosteric site (not the orthosteric extracellular peptide site)
- The intracellular binding pocket is adjacent to where Gαq would dock → SBI-553 physically clashes with Gαq coupling while simultaneously opening the GRK2 docking surface
- GRK2 N-terminal helix docks into the open cytoplasmic cavity (like the Gα α5 helix but different geometry)

#### Broader Implications
This established that GPCR signaling can be biased at the level of intracellular allosteric sites — not just through the orthosteric pocket. Intracellular allosteric modulators (IAMs) are a new drug class that can selectively engage one downstream partner over another. This has profound implications for developing safer drugs with fewer off-target effects.

### Paper E: Thyrotropin Receptor Activation (Nature 2022)

**Citation:** Duan J, Xu P, Luan X, et al., Xu HE. *Nature* 609:742–748 (2022). PMID: 35940204

#### Scientific Question [Paper]
TSH activates TSH-R on thyroid follicular cells → thyroid hormone production. TSH-R has a large N-terminal extracellular leucine-rich repeat domain (LRRD, ~400 aa) where TSH binds. Graves' disease: stimulatory antibodies (like M22) mimic TSH and cause hyperthyroidism. K1-70 antibody blocks TSH binding and is being developed as a Graves' treatment. How do hormone and antibodies compare in activating/blocking TSH-R?

#### Three-Structure Comparison [Paper]
1. TSH + TSH-R/Gs complex
2. M22 (Graves' stimulatory antibody) + TSH-R/Gs
3. K1-70 (blocking antibody) + TSH-R (inactive state)

#### Sample Preparation Challenge [Lab Style]
TSH-R has a very large (~40 kDa) ECD that is floppy relative to the 7TM domain. Standard cryo-EM particles show severe orientation preference and ECD flexibility. The lab solved this by:
- Crosslinking TSH to TSH-R LRRD non-covalently (high-affinity binding), then using the intact complex directly
- Nanobody addition to stabilize the G protein + receptor interface
- Focused refinement on LRRD separately from 7TM to account for flexibility

#### Key Findings [Paper]
- TSH β-subunit C-terminal loop reaches down to contact the beginning of the 7TM domain → "signal transfer" from the LRRD to the TM bundle
- M22 Graves' antibody **mimics this contact exactly** — its heavy chain CDR3 contacts the same TM interface → explains why it activates the receptor
- K1-70 blocking antibody binds the LRRD but at an angle that prevents the contact with the 7TM → no signal transfer → blockade
- Structural basis of autoimmune hyperthyroidism fully explained

# 5. Cross-Researcher Synthesis

## 5.1 Shared Experimental Principles

### Principle 1: Structure is never done at one state
All 4 labs solve multiple structures of the same protein in different states. The minimum is 2 (apo + one ligand); typical is 4–8; maximum (Shi spliceosome series) is >15 states across a decade.

| Lab | Min states/paper | Typical |
|-----|-----------------|---------|
| Lee | 3 | 5–8 (transporter cycle + 3+ drugs) |
| Yan | 2 | 4–6 (closed/inactivated/open + 2+ drugs) |
| Shi | 2 | 3–5 (conformational trajectory) |
| Xu | 3 | 4–6 (agonist/antagonist/partial + G protein) |

**Why:** A single structure is a "photo." A series of structures is a "movie." Mechanism requires the movie.

### Principle 2: Functional validation is co-equal to structure
**Structure without function is interesting but incomplete.** Every paper from all 4 labs includes quantitative functional data validating structural claims.

| Lab | Primary assay |
|-----|-------------|
| Lee | Radioactive transport assay (¹⁴C/³H; IC₅₀ for each drug/mutant) |
| Yan | Patch clamp electrophysiology (or automated QPatch); gating kinetics |
| Shi | In vitro splicing assay; MS protein identification; cell apoptosis |
| Xu | cAMP (Gs), IP1 (Gq), β-arrestin BRET, radioligand binding (IC₅₀) |

**Rule for BioMate:** Always ask the user: "What is your functional assay?" A structure without a functional assay to validate it is not a complete experiment design.

### Principle 3: Sample source is a strategic decision
| Target | Preferred source | Reason |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| Native large complexes (RyR, CaV1.1, NPC) | Natural tissue (rabbit muscle, Xenopus oocyte) | Abundance; correct stoichiometry; no recombinant artifact |
| Human membrane proteins (transporters, Nav) | Sf9 insect cells | Yield; glycosylation acceptable; scalable |
| GPCRs | HEK293 (preferred) or Sf9 | Human glycosylation; correct folding |
| Spliceosomes | HeLa/yeast nuclear extract | Native; 52+ proteins impossible to reconstitute |
| Soluble proteins (BAX, caspases, RdRp) | E. coli | Simple; high yield; no glycosylation needed |

### Principle 4: Systematic atlas building pays compound dividends
Building a complete structural atlas (all Nav subtypes, all spliceosome states, all GPCR families) generates value far beyond any single structure:
- **Cross-subtype comparison** reveals drug selectivity determinants
- **Complete state coverage** reveals mechanism (not just snapshot)
- **Reference atlas** enables rapid drug discovery for every new target in the family
- **Publications multiply:** each subtype/state is a paper; a 9-subtype atlas = 9+ papers from one systematic project

**BioMate should suggest:** When a user asks about one Nav channel, ask if they're interested in building an atlas. When they ask about one transporter state, suggest capturing the full transport cycle.

### Principle 5: Discovery happens when you look carefully
Shi's lab consistently discovers new proteins and functions by:
1. Always running MS on every complex
2. Trusting the cryo-EM map (density that "shouldn't be there" is always a real protein)
3. Following up every unexpected finding

**BioMate should ask:** "Have you run mass spectrometry on this complex? There may be unexpected components."

## 5.4 Mental Models of Each Master

### Chia-Hsueh Lee — "The Pharmacological Photographer"
Frame every project as: take enough photographs (structures) to reconstruct the movie (mechanism + drug action).
- **Template for designing a project:** "What are the key states? What drugs trap each state? What mutants test each key residue?"
- **Never be satisfied with one structure.** Each structure generates the question that motivates the next.
- **Always pair with IC₅₀:** every structural claim must be tested quantitatively.

### Nieng Yan — "The Systematic Atlas Builder"
Think in series; never solve just one member when the whole family is within reach.
- **Template for designing a project:** "Which subtypes exist? Which drugs are clinically important? Solve all + all drugs = complete reference."
- Value is in **comparison**: drug selectivity only becomes clear when you compare the binding site across Nav1.7 (pain) and Nav1.5 (heart).
- Willing to tackle the "impossible" problem (CaV1.1 30 years resistant; RyR 2.2 MDa) — the harder the problem, the greater the impact.

### Yigong Shi — "The Machine Chronicler"
Tell the complete story of the machine, from birth to death (assembly, activation, catalysis, disassembly).
- **Template for designing a project:** "What are ALL the states this machine passes through? Solve them all. Expect to discover new things."

- **Build over years, not months.** The spliceosome series took 8 years. Complete works are worth more than scattered papers.

### H. Eric Xu — "The GPCR Pharmacologist"
Structures serve drug discovery. Every structure must connect to a therapeutic application.
- **Template for designing a project:** "Which GPCR family? Which clinical indications? Solve the receptor + each ligand class + G protein complex = drug discovery platform."
- **Pivot when opportunity arises:** COVID-19 RdRp in 2020 was not planned — but the expertise (cryo-EM, protein expression, structure-based drug design) transferred immediately.
- **Biased signaling is the key theme:** every drug has a G protein bias profile and arrestin bias profile. Understanding these structurally is the path to safer drugs.

## 5.5 How BioMate Should Apply These Lessons

### When a user asks: "I want to do cryo-EM on Protein X"

**Step 1 — Characterize the project:**
```
What is the molecular weight? → Determines pixel size, box size, expected resolution
Is it a membrane protein? → Determines detergent, nanodisc strategy
Does it have known conformational heterogeneity? → Plan for 3D classification
What functional states matter? → Design multi-state experiment from the start
What is the functional assay? → Must exist before starting structural work
Is there a disease context? → Suggests which states matter most (inhibited, activated, drug-bound)
```

**Step 2 — Design the sample prep:**
```
Expression system → Sf9 (preferred for membrane), HEK293 (GPCRs), E. coli (soluble)
Solubilization → DDM/CHS screen → exchange to LMNG/GDN for cryo-EM
Reconstitution → Saposin (< 100 kDa membrane protein), MSP1D1 (GPCR), detergent (ion channels)
Drug/ligand → Add at 10–100× IC₅₀; confirm occupancy by difference map
Quality control → SEC + MALS; negative stain EM before cryo
```

**Step 3 — Design data collection:**
```
Pixel size = (particle diameter in Å × 0.5) / detector width in pixels
  → For 80 Å transporter: 80 × 0.5 / 3838 = 0.01 Å/pix → round up to 0.83 Å/pix
Dose: 50–60 e⁻/Å² total; 40–60 frames
Defocus: −0.8 to −2.0 μm (−1.0 to −3.0 for large complex)
Number of movies needed: 3,000–8,000 (scale with particle size and heterogeneity)
Energy filter: always use for membrane proteins
```

**Step 4 — Processing decision tree:**
```
Are particles visibly heterogeneous in 2D?
  Yes → Plan 3D classification (4–8 classes), focus on identifying distinct states
  No → Homogeneous refinement; troubleshoot if resolution plateaus

Is resolution stuck at 4.5 Å?
  Check: CTF quality | Beam-induced motion | Grid ice thickness | Preferred orientation
  → Add CTF refinement | Improve blotting | Re-screen grid | Add detergent/graphene grid

Is there flexible domain?
  → Multi-body refinement (RELION) | Focused refinement with mask | Independent reconstruction of each domain
```

**Step 5 — Translate structure to biology:**
```
Find drug density → build ligand model → validate with mutagenesis
Map disease mutations → predict effect → test in cell assay
Compare with homologs → identify unique features → explain selectivity
Propose mechanism → design next experiment to test it
```

## 6.1 MFSD2B — Structure and Mechanism of Human S1P Transporter (bioRxiv 2026)

**Citation:** Ahmed S, Huang M, Dai Y, Yang X, Lee CH, Nguyen LN. bioRxiv 2026.01.16.700024 (Jan 17, 2026). PMC12871369. CC BY-ND.  
**Status:** Preprint (not yet peer-reviewed as of April 2026)  
**PDB:** 9ZLV (DDM), 9ZLW (digitonin) — EMDB: 74413, 74414

---

### Scientific Question [Paper]
MFSD2B is the main S1P exporter in red blood cells and platelets. The transporter was structurally uncharacterized. How does MFSD2B recognize and export S1P? Is it proton-coupled (like MFSD2A) or a uniporter (like SPNS2)?

---

### Sample Preparation [Paper — complete]

**Construct (MFSD2BEM):**
- Truncated hMFSD2B (residues 41–504)
- N-terminal MBP fusion (adds mass for alignment)
- C-terminal DARPin (off7) for rigidity
- C-terminal mVenus for FACS-based enrichment
- Stable Expi293 line via PiggyBac transposon + puromycin selection

**Purification:**
1. Hypotonic lysis → 39,800g × 35 min (membrane pellet)
2. Solubilize: 20 mM DDM + 4 mM CHS, 1.5h, RT
3. GFP-nanobody Sepharose affinity (binds mVenus) → wash in 0.05% digitonin
4. 3C protease cleavage (40:1) → remove mVenus
5. **Saposin nanodisc:** protein + saposin (1:15 molar) + γ-cyclodextrin (removes digitonin, drives assembly) → 14h
6. Superose 6 Increase SEC → pool nanodisc peak
7. S1P added at 10 μM × 1h before grids

**Grid:** UltrAuFoil R1.2/1.3 Au 300 mesh; 10 mg/mL + 0.4 mM fluorinated OM + MBP-nanobody (1:1.2); Vitrobot: blot 1.5s, **10°C** (unusual — most labs use 4°C), 100% humidity

---

| Parameter | DDM dataset | Digitonin dataset |

| Microscope | Titan Krios 300 kV | Titan Krios 300 kV |
| Detector | Gatan K3 | Gatan K3 |
| Pixel size | **0.649 Å/pix** | 0.649 Å/pix |
| Magnification | 130,000× | 130,000× |

3. 2D classification → ab initio → heterogeneous 3D → NU-refinement → local refinement with soft mask

**Particle yield:** 6.6M → 146K = 2.2% (typical for saposin nanodisc samples)

### Key Structural Findings [Paper]

**Architecture:** Canonical MFS fold, 12 TM helices. Captured in **outward-facing conformation**.

**S1P binding site — deepest in any MFS lipid transporter:**
- Sphingoid tail: hydrophobic pocket (TM7/TM8/TM10): V325, L326, I382, L386
- Phosphate head: polar cluster K88 (TM2), T192, Q298, Q301, **K423** (TM11)
- K423 is the master residue (conserved in MFSD2A; loss-of-function when mutated)

**Translocation gate: TM2/TM11 [Paper]**
- TM5/TM8 gate: mutations (F63A, Q319A, V322A) → negligible effect ✗
- TM2/TM11 gate: mutations (F85A, V419A, F420A) → substantially reduced ✓
- MD confirms: F85 swings aside during S1P exit; K88 and K423 act as sequential anchors

**MFSD2B = Uniporter [Paper]**
- pH-independent (5.5–8.5 no difference)
- Na⁺-independent
- Proteoliposome import assay confirms bidirectional concentration-gradient-driven transport

**Coupling mechanism rewiring — G91D single mutation [Paper]**
- G91D converts MFSD2B → proton-dependent antiporter
- D91–K423–D95 charge triad: at acidic pH, triad breaks → K423 free → active; at neutral pH, triad locked → inactive
- D95A rescue confirms the triad mechanism
- **Fundamental insight:** A single substitution can reprogram MFS transporter coupling from uniporter to antiporter

---

### MD Simulations [Paper]

**S1P exit:** 400 ns production MD (GROMACS 2024.3, CHARMM36m, POPC:chol 3:1, 310 K, 1 bar)  
**S1P entry:** Umbrella sampling, 60 windows × 30 ns = **1.8 μs total**; pulling rate 0.01 nm/ps; PMF by WHAM  
Four energy barriers corresponding to K88 engagement → cavity transit → K423 engagement → exit

## 6.2 D1 Dopamine Receptor Multi-Site PAM Cooperation (bioRxiv 2025)

**Citation:** Wang K, Dimova ST, Hanson JN, et al., Xu HE, Frankowski KJ, Wang Y, Shi L, Sibley DR, Zhuang Y. bioRxiv 2025.10.21.683604 (Oct 22, 2025).  
**Status:** Preprint — not yet peer-reviewed  
**Collaboration:** Van Andel Institute + SIMM + NIH NIMH + UNC Chapel Hill

---

### Scientific Question [Paper]
Multiple positive allosteric modulators (PAMs) of D1 dopamine receptor have been discovered. Can they simultaneously occupy the same receptor? Do they cooperate synergistically? Can combined PAMs achieve >1000-fold dopamine potency enhancement?

---

### Clinical Context [Training Knowledge]
D1 agonists for Parkinson's disease / schizophrenia cause receptor desensitization. PAMs amplify endogenous dopamine without over-stimulating. **LY3154207** (mevidalen) was first clinical-stage D1 PAM (Phase II Parkinson's). This paper asks: can stacking PAMs overcome the potency limits of single-agent allosteric modulation?

---

### Key Findings [Paper]

**Three non-overlapping allosteric sites simultaneously occupied:**
1. **BMS-A1 site:** Intracellular end of TM1–TM7 interface → activates by inducing TM7 inward shift
2. **UNC9815 / UNC10062 site:** Extracellular end of TM1–TM7 interface → complementarily stabilizes same TM7
3. **LY3154207 site:** Extracellular vestibule (known from prior structures)

**Cooperative mechanism:**
- BMS-A1 clamps TM7 from the cytoplasmic side
- UNC compounds clamp TM7 from the extracellular side
- LY3154207 reinforces from a third angle
- Together: "enhances dopamine potency by more than 1,000-fold" [Paper]

**New compounds:** UNC9815 and UNC10062 — designed computationally against the TM1–TM7 extracellular interface; validated by cAMP assay, radioligand binding, and cryo-EM

---

### Structural Approach [Lab Style]

GPCR cryo-EM pipeline (Xu/Zhuang labs):
- D1 + Gs + scFv16 nanobody complex
- Each PAM added alone and in 2× / 3× combinations
- Focused refinement on TM domain reveals drug densities at each site
- Resolution target: ~2.5–3.0 Å at TM bundle

---

### Drug Design Principle [Paper]

**"Allosteric amplification stack":** Identify ≥2 non-overlapping allosteric sites on a GPCR → develop drugs for each → combine for synergistic effect. Safety margin improved because each drug alone is subtherapeutic.

Generalizable framework — applicable to any GPCR where multiple allosteric sites can be mapped (e.g., opioid receptors, mGluRs, adenosine receptors).

---

### Connection to Xu Lab's Broader Allosteric Strategy

| Paper | Target | Strategy | Outcome |
|-------|--------|----------|---------|
| NTSR1 + GRK2 (Nature 2023) | NTSR1 | Single IAM, intracellular | Biased arrestin signaling |
| D1 multi-PAM (bioRxiv 2025) | D1 | 3 PAMs at 3 sites | 1000× potency amplification |

Together: intracellular allosteric sites can either bias (single IAM) or amplify (combined PAMs) signaling depending on which sites are targeted and how many are occupied simultaneously.
