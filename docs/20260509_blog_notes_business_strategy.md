## Seth Godin: Differentiation

- Stand for one extreme, not the average
- Find the blind spot your competitors share (usually: everyone is targeting the same customer type, same features)
- Build something **incomparable** — if users can directly compare you to a competitor, you're already in a commodity race
- PayPal model: first to be the best at one narrow thing (eBay payments) → expand from there

## Peter Thiel: Niche-First Monopoly

- Start with a niche so small that 100% market share is achievable
- Niche monopoly = pricing power = margin = R&D reinvestment capacity
- "10x better" threshold: users switch when your product is an order of magnitude better on the metric that matters most to them, not 10-20% better
- Monopoly requires doing something that has never been done before in your niche — not a better version of existing

## Three Business Models (Revenue Scaling)

1. **Model 1** — Find 10,000 customers, earn ¥100 each = consumer/mass market (high volume, low margin)
2. **Model 2** — Find 1,000 customers, earn ¥1,000 each = prosumer/SMB (medium volume, medium margin)
3. **Model 3** — Find 100 customers, earn ¥10,000 each = enterprise/B2B (low volume, high margin)

**BioMate is squarely Model 3**: pharma/biotech clients are few, spend is high, ROI is validated by workflow outcome quality.

## YC 2026: Company Brain Concept

The biggest blocker to enterprise AI automation is no longer model capability — it is **domain knowledge access**. Every company has knowledge scattered in people's heads, old emails, Slack threads, support tickets, databases. AI agents cannot operate on unstructured tribal knowledge.

The solution: **Company Brain** — a dynamic map of how the company operates (refund logic, pricing exception rules, on-call response playbooks) extracted from all sources, structured, kept fresh, and made AI-executable. Not a document search chatbot — a dynamic operational knowledge graph.
