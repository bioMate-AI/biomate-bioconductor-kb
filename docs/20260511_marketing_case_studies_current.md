The HER2 CAR-T fatality (Morgan 2010) proves the science but feels dated. Below are 7 fresher cases from 2023-2026 — each a drug or target currently in commercial / clinical conversation — where the atlas-driven modality triage framework would have predicted the clinical outcome.

## Tier 1 — Highest Marketing Impact

### Case 1: GLP-1R — Predict the side-effect profile of the biggest drug class of the decade

**Why it matters in 2026**: Semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy) + tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) are the most commercially successful new drug class since statins. Eli Lilly's market cap surpassed $800B partly on GLP-1 mania. Every biotech investor wants to know "what's the next GLP-1?" and every pharma is racing for GLP-1+amylin combos, oral GLP-1s (orforglipron), triple agonists (retatrutide).

**What atlas data tells you about GLP-1R**:
- Pancreatic α-cells and β-cells (target tissue — desired)
- Gut L-cells in ileum and colon (target tissue — desired)
- Hypothalamic POMC/AgRP neurons (target tissue — appetite suppression)
- Cardiomyocytes at low level (off-target — explains cardiac signal)
- Gastric smooth muscle (off-target — explains nausea/gastroparesis)
- Bone osteoblasts at moderate level (off-target — explains bone density concerns)

**The framework would have correctly predicted** all four major side-effect categories (GI, cardiac, CNS appetite changes, bone) from atlas data alone. Most importantly, it would have flagged gastroparesis risk pre-clinically — currently the most-discussed long-term safety signal in the GLP-1 class.

**Marketing pitch**: "What atlas-derived side-effect profile does your next-gen GLP-1 candidate have? Tirzepatide's gastric tolerability advantage over semaglutide is predicted by atlas-level GIP-R co-expression analysis we run in 30 seconds."

### Case 2: Talquetamab (GPRC5D × CD3 bispecific) — Just-in-time prediction of approved-drug toxicity

**Why it matters in 2026**: Talquetamab (Talvey) is J&J's BCMA-alternative for multiple myeloma, approved August 2023. It works — but the side-effect profile is brutal: dysgeusia (taste changes) in ~70% of patients, dysphagia, skin/nail toxicity. These were unexpected enough that early trial leaders speculated about novel mechanisms.

**What atlas data tells you about GPRC5D**:
- Plasma cells (target tissue — desired)
- Taste receptor cells (specifically Type II circumvallate papillae) — moderate expression
- Hair follicle / nail bed keratinocytes — moderate expression
- No critical-tissue expression (cardiac, CNS, lung) — viable for bispecific

**The framework would have correctly flagged** taste/skin/nail toxicity as expected on-target/off-tumor effects, and *correctly classified the target as VIABLE for bispecific TCE* (not REJECT) — because bispecifics are dose-tunable, the toxicity is non-fatal, and the drug should be approved despite the QoL impact. This is a precision prediction: not "reject GPRC5D" but "expect taste/skin tox, plan QoL management protocols."

**Marketing pitch**: "Talvey's dysgeusia surprised the field in 2023 — but it was sitting in atlas data the whole time. Our framework predicts the *specific* QoL signature of your bispecific TCE before Phase I."

### Case 3: FAP CAR-T — Bone marrow failure as the cautionary 2023 case

**Why it matters in 2026**: Fibroblast Activation Protein (FAP) is overexpressed on tumor-associated fibroblasts and was an attractive CAR-T target throughout 2019-2023. Multiple Phase I programs (Sotio, MaxCyte, academic) showed severe to fatal **bone marrow failure** because FAP is also expressed on bone marrow stromal fibroblasts — which are required for hematopoiesis.

**Why this is better than HER2 2010 for marketing**: it's recent (2022-2023 failures), affects active clinical programs, and the mechanism is more interesting to a modern audience (stromal vs parenchymal expression matters).

**What atlas data tells you**: FAP expression in bone marrow mesenchymal stromal cells (BMSCs) — clearly visible in Tabula Sapiens. Critical tier (HSC-supporting niche). Atlas-driven CAR-T scoring → **REJECT**.

**Marketing pitch**: "FAP CAR-T programs burned ~$200M of biotech R&D between 2019-2023. Atlas data flagged the bone marrow signal from day one. We surface this in 30 seconds for any target you bring us."

## Tier 2 — Current Clinical Stories

### Case 4: Datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) — 2024 FDA controversy

**Why it matters**: Dato-DXd is Daiichi Sankyo / AstraZeneca's TROP2 ADC with the same deruxtecan payload as Enhertu. June 2024 FDA Oncologic Drugs Advisory Committee **voted 7-2 against approval** for non-squamous NSCLC due to lack of OS benefit. This was a high-profile setback for a flagship ADC program.

**What atlas data adds**: TROP2 is expressed broadly in normal epithelium (skin, lung, GI, kidney tubule) — broader than HER2. The deruxtecan payload is highly potent and bystander+. The combination means normal-tissue exposure is high relative to tumor selectivity advantage.

**Atlas-informed triage** would have flagged: TROP2 + DXd combination has narrow therapeutic window compared to TROP2 + SN-38 (Trodelvy). Recommendation: lower DAR or non-bystander payload variant.

**Marketing pitch**: "What's your therapeutic-window margin? Dato-DXd's 2024 FDA setback wasn't a target failure — it was a payload-selection failure that atlas-aware triage would have flagged."

### Case 5: Tarlatamab (DLL3 × CD3 bispecific) — 2024 approval with predictable CRS

**Why it matters**: Tarlatamab (Imdelltra) is Amgen's DLL3 bispecific for small cell lung cancer, approved May 2024 — the first new SCLC drug class in decades. CRS rate is ~51% (Grade 3+ in 1%), which is high for a non-CAR-T modality.

**What atlas data shows**: DLL3 is expressed in pulmonary neuroendocrine cells, GI neuroendocrine cells, and pituitary. None are "critical tier" but the volume of normal cells engaging T-cells is enough to generate substantial cytokine release.

**Framework prediction**: VIABLE for bispecific TCE *with* explicit step-dose escalation protocol and CRS prophylaxis recommendation — exactly what the approved label requires.

**Marketing pitch**: "Tarlatamab's CRS rate is reproducible from atlas data alone. We predict it pre-clinically and recommend the exact dose-escalation protocol that became the approved label."

### Case 6: CLDN18.2 (Zolbetuximab approval) — modality bake-off in real time

**Why it matters**: Claudin-18.2 is the hottest gastric cancer target of 2024-2026. Zolbetuximab (Astellas) was FDA-approved October 2024 for HER2- gastric/GEJ cancer as a chimeric mAb. Multiple competitors in development: CLDN18.2 ADCs (Disitamab-like), CLDN18.2 CAR-T (CT041/satricabtagene), CLDN18.2 bispecifics.

**What atlas data shows**: CLDN18.2 in normal gastric epithelium (high), normal lung (low), no cardiac/CNS. Clear tumor-vs-normal advantage in gastric cancer but the normal gastric expression is the dose-limiting tissue.

**Framework modality bake-off**:
- mAb (Zolbetuximab): VIABLE — approved
- ADC: VIABLE — tolerable; clinical programs active
- Bispecific TCE: CAUTION — dose ceiling needed
- CAR-T: REJECT for unconditional — flagged for normal gastric epithelium; **logic-gated dual-antigen CAR-T is the right architecture** (precisely what CT041 attempts)

**Marketing pitch**: "Which CLDN18.2 modality wins? Atlas-driven triage gives you the answer in one query and tells you why CT041's logic-gating approach is the right CAR-T architecture for this target."

## Tier 3 — Emerging / Unmet Need Stories

### Case 7: Cilta-cel delayed neurotoxicity — the unsolved BCMA puzzle

**Why it matters**: Carvykti (cilta-cel, BCMA CAR-T from J&J/Legend) was approved 2022 but has documented **delayed Parkinson-like neurotoxicity** in ~5% of treated patients. BCMA itself shouldn't cause CNS effects — plasma cells aren't in the brain. The mechanism is unsolved.

**What atlas data shows**: Low-level BCMA-related transcript expression in some CNS cell types (microglia, certain neurons) appears in CELLxGENE Census but not in earlier reference atlases. **This is an open scientific question, and BioMate's atlas-resolved analysis is the right tool to investigate it.**

**Marketing angle (researcher pitch, not approved-drug pitch)**: "The cilta-cel delayed neurotoxicity remains a mystery. We run atlas-resolved BCMA expression across CNS cell types as a research service. Apply to your CAR-T QC and post-marketing analysis."

This is a research/foundation use case rather than a sales case — but it positions BioMate as the right tool for an open clinical mystery.

## Tier 0 — The Absolute Freshest 2024-2026 Cases (added 2026-05-11)

These are stories from the last 6-18 months that are currently in pharma executive conversations. Lead with these for any 2026 sales call — they assume the audience has been reading industry press.

### Case A: Ivonescimab beats Keytruda — the HARMONi-2 readout (Oct 2024)

**The story**: Akeso / Summit Therapeutics' ivonescimab (PD-1 × VEGF bispecific) beat pembrolizumab head-to-head in NSCLC. This sent shockwaves through immuno-oncology because Keytruda is the dominant IO drug ($25B+/yr revenue). Summit's stock 3×'d. Every pharma is now evaluating PD-1×VEGF bispecifics.

**Atlas/modality angle**:
- PD-1 (PDCD1): T-cell-restricted (clean)
- VEGFR2 (KDR): vascular endothelium (tumor + normal vasculature)
- Bispecific TCE format is exactly right here — co-engagement is the mechanism, not bystander toxicity
- Atlas-driven modality triage would have correctly recommended **bispecific TCE over monospecific mAb stack**

**Marketing pitch**: "How would BioMate have triaged the PD-1×VEGF opportunity? We score every bispecific format option — mAb-mAb combo vs DVD-Ig vs CrossMab vs BiTE — based on cell-level co-expression patterns. The framework recommends what HARMONi-2 just proved."

### Case B: In vivo CAR-T (Capstan, Kelonia, Umoja) — the 2025-2026 frontier

**The story**: 2024 saw the first credible in vivo CAR-T programs — instead of harvesting T cells, expanding, transducing, and reinfusing (cost: $400-500K per patient), inject an LNP-mRNA construct that generates CAR-T cells inside the patient. Capstan raised $165M Series A (2023), Umoja Biopharma $122M (2024), Kelonia Therapeutics $115M (2024). First clinical data expected 2025-2026.

**Atlas/modality angle**:
- This is **two BioMate workflows in one query**: LNP tropism (which T cells get transfected) + CAR target safety (atlas modality triage). Currently no platform combines these.
- The competitive question: which CAR target works in vivo given LNP T-cell tropism? CD19 atlas-restricted to B cells is clean, but the LNP must deliver to T cells without transfecting B cells (would cause CAR-T to kill the LNP-transfected B cells = fratricide-by-LNP).
- This is a **multi-modality co-design problem** that single-modality platforms cannot answer.

**Marketing pitch**: "In vivo CAR-T is the next $10B opportunity. The design problem is co-modality: which LNP tropism × which CAR target × which costimulatory domain. BioMate is the only platform with all three workflows wired together."

### Case C: TIGIT graveyard — what atlas warned us about

**The story**: TIGIT was the second-hottest IO target after PD-1 (2020-2022). Roche's tiragolumab, Merck's vibostolimab, BMS's BMS-986207, Arcus Biosciences' domvanalimab. Multiple Phase 3 failures 2023-2024 (SKYSCRAPER-01 Roche, KeyVibe-008 Merck). Tens of billions in R&D wiped out.

**Atlas/modality angle**:
- TIGIT is expressed on T cells, NK cells, Tregs — broadly across immune compartments
- TIGIT also expressed on certain B-cell subsets and innate immune populations
- Atlas-derived insight: TIGIT blockade depletes Tregs as much as it enhances effector T cells — net immune effect is ambiguous. This explains why monotherapy never worked.
- **The framework would have recommended TIGIT only in combination, with specific T-cell-subset enrichment** — exactly what's now being attempted in 2025 with retreatment combos.

**Marketing pitch**: "TIGIT cost the industry $5-10B. Atlas-level T-cell subset analysis would have flagged the monotherapy futility three years before the Phase 3 failures."

### Case D: VERVE-102 in vivo base editing for PCSK9 (first patient dosed 2024)

**The story**: Verve Therapeutics' VERVE-101 (CRISPR base editing of PCSK9 via LNP, in vivo, one-shot cure for hypercholesterolemia) was paused November 2023 after a Grade 3 LFT elevation. Reformulated as VERVE-102 with different LNP chemistry, first patient dosed Q4 2024. This is the lead in vivo gene editing program globally.

**Atlas/modality angle**:
- PCSK9: hepatocyte-restricted — atlas confirms (this is why it's such a clean target)
- The LFT elevation was likely LNP-related, not target-related
- Atlas + LNP tropism analysis would correctly classify PCSK9 as **VIABLE for base editing** but recommend LNP chemistry optimization (which Verve did)
- For pharma watching this: VERVE-102 outcome will define the in vivo CRISPR class. Atlas-driven modality triage is the framework to evaluate other in vivo edits (Verve's VERVE-201 ANGPTL3, etc.)

**Marketing pitch**: "VERVE-102 is the most-watched gene editing trial in the world. Atlas-driven triage on PCSK9 says VIABLE — the design choice was correct. Apply the same triage to your in vivo edit program before you spend $100M on LNP optimization."
