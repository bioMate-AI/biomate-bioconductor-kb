# FDA-Label Eval — n=200 Follow-up (corrects yesterday's n=20 PoC)

**Trigger:** scaled the n=20 PoC to n=200 to get defensible CIs. Some claims
hold, one is corrected.

## Headline numbers (real FDA drug-label nonclinical sections, n=200)

| Model | Synthetic v6 eval (LLM judge) | **Real FDA labels n=200** | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GPT-5** | 0.769 | **0.756** | −1.3pp |
| **Claude Sonnet 4.6** | 0.582 | **0.816** | **+23.4pp** |
| Ranking | GPT-5 ≫ Sonnet (+19pp) | **Sonnet > GPT-5 (+6pp)** | flipped |

±~3pp 95% CI per model at n=200 (binomial). The 6pp Sonnet lead is at the
edge of significance; the 23pp Sonnet underrating by the synthetic eval is
solidly real.

## What the n=20 PoC got right (and what it got wrong)

**Right (confirmed at n=200):**
- The ranking flips. Sonnet > GPT-5 on real FDA labels.
- The synthetic eval was wrong about Sonnet — it underrated Sonnet by ~23pp.
- The production-default-should-be-Sonnet recommendation stands.
- The v7-corpus-from-real-FDA-labels path is still the right direction.

**Wrong (corrected by n=200):**
- The "GPT-5 drops 13pp on real data" claim from the n=20 PoC was **noise**.
  At n=200, GPT-5 only drifts 1.3pp (0.769 → 0.756). The synthetic eval was
  approximately fair to GPT-5; what it got wrong was *understating Sonnet*.
- The "v6 gap to real frontier is 9pp" claim was based on the inflated
  n=20 GPT-5 drop. The real gap is more like:
    - v6 (0.548 synthetic) → estimate ~0.55-0.60 on real data (untested)
    - vs Sonnet (0.816 real) → gap is **20-26pp**, similar to the original
      synthetic-eval claim of 23pp vs GPT-5
- So **v7 training is back to being hard to justify on pure quality**. The
  product story for v7 (sovereignty, per-customer adapter, unit economics)
  still applies, but "we can close the gap" is not a defensible claim.

## Why Sonnet wins on real FDA data (qualitative)

Spot-checked 5 examples where Sonnet beat GPT-5 by ≥0.3 on real labels.
Pattern: Sonnet produces shorter, more reviewer-shaped paragraphs that
match the actual label-style cadence ("Trametinib was not genotoxic in
studies evaluating reverse mutations in bacteria…"). GPT-5 produces
correct-but-verbose paragraphs that read like draft summaries.

The synthetic eval rewarded the GPT-5 style because the synthetic prompts
were generated by GPT-style models, expecting GPT-style responses. The
real FDA labels are written in a sparser, more clinical style that Sonnet
naturally produces.
