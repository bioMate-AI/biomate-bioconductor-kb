{{short description|Biomedical AI research platform}}

'''BioMate AI''' (also referred to as '''BioMate''') is a [[software as a service|software-as-a-service]] [[computational biology|computational biomedicine]] platform developed by BioMate AI, Inc. that enables researchers without specialist bioinformatics training to run complex computational analyses through plain-language requests. The platform uses [[large language model|large language model]]-based agents to route research requests to validated bioinformatics and drug discovery workflows, execute them on cloud infrastructure, apply evidence-graded quality control, and return structured, audit-ready results. Its significance lies in removing the specialist bottleneck that has historically required separate bioinformaticians, computational chemists, and clinical pharmacologists at each stage of a research project. BioMate AI, Inc. was founded in 2025 and is headquartered in [[Houston, Texas]], within the [[Texas Medical Center]].<ref name="llms">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/llms.txt |title=BioMate AI — LLMs.txt index |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>

The platform indexes more than 4,000 validated workflows across 36 biomedical domains, including [[genomics]], [[transcriptomics]], [[drug discovery]], [[structural biology]], [[proteomics]], and [[cryo-electron microscopy]]. It integrates established open-source tools such as [[GATK]], [[STAR (bioinformatics)|STAR]], [[Salmon (software)|Salmon]], [[Seurat]], [[AlphaFold]], [[CryoSPARC]], and [[AutoDock Vina]], covering the full arc from raw sequencing data to regulatory-submission-ready reports.<ref name="about">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/about.html |title=About BioMate AI |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>

== Background ==

Drug discovery and biomedical research depend on dozens of specialized computational tools — [[RNA-seq]] analysis, [[variant calling]], [[molecular docking]], [[ADMET]] profiling, [[physiologically based pharmacokinetic model|PBPK simulation]] — each with distinct software, parameters, and domain expertise requirements. Historically, researchers have needed bioinformaticians, computational chemists, and clinical pharmacologists as separate specialists to operate these tools.<ref name="post1">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/blog-llm-core.html |title=Purpose-Built AI for Science: Coordinating Frontier Models for Reliable Results |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>

Independent research has identified the gap between language model reasoning and deterministic computational execution as a core obstacle in AI-assisted biomedicine. A June 2026 benchmark study from Anthropic (VirBench) evaluated six frontier AI systems on virus sequence retrieval tasks and found that without deterministic tool integration, accuracy ranged from 16.9% to 91.3% across systems with high run-to-run variance; adding a single structured retrieval tool raised all systems above 90% accuracy — demonstrating that reliable biological AI requires validated tool calls, not language model generation alone.<ref name="virbench">{{cite web |url=https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-in-biology |title=Agents in Biology: VirBench Benchmark |website=Anthropic |date=2026-06-08 |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref> A concurrent study published in ''Nature'' described Robin, an AI research system developed by FutureHouse, which demonstrated strong performance in literature synthesis and hypothesis generation but required a separate code execution component (Finch) to perform statistical data analysis tasks such as RNA-seq processing and figure generation — work the language model alone could not reliably complete.<ref name="robin">{{cite journal |author=FutureHouse |title=An AI system for scientific discovery |journal=Nature |date=2026-05-19 |doi=10.1038/s41586-026-10652-y}}</ref>

BioMate AI addresses this execution gap by coupling language model intent-parsing directly to validated, deterministic workflow execution on cloud infrastructure — connecting a researcher's plain-language request to an indexed, containerized pipeline, running it on AWS Batch, applying QC gates to the outputs, and returning structured results. Both the VirBench and Robin findings independently support the architectural premise that language model reasoning and computational execution must be tightly integrated in biomedical AI rather than treated as interchangeable. Beyond workflow execution, the platform also supports literature survey (retrieval-grounded synthesis across PubMed, bioRxiv, ChEMBL, and ClinicalTrials.gov) and experimental protocol generation (step-by-step wet-lab procedures derived from actual run parameters, formatted as SOPs).<ref name="features">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/features.html |title=BioMate AI Features |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-20}}</ref>

== Company ==

BioMate AI, Inc. was founded in 2025 by Yaoyun Zhang, who serves as CEO.<ref name="about"/> The company is based at the [[Texas Medical Center]] in Houston, Texas. The platform is available under a freemium model, with a free tier, a Pro subscription at $49 per month, a Team subscription at $199 per month, and an Enterprise tier with custom pricing.<ref name="pricing">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/pricing.html |title=Pricing — BioMate AI |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>

== Platform ==

=== Workflow routing ===

BioMate uses a hybrid search architecture combining [[word embedding|vector embeddings]], domain-specific scoring, and large language model reasoning to map a natural-language request to the most appropriate workflow in its index.<ref name="features"/> For example, a request to "run differential expression on RNA-seq samples with batch correction" is routed to the [[nf-core]]/rnaseq pipeline followed by nf-core/differentialabundance, with parameters inferred from the user's sample metadata.

=== Execution infrastructure ===

Workflows execute on managed cloud compute infrastructure ([[AWS Batch]]). GPU-accelerated workflows — including [[cryo-electron microscopy|cryo-EM]] reconstruction and [[AlphaFold]] structure prediction — use GPU-equipped instances. Results are stored in cloud object storage and linked to the run provenance record.<ref name="features"/>

=== Quality control and auto-remediation ===

The platform applies configurable [[quality control]] gates to workflow outputs, using a Gold / Silver / Bronze grading system referenced against published community standards. The importance of standardized, tiered QC in high-throughput biomedical data analysis has been established by large-scale consortium projects: the [[ENCODE]] Project formalized tiered data quality standards for genome-wide assays,<ref name="encode">{{cite journal |author=ENCODE Project Consortium |year=2012 |title=An integrated encyclopedia of DNA elements in the human genome |journal=Nature |volume=489 |issue=7414 |pages=57–74 |doi=10.1038/nature11247 |pmid=22955616}}</ref> and clinical sequencing guidelines from the [[American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics|ACMG]] established minimum QC thresholds for next-generation sequencing in diagnostic contexts.<ref name="acmg">{{cite journal |last=Rehm |first=Heidi L. |year=2013 |title=ACMG clinical laboratory standards for next-generation sequencing |journal=Genetics in Medicine |volume=15 |issue=9 |pages=733–747 |doi=10.1038/gim.2013.92 |pmid=23887774}}</ref> When a gate fails — for example, a [[hERG]] flag in an ADMET screen, or a low resolution estimate in cryo-EM reconstruction — the agent proposes a parameter change or alternative approach and re-runs automatically without manual intervention.

=== Drug discovery workflows ===

BioMate supports multiple phases of the drug discovery process, including:

* '''Target identification''' — [[AlphaFold]] structure prediction, pocket druggability analysis via fpocket, and CRISPR essentiality scoring using [[DepMap]] data<ref name="drug1">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/blog-drug-1-target.html |title=Before the First Compound: Computational Target Discovery and Validation |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>
* '''Virtual screening''' — large-scale [[molecular docking]] with [[AutoDock Vina]] and pharmacophore filtering<ref name="drug2">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/blog-drug-2-hit.html |title=From Chemical Space to Hit List: AI-Guided Virtual Screening at Scale |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>
* '''ADMET profiling''' — prediction of absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity properties, including CYP inhibition and [[hERG]] liability<ref name="drug3">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/blog-drug-3-admet.html |title=The ADMET Bottleneck: Profiling Drug-Like Properties Before Synthesis |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>
* '''PBPK simulation''' — [[physiologically based pharmacokinetic model]]ing for first-in-human dose estimation<ref name="drug4">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/blog-drug-4-preclinical.html |title=From Animal Studies to IND Filing: Computational Preclinical Development |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>
* '''IND document assembly''' — automated drafting of FDA [[Investigational New Drug]] application sections §2.6.1–§2.6.5 from underlying study data<ref name="drug4"/>
* '''Bispecific antibody format triage''' — scoring of bispecific antibody formats (CrossMab, IgG-scFv, [[BiTE antibody construct|BiTE]], DART, [[knobs-into-holes|KiH]]) across six axes including CMC complexity, immunogenicity, and manufacturability<ref name="blog-bispecific">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/blog-bispecific-triage.html |title=Bispecific Format Triage |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>
* '''In vivo CAR-T co-design''' — simultaneous design of CAR target, [[lipid nanoparticle]] tropism, and CAR construct with cross-referenced fratricide safety checking<ref name="blog-cart">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/blog-invivo-cart.html |title=In Vivo CAR-T Co-Design |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>
* '''GLP-1 receptor modality selection''' — multi-axis scoring of GLP-1 receptor agonist candidates across pharmacology, receptor profile, liver selectivity, manufacturing complexity, and clinical evidence<ref name="blog-glp1">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/blog-glp1-modality.html |title=GLP-1 Modality Bakeoff |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>
* '''Modality triage''' — a two-workflow chain querying single-cell expression atlases to recommend therapeutic modality (mAb, bispecific, [[antibody-drug conjugate|ADC]], [[CAR T cell|CAR-T]], [[PROTAC]], [[antisense oligonucleotide|ASO]], or small molecule) from target expression data<ref name="blog-modality">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/blog-modality-triage.html |title=Modality Triage from Atlas |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>

=== Bioconductor integration ===

BioMate incorporates 1,818 [[Bioconductor]] packages, making bioinformatics statistical methods available through plain-language requests without requiring users to write code.<ref name="blog-bioconductor">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/blog-bioconductor.html |title=The R Ecosystem, Accessible to Everyone: Bioconductor Methods in BioMate |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>

The Bioconductor ecosystem — comprising over 2,300 R packages for bioinformatics — presents a known challenge for large language models: the APIs are complex, heavily object-oriented (S4 class system), and underrepresented in general-purpose training corpora, leading to high rates of hallucinated or non-functional code generation. To address this, BioMate has published BioMate-KB, a knowledge base of 15,641 real-execution-validated workflow steps extracted across the Bioconductor 3.20 ecosystem, annotated with NAMESPACE validation, EDAM ontology terms, and container links.<ref name="biomatekb">{{cite web |url=https://zenodo.org/record/20616355 |title=BioMate-KB: A Real-Execution-Validated Workflow Knowledge Base for Bioconductor |last=Zhang |first=Yaoyun |year=2026 |publisher=Zenodo |doi=10.5281/zenodo.20616355 |access-date=2026-06-20}}</ref> The top 200 packages — collectively accounting for 57% of all Bioconductor downloads — have been released as an open-access GitHub repository providing structured, machine-readable workflow definitions reusable by AI systems, workflow engines, and educators. The repository has received more than 400 community stars.<ref name="bioconductor-kb">{{cite web |url=https://github.com/bioMate-AI/biomate-bioconductor-kb |title=biomate-bioconductor-kb: Structured Bioconductor Workflow Definitions |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>

A companion preprint introduces an execution-grounded validation hierarchy addressing how such knowledge bases should be validated. It finds that structural grounding (NAMESPACE verification, S4 type checking, vignette cross-referencing) raises LLM function-citation accuracy from 71.4% to 88.2%, but that real end-to-end execution on representative data remains necessary as the final ground truth.<ref name="structure-grounding">{{cite web |url=https://zenodo.org/record/20616543 |title=Structure Grounding Is Not Enough: Real Execution as the Ground Truth for LLM-Generated Bioinformatics Workflows |last=Zhang |first=Yaoyun |year=2026 |publisher=Zenodo |doi=10.5281/zenodo.20616543 |access-date=2026-06-20}}</ref>

=== Data integration and connectors ===

The platform supports connections to laboratory instruments and information systems, including [[Illumina]] BaseSpace, [[Oxford Nanopore Technologies|Oxford Nanopore]] MinKNOW, Benchling [[Laboratory information management system|LIMS]], and standard laboratory instruments (flow cytometers, LC-MS, qPCR, plate readers). It implements the [[Model Context Protocol]] (MCP) for integration with compatible AI assistant environments.<ref name="llms"/>

== Compliance and security ==

BioMate AI states that the platform is [[Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act|HIPAA]]-compliant and offers Business Associate Agreement (BAA) support for healthcare and life science enterprise customers. A private [[virtual private cloud|VPC]] deployment option is available for customers requiring data residency controls. The company states that tenant data is not used to train shared AI models.<ref name="privacy">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/privacy.html |title=Privacy Policy — BioMate AI |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>

== Benchmarks ==

BioMate publishes benchmark results on its website, reporting 94.6% workflow routing accuracy, a 100% pass rate on PBPK validation cases, and an 87.1% score on regulatory language model evaluation.<ref name="benchmarks">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/benchmarks.html |title=Benchmarks — BioMate AI |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref>

The platform's modality triage workflow chain correctly predicted the therapeutic modality for seven of seven oncology drugs approved by the [[Food and Drug Administration|FDA]] in Q4 2024, using only the target gene name and indication as input.<ref name="blog-modality"/>

== Relationship to existing platforms ==

BioMate leverages open-source community resources extensively. The platform can accommodate existing [[Nextflow]] scripts and [[Galaxy (computational biology)|Galaxy]] workflows, allowing researchers to bring established pipelines and run them through BioMate's AI-guided interface.<ref name="compare">{{cite web |url=https://biomate.ai/compare.html |title=BioMate AI vs Galaxy vs Nextflow — Feature Comparison |publisher=BioMate AI |access-date=2026-06-19}}</ref> Community tools such as [[GATK]], [[STAR (bioinformatics)|STAR]], [[DESeq2]], [[AlphaFold]], and the full [[nf-core]] pipeline catalog are available through plain-language requests. The platform also integrates community reference databases including [[gnomAD]], [[ClinVar]], [[GTEx]], [[Reactome]], [[KEGG]], [[STRING]], and [[OpenTargets]] as queryable resources within workflows.<ref name="compare"/>

BioMate also covers drug discovery and laboratory informatics workflows that overlap with commercial offerings from [[Schrödinger (company)|Schrödinger]], [[Dotmatics]], and [[Benchling]], with a focus on AI-orchestrated execution across the full discovery pipeline rather than standalone desktop tools.<ref name="compare"/>
