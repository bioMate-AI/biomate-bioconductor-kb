# How targets are selected — short answer

*In response to Dr. Mohan's question on which channels are used for target selection.*

Target selection does not rely on a single channel such as pathway analysis. It combines several lines of
evidence, **anchored on human genetics**: genetic causal support (GWAS/eQTL, Mendelian randomization); whether
perturbing the gene shifts the disease signature; pathway and gene-regulatory activity; protein-interaction
context; cell-type-specific expression; and — closest to the Lab's data — proteomic and autoantibody evidence.
Known disease–gene associations enter only as a soft prior.

Each channel is scored against its own matched negative control, so a hit reflects biology rather than an
abundance or connectivity artifact. Genetics anchors the ranking, the other channels add or subtract support, and
druggability is a final filter for what can be made into a therapeutic.

Every nominated target comes with an **evidence breakdown** — which channels support it and how strongly — so the
basis for each nomination is visible rather than a black-box score.
