## 0 · The one finding that gates everything federal (read first)

**The US+China corporate structure is, as currently described, a categorical bar to all US federal SBIR/STTR (NIH, NSF, ARPA-H).**

The **Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act of 2026** (S.3971, signed **2026-04-13**, reauthorizing SBIR/STTR through 2031 after the program lapsed 2025-09-30) tightened foreign-risk rules. NIH implemented it via **NOT-OD-26-074**. NIH SEED states HHS **"cannot make an award"** if the small business has "a business entity, parent company, or subsidiary located in the People's Republic of China or another foreign country of concern," applied on every application with **no opportunity to remediate before award**.
- [NIH SEED foreign disclosure & risk management](https://seed.nih.gov/small-business-funding/small-business-program-basics/foreign-disclosure-and-risk-management) (accessed 2026-10-05); [NOT-OD-26-074](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-26-074.html) (accessed 2026-10-05).
- The same "foreign country of concern" regime applies to **NSF SBIR** and **ARPA-H**.

**Implication for this survey.** Part 5A (US federal grants) is scientifically a strong fit but is **blocked unless corporate counsel can cleanly firewall the US entity** (no China subsidiary/parent, ≥51% US-citizen/PR ownership, no key personnel with a *current* PRC institutional affiliation). This is a corporate-structure decision that must precede any US federal grant spend. It does **not** affect: China local grants, Wellcome Leap (apply via US entity), open-data consortia, compute credits, pharma open-innovation programs, or challenges. Those remain actionable now. (Source: grants research pass, 2026-10-05.)
