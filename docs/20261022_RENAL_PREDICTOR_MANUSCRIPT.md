# A curated cross-target radioligand renal-biodistribution dataset, and why structure-based renal-retention prediction does not yet transfer across targets

## Abstract

Renal retention is a dose-limiting toxicity of peptide-receptor radioligand therapy: filtered radiopeptides are reabsorbed in the proximal tubule and the residualizing radiometal is trapped, so for many targeted radioligands the kidney, not the tumour, caps the deliverable activity. The determinants are mechanistically understood — glomerular filtration followed by megalin/cubilin-mediated reabsorption, modulated by net charge, molecular size, cleavable linkers and albumin binding — yet this knowledge is applied empirically, one construct at a time. We asked whether renal retention, unlike target binding, could be learned from structure **once** and transferred across molecular targets, framing a radioligand's behaviour as a *target-specific binding* × *target-agnostic pharmacokinetic (renal)* decomposition. We assemble a structured, renal-protection-stratified, cross-target radioligand renal-biodistribution dataset (163 constructs across eleven target classes, each with provenance, structure, and a mandatory, never-imputed renal-protection flag) and a leave-one-target-out (LOTO) evaluation protocol. Our findings are primarily negative, and we report them as such. **(1)** The field-standard signed net-charge heuristic does **not** rank held-out-target retention (LOTO mean-per-class Spearman ρ ≈ 0), the expected consequence of the known non-monotonic (U-shaped) charge dependence. **(2)** A structure-based learned model does **not** transfer across targets either at this data scale: after correcting a charge-feature computation artifact (below), the cross-target LOTO signal is null-to-negative (construct-level ρ = −0.26, 95% CI [−0.55, +0.09], permutation p = 0.11), and **four pre-registered improvement analyses** — a radiometal/chelator-confound-controlled stratum, a fixed-timepoint analysis, a fuller feature set with fair baselines, and model selection by nested CV — are **all null**. **(3) A cautionary methodological finding:** an earlier version of this analysis reported a significant positive transfer (ρ = 0.27, p = 0.036); we traced it to an artifact in the dominant charge feature (a free N-terminal amine counted on peptides whose α-amine is amide-conjugated to the chelator), and it does not survive correction — a concrete illustration of how charge-feature bugs, which co-vary with target class, can manufacture apparent cross-target signal. We nonetheless contribute the curated dataset, the LOTO protocol with leakage and confound controls, and an interpretable mechanistic grey-box that formalizes the experimentally-established two-route reabsorption biology. The binding constraint is **data** (small n, charge-degenerate within-target constructs, single-timepoint sparsity), not model or feature choice; we specify the scale and the controlled wet-lab test required to settle the question. Data and code are released.

## 1. Introduction

Radioligand therapy (RLT) delivers a cytotoxic radionuclide to tumour cells via a target-binding vector. Its therapeutic index is frequently set not by tumour uptake but by the **kidney**: small filtered radiopeptides are reabsorbed in the proximal tubule by the megalin/cubilin endocytic system and the residualizing radiometal–chelate is retained in lysosomes. Reported kidney doses for ^177Lu radiopeptides vary widely between studies even for a single agent (e.g. ^177Lu-PSMA-617 spans ≈0.4–0.7 Gy/GBq across cohorts), and early αvβ6-directed radiopeptides sit in a similar range; the point is that kidney, not tumour, is often dose-limiting, not a precise per-agent ranking, which the biodistribution literature does not support at single-study precision.

The biology of this retention is mature. Glomerular filtration followed by megalin-mediated reabsorption is established across structurally unrelated radiopeptides (ref. Vegt 2010/2011); the modulating levers — net charge, molecular size, basic-residue content, cleavable linkers, albumin binders, and amino-acid/gelofusine co-infusion — are individually quantified; and the charge dependence is known to be **non-monotonic**: for the α-MSH series, renal uptake is lowest near net charge −1 and rises at both positive and strongly negative charge (ref. Bapst & Eberle 2017), with highly anionic poly-glutamate minigastrins among the most retained radiopeptides. Two distinct reabsorption routes — a cationic, lysine-inhibitable route and an anionic, poly-glutamate-inhibitable route — have been demonstrated experimentally (ref. Melis 2007). (These are prior art; we formalize, not discover, them.)

Despite this, renal optimization remains empirical medicinal chemistry. The computational-prediction literature does not fill the gap: structure-based renal predictors exist for conventional small-molecule drugs but predict whole-body clearance and are target-blind (e.g. Dave & Morris 2015); radioligand kidney-dose models that are genuinely radiopharmaceutical are driven by per-patient PET imaging and are single-target (PSMA), so they cannot evaluate a molecule that has not been synthesized and imaged (e.g. Yazdani 2025). Mechanistic PBPK models carry a kidney compartment but treat retention as a lumped, empirical parameter fit per case and are fit to a single target class (e.g. Zaid 2021; Kletting 2016).

**The question** is therefore whether a model can take a radioligand's molecular structure and predict its kidney retention in a way that **generalizes across molecular targets** — plausible in principle because renal handling, unlike target binding, is a shared physicochemical process (the same megalin system reabsorbs radiopeptides for unrelated receptors), which we make explicit as a binding × PK decomposition. **This paper reports that, at the data scale currently available, the answer is no** — neither the net-charge heuristic nor a corrected structure-based model transfers — and provides the dataset, protocol, controls and cautionary finding needed to pursue the question properly.

**Contributions.** (1) A structured, cross-target, renal-protection-stratified radioligand renal-biodistribution dataset (163 constructs, eleven target classes) with per-record provenance. (2) A LOTO evaluation protocol with construct-level estimand, cluster-bootstrap CIs, within-class permutation tests, a sequence-cluster-out leakage control, and a pinned, reproducible bootstrap. (3) The **negative result** that structure-based renal retention does not transfer across targets at this scale (net-charge heuristic null; corrected learned model null-to-negative; four pre-registered improvement analyses all null) — and the **cautionary methodological finding** that the apparent positive in an earlier analysis was a target-class-correlated charge-feature artifact. (4) An interpretable mechanistic grey-box that formalizes the known two-route reabsorption biology. We are explicit about what is *not* novel (the charge non-monotonicity and two-route biology are prior art, cited) and about the study's data-limited scale.

## 2. Results

### 2.1 A cross-target, protection-stratified renal-biodistribution dataset

We curated kidney biodistribution for 163 radioligand constructs spanning eleven target classes — PSMA (19), SSTR2 (18), GRPR (14), FAP (23), integrin αvβ6 (18), integrin αvβ3 (17), MC1R (10), CCK2 (33), NTS1 (5), GLP-1R (4) and FOLR1 (2) — from primary literature. Each construct carries structure (one-letter sequence and/or canonical SMILES), chelator, radiometal, albumin-binder and cleavable-linker flags, and a **mandatory renal-protection status that is never imputed** (mixing amino-acid/gelofusine-protected with unprotected measurements silently corrupts any charge model). We treat each (construct, timepoint) measurement as one observation with imaging timepoint as a covariate; absolute labels are never pooled across units (Gy/GBq vs %ID/g) or across timepoints. The quantitative analyses use the **mouse %ID/g** subset (116 constructs); after restricting to constructs whose net charge is computable from an identified (chelator, radiometal) and an available structure, **52 constructs enter the charge model and 49 across 7 classes feed the LOTO** (see §2.3 for why 11 classes become 7). Net charge at pH 7.4 is computed deterministically: Henderson–Hasselbalch over ionizable side chains and termini for peptides, plus a **metal-complex charge keyed on the (chelator, radiometal oxidation state)** pair (an unknown combination — e.g. ^99mTc-N4, ^99mTc-HYNIC — is flagged and the construct excluded rather than guessed); a chelator-aware group-contribution calculation on SMILES for small molecules.

**Data-quality disclosures (for honest reuse).** Of 163 rows, 12 lack a resolvable DOI/PMID in the provenance string; verification tiers are 150 primary / 10 cited / 2 abstract / 1 secondary. Nine `construct_id`s recur (e.g. ^177Lu-PSMA-617 appears 4×) — these are **legitimate independent dosimetry measurements of the same agent across studies** (e.g. VISION 0.42, Uijen 0.49, Herrmann 0.43, a further 0.67 Gy/GBq), not data-entry duplicates; they are not yet given distinct suffixed IDs, so any id-keyed join keeps only one, and users should suffix them. "163 constructs" is therefore an upper bound on distinct molecules.

### 2.2 The net-charge heuristic does not rank held-out-target retention

Under leave-one-target-out (LOTO) cross-validation — train on all target classes but one, predict the held-out class, score by the **construct-level** mean within-held-out-target Spearman correlation (median-aggregated per construct so repeated timepoints of one molecule cannot pseudo-replicate; pooling across classes is confounded by per-class scale) — the field-standard **signed net-charge heuristic has mean-per-class ρ ≈ 0.00 (95% CI [−0.33, +0.31], permutation p = 0.999): no better than chance.** This is the expected consequence of the known charge non-monotonicity: a single signed-charge term cannot rank retention when both cationic and anionic ligands are retained.

### 2.3 A structure-based model does not transfer across targets at this scale

A gradient-boosted model on interpretable physicochemical features (net positive/negative charge, residualizing metal, albumin binder, cleavable linker, imaging timepoint, modality) **does not generalize to unseen target classes**: construct-level LOTO mean-per-class **ρ = −0.26 (95% CI [−0.55, +0.09], permutation p = 0.11)** — null-to-negative, CI spanning zero (Fig. 2). Per-class held-out ρ are inconsistent in sign (CCK2 +0.45; αvβ3 −0.32, αvβ6 −0.24, GRPR −0.29, SSTR2 −0.34, MC1R −0.62, GLP-1R −0.45), i.e. the model learned on training targets does not rank constructs within a new target. (NTS1 drops out of the corrected LOTO: its charge-bearing constructs are the ^99mTc-N4 Demotensin series, whose complex charge is flagged as unknown and excluded rather than guessed.) The analysis uses 49 constructs across 7 classes; the four-class-with-≥3-constructs structure and the sign inconsistency are both symptoms of small, heterogeneous data.

**Cautionary note — an earlier positive was a charge-feature artifact.** A prior version of this analysis reported a *significant* positive transfer (ρ = 0.27, 95% CI [0.01, 0.51], permutation p = 0.036). On audit we traced it to the dominant charge feature: a free N-terminal α-amine (≈ +0.8 at pH 7.4) was being counted on peptides whose α-amine is in fact **amide-conjugated to the chelator** (the norm for DOTA-TATE/-TOC and most N-terminally conjugated radiopeptides), a uniform ~+1 over-count. Because conjugation chemistry co-varies with target class, this injected a target-class-correlated signal that inflated apparent cross-target transfer. Correcting it (together with radiometal-aware complex charge, acyclic-chelator exclusion, disulfide-Cys and alcohol-C-terminus handling), and computing the estimand per construct rather than per (construct, timepoint), removes the effect entirely; dropping the corrected charge feature now *raises* the LOTO score, confirming it no longer carries signal. We report this as a concrete field hazard: charge-feature bugs that correlate with target class can manufacture cross-target results.

### 2.4 Pre-registered improvement analyses — all null

Because the corrected result was weaker than hoped, we pre-registered (before running; see `docs/20261024_MANUSCRIPT_COMMITTEE_REVIEW.md`) a fixed set of four principled improvements, each run once and reported in full to avoid post-hoc selection:
1. **Radiometal/chelator-confound-controlled stratum** (^177Lu-DOTA only): nominally feasible by count (SSTR2 7, GRPR 4, αvβ6 3) but αvβ6's three constructs are charge-degenerate A20FMDV2 variants (undefined within-class rank), collapsing it to an effectively 2-class, n=11 test — **no signal** (CI spans zero).
2. **Fixed modal timepoint (4 h):** collapses the primary model to ≤1–3 rankable classes — **underpowered, no signal**.
3. **Fuller features + fair baselines:** adding MW, basic-residue count and a receptor-kidney-expression flag moves the GBM to ρ = +0.02 (p = 0.88); every single-feature baseline (signed/|charge|, MW, basic-residue, timepoint-only) is non-significant (logD and TPSA were not computable for this corpus and were skipped, not fabricated) — **no signal over a fair baseline**.
4. **Model selection by nested CV:** GBM (−0.26), ridge-linear (+0.23) and the mechanistic grey-box (+0.14) **disagree in sign** and none is significant — sign instability across learner classes is itself evidence of no robust signal.

**No pre-registered analysis produced a construct-level signal whose pinned cluster-bootstrap CI excludes zero at p < 0.05.** The binding constraint is data, not model or feature choice.

### 2.5 An interpretable mechanistic grey-box formalizes the two-route biology

We cast the experimentally-established reabsorption biology into a differentiable grey-box: log kidney %ID/g as a sum of a saturable cationic (megalin) route in net positive charge, a saturable anionic route in net negative charge, a residualizing-metal term, an albumin-binder term, a cleavable-linker term, a timepoint term and a small-molecule-route term. Fit across all targets, every term takes a physically sensible sign (both charge routes positive — reproducing the known U-shaped charge dependence as explicit mechanism). We note explicitly that the model **saturates in charge magnitude, not in administered molar dose**, so its two half-saturation constants are phenomenological fitting parameters, **not** mechanistic Michaelis constants and not "physiological" in a dose sense. Under LOTO the grey-box is **not significant** (construct-level ρ = +0.14, 95% CI [−0.18, +0.45], p = 0.40) and does not outperform the black-box model; its value is interpretability and mechanistic transparency, not predictive transfer.

### 2.6 Ablations under the corrected model

Repeating LOTO with feature groups removed (corrected GBM, observation-level, full = −0.20): removing the (corrected) **net charge** feature *raises* the score (Δ = +0.18) — it no longer carries signal — while **imaging timepoint** is now the largest-magnitude feature (Δ = −0.14 on removal), consistent with much of any apparent structure in the data being the within-molecule time trend rather than cross-construct retention ranking. This reverses the earlier (artifact-era) ablation in which charge appeared dominant.

### 2.7 Leakage and confound controls (methodology that stands regardless of outcome)

The protocol includes a **construct-level** estimand (no timepoint pseudo-replication), a **cluster bootstrap by construct**, within-class permutation tests, and a **leave-one-sequence-cluster-out** split (single-linkage by sequence 3-mer Jaccard ≥ 0.5; small molecules by chemotype) to guard against RGD-motif leakage between αvβ3/αvβ6. The bootstrap is now **pinned and reproducible** (seed and n_boot fixed; an earlier irreproducibility — CIs that shifted run-to-run — was root-caused to set-ordering under the Python hash seed and fixed). These controls are the paper's durable methodological contribution; they are what make the negative result trustworthy rather than an under-powered accident.

### 2.8 Why we do not present an in-silico design application

A fitted mechanistic grey-box can be made to reproduce the literature U-shaped charge-vs-retention design rule — but this is **circular**: the U-shape is built into the two positive-coefficient saturable routes, so recovering it is a consistency check on the functional form, not a validation or a prediction. Given the null cross-target transfer (§2.3–2.4), we **do not** use the model for prospective renal-engineering design in this paper; doing so would overstate a model that does not generalize. The established design levers (net charge toward −1 to 0; brush-border-cleavable linkers, −70 to −77% in specific preclinical series; Trachsel 2023, Bendre 2020) remain the medicinal-chemistry prior art, applied by hand, not inferred here.

## 3. Methods

**Dataset curation.** Primary-literature search across eleven target classes; extraction restricted to values stated in tables or verbatim text, with a separately-flagged, lower-confidence figure-digitized tier used only in sensitivity analysis. Records carry DOI/PMID provenance where available (12/163 lack one; see §2.1), species, timepoint and a mandatory renal-protection flag; the same construct measured in multiple tumour models is aggregated to one point (median) to avoid pseudo-replication. Independent dosimetry measurements of the same agent across studies are retained as separate observations and should be suffix-ID'd.

**Charge computation.** Peptides: Henderson–Hasselbalch net charge at pH 7.4 over ionizable side chains and termini, with (i) the N-terminal α-amine suppressed when the chelator is N-terminally amide-conjugated, (ii) disulfide cysteines treated as non-ionizable, (iii) alcohol C-termini (e.g. Thr-ol) carrying no C-terminal carboxylate, plus (iv) the **metal-complex charge keyed on (chelator, radiometal oxidation state)**; unknown (chelator, oxidation) pairs are flagged and excluded. Small molecules: group-contribution Henderson–Hasselbalch over SMARTS-matched ionizable groups with the chelator (macrocyclic *and* acyclic) donor atoms excluded and replaced by the complex charge; phosphonates modeled as diprotic. Paper-stated net charge overrides computation where terminal modifications are not sequence-encoded.

**Models.** Baselines: signed net charge, |net charge|, MW, basic-residue count, timepoint-only. Learned model: gradient-boosted trees on the interpretable feature set; ridge-linear as a low-variance comparator. Mechanistic grey-box: a bounded, ridge-regularized two-route saturable model fit by least squares on log %ID/g.

**Evaluation.** Leave-one-target-out cross-validation; primary metric **construct-level** mean within-held-out-target Spearman (median-aggregated per construct); cluster bootstrap by construct for 95% CIs (pinned seed, n_boot = 2000); within-class permutation tests for p-values; models compared on a common set of classes all can rank. Sequence-cluster-out and temporal splits as robustness. Ablation by feature-group removal. All seeded and reproducible via the released `renal_paper_results.py`.

## 4. Discussion

At the data scale currently available, **structure-based renal-retention prediction does not transfer across molecular targets** — neither the field-standard net-charge heuristic (ρ ≈ 0) nor a corrected learned model (ρ = −0.26, p = 0.11), and no pre-registered improvement (confound-controlled stratum, fixed timepoint, fuller features, model selection) recovers a signal. We initially observed an apparent positive and, on audit, found it to be a target-class-correlated charge-feature artifact; correcting it removed the effect. This is consistent with the possibility that renal handling *is* a shared physicochemical process (the biology supports it) but that the present cross-target corpus is too small, too charge-degenerate within targets, and too timepoint-sparse to learn the mapping — i.e. the result is a **data-limited negative, not a refutation of the biology**. It is equally a caution: the "target-agnostic PK term" is only partly target-agnostic, since receptor-mediated renal uptake (PSMA, SSTR2, GRPR are kidney-expressed) and target-linked metabolic stability (e.g. NTS1 Demotensin analogues) contribute retention that megalin-reabsorption features do not capture.

What survives as a contribution is the **resource and the method**: a provenance-tracked cross-target dataset, a leakage- and confound-controlled LOTO protocol with a pinned bootstrap, an interpretable mechanistic grey-box, and a documented, reproducible account of a charge-feature artifact that the field should guard against. The honest headline is that the net-charge heuristic fails *and* naive structure-ML does not yet beat it cross-target — a useful calibration of expectations for radioligand renal prediction.

**Limitations.** Sample size is ≈50 charge-resolvable constructs across 7 classes in the LOTO; several classes have 2–4 constructs and some are charge-degenerate (e.g. αvβ6 A20FMDV2 variants). Much published kidney biodistribution exists only in access-gated figures; a bounded two-reader figure-digitization attempt yielded only seven defensible records (held in a separate tier, not in the primary analysis). We also observed a data-quality hazard: in two papers, programmatically-retrieved text/supplementary values contradicted the plotted figures, so figure reads were treated as authoritative. The label is mouse %ID/g at an imaging timepoint — a concentration, **not an absorbed dose**: it ignores kidney-mass scaling, time-integration and radionuclide physics, and the mouse-%ID/g → human-Gy/GBq bridge is not closed here; **tumour uptake is not modelled, so the tumour-to-kidney ratio that governs therapeutic index is not predicted.** Species, radiometal, chelator and timepoint are modelled as covariates but leave residual confounding.

**Future work.** (i) Scale the dataset to 400–600 constructs via systematic, human-QC'd figure digitization with an explicit read-error model (the single highest-value lever — the constraint is data, not method); (ii) resolve the within-target charge degeneracy by including constructs that actually vary charge at fixed binder; (iii) report in absorbed-dose (Gy/GBq) units on the human subset, and model tumour-to-kidney ratio, not kidney concentration alone; (iv) **prospective validation**: synthesize a charge-series of a fixed αvβ6 binder, radiolabel, and measure kidney %ID/g against a pre-registered prediction — the only non-circular test. The present study provides the dataset, protocol, controls and cautionary findings required for that program.
