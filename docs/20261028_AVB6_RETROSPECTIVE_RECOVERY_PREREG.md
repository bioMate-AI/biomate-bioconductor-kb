# αvβ6 retrospective recovery benchmark — pre-registration (DRAFT, 2026-10-28)

Status: **not yet run.** No αvβ6 recovery result exists today. This document fixes the question, data, baselines and decision rules before any run. Deck gate G1 ("Engine recovers published αvβ6 binders", status "In progress") refers to this study.

## 1. Question
Given no αvβ6 training signal, does the engine's selector/generator place held-out, published αvβ6 binders above matched non-binders, by more than trivial baselines?

## 2. What the data can and cannot support (measured 2026-10-28, `multitarget/integrin_chembl/data/long_all.csv.gz`)
| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| αvβ6 compounds (ChEMBL-derived, assay-filtered) | 842 from 37 documents (2002–2025) |
| of which macrocycles (largest ring ≥ 9 atoms) | 74 compounds, 8 documents; 51 from CHEMBL4411322 |
| macrocycles with exact pACT ≥ 7 | 31 compounds, 4 documents |
| macrocycles with exact pACT ≥ 8 | 5 compounds, 3 documents |
| all compounds with exact pACT ≥ 7 | 203 (172 non-macrocyclic) |

Consequences, stated in advance:
1. The ChEMBL macrocycle positives are too few and too concentrated (one document = 69 % of macrocycles) for a field-level claim. A document-clustered bootstrap over 4–8 documents will have wide intervals; a null here is **inconclusive**, not negative.
2. The ChEMBL set is dominated by small-molecule RGD mimetics, not cyclic peptides. The deck's target class (cyclic peptides / ncAA macrocycles) is under-represented in public data. This is itself the honest basis for "scarce in public data".
3. Published cyclic-peptide binders not in ChEMBL (A20FMDV2 lineage, Kapp 2017 panel of 34 ligands, Trivehexin monomer, other literature ligands) form the **external set** (E). Structures for E must be transcribed from the papers with the provenance rule already used in `multitarget/integrin_selectivity/ligands.csv`; entries without an unambiguous structure are excluded and listed.

## 3. Design
- **Held-out unit:** the source document (leave-one-document-out, LODO). Compounds from one document never appear in both fit and test sides.
- **Blind rule:** nothing trained or retrieved may contain αvβ6 compounds from the held-out document; for the external set E, from any ChEMBL αvβ6 document that reports the same compound. Retrieval indices and knowledge bases are rebuilt without them and the rebuild is logged.
- **Positives:** primary P1 = macrocycles with exact pACT ≥ 7 (n = 31, 4 documents); secondary P2 = all macrocycles in the 8 documents; external E as above.
- **Decoys:** property-matched macrocycles (heavy-atom count, ring size, ring count, charge, ±1 SD) drawn from CycPeptMPDB and from non-αvβ6 ChEMBL integrin macrocycles, 20 decoys per positive. A decoy set drawn only from non-RGD chemistry is forbidden (it would reward RGD detection); decoys must include RGD-bearing macrocycles active on other integrins.
- **Arms:** S (selector/scorer ranking of positives vs decoys, no αvβ6 fitting); G (generator, trained without αvβ6 rows, motif/scaffold recovery in top-k of N samples). Arm G requires its interface to be frozen in an amendment before running; until then only arm S is pre-registered.

## 4. Baselines (all must be computed)
B0 random (enrichment factor 1); B1 RGD-motif presence (the trivial baseline that most of the signal could reduce to); B2 size/charge-only model; B3 degree/annotation-depth null (for knowledge-graph features). The engine must beat max(B0, B1, B2, B3).

## 5. Metrics and decision rule
- Primary: enrichment factor at top 5 % (EF5) on P1, LODO-pooled; 95 % document-cluster bootstrap (B = 2000).
- Secondary: AUROC, top-1/top-10 recovery per held-out document, EF5 on E.
- **Supported** only if the lower 95 % bound of (engine EF5 − strongest baseline EF5) > 0 on P1 **and** EF5 on E is above the strongest baseline's point estimate.
- **Not supported:** upper bound ≤ 0. **Inconclusive:** otherwise (report as such; do not call it a null of the method).
- No threshold, feature set, decoy ratio or model choice may change after the first test-fold score is seen. Deviations are logged as dated amendments.

## 6. Risks and how they are reported
- Power: see §2. The number of independent documents is the effective sample size and is reported beside every interval.
- Literature leakage: the engine's language/knowledge components were trained on public text and structures that may include these binders. Contamination is checked by n-gram / Tanimoto ≥ 0.9 search of corpora against P1 and E and reported; contaminated items are reported separately.
- Decoy bias: sensitivity analysis with a second decoy source.
- This benchmark cannot show selectivity or tumour residence; those remain wet-lab gates G3 and the residence assay.

## 7. Outputs
`multitarget/integrin_chembl/retro_recovery/` : frozen positive/decoy lists with hashes, per-fold scores, `results.json`, and a result section appended here. Manuscript use: results section of the engine paper, with the §2 limitations stated.

## 8. If the result is null or inconclusive: iterate, under these rules (added 2026-10-28 at the owner's direction)
A null is a diagnosis task, not an endpoint. The engine is improved until it works, and the study is structured so that a later positive is credible:
1. **Diagnose first.** Per-document error analysis (which documents/chemotypes fail, whether failures are RGD-motif, size, or decoy artefacts) before any change. No fix without an identified cause.
2. **Version, don't overwrite.** Each engine change is a new version (v1, v2, …) with its own dated amendment; the v1 result stays in the record permanently.
3. **Tune only on development data.** Model changes are developed on the 4 LODO documents (P1) and the ChEMBL non-macrocycle αvβ6 set. The external literature set E is a **final holdout**: scored once per frozen version, and a version that is changed after seeing its E score is a new version that needs fresh held-out data (new literature ligands, or the first wet-lab round).
4. **Report the whole history**: every version tried, its P1 and E result, and the number of versions, so selection among versions is visible.
5. **Real fixes only.** Improvements must come from the engine (selector, knowledge, training data, all-atom model), not from changing decoys, thresholds or positives to fit.
6. **The decisive test is prospective.** Whatever the retrospective result, the wet-lab SPR/BLI round (gate G2/G3) is the final test of any version.

### 11.2 Arms (all fixed now)
Baselines (all must be computed; direction fixed a priori; both directions reported for the two memorisation-type baselines): B0 random (seeded); B1 RGD indicator (+), heavy atoms (+/−), |charge| (+/−); B3 nearest-neighbour Tanimoto (Morgan r=2, 2,048 bits, chirality on; max over positives of **other-family** targets, excluding any reference with the same flat structure as the item) (+ and −); B4 annotation depth = number of distinct targets (excluding the evaluated target) on which the compound has any labelled pair (+ and −). ## 12. Amendment A4 (2026-10-28, after the first run of baselines and S1 arms) — analysis M is design-confounded; I is primary
**Observation (first and only run of V1; `retro_recovery/results/results_M.json`):** on analysis M the baseline "negated nearest-neighbour Tanimoto to other-family binders" (B3_nn_neg) reaches **AUROC 0.914 [0.891, 0.936], EF5 11.7**, and negated annotation depth (B4_depth_neg) 0.748 / EF5 10.7, while every size/charge/RGD baseline is at chance (0.49–0.51). Cause: M decoys are, by construction, binders of other targets, so each decoy has analogs/series-mates in the other-family binder pool, whereas a target's own positives (whose series is excluded as same-family) do not. **Audited before concluding:** B3 independently recomputed with RDKit `BulkTanimotoSimilarity` on 4 random targets (identical scores, max |diff| 0.0000; AUROC 0.989 / 0.535 / 0.954 / 0.999 both ways) and the harness AUROC equals scikit-learn's to 1e-16 on B3/B4/B1; planted-signal and shuffled-label controls pass on the real items (planted signal SUPPORTED, shuffled INCONCLUSIVE). The number is a property of the decoy design, not a bug.
**Consequence (registered):** because the §5 decision rule takes the strongest baseline, no scorer that does not itself exploit pool membership can be SUPPORTED on M; M therefore cannot adjudicate arms. **Analysis I (same-target measured inactives) is the primary analysis** (its strongest baselines are weak: B3_nn_neg 0.620, B4_depth_neg 0.548); M is retained as a diagnostic of the shortcut and reported with its baselines. This demotion was decided after seeing M's baseline scores; the arms scored at chance on M, so the decision did not favour any arm.
**Registered next version (not run): M2** — decoys additionally matched on B3 (max similarity to the other-family binder pool, ±0.05) and annotation depth (±1) so that pool membership is not informative; must be built and its baselines checked (B3/B4 AUROC ≤ 0.55) before any arm is scored on it; frozen lists + hashes to be committed first.

## 13. Amendment A5 (2026-10-29, BEFORE any arm was scored on M2) — M2 construction, baseline gap, generalised arms, coverage layer
**M2 construction (`retro_recovery/04_build_M2.py`, seed 20261029).** As M (A2) plus: |B3(decoy) − B3(positive)| ≤ tol_B3 and |depth(decoy) − depth(positive)| ≤ tol_depth, where B3 = max Morgan(r2, 2048, chirality) Tanimoto to positives of OTHER-family targets excluding the item's own flat structure (identical to the B3 baseline) and depth = number of other targets with a labelled pair. Registered tolerance policy: (0.05, 1); if family-mean AUROC of B3_nn_neg or B4_depth_neg is outside [0.45, 0.55] tighten once to (0.02, 0). Decided on baselines only.

## 16. Amendment A8 (2026-10-30, BEFORE any scoring) — few-label new-target adaptation (H4b) and zero-shot family-held-out analyses
**Zero-shot (k = 0)** is analysis I (and M2/M3-diagnostic under A7), family-held-out by `folds_v1.csv`. **H4b: does revealing k labelled compounds of a held-out target help?**
Design. Evaluation targets = analysis-I targets (the only analysis with both binders and measured inactives; 46 targets / 26 families; median 205 items per target; all have ≥ 19 inactives and ≥ 5 positives); k ∈ {0, 5, 10, 25}; per (target, k, seed ∈ {0..4}) reveal k compounds, stratified: ⌈k/2⌉ positives and ⌊k/2⌋ inactives (fewer if unavailable; reported), seeded by (target, k, seed), and flat-structure siblings of revealed compounds are removed from the ranked set; rank the remaining compounds of the target. A target enters a given k only if ≥ 20 compounds with ≥ 3 of each class remain. Metrics per target (mean over seeds): AUROC, EF5, top-10 precision; aggregated mean within family then across families, 95 % family-cluster bootstrap (B = 2,000); paired differences vs the strongest baseline at the same k. Target counts per k are reported before any arm is interpreted.
Arms (frozen now): **R** random; **NN** score = max Tanimoto (chirality-aware Morgan r2/2048) to revealed positives − max to revealed inactives (0 if none); **FP-k** chirality-aware count-fingerprint logistic regression fitted on the revealed k only (k ≥ 5); **PT** logistic regression pretrained on labelled pairs of training-fold families only (zero-shot at k = 0; this is a fitted compound-only model, i.e. a no-target control of the C2/C3 kind); **PT+k** the same pretraining set plus the revealed compounds with sample weight 20 (a reweighted refit, not gradient fine-tuning; a GBM was dropped for cost: 690 refits). Plug-in LLM arms: `run_s3.py --k K --seed S` accepts a score file per (k, seed) built from the same reveal sets (`data/h4b_reveals.csv.gz`, frozen with a hash before any scoring); the LLM's own few-label conditioning/fine-tuning is the arm's business and is reported with its cost.
Validity: the no-target gate (A7) is evaluated on each (k)'s remaining-item set (C1, C2, C3 with leave-fold-out); H4b results are control-adjusted: an adaptation arm must beat PT+k's no-target counterpart AND the zero-shot value of the same arm (gain over k = 0) with the family-bootstrap lower bound > 0. Contamination-controlled subset reported side by side (`contamination_flags.csv.gz`). Power: target counts per k and family counts reported; with 26 families the gain test has low power and is stated as such.

## 17. Amendment A9 (2026-10-30, BEFORE the strata and power analyses were run) — analog-distance strata for H4b; cluster-based power
**Trigger:** committee synthesis (`docs/20261030_COMMITTEE_SYNTHESIS.md`): nearest-neighbour few-label gains must be separated from transferable gains; power must be computed on clusters, not compounds.
**Strata (`retro_recovery/13_h4b_strata.py`).** For every remaining item of every eligible (target, k, seed) cell of the frozen H4b reveal sets (A8), s = maximum Tanimoto (chirality-aware Morgan r2, 2,048 bits; identical to the NN arm) to ANY revealed compound of that target (positives or inactives). Stereoisomer siblings of revealed compounds are already removed (A8) and are reported as a count, not as a stratum. Bins fixed now: **close s ≥ 0.70; medium 0.50 ≤ s < 0.70; remote 0.30 ≤ s < 0.50; very remote s < 0.30.** Within each bin and cell, AUROC of each arm (R, NN, FP-k, PT+k, PT; arms as frozen in A8, no refits) is computed on the items in the bin if it has ≥ 10 items with ≥ 3 of each class; per target mean over seeds; mean within family; mean over families; 95 % family-cluster bootstrap (B = 2,000). Reported per bin: share of remaining items, targets/families evaluable, AUROC per arm, and two registered contrasts: **transferable gain = (PT+k or FP-k) − PT (zero-shot, same items)** and **gain over NN = arm − NN**. A few-label gain is called *transferable* only if the lower 95 % bound of the transferable gain in the remote and very remote bins is > 0 AND the arm is not worse than NN; otherwise it is reported as analog lookup. No bin or arm is added after seeing results.
**Cluster-based power (`14_power.py`).** Unit of replication = family (A2). For each primary contrast with stored scores — arm vs strongest baseline in I and M2 (S1a, S1b-KB, S1b-induction, S1c-induction, S1d), and H4b contrasts (NN − PT, PT+k − PT, NN − FP-k at each k) — compute the family-level paired difference vector; report n families, its SD σ_d, the minimal detectable effect at 80 % power / two-sided α = 0.05, MDE = 2.8·σ_d/√n (normal approximation, stated), the number of families needed to detect δ = 0.05, the intraclass correlation of per-target AUROC within families (one-way ANOVA ICC), the design effect 1 + (m̄ − 1)·ICC and the effective number of independent targets. Compound counts are not used as the sample size anywhere.
