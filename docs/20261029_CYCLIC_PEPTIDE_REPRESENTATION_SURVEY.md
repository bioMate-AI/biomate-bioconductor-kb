# Cyclic-peptide representation, stratification and modelling: literature survey

Date: 2026-10-29. Scope: read-only literature survey (web). Purpose: inform stratification features, representation choices, evaluation design and novelty claims for the all-atom cyclic-LLM study.

## Method and evidence-quality caveats

- Every work cited below was located through web search and its page (abstract, PMC/arXiv/bioRxiv page, or PDF) was opened with a fetch tool in this session. The fetch tool returns model-written summaries of pages, not verbatim text, so numbers and phrasings are "as returned by the fetch" and should be re-checked against the primary PDF before use in a manuscript. The one exception: the decoupled-alignment-kernel paper (Amirahmadi et al.) was converted to text locally and read directly; statements from it are verified against the text.
- "Reports" = what the paper states. "Interpretation" = my reading, flagged as such.
- Items I could not open or verify are listed in the "Unverified" section and are not asserted anywhere else.
- Absence of a prior-art hit means only that a limited set of searches found none. It is not proof of novelty.

## 1. CycPeptMPDB

**Li J, Yanagisawa K, Sugita M, Fujie T, Ohue M, Akiyama Y. "CycPeptMPDB: A Comprehensive Database of Membrane Permeability of Cyclic Peptides." J Chem Inf Model 2023, 63(7):2240-2250. DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.2c01573. PMC10091415.** Opened.

Reports (via fetch):
- 7,334 structurally distinct cyclic peptides (7,451 entries including duplicates) from 45 papers and 2 pharma patents; 312 distinct monomers.
- Assay counts: PAMPA 6,941; Caco-2 649; RRCK 186; MDCK 40.
- Topology categories reported by the fetch: circle (about 70%) and lariat (about 30%). Binary class split at logPexp = -6.00 (about 5,113 high vs 2,338 low in the fetched summary).
- Peptides are encoded in HELM, which gives a uniform monomer-level sequence and carries the cyclisation connection.
- Authors acknowledge that permeability values come from heterogeneous experiments; a ranking scheme that prioritises PAMPA picks a representative value per peptide.
- Interpretation: the fetch did not return a "shape" taxonomy beyond circle/lariat or monomer-level N-methyl/D-residue counts; check the primary paper before using shape categories as strata. The database is dominated by PAMPA from a limited number of source papers, many of which are single-series studies (see below), so source/lab is confounded with chemistry.

Data limitations documented by downstream papers (all opened):
- Jiang et al. 2025 (CPMP) state that many CycPeptMPDB publications vary only a few residues around a near-constant sequence; this is also quoted in the MonoSeqCP thesis search snippet (thesis page itself blocked, not opened).
- Amirahmadi et al. (verified in text): "CycPeptMPDB contains near-duplicate series"; permeabilities are log-scaled and clipped to [-10, -4]; P >= -6 is conventionally "permeable"; after averaging duplicate canonical SMILES (chirality retained) there are 7,221 unique peptides and 276 unique monomers in PAMPA.
- Jiang et al. excluded samples with logPexp < -10.0 and note small, imbalanced RRCK/MDCK sets cause underfitting.

Key observations (interpretation, grounded in the above):
1. Reported R2 values (0.6-0.75) come from random splits; the only OOD number found (MultiCycPermea R2 0.19) is much lower. Amirahmadi et al. show every method loses AUC from random to scaffold split (e.g. AttentiveFP 86.2 to 66.1).
2. A fingerprint Tanimoto GP was the top ROC-AUC on both random (0.897) and scaffold (0.804) splits of the length-focused subset, narrowly ahead of the alignment kernel (verified in text). Strong fingerprint baselines are therefore competitive on this data.
3. Stereochemistry is handled only implicitly in most models (chirality atom features, or distinct monomers). The scaffold splits used in the benchmark ignore chirality, so stereoisomers of one scaffold sit in the same bin by construction; whether they appear across train/test depends on the grouping.
4. None of the retrieved papers report a metric for ranking stereoisomers or N-methyl variants within a single scaffold.

Leakage and split issues noted by authors: near-duplicate series (Amirahmadi, Jiang), applicability-domain failures (Geylan), source-driven leakage motivating PAMPA-only analysis (Amirahmadi), and leave-one-cluster-out as a mitigation (Feller and Wilke). Studies using plain random splits (CPMP; the random arm of others) share the leakage problem; CycPeptMP's Kennard-Stone test set is a single small holdout.

## 3. Structural determinants of permeability and binding

**N-methylation (number and position).** White et al., Nat Chem Biol 2011 (DOI 10.1038/nchembio.664; PMC3210067, opened) report on-resin N-methylation of cyclic hexapeptide scaffolds (about 48 methylated products across diastereomeric scaffolds plus virtual enumeration of over 1,000 variants): selectivity of methylation depends on backbone stereochemistry; partially methylated derivatives were more permeable than fully methylated ones; a trimethylated hexapeptide (MW 755) had 28% rat oral bioavailability. Authors note N-methylation may reduce activity for sequences with known bioactivity. Wang et al., PNAS 2014 (DOI 10.1073/pnas.1417611111; PMC4267368, opened) used NMR amide temperature coefficients (less negative than about -4.6 ppb/K indicates a protected amide) to choose which solvent-exposed amides to methylate; lead had 33% rat oral bioavailability. Strength: mechanistically consistent but from a few scaffolds (mostly cyclic hexapeptides) and rat PK. Limit: the position effect is conformation-dependent, so number alone is a weak predictor and "methylate exposed amides" does not generalise automatically (the authors of both papers state limits).

**D-amino acids and backbone stereochemistry.** Rezai et al., JACS 2006, 128:2510 (Lokey group; located via search, abstract-level only, PubMed page blocked): two cyclo[Leu-Leu-Leu-Leu-Pro-Tyr] diastereomers differed by nearly two log units in membrane diffusion, correlating with intramolecular hydrogen bonding and H/D exchange; the best, cyclo[D-Leu-D-Leu-Leu-D-Leu-Pro-Tyr], matched cyclosporin A. Wang 2014 also examines L/D Leu stereoisomers. Strength: clean paired experiments. Limit: one hexapeptide family; the effect is through conformation, so it is not a transferable per-residue rule.

**Intramolecular hydrogen bonds and chameleonicity.** White 2011 and Rezai 2006 tie permeability to IMHB networks and shielded amides. Ramelot et al., Curr Opin Struct Biol 2023, 80:102603 (DOI 10.1016/j.sbi.2023.102603; PMC10923192, opened) review chameleonic peptides that adopt different conformations in aqueous and membrane-like environments; they note that predicting isoenergetic states and high-throughput multi-conformer characterisation remain hard, and ML for small peptides is still developmental. Bhardwaj et al., Cell 2022, 185(19):3520 (DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2022.07.019; PMC9490236, opened): 84 designed 6-12-residue macrocycles with Papp > 1e-6 cm/s, 29 of 35 solved structures matched designs (RMSD < 1.2 A), five permeable designs lacked N-methylation, four showed 7.5-40% rodent oral bioavailability, three were chameleonic. Limit: designs were not required to bind a target.

**Lipophilicity and ring size.** Rand et al., MedChemComm 2012 (DOI 10.1039/C2MD20203D; PMC3488302, opened): in a cyclic-peptide series, logD correlated with both permeability and clearance, logD near 3 was favourable, and charged side chains (Lys, Asp) severely reduced permeability; only five derivatives had PK and only rats were used. Over et al., Nat Chem Biol 2016, 12:1065 (DOI 10.1038/nchembio.2203; Broad Institute page opened): more than 200 non-peptidic macrocycles measured under consistent conditions; 3D dynamic intramolecular interactions relate to permeability. Naylor et al. (LPE, J Med Chem 2018; located in search results only, not opened) is listed under unverified. Ring-size effects: Bhardwaj shows permeable designs from 6 to 12 residues, so size alone is not a barrier; I found no opened source providing a quantitative ring-size law.

**Side-chain vs backbone cyclisation, stapled and bicyclic peptides.** The evidence I opened here is thin. Muppidi et al., Tetrahedron 2014 (DOI 10.1016/j.tet.2014.05.104; abstract page opened) found for stapled BH3 peptides that maximum helicity gave best binding (IC50 41-49 nM) whereas highest hydrophobicity gave best cellular uptake, i.e. affinity and uptake diverge with cross-linker choice. Bicyclic-peptide statements (phage-display bicyclic binders, larger cyclic peptides usually used against extracellular targets) come from search snippets of the Heinis group pages and were not opened as primary papers. CycPeptMPDB is almost entirely backbone-cyclic (circle) plus lariat peptides, so stapled and bicyclic topologies are out of its domain.

**Binding.** For protein-cyclic peptide binding, the only benchmark located was an MM/PBSA(GBSA) assessment with a 50-complex affinity set and an 81-complex decoy set (PMC12665037; search snippet only, not opened). I found no large, family-disjoint ML benchmark for cyclic-peptide binders.

Interpretation: permeability determinants are well supported mechanistically (IMHB shielding, exposed-HBD count, lipophilicity) but through a small number of congeneric series. Per-feature statistics such as "number of N-methyls" are confounded with series and assay in the aggregate database.

## 4. Representations

- **HELM** (Zhang T, Li H, Xi H, Stanton RV, Rotstein SH. J Chem Inf Model 2012, 52:2796-2806; located via search snippets and Pistoia/Wikipedia pages, original paper not opened): hierarchy of complex polymer, simple polymer, monomer, atom; designed for non-natural residues and connections. CycPeptMPDB uses it. Strength: compact, topology explicit, monomer vocabulary reusable. Limit: stereochemistry and N-methylation exist only as monomer identities (a closed vocabulary; 312 monomers in CycPeptMPDB), so unseen ncAAs are out-of-vocabulary.
- **SMILES** with atom-level tokens: encodes chirality (@/@@), N-methyl, ring-closure digits, any ncAA. PeptideCLM and PeptideCLM-2 use it. Limit: tokenisation choices (custom 586 tokens vs k-mers up to 6 characters) change what a model sees; ring-closure digit pairing is a long-range dependency.
- **CHUCKLES-style monomer SMILES concatenation** (PepFoundry; Garzon Otero, Akbari, Mandapati, Bilodeau; J Chem Inf Model, Jan 2026; DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.5c02629; PMC12848965, opened): 157 pre-configured residues including D-isomers and peptoids; explicit atom chirality; ring-closure digits for cycles; atom-mapped RDKit objects. Reports on an antimicrobial classification task that atom-level representations beat one-hot sequences (Morgan F1 above 0.80 vs 0.70; hierarchical graph PepMNet F1 0.877).
- **SELFIES** (Krenn M, Haese F, Nigam A, Friederich P, Aspuru-Guzik A. Mach Learn Sci Technol 2020; arXiv 1905.13741, search result opened): guarantees valid strings; I found no opened evidence of SELFIES being applied to cyclic-peptide permeability, so no claim is made about its fit to macrocycle closure.
- **Graph / 3D / multi-view**: CycPeptMP (atom, monomer, peptide levels, with distance matrices); CPMP (MAT with 3D); PepLand (Zhang R, et al. Brief Bioinform 2025; DOI 10.1093/bib/bbaf367; PMC12315545, opened): multi-view heterogeneous GNN (atom, fragment, junction), pretrained on about 8M canonical peptides then about 9,000 non-canonical ones; reports 16.5% gain on non-canonical cell penetrability; limits: 7.38x slower inference than ESM2, small synthetic-peptide pretraining data, out-of-vocabulary fragments.
- **Generative chemical LMs**: PepINVENT (Geylan G, Janet JP, Tibo A, et al., arXiv 2409.14040; opened abstract) generates peptides with natural and non-natural amino acids; the snippet describes support for linear to multiple macrocyclic topologies. PepALD (Zhang J, Yi S, Ju W, Gu Z; arXiv 2606.14510, June 2026; opened abstract): HELM monomers with chemical embeddings, ring-closure prediction during generation, preference optimisation on affinity rewards. HELM-BERT (above) is an encoder on HELM.
- **All-atom structure models** (AlphaFold3-like for cyclic peptides with ncAAs): a CyclicBoltz1 preprint appeared in search results but was not opened; unverified.

Interpretation on closure and stereo: all of SMILES-based models carry chirality and closure implicitly via characters; HELM-based ones carry them via monomer and connection records. I found no opened paper that tokenises chirality and macrocycle-closure topology as dedicated, separately ablated tokens in an atom-level vocabulary.

## 5. Benchmarks and evaluation pitfalls

- **Aggregate peptide benchmarks.** PepBenchmark (Zhang J, Wang R, Zhou K, Xiao T, Zhu L, Min Y, Wang Y; arXiv 2604.10531, ICLR 2026; opened): 29 canonical and 6 non-canonical datasets in 7 groups, standardised cleaning/splitting, four model families (fingerprint, GNN, PLM, SMILES). It covers general peptide property tasks; I did not verify whether it includes a cyclic-peptide binder task.
- **Near-duplicate and series leakage in cyclic peptides.** See section 1-2: Amirahmadi et al. (canonical-group stratification), Geylan et al. (applicability domain), Feller and Wilke (leave-one-cluster-out). Amirahmadi et al. note performance drops for all methods once near-duplicates are removed.
- **Scaffold splits are not stereo-aware in the benchmark they follow**: Murcko scaffolds computed ignoring chirality (verified in Amirahmadi text). Interpretation: diastereomers can be assigned to the same scaffold bin yet the split is justified as a distribution shift; a stereo-sensitive split would be stricter or looser depending on grouping.
- **Decoy and analogue bias in binding benchmarks.** Chen L, Cruz A, Ramsey S, et al., PLoS One 2019, 14(8):e0220113 (DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0220113; PMC6701836, opened): on DUD-E, CNN models achieved AUC about 0.98 using ligand-only information, i.e. analogue bias and decoy bias; property bias can be reduced by stricter property matching but topology bias persists because fingerprint similarity is used to pick decoys.
- **Train-test leakage in affinity benchmarks.** Graber D, Stockinger P, Meyer F, Mishra S, Horn C, Buller R. Nat Mach Intell 2025 (DOI 10.1038/s42256-025-01124-5; PMC12552120, opened): nearly 600 train-test similarities between PDBbind and CASF, affecting almost half of the test complexes; retraining on PDBbind CleanSplit sharply lowered the performance of Pafnucy and GenScore.
- **Duplication and redundancy.** Huang A, Knight IS, Naprienko S. "Data Leakage and Redundancy in the LIT-PCBA Benchmark," arXiv 2507.21404 (opened): 2,491 2D-identical inactives in both train and validation, analog overlap, and a parameter-free memorisation baseline that matches or exceeds published deep models.
- **Embedding-model evaluation rigour.** Praski M, Adamczyk J, Czech W, arXiv 2508.06199 (opened): across 25 pretrained embedding models and 25 datasets, nearly all show negligible or no improvement over ECFP; only CLAMP (itself fingerprint-based) was significant; authors raise concerns about evaluation rigour in prior work.
- **Context/batch confounding.** Peeling Context from Cause (CLaP), arXiv 2511.06692 (opened abstract): models may use spurious context; not peptide-specific.

Which opened cyclic-peptide papers have these problems (interpretation): CPMP uses a plain random split on a series-heavy database; CycPeptMP uses one small holdout; the aggregate classification numbers of Multi_CycGT are not checkable from what I opened. No opened paper evaluates abundance/decoy shortcuts for cyclic-peptide binders because I found no such benchmark.

## 6. Small versus large LLMs for molecules

- **Fine-tuned large LLM, low data.** Jablonka KM, Schwaller P, Ortega-Guerrero A, Smit B. Nat Mach Intell 2024; DOI 10.1038/s42256-023-00788-1 (infoscience page opened): fine-tuned GPT-3 performs comparably to or better than conventional ML, especially at low data; search snippet reports 50 examples matching models trained on over 1,000 (snippet only).
- **Few-shot ICL with trained backbones.** Fifty C, Leskovec J, Thrun S. arXiv 2310.08863: an in-context learner beats meta-learning at small support sizes on FS-Mol and BACE and is competitive at larger sizes. Liu Y et al. MolecularGPT, arXiv 2406.12950: instruction-tuned open LLM; with two examples beats supervised GNNs on 4 of 7 datasets.
- **Prompting frozen general LLMs.** Guo T et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B, arXiv 2305.18365 (opened): GPT-4 best among five general LLMs on eight chemistry tasks, with sensitivity to in-context settings (the fetch gave no property-prediction numbers).
- **Instruction-tuned open models.** Yu B et al. LlaSMol, COLM 2024, arXiv 2402.09391 (opened): Mistral-based models fine-tuned on a 3M-sample dataset beat GPT-4 and Claude 3 Opus on their chemistry tasks.
- **Caveats on ICL for property regression.** arXiv 2603.25857 (Busch M, Tacke M, Lamaka SV, Zheludkevich ML, Cyron CJ, Feiler C, Aydin RC; opened): blinding study on memorisation and knowledge conflicts across nine LLM variants; no evidence of verbatim retrieval on legacy benchmarks. A separate "Evaluating In-Context Learning in LLMs for Molecular Property Regression" was surfaced in search (seven LLMs, 56 tasks; LLMs near-perfect on raw molecular weight via shortcut cues but degraded under nonlinear transformation while ML baselines stayed robust); only the search snippet was seen, so it is listed under unverified.
- **Fingerprints vs learned representations.** Praski et al. (section 5) is the strongest opened evidence that ECFP is hard to beat; in cyclic peptides, the Tanimoto GP of Amirahmadi et al. topped both splits of the length-focused benchmark.
- **Peptide-specific small LMs.** PeptideCLM (about tens of millions of parameters, 6 layers) and PeptideCLM-2 (32M-337M) show that sub-billion encoders pretrained on peptide SMILES are the reference class for this task; I found no opened study that directly compares a prompted frontier LLM against these on CycPeptMPDB.

Interpretation: evidence for LLM sample efficiency is on general-chemistry tasks with fine-tuning or in-context examples, and it is mixed once shortcuts and memorisation are controlled. No opened source tests a feedback loop (iterative measurement-in-the-loop correction) for peptide property ranking.

## Implications for our study

**Stratification features worth adding.** Stratify and report by: source publication / lab and assay type (PAMPA, Caco-2, RRCK, MDCK), because values are heterogeneous and series-clustered; canonical-SMILES group (chirality retained) and monomer-composition cluster to expose near-duplicate series; ring size / sequence length (6, 7, 10-mers are the well-covered lengths); topology (circle vs lariat; stapled and bicyclic are outside CycPeptMPDB); N-methyl count and position (exposed vs IMHB-protected amide) and D-residue count and position; backbone-stereo pattern relative to the parent sequence (for paired diastereomer analysis); lipophilicity (logD/ALogP) and exposed-HBD count; and the clipped-value flag (logPexp at -10 / -4 bounds). Within-series membership should be a recorded covariate so within-scaffold and across-scaffold performance can be separated.

**Representation needs.** The representation must keep chirality per residue, N-methylation at amide level, arbitrary ncAA side chains (out-of-vocabulary risk is the weak point of closed HELM monomer sets and of PepLand), and the macrocycle closure/topology (circle, lariat, side-chain bridges). Atom-level SMILES covers these implicitly but forces the model to infer closure from paired digits; HELM covers topology explicitly but not unseen monomers. A fair comparison should include at least a Morgan/Tanimoto GP or GBM, a monomer-level HELM model (HELM-BERT-style) and an atom-level SMILES model (PeptideCLM-style) as controls, with stereo-ablation (flip D to L, strip chirality) run on each.

**Evaluation pitfalls to avoid.** Do not use random splits as the headline (random-to-scaffold drops of 10-20 AUC points are documented). Use group-disjoint splits by canonical SMILES group, monomer-set cluster and source publication, and report leave-one-cluster-out. Use stereo-aware scaffold definitions (the benchmark's Murcko scaffolds ignore chirality). Average or group duplicates before splitting. Always include a strong fingerprint-kernel/GBM baseline, since it matches or beats several deep models here. For any binding benchmark: apply DUD-E-style audits (ligand-only baseline, property-matched decoys, topology bias), LIT-PCBA-style duplicate checks (2D-identical items across splits, a zero-parameter nearest-neighbour baseline), and PDBbind-CleanSplit-style structure/target similarity filtering. For LLM evaluations, test memorisation (blinding) and shortcut properties (molecular weight, length).

**Claims that look already published (so not novel).** Deep and kernel models on CycPeptMPDB; monomer-aware/HELM-based permeability prediction (MonoSeqCP thesis, MD-GAK, HELM-BERT); SMILES chemical LMs that natively encode ncAAs and cyclisation (PeptideCLM, PeptideCLM-2); scaffold and leakage-controlled splits for CycPeptMPDB (Liu et al., Geylan et al., Amirahmadi et al.); the finding that stereoisomer and N-methyl pattern change permeability by orders of magnitude (Rezai 2006, White 2011); chameleonic/IMHB mechanisms (Ramelot 2023, Bhardwaj 2022); the general observation that fine-tuned LLMs are sample-efficient on small chemistry sets (Jablonka 2024) and that fingerprints are hard to beat (Praski 2025).
