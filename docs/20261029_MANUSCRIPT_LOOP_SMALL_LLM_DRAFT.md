## Abstract (v1; results limited to what has run)
**Background.** Closed design–test–learn loops are widely proposed for cyclic-peptide discovery, but claims of benefit are rarely tested against controls that separate the effect of feedback from the effect of additional data, and cyclic-peptide benchmarks are frequently confounded by near-duplicate series, laboratory concentration and decoy shortcuts.
**Objective.** Two aims, one paper. Aim A: test whether a loop-native in-house hybrid cyclic-peptide language model (IH-LM; knowledge-prior, laboratory-offset, analog-retrieval and edit components updated from verified feedback) matches or exceeds larger pretrained peptide and chemistry language models fine-tuned on the same revealed labels, and whether the loop improves a model beyond what the same data would. Aim B: build and audit the retrospective evaluation instrument that tests Aim A.
**Methods.** Retrospective replay in which labels are revealed only for selected compounds; five pre-registered controls (data-matched one-shot, random feedback, shuffled labels, frozen model, knowledge off); a family-disjoint, hash-frozen benchmark with validity gates (no-target and typicality controls); leave-laboratories-out evaluation; stereoisomer-resolved ranking; per-source curves; cost accounting in parameters and GPU-hours.
**Results available at v1.** (i) With a gradient-boosted learner on within-source permeability labels (30 seeds), the loop beat random selection in-distribution (+186.3 hits per 500 selections, 95 % CI 158.5 to 209.0) and in a near-duplicate-aware regime (+163.4, 128.1 to 196.3), but the pre-registered claim of an advantage in both primary regimes was not met: under a scaffold-shifted start the difference was +22.2 (−1.6 to 44.1) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/results/l5_aggregate.json]. A physicochemical rule scored equal to the loop under that shift (difference −0.5, −20.0 to 17.2), and no knowledge-prior variant improved on the loop (all fifteen 99 % intervals include zero). (ii) The in-distribution loop concentrates its picks on one laboratory's series (346 of 500 picks from 2020_Townsend against 206 for random selection) [VERIFIED: same file]. (iii) No benchmark in the study passes the validity gate cleanly; no zero-shot arm exceeded the strongest baseline on the primary analysis (AUROC 0.620) [REPORTED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md], and a plain nearest-neighbour baseline to a handful of revealed compounds reaches AUROC 0.753 (5 labels) to 0.869 (25 labels) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/multitarget/integrin_chembl/retro_recovery/results/h4b_baselines.json]. (iv) Cheap-tier general-purpose language models prompted in context with the same revealed labels were far behind a gradient-boosted model on the same candidate subsets (exploratory; 4 seeds) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/results/l7_aggregate.json].
**Not available at v1.** The IH-LM and larger pretrained models have not been run through the loop; the central hypothesis is untested here (Section 5.7).
**Conclusions allowed by the evidence.** The loop selects better than random from a finite library under in-distribution conditions with a simple learner, the advantage under scaffold shift is not established, and simple physicochemical priors match it there. These results define the baselines the central comparison must beat.
**Limitations stated up front.** Retrospective evaluation can only reveal labels of compounds that already have measurements, so it does not test whether a model generates better novel molecules; the task is permeability ranking on one public database dominated by two laboratories; the central comparison awaits GPU runs (Section 5.7).

## 1. Introduction
Cyclic peptides occupy a space between small molecules and biologics and are an active target for computational design, but their data are scarce, heterogeneous and clustered in analogue series. CycPeptMPDB, the principal public resource for membrane permeability, collects 7,334 structurally distinct peptides from 45 papers and two patents, measured mainly by PAMPA (6,941 measurements) with smaller Caco-2, RRCK and MDCK sets [LIT-VERIFIED: Li et al., J Chem Inf Model 2023, PMC10091415]. Many models have been trained on it. Reported performance depends strongly on how the data are split: in one benchmark every method lost discrimination when moving from a random to a scaffold split, for example 86.2 → 66.1 ROC-AUC for AttentiveFP [LIT-VERIFIED: Amirahmadi et al., arXiv 2511.21566, Table 3, read in converted PDF]. In the same study a Tanimoto-kernel Gaussian process on fingerprints attained the highest ROC-AUC on both splits (0.897 random, 0.804 scaffold) [LIT-VERIFIED: same, Table 6]. A large comparison of 25 pretrained molecular embeddings on 25 datasets found that nearly all showed negligible or no improvement over ECFP fingerprints [LIT-VERIFIED: Praski et al., arXiv 2508.06199]. A claim that a learned model improves on a fingerprint baseline therefore needs a baseline of that strength and an evaluation that does not reward memorising series.

Closed loops add a further question. A loop that selects compounds, receives measurements and updates a model may improve because the model learns from feedback, or merely because it has seen more data, or because it locates the one laboratory whose series dominate the positives. In the permeability data used here, after within-source standardisation 52.8 % of the hits still come from a single source (41 % of the peptides) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/PREREG_LOOP.md, L5 addendum]. Separating these explanations requires controls that are rarely reported.

We therefore study one question with a design built to be falsified: does a closed loop with verified feedback and knowledge improve a loop-native cyclic-peptide language model (IH-LM), and how does the result compare with a fingerprint baseline and with larger pretrained peptide and chemistry language models fine-tuned on the same revealed labels? The contributions are stated conservatively. (i) A retrospective replay protocol with five pre-registered controls, a corrected shuffled-label null, and per-model cost accounting in parameters and GPU-hours. (ii) A family-disjoint cyclic-peptide binder evaluation with frozen, hash-verified lists and two validity gates (a no-target control and a typicality control) that a benchmark must pass before any arm is judged. (iii) A stereoisomer-resolved ranking protocol with a single revealed/target split per group, together with a demonstration that leave-one-out evaluation of this task is artifactual. (iv) A documented audit trail, including errors found in our own earlier code and evaluation. We do not claim a new model architecture or tokenisation (Section 8).

## 2. Related work
**Data and permeability models.** CycPeptMPDB encodes peptides in HELM, defines Circle (head-to-tail, about 70 %) and Lariat (about 30 %) shapes, and selects a representative value when a peptide has several assay readouts by assay rank (PAMPA, Caco-2, RRCK, MDCK) [LIT-VERIFIED: PMC10091415]. CycPeptMP combines atom-, monomer- and peptide-level features in a fusion model with data augmentation and reports a mean absolute error of 0.355 and correlation 0.883 [LIT-VERIFIED: Li, Yanagisawa, Akiyama, Brief Bioinform 2024, abstract via Europe PMC; the split protocol was not verified]. PeptideCLM is a 6-layer, 12-head, 768-dimensional BERT-style SMILES model pretrained on 23 M molecules (10 M modified or cyclic peptides); on CycPeptMPDB it was evaluated by leave-one-cluster-out over six k-means clusters and reported ROC-AUC 0.781 ± 0.067 [LIT-VERIFIED: Feller and Wilke, PMC11326283]. PeptideCLM-2 scales this to 32 M, 114 M and 337 M parameters with a 405-token k-mer vocabulary; the largest model reports AUROC 0.836 ± 0.011 (hybrid objective) on permeability using splits from prior work [LIT-VERIFIED: Feller et al., PMC12803269, version dated June 2026]. HELM-BERT applies a DeBERTa encoder to HELM notation, pretrained on 39,079 peptides, and reports gains over SMILES-based language models on cyclic permeability and peptide–protein interaction [LIT-VERIFIED: Lee et al., arXiv 2512.23175, abstract only]. A decoupled alignment kernel reports that stereochemistry and macrocycle-closure ablations remain future work and that its scaffold splits compute Murcko scaffolds ignoring chirality [LIT-VERIFIED: Amirahmadi et al., Section 2 and Limitations in the converted PDF]. An applicability-domain-aware protocol (Geylan et al., Digital Discovery 2024) is cited by that work as the basis of its splits [LIT-UNVERIFIED: the paper itself could not be opened in this pass; it is cited here only as described by Amirahmadi et al.]. A 13-method benchmark by Liu et al. is likewise known only through citation in the same paper [LIT-UNVERIFIED].

**Mechanisms.** Partially N-methylated cyclic hexapeptides were more permeable than fully methylated ones, and one trimethylated compound showed 28 % oral bioavailability in rat [LIT-VERIFIED: White et al., Nat Chem Biol 2011, abstract]. Computationally designed macrocycles of 6–12 residues included 84 designs with apparent permeability above 1 × 10⁻⁶ cm/s, and 29 of 35 solved structures matched the design models within 1.2 Å backbone RMSD [LIT-VERIFIED: Bhardwaj et al., Cell 2022, abstract]. These mechanistic results come from small congeneric series and should not be read as per-feature effect sizes for the aggregate database.

**Benchmark pitfalls.** Ligand-only convolutional models reached AUC 0.98 on DUD-E, attributed to analogue and decoy bias [LIT-VERIFIED: Chen et al., PLoS One 2019]. Nearly 600 train–test similarities between PDBbind and CASF involved 49 % of CASF complexes, and retraining on a filtered split sharply reduced the performance of some models [LIT-VERIFIED: Graber et al., Nat Mach Intell 2025]. LIT-PCBA contains 2,491 2D-identical inactives across training and validation, and a parameter-free memorisation baseline matches or exceeds published deep models [LIT-VERIFIED: Huang et al., arXiv 2507.21404]. Our evaluation design follows the lessons of these audits.

**Language models for molecules with few examples.** Fine-tuned GPT-3 was reported to perform comparably to or better than conventional machine learning in the low-data limit [LIT-VERIFIED at search level: Jablonka et al., Nat Mach Intell 2024, DOI 10.1038/s42256-023-00788-1]. We found no study in our search that compares a small peptide language model inside a feedback loop with larger pretrained peptide or chemistry language models fine-tuned on the same revealed labels for peptide property ranking; the absence of a hit reflects limited search coverage and is not proof of novelty. Work that used simulated assay values or that built a system without replaying measured labels is not cited as validation (Section 4.3.1). ## 3. Hypotheses
Pre-specified in `docs/20261029_HYPOTHESES_SMALL_LLM_LOOP.md` [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_HYPOTHESES_SMALL_LLM_LOOP.md].
- **H1 sample efficiency:** IH-LM's learning curve lies above the baseline's at small numbers of revealed labels and the gap shrinks as labels grow.
- **H2 stereochemical resolution:** with a single seeded revealed/target split per flat-structure group, a stereo-aware model ranks target stereoisomers above chance and above a chirality-aware fingerprint GBM; stereo-blind features score exactly 0 under this protocol.
- **H3 knowledge helps when feedback is scarce:** the knowledge-on minus knowledge-off gap is largest at the smallest number of labels.
- **H4 transfer:** a small-n advantage persists on held-out target families and not only in distribution.
- **H5 IH-LM ≈ larger pretrained at equal information:** IH-LM with the loop is non-inferior to the strongest larger pretrained peptide/chemistry language model run through the same loop and fine-tuned on the same revealed labels (ratio lower bound > 0.90), at lower cost in parameters and GPU-hours. **Untested at v1.** Decision rules are those of `PREREG_LOOP.md` L7b, to be amended before any run so that the comparison set is the pretrained-model list [PENDING:docs/20261030_PEPTIDE_CHEM_LM_MODEL_SURVEY.md].
- **Null and competing outcomes (reported if they hold):** N1 the fingerprint GBM is as good or better at every n; N2 larger pretrained models dominate through scale, prior pretraining or corpus overlap with the evaluation data; N3 apparent gains reflect location of one laboratory's series; N4 feedback does not matter (the one-shot control equals the loop, or shuffled feedback also improves).

## 4. Methods
### 4.1 Data and its audit
Primary task data: CycPeptMPDB PAMPA measurements, 6,960 peptides after the loader filters (detection-floor values removed, structures merged), with labels standardised within each literature source (sources with fewer than 20 peptides pooled) [VERIFIED:docs/20261028_CODE_DATA_EVAL_AUDIT_LOG.md A1; research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/PREREG_LOOP.md L3/L5]. The audit found: duplicate structures were merged (7,027 rows → 6,960 identifiers → 6,958 unique canonical SMILES); the generic `Permeability` column differs from `PAMPA` in 73 of 7,027 rows (the PAMPA column is used from L5 onward); the 6,960 peptides correspond to 2,460 flat (stereo-free) structures and 2,380 distinct chirality-blind Morgan fingerprints, so about two thirds of the dataset are stereoisomers that a chirality-blind fingerprint cannot separate; 4,912 peptides (71 %) belong to flat-structure groups of two or more, with a median within-group label spread of 0.86 log units [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_HYPOTHESES_SMALL_LLM_LOOP.md §1; docs/20261028_CODE_DATA_EVAL_AUDIT_LOG.md A1]. HELM connection records give the topology of all 8,466 entries: head-to-tail 5,528, side-chain-to-C-terminus lariat 1,526, N-terminus-to-side-chain 1,412; no disulfide, stapled or bicyclic peptide occurs [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_CYCLIC_PEPTIDE_STRATIFICATION_ANALYSIS.md Part A §A0], so conclusions do not extend to those topologies. Binding data: 17,120 peptide macrocycles across 851 targets from a merged BindingDB/ChEMBL table, restricted to ring size ≥ 9 and ≥ 3 backbone amides [VERIFIED:docs/20261028_AVB6_RETROSPECTIVE_RECOVERY_PREREG.md §9, A1 audit result]. No cyclic-peptide solubility data exist in the repository; solubility is not analysed.

### 4.2 Evaluation design for binding (family-disjoint, frozen, gated)
- **Held-out unit:** target family (Pfam-Jaccard families; not sequence-identity families), in a five-fold family-held-out rotation with size-balanced packing [REPORTED:docs/20261028_AVB6_RETROSPECTIVE_RECOVERY_PREREG.md §14 (A6), `retro_recovery/data/folds_v1.csv`; hash to be re-verified before submission].
- **Frozen lists:** compounds, pair labels, targets and item tables were frozen with SHA-256 hashes before any scorer ran (for example `items_I.csv.gz`: 5da885424abe6cdeddff3b422524ce93bc617bb9ec7116965241feaedaae601e) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/multitarget/integrin_chembl/retro_recovery/data/FROZEN_HASHES.json].
- **Analyses:** I (primary) uses same-target measured inactives as decoys (46 targets in 26 families) [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §3 and §1]. M uses property-matched decoys that are binders of other targets; it was shown to be decided by a nearest-neighbour similarity shortcut (strongest baseline AUROC 0.914) and is diagnostic only [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §2, §4]. M2 matches decoys on similarity to the reference pool and brought that baseline to 0.530 [VERIFIED: same §2]; it was then found invalid by the typicality control below, and the attempted replacement M3 (typicality plus similarity plus proxy matching) is not achievable (Section 5.4).
- **Validity gates:** a no-target control and a typicality control (atom-LM likelihood and simple proxies) must be compatible with chance before any arm is judged. On M2 the atom language model, which has no target information, scored AUROC 0.665 (95 % CI 0.557–0.774) and exceeded the strongest baseline, so M2 cannot adjudicate arms [VERIFIED:docs/20261028_V5_PREREG_AND_LOOP_INTERFACE.md §6 table row A2].
- **Contamination:** compound-level flags mark positives present in pretraining corpora; results are reported for all items and for the contamination-controlled subset [REPORTED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §6].
- **Stereoisomer and duplicate handling:** compounds are grouped by flat structure; stereoisomers are never split across the train and target sides in a fold-level claim, and the within-group ranking protocol of Section 4.3 is used where stereo resolution is measured.
- **Decision rule:** an arm is SUPPORTED only if the lower 95 % bound of its difference from the strongest baseline exceeds zero for both AUROC and top-5 % enrichment, with family-clustered bootstrap intervals [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §1].

### 4.3 Retrospective loop replay
The replay reveals the measured label of a compound only after a model selects it. Each sequence starts from 100 labelled peptides; in each of 10 rounds a policy selects 50 pool peptides, their labels are revealed, and the model is updated [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/PREREG_LOOP.md L5]. Regimes: A, in-distribution random start; B, scaffold-shifted start from the two largest sources with the pool excluding shared scaffolds (no leakage by three measures, audit A1); C, in-distribution start with the pool excluding every peptide of the same stereo-free structure as an initial peptide (near-duplicate sensitivity). Regime L (leave-laboratories-out; L7f): held-out sources are a seeded random set of laboratories with at least 20 peptides, excluding the two largest (2020_Townsend, 2021_Kelly, 65 % of peptides), drawn until about 25 % of peptides are held out; the initial labels and pool come from training sources only [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7f]. Hit definitions: top 10 % of within-source standardised label (primary) and a second definition (within-group top 10 %) recorded for sensitivity [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L5 addendum]. Version record: L3 used chirality-blind features and pooled knowledge signals (version 1); L5 uses chirality-aware count Morgan fingerprints, the PAMPA column, source-standardised knowledge signals and tie-breaking by the task seed (version 2) [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L5].

For the replays that are cited, the preceding-summary caveat applies: a finite library permits only selection among compounds that already have measurements.

**Controls (applied to every model pair).** (1) data-matched one-shot: all revealed labels in a single update with the same total gradient budget; (2) random feedback; (3) shuffled-label feedback, corrected to a global shuffle of the pool's label vector without replacement, because within-batch shuffling preserves the selected batch's label distribution (found by the planted-signal control: the within-batch null gained +0.026 AUROC); the initial 100 labels remain true; (4) frozen model; (5) knowledge off [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7b, L7d]. Shuffled-label feedback must not improve; failure voids that model.

**Stereoisomer-resolved ranking.** For each flat-structure group with at least 3 members in the pool and non-zero label variance, one seeded member is the revealed sibling and the remaining members are targets; only target members are ranked; all members are removed from the pool and the initial and revealed sets. Under this protocol a stereo-blind scorer scores exactly 0. Leave-one-out evaluation is not used because a leave-one-out group-mean predictor has within-group Spearman correlation of exactly −1.0 (verified on 340 groups; random scores −0.025) [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_BASELINE_ERROR_ANALYSIS.md; PREREG_LOOP.md L7d].

**Additional held-out slices.** E (random held-out), E_f (stereoisomer groups) and E_s (low-data sources: sources with fewer than 400 peptides, a seeded sample of min(40, 30 % of the source) per source), logged at every round and at learning-curve points of 0, 25, 50 and 100 labels [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7c, L7d].

### 4.4 Models
**Baselines that have run.** Histogram gradient-boosted regression on chirality-aware Morgan count fingerprints (150 iterations, learning rate 0.1), a physicochemical-descriptor variant, a k-nearest-neighbour baseline and a Gaussian-process baseline (the last registered, [PENDING:no result at v1]) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/PREREG_LOOP.md L5, L7f, L7i; docs/20261029_BASELINE_ERROR_ANALYSIS.md].
**P0: proof-of-pipeline baseline, not a foundation model.** A 25.5 M-parameter atom-level SMILES language model pretrained for 49 minutes on one GPU on 2.87 M ChEMBL-derived molecules, of which 49,428 are macrocycles (1.7 %) [VERIFIED:S3 cyclic_peptide/allatom/pretrain/pretrain.json, read 2026-10-28]. It exists to prove the pipeline and as a typicality control; **no foundation-model claim is made from it.** Its pretraining corpus is ChEMBL-derived (Section 9.1 licence note) and overlaps some benchmark positives (Section 4.2).
**IH-LM (central model; design, no result).** A loop-native in-house hybrid model: a standard transformer backbone (our implementation; optional initialisation from open weights, disclosed) with an atom-level tokenizer that preserves chirality and topology (prior-art overlap acknowledged, not claimed as novel) and five loop components: M1 a knowledge-prior channel with evidence-weighted fusion whose weight shrinks as feedback accumulates (n0/(n0+n)); M2 a laboratory/assay offset module estimated from anchor compounds; M3 analog-retrieval cross-attention over the nearest revealed analogs; M4 an edit-space head predicting the effect of N-methyl, D-flip, residue and ring edits; M5 a dual-memory loop interface (fast symbolic memory updated in closed form from graded results, slow neural memory updated by fine-tuning or DPO). The ablation ladder R0 (plain) → +K_phys → +laboratory offset → +analog retrieval → +edit head → +dual-memory updates is the Aim-A experiment. Status at drafting: the code and a unit-test file exist, the pretraining plan P1 and its quality gates are pre-registered before any training, and nothing is trained or evaluated [VERIFIED:docs/20261030_IHLM_ARCHITECTURE_SPEC.md; docs/20261030_IHLM_PRETRAINING_PLAN_P1.md ("nothing is trained or launched")]. Provenance and disclosure of what is standard and what is ours: `docs/20261030_IHLM_PROVENANCE.md`.
**Larger pretrained comparators (fine-tuned plainly in the same loop and under the same controls).** Candidate list from the model survey, not re-verified by the drafter: PeptideCLM-2 (32 M, 114 M, 337 M parameters), HELM-BERT (55 M; its pretraining corpus includes CycPeptMPDB), ChemFM, SMI-TED, ESM-2 as a sequence-only control, with PeptideCLM and ChemBERTa-77M as already-adapted small references [LIT-UNVERIFIED for sizes and licences beyond those in Section 2; source: docs/20261030_PEPTIDE_CHEM_LM_MODEL_SURVEY.md]. Which of these are run is [PENDING:GPU capacity; Section 5.7]; the survey warns that several include evaluation-corpus structures in pretraining, so per-model overlap is reported.
**v4.** A 5 M-parameter monomer-vocabulary model, usable only on the fraction of compounds its converter maps (2.5 % of primary-analysis items) [VERIFIED:docs/20261028_V5_PREREG_AND_LOOP_INTERFACE.md §6]. The v4 audit found that the permeability effect of its conditioning was scored by a regressor with cross-validated R² of 0.069, that 9.1 % of its permeability-evaluation peptides appear verbatim in its training set, and that 34.7 % of CycPeptMPDB rows are Lariat peptides for which it has no topology token [VERIFIED:docs/20261028_V4_LLM_AUDIT_AND_EVAL.md rows 10, 12, 13]. v4 results are reported as inconclusive by coverage and are not used to support any hypothesis.

### 4.5 Cost accounting, contamination checks and an exploratory side note
**Cost accounting.** Every model reports parameters, fine-tune steps, wall-clock and GPU-hours per arm (GPU-seconds converted to GPU-hours; no conversion to currency in the primary comparison) and, where relevant, spot price and instance type as an audit record [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7b for the per-arm accounting design; amendment pending for GPU-hour reporting of the pretrained-model arms]. The cost axis of the central comparison is parameters and GPU-hours.
**Contamination.** Larger pretrained models may have seen CycPeptMPDB or its source literature in pretraining. For each pretrained model we record its pretraining corpus where documented, overlap with our evaluation compounds (flat-structure and Tanimoto ≥ 0.9 matches), use within-source standardised labels, and report results on the contamination-controlled subset [PENDING:per-model overlap table]. The stereo-scrambled memorisation probe pre-registered for generative models is applied only to models that can complete a SMILES prefix [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7b for the probe design].
**Exploratory side note (not part of the central comparison).** In-context prompting of general-purpose language models through an API (a cheap-tier model from each of two families, 4 seeds, budget-limited) was pre-registered as exploratory. On the same shared 150-candidate subsets, with 4 seeds per regime (below the pre-registered 8-pair power rule, so inconclusive), the in-context models gained little from feedback and a gradient-boosted model using the same revealed labels was far ahead: in the in-distribution regime Haiku-class loop 28.3 hits (frozen 28.5, zero-shot 26.8, random subset 19.8) against 44.8 for the gradient-boosted model (95 % CI 40.5 to 49.0); in the leave-laboratories-out regime Gemini-class loop 31.8 (random 34.5) against 62.5 (59.3 to 65.8) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/results/l7_aggregate.json; spend and failed-control models in docs/20261029_L7_RESULTS_LOG.md]. ### 5.2 Replay with controls (L7; GBM and physchem arms)
10 paired seeds per regime unless stated; paired percentile bootstrap [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/results/l7_aggregate.json].
- **Leave-laboratories-out (regime L), plain GBM:** loop minus random feedback +165.0 hits (156.3 to 175.6); minus frozen model +13.2 (2.6 to 23.7); minus shuffled-label feedback +79.5 (67.9 to 93.4). Physicochemical-descriptor channel: in-distribution A loop − random feedback +123.8 (112.5 to 133.5); scaffold-shifted B +20.0 (15.6 to 24.6) (10 seeds; the plain GBM B has only 2 seeds and is inconclusive).
- **Feedback versus data (the data-matched control).** In regime L the held-out ranking gain of the loop minus the data-matched one-shot update is +0.003 (95 % CI −0.034 to 0.038): sequential selection does not add held-out ranking gain beyond the same labels given at once. The loop minus random-feedback held-out gain is −0.072 (−0.113 to −0.014): random feedback improves held-out ranking more than the loop does. The loop's yield advantage is selection (exploitation), not better held-out prediction.
- **Controls.** The shuffled-label and frozen controls pass in the arms inspected (plain GBM regimes A and L; physicochemical channel regimes A and B): shuffled feedback does not improve held-out ranking. **A knowledge control fails:** feeding shuffled knowledge signals still raised held-out AUROC (regime A: +0.056, 0.040 to 0.070; regime L: +0.081, 0.046 to 0.116; control marked not passed), so any K_phys effect on ranking cannot be attributed to the knowledge content with these arms.

### 5.3 Knowledge channel K_phys (mined from training laboratories only)
Rules were mined from laboratories with at least 20 labelled peptides (21 laboratories, 6,776 peptides) and tested leave-one-laboratory-out against within-source labels [REPORTED:docs/20261029_KB_AUDIT_AND_GAPS.md §9]: K_phys median within-laboratory Spearman 0.25 (95 % laboratory-bootstrap CI 0.13 to 0.49; positive in 19 of 21 laboratories; pooled held-out 0.39); K_nme median −0.01 (positive in 10 of 20); the D↔L flip has no consistent direction (4 of 8 laboratories). ### 5.5 Stratified errors and substructure effects (baseline models)
[REPORTED:docs/20261029_CYCLIC_PEPTIDE_STRATIFICATION_ANALYSIS.md Part B; 19 unit tests; deviations B0 stated there.]
- **Skill depends on the laboratory being seen.** Within-source Spearman for the fingerprint GBM: 0.67 (blind) and 0.71 (chirality-aware) grouped by flat structure, **0.31 / 0.30 under leave-laboratories-out**; the descriptor GBM 0.67 grouped and 0.26 leave-labs-out. Chirality-aware features add about 0.04 overall (0.50 to 0.62 with 3–4 N-methyl groups; 0.38 to 0.64 for sources of 50–299 peptides).
- **Features.** Physicochemical descriptors alone reach 0.61 of the full 0.67; removing them costs 0.10, removing chirality 0.03; other families alone 0.23 to 0.36 and redundant.
- **Extrapolation.** Descriptors extrapolate to unseen chemistry better than fingerprints (unseen anonymous monomers: 0.49 vs 0.14–0.23; unseen peptoids 0.61 vs 0.43–0.45); fingerprints are better within seen chemistry.
- **N-methylation is not a monotone rule.** The count rule scores 0.25 overall within source and **−0.30 (−0.41 to −0.12) among peptides with five or more N-methyl groups**.
- **Source concentration.** Several strata are essentially one laboratory (for example Lariat side-chain-to-C-terminus peptides are 99.8 % 2021_Kelly; length 7 is 97.6 % 2020_Townsend); stratum results there are laboratory results.
- **Matched pairs.** Adding an N-methyl to the same peptide raises permeability on average (+0.45 z, 99 % CI 0.19 to 0.71; 4,139 pairs, 10 laboratories; 9 of 10 agree in sign); D↔L flips have no consistent direction (−0.03 z, −0.09 to 0.03; 7,423 pairs, 8 laboratories) [REPORTED:docs/20261029_KB_AUDIT_AND_GAPS.md §9].
Baseline diagnostics from the earlier error analysis (chirality-aware features +0.05 Spearman; worst errors not concentrated in stereoisomer groups; small recent sources worst, for example Faris 0.49 and Bhardwaj 0.42; 28 % of identical-fingerprint pairs differ by more than 1 SD) are exploratory [REPORTED:docs/20261029_BASELINE_ERROR_ANALYSIS.md].

## 6. Discussion
**What the evidence supports.** (i) With a simple learner, model-guided selection from a finite library beats random selection in-distribution by a wide margin that survives removing near-duplicates, and part of that margin is the location of one laboratory's series; under scaffold shift the margin is not established and a label-free physicochemical rule matches it (5.1). (ii) Sequential selection does not improve held-out prediction beyond the same labels given at once, and random feedback improves it more; the loop's benefit measured here is yield, not model improvement (5.2). (iii) Literature knowledge enters usefully only as a weak physicochemical prior (K_phys), which gives no measurable yield gain and cannot be separated from a shuffled-knowledge effect with the present arms (5.2, 5.3). (iv) Cross-laboratory skill is about half of within-laboratory skill, and for few-label transfer the strongest baseline is nearest-neighbour lookup (5.4, 5.5). [interpretation]
**Hypothesis status.**
| hypothesis | status at v1 |
|---|---|
| H1 sample efficiency (IH-LM vs baselines) | not tested for language models; only GBM learning behaviour reported |
| H2 stereoisomer resolution | not tested for language models |
| H3 knowledge helps when feedback is scarce | K_phys: no yield gain (A +2.4, −1.1 to 5.4; L −0.1, −3.8 to 4.8); knowledge-shuffled control fails; inconclusive |
| H4 transfer to held-out families | benchmark has no clean zero-shot test; few-label nearest-neighbour baseline 0.753–0.869; language-model arms not run |
| H5 IH-LM ≈ larger pretrained | untested |
| N1 fingerprint GBM as good as any model | not refuted; cheap-tier prompted LLMs far behind GBM (exploratory) |
| N3 gains reflect one laboratory's series | partly supported (69 % of in-distribution picks from one laboratory; 52.8 % of hits) |
| N4 feedback does not matter beyond data | consistent for held-out ranking in regime L (loop − one-shot +0.003, −0.034 to 0.038) for GBM |
**What the next experiments must show.** IH-LM must beat nearest-neighbour at equal k, beat the physicochemical rule under shift, pass the shuffled-label and shuffled-knowledge controls, and do so on a benchmark that passes the validity gate or with control-adjusted reporting.

## 7. Limitations
1. **Retrospective only.** The replay reveals labels of compounds that already have measurements. It supports statements about learning from graded feedback and ranking, not about generating better novel molecules, which requires a prospective experimental round.
2. **Narrow task and data.** Permeability ranking on one public database with series clustering and laboratory concentration (Section 4.1), and no stapled, bicyclic or disulfide peptides. Conclusions do not transfer to binding or other properties without further evidence.
3. **Comparison-set coverage.** The larger models are those pretrained peptide and chemistry models that are available and can be fine-tuned [candidate list in docs/20261030_PEPTIDE_CHEM_LM_MODEL_SURVEY.md, not re-verified; none run at v1]; a non-inferiority result applies to the models tested and not to all larger models. General-purpose API language models are outside the central comparison (Section 4.5).
4. **Contamination.** Larger pretrained models and our own pretraining corpora may overlap CycPeptMPDB, its source literature or benchmark positives; we use within-source labels, per-model overlap checks and contamination-controlled subsets, which reduce but do not remove the concern.
5. **Benchmark limits.** Families are Pfam-Jaccard families and not sequence-identity families; analysis I has 26 families and low power; the matched-decoy analyses select an isolated-chemotype subset; the knowledge-base coverage is below 7 % of targets.
6. **Model coverage.** v4's converter maps few benchmark compounds (2.5–5.4 %), so v4 results on the benchmark are inconclusive by coverage; v5 was not trained (superseded by the IH-LM plan).
7. **Statistical power.** The number of paired sequences, targets and families is limited; contrasts with fewer than 8 paired sequences are exploratory.
8a. **Scaffold-shift result.** The pre-registered loop-beats-random claim was not met under the scaffold-shifted regime for the primary hit definition (+22.2, −1.6 to 44.1) and the two hit definitions disagree; a physicochemical rule matches the loop there. Earlier version-1 numbers showing a gain in both regimes were superseded (Appendix C).
8b. **Laboratory concentration and leave-laboratories-out skill.** 52.8 % of within-source hits come from one laboratory; skill roughly halves when the test laboratory is unseen (0.67 to 0.30 Spearman); several strata are single-laboratory.
8c. **No clean benchmark.** No benchmark passes the validity gate cleanly (Section 5.4); M3 is not achievable; the knowledge-shuffled control fails in the loop arms.
8d. **The central comparison is not run.** IH-LM and the larger pretrained models have not been run through the loop, and P1 pretraining has not started; the hypotheses H1–H5 for language models are untested (Section 5.7). The P0 atom-level model is a proof of pipeline, not a foundation model.
8. **Evidence base is retrospective and public.** The replays cited as closed-loop evidence (Section 4.3.1) use real measured labels from the literature, with the limits listed there (finite library, analog hit-expansion, laboratory concentration, three preliminary seeds for the within-publication replay). The `c2_retro_loop` numbers await reproduction. The simulated integration tests and the earlier verifier-ladder system are not evidence of improvement.
9. **No measured data from our own campaigns.** The wet-lab ingestion pipeline (`wetlab_kd_to_pairs.py`, `campaign_assay_to_pairs.py`) and the closed-loop fixtures were exercised only on simulated assay values; the study contains no measured data from our own experimental campaigns. The ingestion code itself requires each manifest to declare a measured or simulated origin and rejects a mismatch (read in `campaign_assay_to_pairs.py`), but whether any measured campaign file has ever passed through it is not established by this study [REPORTED:coordinator message 2026-10-29; consistent with the project note that the closed loop is synthetic-only]. All feedback in the replay is revealed from public literature measurements.
10. **Self-correction history.** Several evaluation flaws were found in our own earlier designs (Section 4.8); other undetected flaws may remain, and all code and frozen lists are provided for independent checking.

## 8. Prior art and novelty statement
We do not claim a new model architecture, tokenisation or training objective. Chirality is represented implicitly in existing SMILES- and HELM-based models, and topology-aware peptide encoders exist [LIT-VERIFIED for PeptideCLM, PeptideCLM-2 and HELM-BERT as described in Section 2; other tokenisation prior art in the survey is LIT-UNVERIFIED], so a general claim about chirality-aware or topology-aware tokenisation would overlap published work. The stereoisomer-resolved ranking is offered as an evaluation protocol: the retrieved studies do not report a within-scaffold stereoisomer ranking metric, and one study lists stereochemistry and closure ablations as future work [LIT-VERIFIED: Amirahmadi et al., Limitations]; this is a statement about our searches, not proof that no such metric exists. The family-disjoint matched-decoy design borrows its rationale from published audits of decoy and similarity bias (Section 2); we found no cyclic-peptide binder benchmark of this form, within limited search coverage. The comparison of a small model in a feedback loop against larger pretrained peptide and chemistry models fine-tuned on the same revealed labels was not found in our searches; its components (peptide language models, fine-tuning, active learning) are published. The survey's own risk table rates the tokenisation claim high, the loop-versus-large comparison medium, the benchmark low-to-medium and the stereo metric medium-to-low for protocol (`docs/20261029_CYCLIC_PEPTIDE_REPRESENTATION_SURVEY.md`).
