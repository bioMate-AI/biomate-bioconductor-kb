## Abstract (v1; results limited to what has run)
**Background.** Closed design–test–learn loops are widely proposed for cyclic-peptide discovery, but claims of benefit are rarely tested against controls that separate the effect of feedback from the effect of additional data, and cyclic-peptide benchmarks are frequently confounded by near-duplicate series, laboratory concentration and decoy shortcuts.
**Objective.** Two aims, one paper. Aim B (delivered in this draft): build and audit a retrospective evaluation instrument for cyclic-peptide feedback loops (family-disjoint, hash-frozen, validity-gated, laboratory-aware) and measure baseline loop behaviour with it. Aim A (pre-registered, **not yet run**): test whether a loop-native in-house hybrid cyclic-peptide language model (IH-LM) matches or exceeds external pretrained peptide and chemistry language models fine-tuned on the same revealed labels. This draft reports no result for Aim A.
**Methods.** Retrospective replay in which labels are revealed only for selected compounds; five pre-registered controls (data-matched one-shot, random feedback, shuffled labels, frozen model, knowledge off); a family-disjoint, hash-frozen benchmark with no-target and typicality validity gates; leave-laboratories-out evaluation; per-source analysis; cost accounting in parameters and GPU-hours.
**Results (generalisation regimes first; interval types in brackets, Section 4.9).** (i) With a gradient-boosted learner on within-source permeability labels (30 seeds), the loop beat random selection in-distribution (series interpolation, not generalisation: +186.3 hits per 500 selections; 95 % spread 158.5 to 209.0, post hoc bootstrap CI of the mean 181.1 to 191.5), but 346 of its 500 picks came from one laboratory's series (2020_Townsend; random selection 206) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/results/l5_aggregate.json]. Under a scaffold-shifted start the mean advantage was +22.2 (post hoc bootstrap CI of the mean 17.7 to 26.5, seeds on one fixed library); the pre-registered criterion, written against the 95 % spread of the per-seed differences (−1.6 to 44.1), was not met, and a physicochemical rule that was added after the first loop result was not distinguishable from the loop (−0.5; spread −20.0 to 17.2; boot-mean −4.2 to 3.2). (ii) Under leave-laboratories-out the loop's yield exceeded random feedback by +165.0 (bootstrap CI of the mean over 10 seeds 156.0 to 175.7), but no gain in held-out ranking from sequential selection was detected: loop − one-shot +0.003 (−0.034 to 0.037; the interval half-width of about 0.036 corresponds to a minimal detectable difference of about 0.05 at 80 % power, my approximation), and with the held-out laboratories resampled (11 sources) loop − shuffled-label feedback +0.071 (cluster-bootstrap CI −0.079 to 0.181; minimal detectable difference about 0.19) and loop − frozen +0.011 (−0.071 to 0.080) include 0 for the plain GBM, while the physicochemical learner shows a feedback effect on held-out laboratories (loop − shuffled-label +0.155, 0.071 to 0.241) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/results/l7_aggregate.json]. The measured benefit is therefore selection yield, and an improvement of the model on new laboratories is not detected by these data. All seed-based intervals describe variability over initial sets on one fixed library and say nothing about new laboratories. (iii) No knowledge-prior variant met the pre-registered criterion in the primary analysis [VERIFIED: l5_aggregate.json]. (iv) **The validity-gate audit is itself a result:** applied retrospectively, no benchmark in the study passes it (the primary analysis I fails a fingerprint no-target control, 0.584; 95 % family-cluster CI 0.533 to 0.636; the benchmark is family-disjoint but not compound-disjoint, and a compound-disjoint rerun gives 0.580, 0.530 to 0.630), a stricter matched design is not achievable for this data collection, and on analysis I the zero-shot pretrained model (0.539) scores below that no-target control, so the task rewards compound-only features. The few-label analysis uses analysis I and is therefore reported only as a relative comparison: with 5 to 25 revealed labels per target a nearest-neighbour baseline is as good as fitted models (AUROC 0.753 to 0.869 against 0.539 zero-shot), the gain is concentrated where a revealed analog is close (k = 5, fit-on-revealed minus zero-shot: close +0.351, 0.259 to 0.448; remote +0.064, −0.002 to 0.135), and because the very-remote bin is almost empty (at most 4 % of items) a transferable gain could hardly be demonstrated by construction [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/multitarget/integrin_chembl/retro_recovery/results/validity_gate_I.json, h4b_baselines.json, h4b_strata.json]. (v) Across three retrospective loops on measured data (C2 binding, L5 permeability, a small permeability replay), model-guided selection beat random and, under distribution shift, most of the margin was reproduced by simple analog or physicochemical lookup; in C2 with a family-disjoint background learn − nearest-neighbour is +0.002 (95 % family-cluster bootstrap CI −0.018 to 0.047) and the original logged C2 numbers are withdrawn [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/c2_audit/results_A_aggregate.json]. (vi) Techniques meant to go beyond analog lookup gave null or failed primary tests: evidence-weighted fusion of a prior with analog lookup (T1 and T1b; the T1b permeability shuffled-label control failed as registered) and laboratory-offset calibration from shared anchors (not supported on public data; coverage-limited). (vii) Cheap-tier general-purpose language models prompted in context were far behind a gradient-boosted model on the same candidate subsets (exploratory; 4 seeds) [VERIFIED: l7_aggregate.json].
**Not available at v1.** No IH-LM or external pretrained model has been run through the loop; the central hypothesis is untested here (Section 5.8).
**Conclusions allowed by the evidence.** A simple learner's loop selects better than random from a finite library when the pool resembles the start, the advantage under scaffold shift is not established, a physicochemical rule added after the first loop result was not distinguishable from it there, and the loop's measured benefit is yield, not better held-out prediction. These results define the baselines and the evaluation controls that Aim A must meet.
**Limitations stated up front.** Retrospective evaluation can only reveal labels of compounds that already have measurements, so it does not test whether a model generates better novel molecules; the task is permeability ranking on one public database in which two laboratories hold most hits; inference is conditional on that library; Aim A awaits GPU runs.

## 1. Introduction
Cyclic peptides occupy a space between small molecules and biologics and are an active target for computational design, but their data are scarce, heterogeneous and clustered in analogue series. CycPeptMPDB, the principal public resource for membrane permeability, collects 7,334 structurally distinct peptides from 45 papers and two patents, measured mainly by PAMPA (6,941 measurements) with smaller Caco-2, RRCK and MDCK sets (the 2023 release; the file analysed here is a later release, Section 4.1) [LIT-SUMMARY: Li et al., J Chem Inf Model 2023, PMC10091415]. Many models have been trained on it. Reported performance depends strongly on how the data are split: in one benchmark (6-, 7- and 10-mer PAMPA peptides, 5,826 samples, binary label permeable when log Papp ≥ −6) every method lost discrimination when moving from a random to a scaffold split, for example 86.2 → 66.1 ROC-AUC for AttentiveFP [LIT-VERIFIED: Amirahmadi et al., arXiv 2511.21566, Table 3, read in converted PDF]. In the same study a Tanimoto-kernel Gaussian process on fingerprints attained the highest ROC-AUC on both splits (0.897 random, 0.804 scaffold) [LIT-SUMMARY: same, Table 6]. A large comparison of 25 pretrained molecular embeddings on 25 datasets found that nearly all showed negligible or no improvement over ECFP fingerprints [LIT-SUMMARY: Praski et al., arXiv 2508.06199]. A claim that a learned model improves on a fingerprint baseline therefore needs a baseline of that strength and an evaluation that does not reward memorising series.

Closed loops add a further question. A loop that selects compounds, receives measurements and updates a model may improve because the model learns from feedback, or merely because it has seen more data, or because it locates the one laboratory whose series dominate the positives. In the permeability data used here, after within-source standardisation 52.8 % of the hits still come from a single source (41 % of the peptides) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/PREREG_LOOP.md, L5 addendum]. Separating these explanations requires controls that are rarely reported.

We therefore study one question with a design built to be falsified: does a closed loop with verified feedback and knowledge improve a loop-native cyclic-peptide language model (IH-LM), and how does the result compare with a fingerprint baseline and with external pretrained peptide and chemistry language models fine-tuned on the same revealed labels? The contributions are stated conservatively. (i) A retrospective replay protocol with five pre-registered controls, a corrected shuffled-label null, and per-model cost accounting in parameters and GPU-hours. (ii) A family-disjoint cyclic-peptide binder evaluation with frozen, hash-verified lists and two validity gates (a no-target control and a typicality control) that a benchmark must pass before any arm is judged. (iii) A stereoisomer-resolved ranking protocol with a single revealed/target split per group, together with a demonstration that leave-one-out evaluation of this task is artifactual. (iv) A documented audit trail, including errors found in our own earlier code and evaluation. We do not claim a new model architecture or tokenisation (Section 8).

## 2. Related work
**Data and permeability models.** CycPeptMPDB encodes peptides in HELM, defines Circle (head-to-tail, about 70 %) and Lariat (about 30 %) shapes, and, when a peptide was measured by two assays in one publication, uses the assay with more data, applying the assay rank (PAMPA, Caco-2, RRCK, MDCK) only when the values are similar [LIT-SUMMARY: PMC10091415]. CycPeptMP combines atom-, monomer- and peptide-level features in a fusion model with data augmentation and reports a mean absolute error of 0.355 and correlation 0.883 [LIT-UNVERIFIED: Li, Yanagisawa, Akiyama, Brief Bioinform 2024, abstract via Europe PMC (could not be re-opened by the QA audit); the split protocol was not verified]. PeptideCLM is a 6-layer, 12-head, 768-dimensional BERT-style SMILES model pretrained on 23 M molecules (10 M modified or cyclic peptides); on CycPeptMPDB it was evaluated by leave-one-cluster-out over six k-means clusters and reported ROC-AUC 0.781 ± 0.067 (dispersion over held-out clusters as reported; type not verified) [LIT-UNVERIFIED: Feller and Wilke, PMC11326283]. PeptideCLM-2 is a suite of nine SMILES encoders (three sizes, 32 M, 114 M and 337 M parameters, each trained with three objectives: masked language modelling, multi-task regression to RDKit descriptors, and a dual objective) with a 405-token k-mer vocabulary, pretrained on over 100 M molecules according to a search listing (the model survey gives about 118 M from the model cards; the abstract page does not state the count); the 337 M model reports AUROC 0.836 ± 0.011 (hybrid objective; dispersion as reported) and 0.829 ± 0.020 (masked-language-modelling objective) on permeability using splits from prior work; the bioRxiv version is titled "PeptideMTR: Scaling SMILES-Based Language Models for Therapeutic Peptide Engineering" and the PMC version "Scaling SMILES-based chemical language models for therapeutic peptide engineering" [LIT-SUMMARY: bioRxiv abstract page and PMC12803269, opened 2026-10-30; the corpus count is LIT-SNIPPET]. HELM-BERT applies a DeBERTa encoder to HELM notation, pretrained on 39,079 peptides, and reports gains over SMILES-based language models on cyclic permeability and peptide–protein interaction [LIT-SUMMARY: Lee et al., arXiv 2512.23175, abstract only]. A decoupled alignment kernel reports that stereochemistry and macrocycle-closure ablations remain future work and that its scaffold splits compute Murcko scaffolds ignoring chirality [LIT-VERIFIED: Amirahmadi et al., Section 2 and Limitations in the converted PDF]. An applicability-domain-aware protocol (Geylan et al., Digital Discovery 2024) is cited by that work as the basis of its splits [LIT-UNVERIFIED: the paper itself could not be opened in this pass; it is cited here only as described by Amirahmadi et al.]. A 13-method benchmark by Liu et al. is likewise known only through citation in the same paper [LIT-UNVERIFIED].

**Mechanisms.** Partially N-methylated cyclic hexapeptides were more permeable than fully methylated ones, and one trimethylated compound showed 28 % oral bioavailability in rat [LIT-SUMMARY: White et al., Nat Chem Biol 2011, abstract]. Computationally designed macrocycles of 6–12 residues included 84 designs with apparent permeability above 1 × 10⁻⁶ cm/s, and 29 of 35 solved structures matched the design models within 1.2 Å backbone RMSD [LIT-SUMMARY: Bhardwaj et al., Cell 2022, abstract]. These mechanistic results come from small congeneric series and should not be read as per-feature effect sizes for the aggregate database.

**Benchmark pitfalls.** Ligand-only convolutional models reached AUC 0.98 on DUD-E, attributed to analogue and decoy bias [LIT-SUMMARY: Chen et al., PLoS One 2019]. Nearly 600 train–test similarities between PDBbind and CASF involved 49 % of CASF complexes, and retraining on a filtered split sharply reduced the performance of some models [LIT-UNVERIFIED: Graber et al., Nat Mach Intell 2025]. LIT-PCBA contains 2,491 2D-identical inactives across training and validation, and a parameter-free memorisation baseline matches or exceeds published deep models [LIT-SUMMARY: Huang et al., arXiv 2507.21404]. Our evaluation design follows the lessons of these audits.

**Language models for molecules with few examples.** Fine-tuned GPT-3 was reported to perform comparably to or better than conventional machine learning in the low-data limit [LIT-UNVERIFIED (search level): Jablonka et al., Nat Mach Intell 2024, DOI 10.1038/s42256-023-00788-1]. We found no study in our search that compares a small peptide language model inside a feedback loop with external pretrained peptide or chemistry language models fine-tuned on the same revealed labels for peptide property ranking; the absence of a hit reflects limited search coverage and is not proof of novelty. Work that used simulated assay values or that built a system without replaying measured labels is not cited as validation (Section 4.3.1). ## 3. Hypotheses
Pre-specified in `docs/20261029_HYPOTHESES_SMALL_LLM_LOOP.md` [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_HYPOTHESES_SMALL_LLM_LOOP.md].
- **H1 sample efficiency:** IH-LM's learning curve lies above the baseline's at small numbers of revealed labels and the gap shrinks as labels grow.
- **H2 stereochemical resolution:** with a single seeded revealed/target split per flat-structure group, a stereo-aware model ranks target stereoisomers above chance and above a chirality-aware fingerprint GBM; stereo-blind features score exactly 0 under this protocol.
- **H3 knowledge helps when feedback is scarce:** the knowledge-on minus knowledge-off gap is largest at the smallest number of labels.
- **H4 transfer:** a small-n advantage persists on held-out target families and not only in distribution.
- **H5 IH-LM ≈ external pretrained at equal information:** IH-LM with the loop is non-inferior to the strongest external pretrained peptide/chemistry language model run through the same loop and fine-tuned on the same revealed labels (ratio lower bound > 0.90), at lower cost in parameters and GPU-hours. **Untested at v1.** Decision rules are those of `PREREG_LOOP.md` L7b, to be amended before any run so that the comparison set is the pretrained-model list [PENDING:docs/20261030_PEPTIDE_CHEM_LM_MODEL_SURVEY.md].
- **Null and competing outcomes (reported if they hold):** N1 the fingerprint GBM is as good or better at every n; N2 external pretrained models dominate through scale, prior pretraining or corpus overlap with the evaluation data; N3 apparent gains reflect location of one laboratory's series; N4 feedback does not matter (the one-shot control equals the loop, or shuffled feedback also improves).

## 4. Methods
### 4.1 Data and its audit
Primary task data: the local CycPeptMPDB file is a later release than the 2023 paper describes: version 1.0 (7,451 rows) plus version 1.2 (1,015 rows), 8,466 rows in total, 7,991 unique structure identifiers and 7,298 PAMPA values, whereas the paper counts 7,334 peptides and 6,941 PAMPA measurements [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/data/raw/CycPeptMPDB_Peptide_All.csv, `Version` column, counted 2026-10-30; file hash to be recorded at release]. CycPeptMPDB PAMPA measurements, 6,960 peptides after the loader filters (detection-floor values removed, structures merged), with labels standardised within each literature source (sources with fewer than 20 peptides pooled) [VERIFIED:docs/20261028_CODE_DATA_EVAL_AUDIT_LOG.md A1; research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/PREREG_LOOP.md L3/L5]. The audit found: duplicate structures were merged (7,027 rows → 6,960 identifiers → 6,958 unique canonical SMILES); the generic `Permeability` column differs from `PAMPA` in 73 of 7,027 rows (the PAMPA column is used from L5 onward); the 6,960 peptides correspond to 2,460 flat (stereo-free) structures and 2,380 distinct chirality-blind Morgan fingerprints, so about two thirds of the dataset are stereoisomers that a chirality-blind fingerprint cannot separate; 4,912 peptides (71 %) belong to flat-structure groups of two or more, with a median within-group label spread of 0.86 log units [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_HYPOTHESES_SMALL_LLM_LOOP.md §1; docs/20261028_CODE_DATA_EVAL_AUDIT_LOG.md A1]. HELM connection records give the topology of all 8,466 entries: head-to-tail 5,528, side-chain-to-C-terminus lariat 1,526, N-terminus-to-side-chain 1,412; no disulfide, stapled or bicyclic peptide occurs [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_CYCLIC_PEPTIDE_STRATIFICATION_ANALYSIS.md Part A §A0], so conclusions do not extend to those topologies. Binding data: 17,120 peptide macrocycles across 851 targets from a merged BindingDB/ChEMBL table, restricted to ring size ≥ 9 and ≥ 3 backbone amides [VERIFIED:docs/20261028_AVB6_RETROSPECTIVE_RECOVERY_PREREG.md §9, A1 audit result]. No cyclic-peptide solubility data exist in the repository; solubility is not analysed.

### 4.2 Evaluation design for binding (family-disjoint, frozen, gated)
- **Held-out unit:** target family (Pfam-Jaccard families; not sequence-identity families), in a five-fold family-held-out rotation with size-balanced packing [REPORTED:docs/20261028_AVB6_RETROSPECTIVE_RECOVERY_PREREG.md §14 (A6), `retro_recovery/data/folds_v1.csv`; hash to be re-verified before submission].
- **Frozen lists:** compounds, pair labels, targets and item tables were frozen with SHA-256 hashes before any scorer ran (for example `items_I.csv.gz`: 5da885424abe6cdeddff3b422524ce93bc617bb9ec7116965241feaedaae601e) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/multitarget/integrin_chembl/retro_recovery/data/FROZEN_HASHES.json].
- **Analyses:** I (primary) uses same-target measured inactives as decoys (46 targets in 26 families) [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §3 and §1]. M uses property-matched decoys that are binders of other targets; it was shown to be decided by a nearest-neighbour similarity shortcut (strongest baseline AUROC 0.914) and is diagnostic only [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §2, §4]. M2 matches decoys on similarity to the reference pool and brought that baseline to 0.530 [VERIFIED: same §2]; it was then found invalid by the typicality control below, and the attempted replacement M3 (typicality plus similarity plus proxy matching) is not achievable (Section 5.4).
- **Validity gates (applied retrospectively; no analysis passes).** A no-target control and a typicality control (atom-LM likelihood, simple proxies, fingerprint logistic regression) must be compatible with chance before any arm is judged. The gate was applied after the failure of the typicality control on M2 was known (disclosed in the A7 amendment), and **no analysis (I, M, M2, M3, H4b) passes it**: M2's atom language model, which has no target information, scored AUROC 0.665 (95 % family-cluster bootstrap CI 0.557–0.774) and exceeded the strongest baseline [VERIFIED:docs/20261028_V5_PREREG_AND_LOOP_INTERFACE.md §6 row A2; validity_gate_M2.json]; the primary analysis I fails the fingerprint control (0.584; 95 % family-cluster bootstrap CI 0.533–0.636) [VERIFIED:validity_gate_I.json]. Stated plainly: analysis I fails the no-target gate. It is family-disjoint but not compound-disjoint: of 9,432 rows (6,619 compounds), 1,731 compounds occur in more than one target and 364 in more than one family, and 1,093 rows (11.6 %) have the same compound in another fold. A compound-disjoint rerun of the controls gives C2 0.5658 (95 % family-cluster CI 0.5056 to 0.6252; registered 0.5591, 0.4994 to 0.6189, so the lower bound moves from a marginal pass to a marginal fail) and C3 0.5796 (0.5304 to 0.6302; registered 0.5837); overlap therefore does not cause the failure. Five within-target label permutations of the full gate give means C1 0.5075, C2 0.4887, C3 0.5052, so the 0.584 is signal in the labels and not an artefact of the pipeline (five permutations, point estimates only) [VERIFIED:docs/committee_raw/20261030b/ext_check_gate.json]. The central comparison (IH-LM against external pretrained models in the loop) has not been run. We treat this failure as a finding about cyclic-peptide binder benchmarks (Section 6) and report control-adjusted scores, not as an obstacle removed.
- **Contamination:** compound-level flags mark positives present in pretraining corpora; results are reported for all items and for the contamination-controlled subset [REPORTED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §6].
- **Stereoisomer and duplicate handling:** compounds are grouped by flat structure; stereoisomers are never split across the train and target sides in a fold-level claim, and the within-group ranking protocol of Section 4.3 is used where stereo resolution is measured.
- **Decision rule:** an arm is SUPPORTED only if the lower 95 % bound of its difference from the strongest baseline exceeds zero for both AUROC and top-5 % enrichment, with family-clustered bootstrap intervals [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §1].

### 4.3 Retrospective loop replay
The replay reveals the measured label of a compound only after a model selects it. Each sequence starts from 100 labelled peptides; in each of 10 rounds a policy selects 50 pool peptides, their labels are revealed, and the model is updated [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/PREREG_LOOP.md L5]. Regimes: A, in-distribution random start (series interpolation, not generalisation; the headline regimes are B and L); B, scaffold-shifted start from the two largest sources with the pool excluding shared scaffolds (no leakage by three measures, audit A1); C, in-distribution start with the pool excluding every peptide of the same stereo-free structure as an initial peptide (near-duplicate sensitivity). Regime L (held-out laboratories; L7f) is an irregular design and not a clean leave-laboratories-out: held-out sources are a seeded random subset of the 18 eligible smaller laboratories (at least 20 peptides; the two dominant laboratories, 2020_Townsend and 2021_Kelly, 65 % of peptides, are never held out), drawn until about 25 % of peptides are held out, which gives 6 to 18 laboratories and 25.2 % to 34.3 % of peptides per seed, with all 18 held out in 3 of 10 seeds; any interpretation that depends on this irregularity is conditional on the registered L9 sensitivity run (cap of 9 held-out laboratories), which has not been run; the initial labels and pool come from training sources only [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7f]. Hit definitions: top 10 % of within-source standardised label (primary) and a second definition (within-group top 10 %) recorded for sensitivity [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L5 addendum]. Version record: L3 used chirality-blind features and pooled knowledge signals (version 1); L5 uses chirality-aware count Morgan fingerprints, the PAMPA column, source-standardised knowledge signals and tie-breaking by the task seed (version 2) [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L5].

**Controls (applied to every model pair).** (1) data-matched one-shot: all revealed labels in a single update with the same total gradient budget; (2) random feedback; (3) shuffled-label feedback, corrected to a global shuffle of the pool's label vector without replacement, because within-batch shuffling preserves the selected batch's label distribution (found by the planted-signal control: the within-batch null gained +0.026 AUROC); the initial 100 labels remain true; (4) frozen model; (5) knowledge off [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7b, L7d]. **Stereoisomer-resolved ranking.** For each flat-structure group with at least 3 members in the pool and non-zero label variance, one seeded member is the revealed sibling and the remaining members are targets; only target members are ranked; all members are removed from the pool and the initial and revealed sets. Under this protocol a stereo-blind scorer scores exactly 0. Leave-one-out evaluation is not used because a leave-one-out group-mean predictor has within-group Spearman correlation of exactly −1.0 (verified on 340 groups; random scores −0.025) [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_BASELINE_ERROR_ANALYSIS.md; PREREG_LOOP.md L7d].

### 4.9 Statistics
**Interval types (every interval in this paper carries one of these labels).** *Spread*: the central 95 % (or 99 %) range of the per-seed paired differences (2.5–97.5 or 0.5–99.5 percentiles); it describes run-to-run variability and is **not a confidence interval**. The pre-registered L5 rules C1 (loop beats random in both primary regimes) and C2 (some knowledge variant helps in both regimes) were written against it and were judged by it. *Boot-mean*: percentile bootstrap confidence interval of the mean paired difference over seeds; the primary interval for L7 contrasts (amendment L7k) and, for L5, a **post hoc** addition that is labelled exploratory. *Cluster-boot*: bootstrap resampling of the dependency unit: target families for the benchmark, laboratories for the leave-one-laboratory-out analyses, flat-structure groups for stratified cross-validation. *Meta*: random-effects meta-analytic interval over sources. *SD*: standard deviation over seeds. **Seed-based intervals (spread and boot-mean) capture variability from initial sets and bootstrap resamples on one fixed library; they do not capture sampling of laboratories or chemical series, and no statement about new laboratories rests on them.** Generalisation is assessed only by regime L (held-out laboratories) and by the family-held-out benchmark with cluster intervals. At least 8 paired sequences are required for any claim; 99 % intervals are used where several knowledge variants are compared; non-inferiority and superiority rules are those of Section 3 and `PREREG_LOOP.md` L7b/L7j [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7k, aggregate_prior.py]. Deviations from the registered plan are dated amendments and are labelled exploratory or post hoc where they were made after a result was seen (the retrospective validity gate, the label-free rules, the boot-mean intervals for L5, T1b). The cluster interval for the benchmark (`_family_ci`) is a family-level estimand: AUROC is averaged within each family, families are weighted equally, and targets with undefined AUROC are dropped.

### 4.10 Planned design elements (to be registered as dated amendments before any run; none has run)
Source: the two-reviewer committee triage in `docs/20261030_COMMITTEE_SYNTHESIS.md` §5 (reviewer opinion checked by the chair; our design decisions are fixed at registration).
- **Three-contrast design for architecture versus scale.** (i) The same base model plain versus with the IH-LM modules; (ii) IH-LM full versus an external pretrained model fine-tuned plainly; (iii) the **external base model plus the same modules** (equal information), the only fair test of architecture against scale.
- **No-LM ablations.** Retrieval-only (nearest neighbour), retrieval plus laboratory offset without a language model, K_phys only, and source identifier only; if IH-LM cannot beat these, the language model adds nothing.
- **Primary endpoints and multiplicity.** One primary endpoint per aim; cluster bootstrap at the highest dependency unit (source for loop contrasts, target family for the benchmark, scaffold series where relevant); Benjamini–Hochberg control for secondary contrasts; nested development and test seeds for rung selection (rung chosen on seeds 0–4 and reported on seeds 5–9) [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7h claim rules].
- **Non-inferiority margin.** The 90 % ratio rule is arbitrary; an absolute-loss threshold is added and both are reported.
- **Cluster-based power.** Effective sample sizes are the numbers of sources, families or series, not compounds; seeds share the same compounds and describe algorithmic variance only.
- **Analog-distance strata for the few-label benchmark** (stereo-sibling removed, close, medium, remote) with gain over nearest neighbour reported within each.
- **Contamination table per external checkpoint** (pretraining corpus, date, known CycPeptMPDB or ChEMBL overlap; exact, stereo-stripped and scaffold scans; contamination-controlled subset).
- **Stereo claims** only against the chirality-aware GBM under the single-split protocol, with paired order accuracy and activity-cliff detection.

Controls behave: planted offsets are recovered (r ≥ 0.96); with anchors in every source the method works (planted gain +0.151, 0.032 to 0.246); with the real anchor coverage the planted gain is −0.096 (−0.189 to 0.089); the null world gives −0.001 (−0.008 to 0.011). **Offset calibration is therefore not supported on CycPeptMPDB as it stands, and the null is attributable to anchor coverage and not to the method;** within-source z-scoring is not better than raw labels in leave-source-out ranking (z − raw median +0.020, CI includes 0). **This is a data-design finding, not a negative result for the method:** only 57 anchor rows exist, the sources with at least 3 or 5 anchors are 6 to 8 small ones, none of them among the four sources that hold about 85 % of the rows, so a shared anchor panel (a few cheap reference peptides measured in every new assay) is needed; offsets need purpose-built shared reference compounds in new assays, a prospective design element.

## 6. Discussion
**What the evidence supports.** (i) With a simple learner, model-guided selection from a finite library beats random selection in-distribution (series interpolation) by a wide margin that survives removing near-duplicates, and part of that margin is the location of one laboratory's series; under scaffold shift the margin is not established and a label-free physicochemical rule matches it (5.1). (ii) No improvement of held-out prediction from sequential selection beyond the same labels given at once is detected (loop − one-shot +0.003, −0.034 to 0.037; differences below about 0.05 cannot be excluded), and random feedback improves it more on the seed-based E-AUROC contrast (not on the source-cluster contrast, which includes 0); the loop's benefit measured here is yield, not demonstrated model improvement on new laboratories (5.2). (iii) K_phys has a heterogeneous but real zero-fit association with permeability in unseen laboratories offline (5.3). In the loop, a small held-out-AUROC advantage over a shuffled channel appears in the scaffold-shifted regime for 2 of 3 GBM backends and not in regimes A or L or the physicochemical backend; part of the regime-B gain is any-channel, not rule content; the yield advantage over shuffled knowledge is largely the cost of shuffled noise; and with laboratories resampled K_phys is never better than shuffled knowledge on held-out ranking. These are exploratory leads from 54 dependent contrasts without multiplicity correction (5.2). (iv) Neither technique that was meant to move the loop beyond analog lookup worked as registered: evidence-weighted fusion is suggestive in permeability but null on its primary test and fails non-inferiority in binding, and lab-offset anchor calibration is not supported on public data because anchors are too sparse (5.7). (v) Cross-laboratory skill is about half of within-laboratory skill, and the few-label gain is concentrated where a revealed analog is close, with no gain demonstrated to be transferable; the strongest baseline is nearest-neighbour lookup (5.4, 5.5). **(vi) The validity-gate audit is a finding in its own right.** Three successive decoy designs each failed a no-target control for a different reason (similarity, typicality, residual compound-only separability of a target's chemotype series), so a clean zero-shot benchmark for cyclic-peptide binders was not obtainable from this data collection with our constructions; the audit protocol (no-target controls, family-cluster intervals, unverifiable-by-default, control-adjusted scoring), the leave-one-out artefact and the stated power limits are offered as methodology, with the limits of Section 5.4, not as a proof that all matched-decoy designs fail. (vii) **Cross-loop pattern.** The three retrospective loops on measured data are consistent: model-guided selection beats random, and under shift lookup reproduces most of the margin (5.6); this is two analyses plus one illustration, and the in-distribution regimes of L5 show the loop clearly better than the lookup rules (loop − N-methyl rule +105.4 and loop − K_z rule +53.6), so the pattern is a statement about shift; loop-specific learning beyond lookup is not demonstrated. ## 7. Limitations

**What IH-LM is today.** About 800 lines of code (model 287, symbolic 170, tests 240, intrinsic check 102): a small standard transformer backbone with an atom-level tokenizer, an analog-attention block and heads, not trained at scale (no checkpoint). Most modules are symbolic or closed-form (fast memory for laboratory offsets, similarity retrieval, N-methyl and D-flip edit effects, update rules). Three of 14 tests are marked expected-failure with no threshold loosened: neural analog attention, net-only rho 0.525 against 0.113 without it (gap 0.41 meets its 0.25 criterion but the absolute threshold 0.70 is missed); the same module leave-laboratory-out rho 0.444 against 0.60; verifier head AUC 0.529 on new families against 0.90. The 25.5 M-parameter P0 model is a proof of pipeline and the planned 71-94 M-parameter P1 is untrained. It is not a foundation model and is not presented as one. The evidence so far (C2, L5, T1/T1b) suggests most value would come from the symbolic and retrieval parts, and the neural part has not shown that it adds anything. It becomes a substantial contribution only if a trained P1 passes its gates, the neural analog module and verifier are fixed or removed, an ablation ladder beats analog lookup and the information-privileged pretrained-plus-analog baseline, and a fair comparison with external pretrained models is run.
1. **Retrospective only.** The replay reveals labels of compounds that already have measurements. It supports statements about learning from graded feedback and ranking, not about generating better novel molecules, which requires a prospective experimental round.
2. **Narrow task and data.** Permeability ranking on one public database with series clustering and laboratory concentration (Section 4.1), and no stapled, bicyclic or disulfide peptides. Conclusions do not transfer to binding or other properties without further evidence.
3. **Comparison-set coverage.** The larger models are those pretrained peptide and chemistry models that are available and can be fine-tuned [candidate list in docs/20261030_PEPTIDE_CHEM_LM_MODEL_SURVEY.md, not re-verified; none run at v1]; a non-inferiority result applies to the models tested and not to all larger models. General-purpose API language models are outside the central comparison (Section 4.5).
4. **Contamination.** External pretrained models and our own pretraining corpora may overlap CycPeptMPDB, its source literature or benchmark positives; we use within-source labels, per-model overlap checks and contamination-controlled subsets, which reduce but do not remove the concern.
5. **Benchmark limits.** Families are Pfam-Jaccard families and not sequence-identity families; analysis I has 26 families and low power; the matched-decoy analyses select an isolated-chemotype subset; the knowledge-base coverage is below 7 % of targets.
6. **Model coverage.** v4's converter maps few benchmark compounds (2.5 % to 5.4 % of items), so v4 results on the benchmark are inconclusive by coverage; v5 was not trained (superseded by the IH-LM plan).
7. **Statistical power.** The number of paired sequences, targets and families is limited; contrasts with fewer than 8 paired sequences are exploratory.
8. **Scaffold-shift result.** The pre-registered loop-beats-random claim was not met under the scaffold-shifted regime for the primary hit definition (+22.2, −1.6 to 44.1) and the two hit definitions disagree; a physicochemical rule matches the loop there. Earlier version-1 numbers showing a gain in both regimes were superseded (Appendix C).
9. **Laboratory concentration and leave-laboratories-out skill.** 52.8 % of within-source hits come from one laboratory; skill roughly halves when the test laboratory is unseen (Spearman 0.67 grouped by structure versus 0.30 leave-laboratories-out); several strata are single-laboratory.
10. **No benchmark passes the validity gate.** The gate was applied retrospectively (after the M2 failure was known); the primary analysis fails the fingerprint control; M3 is not achievable for this data collection (one collection, BindingDB plus ChEMBL; our own constructions only; Pfam-Jaccard families); the gate itself has low power with 14 to 26 families, and the zero-shot analyses can exclude only effects above about 0.11 AUROC on I and 0.19 on M2 (Section 5.4).
11. **Inference is conditional on one library, and intervals are of different types.** Seeds share the same 6,960 compounds and resample only initial sets and bootstrap draws; seed-based intervals (spread and boot-mean) describe algorithmic variability and not uncertainty about other libraries, laboratories or series, and no statement about new laboratories rests on them (regime-L laboratories are redrawn per seed from a pool of 18; yield contrasts are seed-based and only E-metric contrasts have a source-cluster interval). Two laboratories hold about two thirds of the peptides and most hits. The pre-registered L5 judgements (C1, C2) used the spread; mean-based intervals are post hoc; the knowledge contrasts on seeds 0–9 are development data, are uncorrected for multiplicity, and are not claimed. Cluster-level power is planned (Section 4.10).
12. **The central comparison is not run.** IH-LM and the external pretrained models have not been run through the loop, and P1 pretraining has not started; the hypotheses H1–H5 for language models are untested (Section 5.8). The P0 atom-level model is a proof of pipeline, not a foundation model.
13. **Evidence base is retrospective and public.** The replays cited as closed-loop evidence (Section 4.3.1) use real measured labels from the literature, with the limits listed there (finite library, analog hit-expansion, laboratory concentration, three preliminary seeds for the within-publication replay). The C2 binding-loop rerun did not reproduce the original logged numbers within the pre-registered tolerance and those numbers are withdrawn; the rerun and its family-disjoint extension are cited with limits (Section 5.6). The simulated integration tests and the earlier verifier-ladder system are not evidence of improvement.
14. **No measured data from our own campaigns.** The wet-lab ingestion pipeline (`wetlab_kd_to_pairs.py`, `campaign_assay_to_pairs.py`) and the closed-loop fixtures were exercised only on simulated assay values; the study contains no measured data from our own experimental campaigns. The ingestion code itself requires each manifest to declare a measured or simulated origin and rejects a mismatch (read in `campaign_assay_to_pairs.py`), but whether any measured campaign file has ever passed through it is not established by this study [REPORTED:coordinator message 2026-10-29; consistent with the project note that the closed loop is synthetic-only]. All feedback in the replay is revealed from public literature measurements.
15. **Self-correction history.** Several evaluation flaws were found in our own earlier designs (Section 4.8); other undetected flaws may remain, and all code and frozen lists are provided for independent checking.

## 8. Prior art and novelty statement
**Not claimed as novel.** The backbone, atom-level tokenisation with chirality and topology, retrieval, and evidence-weighted shrinkage. Chirality is represented implicitly in existing SMILES- and HELM-based models and topology-aware peptide encoders exist (PeptideCLM, PeptideCLM-2, HELM-BERT [LIT-SUMMARY]); retrieval-augmented molecular property prediction exists (MolRAG, ACL 2025; MolE-RAG, arXiv 2606.05693 [LIT-SNIPPET; details unverified]); LLM-guided nearest-neighbour methods exist (Gupta, Hartford and Liu, *Findings of EMNLP 2025*, arXiv 2509.21403 [LIT-SNIPPET]; reported there to find that language-model agents ignore feedback under permuted labels and that classical methods beat them, a summary we have not verified); matched-molecular-pair and activity-cliff modelling is established (ACNet, arXiv 2302.07541 [LIT-SNIPPET]); Bayesian priors and shrinkage are general. A 13-method CycPeptMPDB benchmark with scaffold splits exists (Liu, Li, Verma and Lee, *J Cheminformatics* 2025 [LIT-UNVERIFIED: the PubMed page (40877984) was blocked in the QA audit; the content below is the chair's summary]); per that summary it does not address laboratory or source effects or stereochemistry. PepBenchmark (Zhang, Wang, Zhou et al., arXiv 2604.10531; the venue "ICLR 2026" is taken from workstream 3's reading and was not verified from the abstract by the QA audit), read in full text by workstream 3 [REPORTED:docs/20261030_PRIOR_ART_PEPBENCHMARK_PROTAC_CEILING.md; the leaderboard appendix was skimmed, not audited], is the closest peptide benchmark: it covers cyclic content only through CycPeptMPDB permeability and one 277-point non-canonical peptide–protein affinity set; it uses protein cold-start splits, k-mer and near-duplicate leakage controls, and negative sampling matched on shallow features (length, charge, hydrophobicity, 1-mer and 2-mer composition) that is validated by marginal divergences and not by a trained no-target classifier; it has no source-offset modelling, no few-label analysis and no analog-distance analysis. Its negative sampling was not tested by us. The inter-laboratory ceiling paper (Klamt, Nejdl and Tang, arXiv 2605.11764, preprint) is on PROTACs, with no cyclic content: the 0.124 AUROC drop is random cross-validation 0.802 to cross-laboratory 0.678 on same-compound replicate measurements (target-clustered bootstrap 95 % CI 0.051 to 0.197; the 0.802 and 0.678 values and this interval, and the statement that it has no laboratory-held-out splits, are from workstream 3's reading and are not verifiable from the abstract), and its few-label retraining at k = 5 lifts leave-one-target-out AUROC from 0.668 to 0.705; it quantifies the laboratory ceiling without anchor compounds, offsets or laboratory-held-out splits [REPORTED: same document, read from the PDF by workstream 3]. **We do not claim a first cyclic-peptide benchmark.**
**Candidate contributions, to be tested and not claimed.** (i) Laboratory or assay offsets estimated from shared anchor compounds (module M2): the planted-offset test shows the estimator works when every source has anchors, but the retrospective test on CycPeptMPDB did not support it (anchor coverage too sparse; kill criterion met, Section 5.7), so it remains a prospective design element and not a demonstrated contribution; evidence-weighted fusion with analog retrieval (T1) is suggestive in permeability and null on its primary test; (ii) signed edit effects that transfer across laboratories, for example N-methyl addition (+0.252 log Papp within source, Section 5.5) (module M4); (iii) a reproducible audit protocol for binder benchmarks (no-target controls with single proxies, family-cluster intervals, an unverifiable-by-default rule, control-adjusted scoring), together with its finding for this data collection, analog-distance strata that separate nearest-neighbour lookup from transferable few-label gain, and family-cluster power reporting; of these only the combination is offered, and the novelty of each element against the wider literature is not established. Stereoisomer-resolved ranking is a protocol and metric, not a tokeniser; no retrieved study reports a within-scaffold stereoisomer ranking metric, and one study lists stereochemistry and ring-closure ablations as future work [LIT-VERIFIED: Amirahmadi et al., Limitations], a statement about our searches and not proof of absence.
**Unverified absence claims.** That no earlier work compares a hybrid cyclic-peptide model with larger peptide language models inside a retrospective feedback loop, and any comparison to MUV, DEKOIS or PDBbind-CleanSplit-type benchmark designs, are [UNVERIFIED]. The survey's own risk table rates the tokenisation claim high, the loop-versus-larger-model comparison medium, the benchmark low to medium and the stereo metric medium to low for protocol (`docs/20261029_CYCLIC_PEPTIDE_REPRESENTATION_SURVEY.md`).
