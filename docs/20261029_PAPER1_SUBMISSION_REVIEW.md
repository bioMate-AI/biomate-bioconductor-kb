## Scientific corrections before submission

1. **Correct the source-contribution denominator.** Results says Townsend accounts for “89% of the gain”. `paper1_cluster.py` defines `top_share` as the absolute source contribution divided by the sum of absolute contributions, not the net gain. Use “88.7% of the absolute source contributions”; apply the same precision to Figure 2's 88.7% and 53.7%. The 0.9-hit omission value is subtraction from observed contributions, not a rerun after removing Townsend from training/acquisition. Say so in Results as well as the legend.

2. **Remove the unsupported upper-bound interpretation.** The single-seed normalisation diagnostic does not establish a mathematical or empirical upper bound on prospective yield. Replace “It establishes why … upper bounds” with “These are transductive estimates; their prospective bias remains unquantified.” The abstract, introduction and limitations already acknowledge unrevealed labels, lack of a replicated causal-normalisation arm, and the distinction between held-out ranking and training-source acquisition well.

3. **Identify full versus candidate-subset ranking contrasts.** Figure 2c uses full-pool aware rho change 0.073, source-t interval 0.006–0.140; the Results/Table 2 aware value 0.0752, bootstrap interval 0.0373–0.1148, is `loop_sub - frozen_sub`. Table 2's shuffled contrasts are also subset contrasts, while random-feedback contrasts are full-pool. Label these protocols explicitly so the reader does not infer contradictory intervals for one experiment. The yield bootstrap interval 34.6–58.2 belongs to `l7_aggregate.json`; the separate cluster recomputation has 34.2–58.2. Preserve source provenance rather than silently mixing outputs.

4. **Correct Figure 3's null-design description in manuscript and generator.** The legend calls all five cells “clean synthetic benchmarks”, but VI/VM2 are permutations on real benchmark structure. Use “planted-valid null benchmarks (permuted real benchmark structures and synthetic family structures)”. Retain the distinction between these matched comparisons and the earlier independent positive-control experiment.

5. **Limit shortcut detection to tested cells.** Supplementary Note D currently implies all four shortcut types were tested at both AUROC 0.65 and 0.70 in every cell. `gcal_eval.json` contains censoring only at 0.65 in the 25-family S design; other tested types occur at both levels in the 20-, 25- and 47-family designs. Say “all tested cells” and list this exception. The observed 100% detection and approximate lower bound 0.94 are supported for those cells. Zero VALID events is an observed rate, not proof of a population probability of zero; the stored upper 95% bound is approximately 0.060 per 60-record cell.

6. **State the exact calibrated acceptance rule.** `gcal_eval.py` accepts false alarms when point rate ≤0.10 AND upper 95% bound ≤0.15. The 20-family interval extends to 0.114, so passing this rule does not demonstrate that the population rate is below 10%. Supplementary Note D should give the rule when saying all criteria passed. Explain that real-benchmark permutation tests use a separate raw maximum statistic, 400/200 permutations and add-one correction; the reported rounded p=0.0025/0.005 and “smallest attainable” wording are supported.

7. **Make historical versus calibrated interval provenance explicit.** The original analysis-I primary intervals in Results differ from the intervals retained in `gcal_eval.json` (for example fingerprint 0.533–0.636 versus 0.5215–0.6478). The text already says “original analysis”, which helps; cite the original result file in the numerical manifest and do not imply these are recalibrated confidence intervals. Calibration has 25 evaluable families for some controls versus 26 for fingerprint; give these denominators with control results.

## Submission blockers and required owner input

- **Author and declaration metadata are absent:** author list/order, affiliations, corresponding author/email, individual contributions, acknowledgements/funding, and competing-interest declaration. These must come from the authors; do not invent a no-conflict/no-funding statement. Nature explicitly requires contribution and competing-interest statements and specifies these manuscript elements in its [initial-submission guidance](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/initial-submission) and [formatting guide](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/formatting-guide).
- **Reviewer access and reproducibility package are unresolved.** Current data/code availability statements identify no accessible repository, release, reviewer link or reconstruction package. A public DOI is not necessarily mandatory at initial submission, but editors/reviewers need a concrete access route. Include release/version identifiers, exact benchmark lists/hashes, source-data tables, code/environment instructions and frozen registration records. Verify CycPeptMPDB redistribution terms before packaging its measurements; reconstruction/identifier lists can avoid unsupported redistribution.
- **Reporting and submission files:** prepare the applicable Nature Reporting Summary, complete manuscript with readable figures, Supplementary Information/source data, and a cover letter. Authors must confirm the destination journal, related submissions, authorship agreement and declaration details. Nature requests a Reporting Summary for life-sciences research and prefers an integrated Word/PDF at initial submission ([guidance](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/initial-submission)). The final image/layout check is still pending, so this review does not certify figure readiness.
- **Reference verification remains incomplete.** This review did not independently validate all eleven references. Verify author/title/DOI/arXiv correspondence and publication status, especially recent references 2, 3, 6, 10 and 11; add primary citations for BindingDB, ChEMBL and the actual named pretrained models/software as appropriate. Code-relative registration labels alone are insufficient evidence of prospective registration: provide accessible dated records and disclose amendments.

## Findings that should remain intact

The 47.3-hit source interval −53.011–147.611 (14 clusters), blind interval −7.306–33.706 (16), yields 248.6/201.3/62.5, and exact-potency GP values 0.628 overall/0.458 hard are supported by the inspected records. The six hard-stratum screening intervals include zero; 86 test targets and 21 families agree with the stated evaluation. The calibrated real-I flags are censoring, Ki/Kd and oxygen fraction; no primary control individually exceeds q. The gate's original false-positive failure is disclosed, not concealed. Seed intervals, source intervals and the registered central spread are correctly distinguished. Non-detection is generally not treated as equivalence, and the manuscript avoids claiming a new model, prospective discovery or a demonstrated language-model-loop advantage.

## Editorial improvements, not scientific blockers

Shorten rule-like prose in Methods (“Margins should…”, “Controls must…”), replacing it with what this study actually did. Define the hard-stratum similarity cutoff and every acquisition/model hyperparameter in Methods or an indexed supplement. Replace “matched” with “did not detectably outperform” for the scaffold-shift rule comparison unless an equivalence criterion was met. The abstract would be clearer with “at the tested AUROCs of 0.65 and 0.70” rather than “0.65 or above”. Supplementary Table 3's “so negative” should instead say “excludes a gain of +0.05 under this analysis”; avoid reverting to categorical null claims in supplements.

The central methodological story is coherent. Its fit to Nature's broad-interest editorial threshold remains uncertain; no review can promise acceptance, and stylistic polish does not resolve novelty or journal-fit questions.
