## Abstract (v1; results limited to what has run)
**Background.** Closed design–test–learn loops are widely proposed for cyclic-peptide discovery, but claims of benefit are rarely tested against controls that separate the effect of feedback from the effect of additional data, and cyclic-peptide benchmarks are frequently confounded by near-duplicate series, laboratory concentration and decoy shortcuts.
**Objective.** Two aims, one paper. Aim B (delivered in this draft): build and audit a retrospective evaluation instrument for cyclic-peptide feedback loops (family-disjoint, hash-frozen, validity-gated, laboratory-aware) and measure baseline loop behaviour with it. Aim A (pre-registered, **not yet run**): test whether a loop-native in-house hybrid cyclic-peptide language model (IH-LM) matches or exceeds larger pretrained peptide and chemistry language models fine-tuned on the same revealed labels. This draft reports no result for Aim A.
**Methods.** Retrospective replay in which labels are revealed only for selected compounds; five pre-registered controls (data-matched one-shot, random feedback, shuffled labels, frozen model, knowledge off); a family-disjoint, hash-frozen benchmark with no-target and typicality validity gates; leave-laboratories-out evaluation; per-source analysis; cost accounting in parameters and GPU-hours.
**Results (generalisation regimes first; interval types in brackets, Section 4.9).** (i) With a gradient-boosted learner on within-source permeability labels (30 seeds), the pre-registered claim that the loop beats random selection in both primary regimes was **not met by the letter of the rule**, which was written against the 95 % *spread* of the per-seed paired differences: under a scaffold-shifted start the difference was +22.2 hits per 500 selections (spread −1.6 to 44.1; post hoc bootstrap CI of the mean 17.7 to 26.5), and a label-free physicochemical rule scored equal to the loop (−0.5; spread −20.0 to 17.2; post hoc bootstrap CI of the mean −4.2 to 3.2) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/results/l5_aggregate.json]. In-distribution, which is series interpolation and not generalisation, the loop gained +186.3 (spread 158.5 to 209.0; post hoc bootstrap CI of the mean 181.1 to 191.5). (ii) Under leave-laboratories-out, the loop's yield exceeded random feedback by +165.0 (bootstrap CI of the mean over 10 seeds 156.0 to 175.7), but its held-out ranking gain was no better than the same labels given at once (+0.003; bootstrap CI of the mean −0.034 to 0.037) and lower than random feedback on a seed-based E-AUROC contrast (−0.072; −0.114 to −0.013); with the held-out laboratories resampled (11 sources), the within-source ranking contrasts of the loop against shuffled-label feedback (+0.071; cluster-bootstrap CI −0.079 to 0.181), frozen (+0.011) and random feedback (−0.032) all include 0, whereas in regimes A and B the loop beats shuffled and frozen feedback under both interval types: the benefit measured is selection yield, not demonstrated model improvement on new laboratories [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/results/l7_aggregate.json]. **All seed-based intervals describe variability over initial sets on one fixed library of 6,960 peptides and say nothing about new laboratories.** (iii) The in-distribution loop concentrates on one laboratory's series (346 of 500 picks from 2020_Townsend, against 206 for random selection), and no knowledge-prior variant met the pre-registered criterion in the primary analysis [VERIFIED: l5_aggregate.json]. (iv) **The validity-gate audit is itself a result:** applied retrospectively, no benchmark in the study passes it (the primary analysis fails a fingerprint no-target control, 0.584; family-cluster bootstrap CI 0.533 to 0.636), a stricter matched design is not achievable for this data collection, and a plain nearest-neighbour baseline to a handful of revealed compounds reaches AUROC 0.753 (5 labels) to 0.869 (25 labels) against 0.539 zero-shot [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/multitarget/integrin_chembl/retro_recovery/results/validity_gate_I.json, h4b_baselines.json]. The few-label gain is concentrated where a revealed analog is close (k = 5, fit-on-revealed minus zero-shot: close +0.351, family-cluster CI 0.259 to 0.448; remote +0.064, −0.002 to 0.135), and by the registered rule no few-label gain is demonstrated to be transferable [VERIFIED:retro_recovery/results/h4b_strata.json]. (v) Cheap-tier general-purpose language models prompted in context were far behind a gradient-boosted model on the same candidate subsets (exploratory; 4 seeds) [VERIFIED: l7_aggregate.json]. (vi) Techniques meant to go beyond analog lookup gave null or failed primary tests (T1 and its follow-up T1b, with the T1b permeability shuffled-label control failing as registered): evidence-weighted fusion of a prior with analog lookup (permeability: null on the registered primary variant; binding: fails non-inferiority) and laboratory-offset calibration from shared anchors (not supported on public data; coverage-limited, with the estimator working under full planted coverage) [REPORTED:docs/20261030_TECHNIQUE_T1_FUSION.md; VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/c2_audit/results_B_aggregate.json]. (viii) Across the retrospective loops on measured data (C2 binding, L5 permeability, a small permeability replay), model-guided selection beats random and, under distribution shift, most of the margin is reproduced by simple analog or physicochemical lookup; in C2 with a family-disjoint background learn − nearest-neighbour is +0.002 (95 % family-cluster bootstrap CI −0.018 to 0.047) and the original logged C2 numbers are withdrawn (not reproduced within tolerance) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/c2_audit/results_A_aggregate.json]. (vii) Exploratory leads, not established: K_phys shows a small held-out-AUROC advantage over a shuffled channel in the scaffold-shifted regime B in 2 of 3 GBM backends (+0.015 and +0.033) and in none of regimes A, L or the physicochemical backend; yield advantages over shuffled knowledge are largely the cost of shuffled noise (the shuffled channel lowers yield in 6 of 9 configurations); with laboratories resampled K_phys is never better than shuffled knowledge on held-out ranking; 15 of 54 dependent contrasts exclude 0 at 99 % with no multiplicity correction, and confirmatory seeds 10–29 are the test.
**Not available at v1.** No IH-LM or larger pretrained model has been run through the loop; the central hypothesis is untested here (Section 5.8).
**Conclusions allowed by the evidence.** A simple learner's loop selects better than random from a finite library when the pool resembles the start, the advantage under scaffold shift is not established, simple physicochemical priors match it there, and the loop's measured benefit is yield, not better held-out prediction. These results define the baselines and the evaluation controls that Aim A must meet.
**Limitations stated up front.** Retrospective evaluation can only reveal labels of compounds that already have measurements, so it does not test whether a model generates better novel molecules; the task is permeability ranking on one public database in which two laboratories hold most hits; inference is conditional on that library; Aim A awaits GPU runs.

### 4.2 Evaluation design for binding (family-disjoint, frozen, gated)
- **Held-out unit:** target family (Pfam-Jaccard families; not sequence-identity families), in a five-fold family-held-out rotation with size-balanced packing [REPORTED:docs/20261028_AVB6_RETROSPECTIVE_RECOVERY_PREREG.md §14 (A6), `retro_recovery/data/folds_v1.csv`; hash to be re-verified before submission].
- **Frozen lists:** compounds, pair labels, targets and item tables were frozen with SHA-256 hashes before any scorer ran (for example `items_I.csv.gz`: 5da885424abe6cdeddff3b422524ce93bc617bb9ec7116965241feaedaae601e) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/multitarget/integrin_chembl/retro_recovery/data/FROZEN_HASHES.json].
- **Analyses:** I (primary) uses same-target measured inactives as decoys (46 targets in 26 families) [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §3 and §1]. M uses property-matched decoys that are binders of other targets; it was shown to be decided by a nearest-neighbour similarity shortcut (strongest baseline AUROC 0.914) and is diagnostic only [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §2, §4]. M2 matches decoys on similarity to the reference pool and brought that baseline to 0.530 [VERIFIED: same §2]; it was then found invalid by the typicality control below, and the attempted replacement M3 (typicality plus similarity plus proxy matching) is not achievable (Section 5.4).
- **Validity gates (applied retrospectively; no analysis passes).** A no-target control and a typicality control (atom-LM likelihood, simple proxies, fingerprint logistic regression) must be compatible with chance before any arm is judged. The gate was applied after the failure of the typicality control on M2 was known (disclosed in the A7 amendment), and **no analysis (I, M, M2, M3, H4b) passes it**: M2's atom language model, which has no target information, scored AUROC 0.665 (95 % family-cluster bootstrap CI 0.557–0.774) and exceeded the strongest baseline [VERIFIED:docs/20261028_V5_PREREG_AND_LOOP_INTERFACE.md §6 row A2; validity_gate_M2.json]; the primary analysis I fails the fingerprint control (0.584; 95 % family-cluster bootstrap CI 0.533–0.636) [VERIFIED:validity_gate_I.json]. We treat this failure as a finding about cyclic-peptide binder benchmarks (Section 6) and report control-adjusted scores, not as an obstacle removed.
- **Contamination:** compound-level flags mark positives present in pretraining corpora; results are reported for all items and for the contamination-controlled subset [REPORTED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §6].
- **Stereoisomer and duplicate handling:** compounds are grouped by flat structure; stereoisomers are never split across the train and target sides in a fold-level claim, and the within-group ranking protocol of Section 4.3 is used where stereo resolution is measured.
- **Decision rule:** an arm is SUPPORTED only if the lower 95 % bound of its difference from the strongest baseline exceeds zero for both AUROC and top-5 % enrichment, with family-clustered bootstrap intervals [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §1].

### 4.9 Statistics
**Interval types (every interval in this paper carries one of these labels).** *Spread*: the central 95 % (or 99 %) range of the per-seed paired differences (2.5–97.5 or 0.5–99.5 percentiles); it describes run-to-run variability and is **not a confidence interval**. The pre-registered L5 rules C1 (loop beats random in both primary regimes) and C2 (some knowledge variant helps in both regimes) were written against it and were judged by it. *Boot-mean*: percentile bootstrap confidence interval of the mean paired difference over seeds; the primary interval for L7 contrasts (amendment L7k) and, for L5, a **post hoc** addition that is labelled exploratory. *Cluster-boot*: bootstrap resampling of the dependency unit: target families for the benchmark, laboratories for the leave-one-laboratory-out analyses, flat-structure groups for stratified cross-validation. *Meta*: random-effects meta-analytic interval over sources. *SD*: standard deviation over seeds. **Seed-based intervals (spread and boot-mean) capture variability from initial sets and bootstrap resamples on one fixed library; they do not capture sampling of laboratories or chemical series, and no statement about new laboratories rests on them.** Generalisation is assessed only by regime L (held-out laboratories) and by the family-held-out benchmark with cluster intervals. At least 8 paired sequences are required for any claim; 99 % intervals are used where several knowledge variants are compared; non-inferiority and superiority rules are those of Section 3 and `PREREG_LOOP.md` L7b/L7j [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7k, aggregate_prior.py]. No threshold, feature set, model or contrast is chosen after seeing a result; changes are dated amendments.

## 6. Discussion
**What the evidence supports.** (i) With a simple learner, model-guided selection from a finite library beats random selection in-distribution (series interpolation) by a wide margin that survives removing near-duplicates, and part of that margin is the location of one laboratory's series; under scaffold shift the margin is not established and a label-free physicochemical rule matches it (5.1). (ii) Sequential selection does not improve held-out prediction beyond the same labels given at once, and random feedback improves it more on the seed-based E-AUROC contrast (not on the source-cluster contrast, which includes 0); the loop's benefit measured here is yield, not demonstrated model improvement on new laboratories (5.2). (iii) K_phys has a heterogeneous but real zero-fit association with permeability in unseen laboratories offline (5.3). In the loop, a small held-out-AUROC advantage over a shuffled channel appears in the scaffold-shifted regime for 2 of 3 GBM backends and not in regimes A or L or the physicochemical backend; part of the regime-B gain is any-channel, not rule content; the yield advantage over shuffled knowledge is largely the cost of shuffled noise; and with laboratories resampled K_phys is never better than shuffled knowledge on held-out ranking. These are exploratory leads from 54 dependent contrasts without multiplicity correction (5.2). (vi) **Cross-loop pattern.** The three retrospective loops on measured data agree: model-guided selection beats random, and under shift lookup reproduces most of the margin (5.6); loop-specific learning beyond lookup is not demonstrated. (iii-b) Neither technique that was meant to move the loop beyond analog lookup worked as registered: evidence-weighted fusion is suggestive in permeability but null on its primary test and fails non-inferiority in binding, and lab-offset anchor calibration is not supported on public data because anchors are too sparse (5.7). ## 7. Limitations
1. **Retrospective only.** The replay reveals labels of compounds that already have measurements. It supports statements about learning from graded feedback and ranking, not about generating better novel molecules, which requires a prospective experimental round.
2. **Narrow task and data.** Permeability ranking on one public database with series clustering and laboratory concentration (Section 4.1), and no stapled, bicyclic or disulfide peptides. Conclusions do not transfer to binding or other properties without further evidence.
3. **Comparison-set coverage.** The larger models are those pretrained peptide and chemistry models that are available and can be fine-tuned [candidate list in docs/20261030_PEPTIDE_CHEM_LM_MODEL_SURVEY.md, not re-verified; none run at v1]; a non-inferiority result applies to the models tested and not to all larger models. General-purpose API language models are outside the central comparison (Section 4.5).
4. **Contamination.** Larger pretrained models and our own pretraining corpora may overlap CycPeptMPDB, its source literature or benchmark positives; we use within-source labels, per-model overlap checks and contamination-controlled subsets, which reduce but do not remove the concern.
5. **Benchmark limits.** Families are Pfam-Jaccard families and not sequence-identity families; analysis I has 26 families and low power; the matched-decoy analyses select an isolated-chemotype subset; the knowledge-base coverage is below 7 % of targets.
6. **Model coverage.** v4's converter maps few benchmark compounds (2.5 % to 5.4 % of items), so v4 results on the benchmark are inconclusive by coverage; v5 was not trained (superseded by the IH-LM plan).
7. **Statistical power.** The number of paired sequences, targets and families is limited; contrasts with fewer than 8 paired sequences are exploratory.
8a. **Scaffold-shift result.** The pre-registered loop-beats-random claim was not met under the scaffold-shifted regime for the primary hit definition (+22.2, −1.6 to 44.1) and the two hit definitions disagree; a physicochemical rule matches the loop there. Earlier version-1 numbers showing a gain in both regimes were superseded (Appendix C).
8b. **Laboratory concentration and leave-laboratories-out skill.** 52.8 % of within-source hits come from one laboratory; skill roughly halves when the test laboratory is unseen (Spearman 0.67 grouped by structure versus 0.30 leave-laboratories-out); several strata are single-laboratory.
8c. **No benchmark passes the validity gate.** The gate was applied retrospectively (after the M2 failure was known); the primary analysis fails the fingerprint control; M3 is not achievable for this data collection (one collection, BindingDB plus ChEMBL; our own constructions only; Pfam-Jaccard families); the gate itself has low power with 14 to 26 families, and the zero-shot analyses can exclude only effects above about 0.11 AUROC on I and 0.19 on M2 (Section 5.4).
8e. **Inference is conditional on one library, and intervals are of different types.** Seeds share the same 6,960 compounds and resample only initial sets and bootstrap draws; seed-based intervals (spread and boot-mean) describe algorithmic variability and not uncertainty about other libraries, laboratories or series, and no statement about new laboratories rests on them (regime-L laboratories are redrawn per seed from a pool of 18; yield contrasts are seed-based and only E-metric contrasts have a source-cluster interval). Two laboratories hold about two thirds of the peptides and most hits. The pre-registered L5 judgements (C1, C2) used the spread; mean-based intervals are post hoc; the knowledge contrasts on seeds 0–9 are development data, are uncorrected for multiplicity, and are not claimed. Cluster-level power is planned (Section 4.10).
8d. **The central comparison is not run.** IH-LM and the larger pretrained models have not been run through the loop, and P1 pretraining has not started; the hypotheses H1–H5 for language models are untested (Section 5.8). The P0 atom-level model is a proof of pipeline, not a foundation model.
8. **Evidence base is retrospective and public.** The replays cited as closed-loop evidence (Section 4.3.1) use real measured labels from the literature, with the limits listed there (finite library, analog hit-expansion, laboratory concentration, three preliminary seeds for the within-publication replay). The `c2_retro_loop` numbers await reproduction. The simulated integration tests and the earlier verifier-ladder system are not evidence of improvement.
9. **No measured data from our own campaigns.** The wet-lab ingestion pipeline (`wetlab_kd_to_pairs.py`, `campaign_assay_to_pairs.py`) and the closed-loop fixtures were exercised only on simulated assay values; the study contains no measured data from our own experimental campaigns. The ingestion code itself requires each manifest to declare a measured or simulated origin and rejects a mismatch (read in `campaign_assay_to_pairs.py`), but whether any measured campaign file has ever passed through it is not established by this study [REPORTED:coordinator message 2026-10-29; consistent with the project note that the closed loop is synthetic-only]. All feedback in the replay is revealed from public literature measurements.
10. **Self-correction history.** Several evaluation flaws were found in our own earlier designs (Section 4.8); other undetected flaws may remain, and all code and frozen lists are provided for independent checking.


## 8. Prior art and novelty statement
**Not claimed as novel.** The backbone, atom-level tokenisation with chirality and topology, retrieval, and evidence-weighted shrinkage. Chirality is represented implicitly in existing SMILES- and HELM-based models and topology-aware peptide encoders exist (PeptideCLM, PeptideCLM-2, HELM-BERT [LIT-SUMMARY]); retrieval-augmented molecular property prediction exists (MolRAG, ACL 2025; MolE-RAG, arXiv 2606.05693 [LIT-SNIPPET; details unverified]); LLM-guided nearest-neighbour methods exist (Gupta, Hartford and Liu, *Findings of EMNLP 2025*, arXiv 2509.21403 [LIT-SNIPPET]; reported there to find that language-model agents ignore feedback under permuted labels and that classical methods beat them, a summary we have not verified); matched-molecular-pair and activity-cliff modelling is established (ACNet, arXiv 2302.07541 [LIT-SNIPPET]); Bayesian priors and shrinkage are general. A 13-method CycPeptMPDB benchmark with scaffold splits exists (Liu, Li, Verma and Lee, *J Cheminformatics* 2025 [LIT-UNVERIFIED: the PubMed page (40877984) was blocked in the QA audit; the content below is the chair's summary]); per that summary it does not address laboratory or source effects or stereochemistry. PepBenchmark (Zhang, Wang, Zhou et al., arXiv 2604.10531; the venue "ICLR 2026" is taken from workstream 3's reading and was not verified from the abstract by the QA audit), read in full text by workstream 3 [REPORTED:docs/20261030_PRIOR_ART_PEPBENCHMARK_PROTAC_CEILING.md; the leaderboard appendix was skimmed, not audited], is the closest peptide benchmark: it covers cyclic content only through CycPeptMPDB permeability and one 277-point non-canonical peptide–protein affinity set; it uses protein cold-start splits, k-mer and near-duplicate leakage controls, and negative sampling matched on shallow features (length, charge, hydrophobicity, 1-mer and 2-mer composition) that is validated by marginal divergences and not by a trained no-target classifier; it has no source-offset modelling, no few-label analysis and no analog-distance analysis. Its negative sampling was not tested by us. The inter-laboratory ceiling paper (Klamt, Nejdl and Tang, arXiv 2605.11764, preprint) is on PROTACs, with no cyclic content: the 0.124 AUROC drop is random cross-validation 0.802 to cross-laboratory 0.678 on same-compound replicate measurements (target-clustered bootstrap 95 % CI 0.051 to 0.197; the 0.802 and 0.678 values and this interval, and the statement that it has no laboratory-held-out splits, are from workstream 3's reading and are not verifiable from the abstract), and its few-label retraining at k = 5 lifts leave-one-target-out AUROC from 0.668 to 0.705; it quantifies the laboratory ceiling without anchor compounds, offsets or laboratory-held-out splits [REPORTED: same document, read from the PDF by workstream 3]. **We do not claim a first cyclic-peptide benchmark.**
**Candidate contributions, to be tested and not claimed.** (i) Laboratory or assay offsets estimated from shared anchor compounds (module M2): the planted-offset test shows the estimator works when every source has anchors, but the retrospective test on CycPeptMPDB did not support it (anchor coverage too sparse; kill criterion met, Section 5.7), so it remains a prospective design element and not a demonstrated contribution; evidence-weighted fusion with analog retrieval (T1) is suggestive in permeability and null on its primary test; (ii) signed edit effects that transfer across laboratories, for example N-methyl addition (+0.252 log Papp within source, Section 5.5) (module M4); (iii) a reproducible audit protocol for binder benchmarks (no-target controls with single proxies, family-cluster intervals, an unverifiable-by-default rule, control-adjusted scoring), together with its finding for this data collection, analog-distance strata that separate nearest-neighbour lookup from transferable few-label gain, and family-cluster power reporting; of these only the combination is offered, and the novelty of each element against the wider literature is not established. Stereoisomer-resolved ranking is a protocol and metric, not a tokeniser; no retrieved study reports a within-scaffold stereoisomer ranking metric, and one study lists stereochemistry and ring-closure ablations as future work [LIT-VERIFIED: Amirahmadi et al., Limitations], a statement about our searches and not proof of absence.
**Unverified absence claims.** That no earlier work compares a hybrid cyclic-peptide model with larger peptide language models inside a retrospective feedback loop, and any comparison to MUV, DEKOIS or PDBbind-CleanSplit-type benchmark designs, are [UNVERIFIED]. def cluster_contrast(tab_a, tab_b, min_seeds=3, B=B_BOOT, lo=2.5, hi=97.5):
```python
    """Paired contrast arm A minus arm B on the within-source rho. POINT ESTIMATE = mean over sources of the per-source mean (over the seeds in which that source was held out/evaluated).
    ci_cluster: sources resampled with replacement (the interval to cite). ci_seed_only: seeds resampled with replacement, sources fixed, with the SAME statistic recomputed on each resample
    (per-source mean over the resampled seeds in which the source appears, then mean over sources) -- an interval for the reported mean even when sources are present in only some seeds."""
    d = {}
    for s in sorted(set(tab_a) & set(tab_b)):                     # sorted: set iteration order of strings varies between processes (hash randomisation) and would change the resampling
        common = sorted(set(tab_a[s]) & set(tab_b[s]))
        if len(common) >= min_seeds: d[s] = {k: tab_a[s][k] - tab_b[s][k] for k in common}
    if len(d) < 2: return dict(n_sources=len(d), mean=None, ci_cluster=None, ci_seed_only=None, interval_type="insufficient clusters")
    per_src = np.asarray([np.mean(list(v.values())) for v in d.values()], float); srcs_sorted = sorted(d)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(_seed(per_src, lo, hi)); bs = [float(rng.choice(per_src, len(per_src)).mean()) for _ in range(B)]
    seeds_all = sorted({k for v in d.values() for k in v}); rng2 = np.random.default_rng(_seed(per_src, lo, hi) + 1); bs2 = []
    for _ in range(B):
        draw = rng2.choice(len(seeds_all), len(seeds_all)); cnt = np.bincount(draw, minlength=len(seeds_all)); w = dict(zip(seeds_all, cnt)); vals = []
        for s in srcs_sorted:
            den = sum(w[k] for k in d[s]); 
            if den > 0: vals.append(sum(w[k] * x for k, x in d[s].items()) / den)
        bs2.append(float(np.mean(vals)))
    r4 = lambda x: round(float(x), 4)
    return dict(n_sources=len(d), n_seeds=len(seeds_all), mean=r4(per_src.mean()), ci_cluster=[r4(np.percentile(bs, lo)), r4(np.percentile(bs, hi))], ci_seed_only=[r4(np.percentile(bs2, lo)), r4(np.percentile(bs2, hi))],
                interval_type="bootstrap CI of the mean over SOURCES (cluster); ci_seed_only = bootstrap over seeds, sources fixed, same statistic recomputed", ci_level_pct=round(hi - lo, 1))
```

```python
    sp, conc, tot = [], 0, 0
    for f, items in groups.items():
        s = [score_of.get(i["smiles"]) for i in items]
        if any(x is None for x in s): continue
        y = [i["label"] for i in items]
        if np.std(y) == 0: continue          # rule: groups with no label variance are excluded (Spearman undefined)
        sp.append(0.0 if len(set(s)) == 1 else float(spearmanr(s, y)[0]))
        for a in range(len(items)):
            for b in range(a + 1, len(items)):
                if y[a] != y[b] and s[a] != s[b]: tot += 1; conc += ((s[a] > s[b]) == (y[a] > y[b]))
    return sp, conc, tot
```

```python
def reliability_perm(sim_rr, y_rev, p=4, lam=0.05, min_mass=0.01):
    """Leave-one-out reliability of the analog estimate from the revealed set only. sim_rr (k,k) Tanimoto among revealed, y_rev raw labels.
    For each revealed j with analog mass sum_i s_ji^p > min_mass: A^{-j}_j from the other revealed items; r_hat = clip(Spearman(A^{-j}, y), 0, 1);
    r = (m r_hat + M0 R0) / (m + M0), m = number of usable revealed items (0 usable -> r = R0)."""
    y = np.asarray(y_rev, float); k = len(y)
    if k < 3: return R0
    W = np.asarray(sim_rr, float) ** p; np.fill_diagonal(W, 0.0); mass = W.sum(1); ok = mass > min_mass; m = int(ok.sum())
    if m < 3: return (m * 0.0 + M0 * R0) / (m + M0)
    Aloo = np.array([(W[j] @ (y - (y.sum() - y[j]) / (k - 1))) / (mass[j] + lam) if ok[j] else np.nan for j in range(k)])
```
