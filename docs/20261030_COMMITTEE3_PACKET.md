# PACKET 1. MANUSCRIPT (CURRENT main draft; selected sections verbatim)

## Abstract
**Background.** Closed design–test–learn loops are widely proposed for cyclic-peptide discovery, but claims of benefit are rarely tested against controls that separate the effect of feedback from the effect of additional data, and cyclic-peptide benchmarks are frequently confounded by near-duplicate series, laboratory concentration and decoy shortcuts.
**Objective.** Two aims, one paper. Aim B (delivered in this draft): build and audit a retrospective evaluation instrument for cyclic-peptide feedback loops (Pfam-family-disjoint, hash-frozen, validity-gated, laboratory-aware) and measure baseline loop behaviour with it. Aim A (pre-registered, **not yet run**): test whether a loop-native in-house hybrid cyclic-peptide language model (IH-LM) matches or exceeds external pretrained peptide and chemistry language models fine-tuned on the same revealed labels. This draft reports no result for Aim A. Contributions by type (Section 1.1): TECHNIQUE, delivered and audited: validity gates and evaluation protocols; TECHNIQUE, tested only in planted worlds and null on public data: anchor-compound offset calibration; TECHNIQUE, planned: provenance-aware acquisition; SYSTEM, design built and tested on planted signals only, with no performance claim: the loop-native scaffold, whose ablation ladder is planned.
**Methods.** Retrospective replay in which labels are revealed only for selected compounds; five pre-registered controls (data-matched one-shot, random feedback, shuffled labels, frozen model, knowledge off); a Pfam-family-disjoint, hash-frozen benchmark with no-target and typicality validity gates; leave-laboratories-out evaluation; per-source analysis; cost accounting in parameters and GPU-hours.
**Results (generalisation regimes first; interval types in brackets, Section 4.9).** (i) With a gradient-boosted learner on within-source permeability labels (30 seeds), the loop beat random selection in-distribution (series interpolation, not generalisation: +186.3 hits per 500 selections; 95 % spread 158.5 to 209.0, post hoc bootstrap CI of the mean 181.1 to 191.5), but 346 of its 500 picks came from one laboratory's series (2020_Townsend; random selection 206). This gain is likely overstated: labels were standardised within source and hits thresholded using every label of the source (a transductive convention), and a single-seed indicative check gave 251 hits with this convention against 151 with statistics estimated only from labelled peptides, 64 with raw pooled labels and 47 for random selection; a causal-normalisation arm is being run on 10 or more seeds [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/results/l5_aggregate.json]. Under a scaffold-shifted start the mean advantage was +22.2 (post hoc bootstrap CI of the mean 17.7 to 26.5, seeds on one fixed library); the pre-registered criterion, written against the 95 % spread of the per-seed differences (−1.6 to 44.1), was not met, and a physicochemical rule that was added after the first loop result was not distinguishable from the loop (−0.5; spread −20.0 to 17.2; boot-mean −4.2 to 3.2). (ii) Under leave-laboratories-out the loop's yield exceeded random feedback by +186.1 for the chirality-aware GBM (bootstrap CI of the mean over 10 seeds 179.5 to 192.9; chirality-blind GBM +165.0, 156.0 to 175.7), but no gain in held-out ranking from sequential selection over the same labels was detected (aware loop − one-shot −0.006, −0.033 to 0.025; blind +0.003, −0.034 to 0.037; the aware half-width of about 0.029 corresponds to a minimal detectable difference of about 0.04 at 80 % power, my approximation). With the held-out sources as the unit (95 % cluster-bootstrap CIs; 6, 9 and 11 sources in regimes A, B and L) feedback effects on within-source ranking are learner- and regime-dependent: in regimes A and B loop − shuffled-label feedback excludes 0 for every learner (chirality-aware +0.293, 0.203 to 0.369, and +0.283, 0.199 to 0.387); in regime L loop − frozen excludes 0 for the chirality-aware (+0.075, 0.037 to 0.115) and physicochemical (+0.077, 0.028 to 0.132) learners but not the chirality-blind one (+0.011, −0.071 to 0.080), and loop − shuffled-label excludes 0 only for the physicochemical learner (+0.155, 0.071 to 0.241; chirality-aware +0.034, −0.063 to 0.120, blind +0.071, −0.079 to 0.181; minimal detectable difference about 0.13 to 0.19) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/results/l7_aggregate.json]. The measured benefit is therefore mainly selection yield, with a small, learner-dependent feedback effect on held-out ranking in regime L, and not a consistent improvement of the model on new laboratories. All seed-based intervals describe variability over initial sets on one fixed library and say nothing about new laboratories. (iii) No knowledge-prior variant met the pre-registered criterion in the primary analysis [VERIFIED: l5_aggregate.json]. (iv) **The validity-gate audit is itself a result:** applied retrospectively, no benchmark in the study passes it (the primary analysis I fails a fingerprint no-target control, 0.584; 95 % family-cluster CI 0.533 to 0.636; the benchmark is Pfam-family-disjoint but not compound-disjoint, and the verdict holds under flat-structure-disjoint training (0.575, 0.524 to 0.628), Tanimoto purging (0.579), protein-level folds (0.596) and the strict compound-disjoint list (0.621, 0.555 to 0.685); equivalence status NOT_VALID_BIASED under every variant), a stricter matched design is not achievable for this data collection, and on analysis I the zero-shot pretrained model and that no-target control are not distinguishable (0.539, 0.478 to 0.605 against 0.584, 0.533 to 0.636; the intervals overlap broadly and no paired family-cluster test was computed). The few-label analysis uses analysis I and is therefore reported only as a relative comparison: with 5 to 25 revealed labels per target a nearest-neighbour baseline is as good as fitted models (AUROC 0.753 to 0.869 against 0.539 zero-shot), the gain is concentrated where a revealed analog is close (k = 5, fit-on-revealed minus zero-shot: close +0.351, 0.259 to 0.448; remote +0.064, −0.002 to 0.135), and because the very-remote bin is almost empty (at most 4 % of items) a transferable gain could hardly be demonstrated by construction [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/multitarget/integrin_chembl/retro_recovery/results/validity_gate_I.json, h4b_baselines.json, h4b_strata.json]. The labels of analysis I are confounded by censoring (63.6 % of its inactives are '>' censored values against 3.1 % of positives (any censoring relation, '>' or '<'; 2.5 % carry '>'), and a censoring indicator alone gives a per-target AUROC of 0.206, 0.134 to 0.280, i.e. 0.794 inverted, stronger than any compound-based control), and the Pfam-family-disjoint folds are not protein-, compound- or analogue-disjoint, so the αvβ6 and integrin results are not truly held out. (v) Across three retrospective loops on measured data (C2 binding, L5 permeability, a small permeability replay), model-guided selection beat random and, under distribution shift, most of the margin was reproduced by simple analog or physicochemical lookup; in C2 with a family-disjoint background learn − nearest-neighbour is +0.002 (95 % family-cluster bootstrap CI −0.018 to 0.047) and the original logged C2 numbers are withdrawn [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/c2_audit/results_A_aggregate.json]. (vi) Techniques meant to go beyond analog lookup gave null or failed primary tests: evidence-weighted fusion of a prior with analog lookup (T1 and T1b; the T1b permeability shuffled-label control failed as registered) and laboratory-offset calibration from shared anchors (not supported on public data; coverage-limited). (vii) Cheap-tier general-purpose language models prompted in context were far behind a gradient-boosted model on the same candidate subsets (exploratory; 4 seeds) [VERIFIED: l7_aggregate.json].
**Not available at v1.** No IH-LM or external pretrained model has been run through the loop; the central hypothesis is untested here (Section 5.8).
**Conclusions allowed by the evidence.** A simple learner's loop selects better than random from a finite library when the pool resembles the start, the advantage under scaffold shift is not established, a physicochemical rule added after the first loop result was not distinguishable from it there, and the loop's measured benefit is yield, not better held-out prediction. These results define the baselines and the evaluation controls that Aim A must meet.
**Limitations stated up front.** Retrospective evaluation can only reveal labels of compounds that already have measurements, so it does not test whether a model generates better novel molecules; the task is permeability ranking on one public database in which two laboratories hold most hits; inference is conditional on that library; Aim A awaits GPU runs.

### 4.2 Evaluation design for binding (Pfam-family-disjoint, frozen, gated)
- **Held-out unit (qualified by the independent data review):** the folds are Pfam-family-disjoint folds, **not protein-, compound- or analogue-disjoint** (see Appendix F): the same UniProt subunits appear in different families and folds (29 of 775 accessions occur in more than one family), 6 of 46 targets in analysis I and 9 of 20 in M2 (44 % of M2 items) have a same-protein target in another fold, and the αvβ6 complex and β6 alone are one antigen counted twice (77 labelled pairs each, 70 shared compounds); αvβ6 and integrin results are therefore not truly held out and blind-rule statements for 'other-family' arms are qualified (protein-level folds_v2 now exists and is frozen; see Appendix G). Target family (Pfam-Jaccard families; not sequence-identity families), in a five-fold family-held-out rotation with size-balanced packing [REPORTED:docs/20261028_AVB6_RETROSPECTIVE_RECOVERY_PREREG.md §14 (A6), `retro_recovery/data/folds_v1.csv`; hash to be re-verified before submission].
- **Frozen lists:** compounds, pair labels, targets and item tables were frozen with SHA-256 hashes before any scorer ran (for example `items_I.csv.gz`: 5da885424abe6cdeddff3b422524ce93bc617bb9ec7116965241feaedaae601e) [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/multitarget/integrin_chembl/retro_recovery/data/FROZEN_HASHES.json].
- **Analyses:** I (primary) uses same-target measured inactives as decoys (46 targets in 26 families) [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §3 and §1]. M uses property-matched decoys that are binders of other targets; it was shown to be decided by a nearest-neighbour similarity shortcut (strongest baseline AUROC 0.914) and is diagnostic only [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §2, §4]. M2 matches decoys on similarity to the reference pool and brought that baseline to 0.530 [VERIFIED: same §2]; it was then found invalid by the typicality control below, and the attempted replacement M3 (typicality plus similarity plus proxy matching) is not achievable (Section 5.4).
- **Validity gates (applied retrospectively; no analysis passes).** A no-target control and a typicality control (atom-LM likelihood, simple proxies, fingerprint logistic regression) must be compatible with chance before any arm is judged. The gate was applied after the failure of the typicality control on M2 was known (disclosed in the A7 amendment), and **no analysis (I, M, M2, M3, H4b) passes it**: M2's atom language model, which has no target information, scored AUROC 0.665 (95 % family-cluster bootstrap CI 0.557–0.774) and exceeded the strongest baseline [VERIFIED:docs/20261028_V5_PREREG_AND_LOOP_INTERFACE.md §6 row A2; validity_gate_M2.json]; the primary analysis I fails the fingerprint control (0.584; 95 % family-cluster bootstrap CI 0.533–0.636) [VERIFIED:validity_gate_I.json]. Stated plainly: analysis I fails the no-target gate. It is Pfam-family-disjoint but not compound-disjoint: of 9,432 rows (6,619 compounds), 1,731 compounds occur in more than one target and 364 in more than one family, and 1,093 rows (11.6 %) have the same compound in another fold. A compound-disjoint rerun of the controls gives C2 0.5658 (95 % family-cluster CI 0.5056 to 0.6252; registered 0.5591, 0.4994 to 0.6189, so the lower bound moves from a marginal pass to a marginal fail) and C3 0.5796 (0.5304 to 0.6302; registered 0.5837); overlap therefore does not cause the failure. Five within-target label permutations of the full gate give means C1 0.5075, C2 0.4887, C3 0.5052, so the 0.584 is signal in the labels and not an artefact of the pipeline (five permutations, point estimates only) [VERIFIED:docs/committee_raw/20261030b/ext_check_gate.json]. The central comparison (IH-LM against external pretrained models in the loop) has not been run. We treat this failure as a finding about cyclic-peptide binder benchmarks (Section 6) and report control-adjusted scores, not as an obstacle removed.
- **Contamination:** compound-level flags mark positives present in pretraining corpora; results are reported for all items and for the contamination-controlled subset [REPORTED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §6].
- **Stereoisomer and duplicate handling:** compounds are grouped by flat structure; stereoisomers are never split across the train and target sides in a fold-level claim, and the within-group ranking protocol of Section 4.3 is used where stereo resolution is measured.
- **Decision rule:** an arm is SUPPORTED only if the lower 95 % bound of its difference from the strongest baseline exceeds zero for both AUROC and top-5 % enrichment, with family-clustered bootstrap intervals [VERIFIED:docs/20261029_RETRO_RECOVERY_RESULTS_V2.md §1].

### 4.3 Retrospective loop replay
The replay reveals the measured label of a compound only after a model selects it. Each sequence starts from 100 labelled peptides; in each of 10 rounds a policy selects 50 pool peptides, their labels are revealed, and the model is updated [VERIFIED:research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/loop_retro/PREREG_LOOP.md L5]. Regimes: A, in-distribution random start (series interpolation, not generalisation; the headline regimes are B and L); B, scaffold-shifted start from the two largest sources with the pool excluding shared scaffolds (no leakage by three measures, audit A1); C, in-distribution start with the pool excluding peptides of the same stereo-free structure as an initial peptide; this removes identical structures only, not analogues (26.9 % of the pool still has a nearest initial peptide at Tanimoto ≥ 0.9), so C is an identical-structure sensitivity and not a near-duplicate-free regime (to be replaced by a Tanimoto ≥ 0.8 purge). Regime L (held-out laboratories; L7f) is an irregular design and not a clean leave-laboratories-out: held-out sources are a seeded random subset of the 18 eligible smaller laboratories (at least 20 peptides; the two dominant laboratories, 2020_Townsend and 2021_Kelly, 65 % of peptides, are never held out), drawn until about 25 % of peptides are held out, which gives 6 to 18 laboratories and 25.2 % to 34.3 % of peptides per seed, with all 18 held out in 3 of 10 seeds; any interpretation that depends on this irregularity is conditional on the registered L9 sensitivity run (cap of 9 held-out laboratories), which has not been run; the initial labels and pool come from training sources only [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7f]. Hit definitions: top 10 % of within-source standardised label (primary) and a second definition (within-group top 10 %) recorded for sensitivity [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L5 addendum]. Version record: L3 used chirality-blind features and pooled knowledge signals (version 1); L5 uses chirality-aware count Morgan fingerprints, the PAMPA column, source-standardised knowledge signals and tie-breaking by the task seed (version 2) [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L5].

### 4.9 Statistics
**Interval types (every interval in this paper carries one of these labels).** *Spread*: the central 95 % (or 99 %) range of the per-seed paired differences (2.5–97.5 or 0.5–99.5 percentiles); it describes run-to-run variability and is **not a confidence interval**. The pre-registered L5 rules C1 (loop beats random in both primary regimes) and C2 (some knowledge variant helps in both regimes) were written against it and were judged by it. *Boot-mean*: percentile bootstrap confidence interval of the mean paired difference over seeds; the primary interval for L7 contrasts (amendment L7k) and, for L5, a **post hoc** addition that is labelled exploratory. *Cluster-boot*: bootstrap resampling of the dependency unit: target families for the benchmark, laboratories for the leave-one-laboratory-out analyses, flat-structure groups for stratified cross-validation. *Meta*: random-effects meta-analytic interval over sources. *SD*: standard deviation over seeds. **Seed-based intervals (spread and boot-mean) capture variability from initial sets and bootstrap resamples on one fixed library; they do not capture sampling of laboratories or chemical series, and no statement about new laboratories rests on them.** Generalisation is assessed only by regime L (held-out laboratories) and by the family-held-out benchmark with cluster intervals. At least 8 paired sequences are required for any claim; 99 % intervals are used where several knowledge variants are compared; non-inferiority and superiority rules are those of Section 3 and `PREREG_LOOP.md` L7b/L7j [VERIFIED:PREREG_LOOP.md L7k, aggregate_prior.py]. Deviations from the registered plan are dated amendments and are labelled exploratory or post hoc where they were made after a result was seen (the retrospective validity gate, the label-free rules, the boot-mean intervals for L5, T1b). The cluster interval for the benchmark (`_family_ci`) is a family-level estimand: AUROC is averaged within each family, families are weighted equally, and targets with undefined AUROC are dropped.

## 6. Discussion
**What the evidence supports.** (i) With a simple learner, model-guided selection from a finite library beats random selection in-distribution (series interpolation) by a wide margin that survives removing near-duplicates, and part of that margin is the location of one laboratory's series; under scaffold shift the margin is not established and a label-free physicochemical rule matches it (5.1). (ii) No improvement of held-out prediction from sequential selection beyond the same labels given at once is detected (chirality-aware loop − one-shot −0.006, −0.033 to 0.025; blind +0.003, −0.034 to 0.037; differences below about 0.04 to 0.05 cannot be excluded); at the source-cluster level the feedback effect on held-out ranking is learner-dependent in regime L (loop − frozen excludes 0 for the aware and physicochemical learners, not the blind one; loop − shuffled-label only for the physicochemical one), so the loop's benefit measured here is mainly yield and only partially model improvement on new laboratories (5.2). (iii) K_phys has a heterogeneous but real zero-fit association with permeability in unseen laboratories offline (5.3). In the loop, a small held-out-AUROC advantage over a shuffled channel appears in the scaffold-shifted regime for 2 of 3 GBM backends and not in regimes A or L or the physicochemical backend; part of the regime-B gain is any-channel, not rule content; the yield advantage over shuffled knowledge is largely the cost of shuffled noise; and with laboratories resampled K_phys is never better than shuffled knowledge on held-out ranking. These are exploratory leads from 54 dependent contrasts without multiplicity correction (5.2). (iv) Neither technique that was meant to move the loop beyond analog lookup worked as registered: evidence-weighted fusion is suggestive in permeability but null on its primary test and fails non-inferiority in binding, and lab-offset anchor calibration is not supported on public data because anchors are too sparse (5.7). (v) Cross-laboratory skill is about a third of within-structure-group skill for fingerprints, and the few-label gain is concentrated where a revealed analog is close, with no gain demonstrated to be transferable; the strongest baseline is nearest-neighbour lookup (5.4, 5.5). **(vi) The validity-gate audit is a finding in its own right.** Three successive decoy designs each failed a no-target control for a different reason (similarity, typicality, residual compound-only separability of a target's chemotype series), so a clean zero-shot benchmark for cyclic-peptide binders was not obtainable from this data collection with our constructions; the audit protocol (no-target controls, family-cluster intervals, unverifiable-by-default, control-adjusted scoring), the leave-one-out artefact and the stated power limits are offered as methodology, with the limits of Section 5.4, not as a proof that all matched-decoy designs fail. (vii) **Cross-loop pattern.** The three retrospective loops on measured data are consistent: model-guided selection beats random, and under shift lookup reproduces most of the margin (5.6); this is two analyses plus one illustration, and the in-distribution regimes of L5 show the loop clearly better than the lookup rules (loop − N-methyl rule +105.4 and loop − K_z rule +53.6), so the pattern is a statement about shift; loop-specific learning beyond lookup is not demonstrated. ## 7. Limitations

**What IH-LM is today.** About 830 lines of code (model 287, symbolic 195, tests 248, intrinsic check 102): a small standard transformer backbone with an atom-level tokenizer, an analog-attention block and heads, not trained at scale (no checkpoint). Most modules are symbolic or closed-form (fast memory for laboratory offsets, similarity retrieval, N-methyl and D-flip edit effects, update rules). Three of 15 tests are marked expected-failure with no threshold loosened: neural analog attention, net-only rho 0.525 against 0.113 without it (gap 0.41 meets its 0.25 criterion but the absolute threshold 0.70 is missed); the same module leave-laboratory-out rho 0.444 against 0.60; verifier head AUC 0.529 on new families against 0.90. The 25.5 M-parameter P0 model is a proof of pipeline and the planned 71-94 M-parameter P1 is untrained. It is not a foundation model and is not presented as one. The test results above come from a synthetic planted-signal test world, not from real data; no IH-LM module has been run on CycPeptMPDB or the binding benchmark, so no statement about which parts would add value is made. It becomes a substantial contribution only if a trained P1 passes its gates, the neural analog module and verifier are fixed or removed, an ablation ladder beats analog lookup and the information-privileged pretrained-plus-analog baseline, and a fair comparison with external pretrained models is run.
1. **Retrospective only.** The replay reveals labels of compounds that already have measurements. It supports statements about learning from graded feedback and ranking, not about generating better novel molecules, which requires a prospective experimental round.
2. **Narrow task and data.** Permeability ranking on one public database with series clustering and laboratory concentration (Section 4.1), and no stapled, bicyclic or disulfide peptides. Conclusions do not transfer to binding or other properties without further evidence.

```python
def cluster_contrast(tab_a, tab_b, min_seeds=3, B=B_BOOT, lo=2.5, hi=97.5):
    """Paired contrast arm A minus arm B on the within-source rho. POINT ESTIMATE = mean over sources of the per-source mean (over the seeds in which that source was held out/evaluated).
    ci_cluster: sources resampled with replacement (the interval to cite). ci_seed_only: seeds resampled with replacement, sources fixed, with the SAME statistic recomputed on each resample
    (per-source mean over the resampled seeds in which the source appears, then mean over sources) -- an interval for the reported mean even when sources are present in only some seeds."""
    d = {}
    for s in sorted(set(tab_a) & set(tab_b)):                     # sorted: set iteration order of strings varies between processes (hash randomisation) and would change the resampling
        common = sorted(set(tab_a[s]) & set(tab_b[s]))
        dd = {k: tab_a[s][k] - tab_b[s][k] for k in common}; dd = {k: v for k, v in dd.items() if np.isfinite(v)}        # NaN guard (committee 2): a non-finite per-seed value is dropped, never propagated into a mean or a bootstrap
        if len(dd) >= min_seeds: d[s] = dd
    if len(d) < 2: return dict(n_sources=len(d), mean=None, ci_cluster=None, ci_seed_only=None, interval_type="insufficient clusters")
    per_src = np.asarray([np.mean(list(v.values())) for v in d.values()], float); srcs_sorted = sorted(d)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(_seed(per_src, lo, hi)); bs = [float(rng.choice(per_src, len(per_src)).mean()) for _ in range(B)]
    seeds_all = sorted({k for v in d.values() for k in v}); rng2 = np.random.default_rng(_seed(per_src, lo, hi) + 1); bs2 = []
    for _ in range(B):
        draw = rng2.choice(len(seeds_all), len(seeds_all)); cnt = np.bincount(draw, minlength=len(seeds_all)); w = dict(zip(seeds_all, cnt)); vals = []
        for s in srcs_sorted:
            den = sum(w[k] for k in d[s]); 
            if den > 0: vals.append(sum(w[k] * x for k, x in d[s].items()) / den)
        bs2.append(float(np.mean(vals)))
    r4 = lambda x: round(float(x), 4)
    return dict(n_sources=len(d), n_seeds=len(seeds_all), mean=r4(per_src.mean()), ci_cluster=[r4(np.percentile(bs, lo)), r4(np.percentile(bs, hi))], ci_seed_only=[r4(np.percentile(bs2, lo)), r4(np.percentile(bs2, hi))],
                interval_type="bootstrap CI of the mean over SOURCES (cluster); ci_seed_only = bootstrap over seeds, sources fixed, same statistic recomputed", ci_level_pct=round(hi - lo, 1))
```

```python
def assign_status(res):
    """A11: validity is a two-sided EQUIVALENCE claim, not 'the interval contains 0.5'.
    VALID only if every control is evaluable AND no control excludes chance AND every PRIMARY control's 90 % family-cluster interval (TOST, alpha = 0.05 per side) lies inside [0.5-DELTA, 0.5+DELTA].
    NaN / unevaluable / < MIN_FAMILIES controls make the benchmark NOT_VALID_UNVERIFIABLE (explicit, never skipped). Zero-variance (constant) secondary controls are listed as DEGENERATE."""
    biased, unver, noteq, degenerate = [], [], [], []
    for k, v in res["primary"].items():
        if isinstance(v, str) or v["lo"] != v["lo"] or v["n_families"] < MIN_FAMILIES: unver.append(f"primary:{k}"); continue
        if v["lo"] > 0.5 or v["hi"] < 0.5: biased.append(f"primary:{k}")
        if not (v["lo90"] >= 0.5 - DELTA_PRIMARY and v["hi90"] <= 0.5 + DELTA_PRIMARY): noteq.append(f"primary:{k}")
    for k, v in res["secondary"].items():
        if not isinstance(v, dict) or v["lo"] != v["lo"] or v["n_families"] < MIN_FAMILIES:
            unver.append(f"secondary:{k}")
        elif v["lo"] == v["hi"]: degenerate.append(k)
        elif v["lo"] > 0.5 or v["hi"] < 0.5: biased.append(f"secondary:{k}")
    status = "NOT_VALID_BIASED" if biased else "NOT_VALID_UNVERIFIABLE" if unver else "NOT_VALID_NOT_EQUIVALENT" if noteq else "VALID"
    res.update(status=status, VALID=status == "VALID", equivalence_margin_primary=DELTA_PRIMARY, biased_controls=biased, unverifiable_controls=unver, not_equivalent_controls=noteq, degenerate_controls=degenerate)
    res["failed_controls"] = biased + [f"unverifiable:{x}" for x in unver] + [f"not_equivalent:{x}" for x in noteq]
    return res
```

```python
def causal_z(D, idx, k=5):
    """CAUSAL within-source standardisation (amendment A2): training targets for the revealed peptides `idx`, computed ONLY from the raw labels of the revealed peptides.
    Per source s with n_s revealed labels: mean/variance shrunk to the global revealed mean/variance with prior weight k (=5): mu_s=(n_s*m_s+k*mu_g)/(n_s+k), var_s=(n_s*v_s+k*v_g)/(n_s+k);
    a source with no revealed label uses the global statistics. Unrevealed labels are never read."""
    idx = np.asarray(idx); yr = D["yraw"][idx]; sc = D["src_code"][idx]
    mu_g, v_g = float(yr.mean()), float(max(yr.var(), 1e-12)); out = np.empty(len(idx))
    for s in np.unique(sc):
        m = sc == s; n = int(m.sum()); ms, vs = float(yr[m].mean()), float(yr[m].var())
        mu = (n * ms + k * mu_g) / (n + k); var = (n * vs + k * v_g) / (n + k); out[m] = (yr[m] - mu) / np.sqrt(max(var, 1e-12))
    return out
```

```python
def select_diverse(score, U, D, lam, cap_src, cap_flat, flat_count, batch=BATCH):
    """Diversity-aware sequential batch selection (pre-registration 1.5). `score` is aligned with U. Returns positions into U."""
    n = len(U); avail = np.ones(n, bool); msim = np.zeros(n)
    srcU = D["src_code"][U]; flatU = D["flat_code"][U]; src_cnt = np.zeros(len(D["src_names"]), int)
    fc = np.array([flat_count.get(int(f), 0) for f in flatU], int); BU = D["B"][U]; BUn = D["Bn"][U]; picks = []
    for _ in range(min(batch, n)):
        elig = avail & (src_cnt[srcU] < cap_src) & (fc < cap_flat)
        if not elig.any(): elig = avail                                    # caps relaxed only when nothing eligible remains
        val = np.where(elig, score - lam * msim, -np.inf)
        j = int(np.argmax(val)); picks.append(j); avail[j] = False
        src_cnt[srcU[j]] += 1; fc[flatU == flatU[j]] += 1
        inter = BU @ BU[j]; sim = inter / np.maximum(BUn + BUn[j] - inter, 1.0); msim = np.maximum(msim, sim)
    return picks
```

**Interpretation.** In this simulation, with chemistry-independent additive lab offsets, a ridge learner and within-lab ranking as the metric, offset calibration cannot buy ≥ 0.03 Spearman reliably at any realistic anchor design: the offset is a lab-constant term that the downstream within-lab ranking is already almost invariant to, so even perfect knowledge gives a small gain (≤ ~0.025 at τ = 1.0).
