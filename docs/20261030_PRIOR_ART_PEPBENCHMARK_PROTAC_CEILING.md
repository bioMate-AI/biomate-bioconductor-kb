# Prior-art note: PepBenchmark and the PROTAC inter-laboratory ceiling paper (2026-10-30)
Question (committee synthesis, WS3 item 3): does either paper already cover cyclic content, family-disjoint splits, matched decoys or leakage controls? Method: both PDFs downloaded from arXiv and read as extracted text (`pdftotext`); section/appendix references below are to those texts. **What was and was not read:** PepBenchmark — abstract, introduction, related work, §3–§6, Appendix A (limitations), Appendix B (dataset descriptions for the non-canonical and peptide–protein sets), Appendix D (cleaning, negative sampling, splitting) and the non-canonical processing sections; the full leaderboard tables in Appendix H were skimmed, not audited. PROTAC paper — abstract, §1–§6, §7 limitations, Appendix C (similarity sweep) and I (few-shot protocol); Appendices A, F, K, L, N, O (replication and ICC grids) were not read line by line. Neither paper contains a result we need to reproduce; claims below are limited to what the text states.

## 1. PepBenchmark (Zhang, Wang, Zhou et al., ICLR 2026, arXiv 2604.10531)
| our question | what the paper does | covers it? |
|---|---|---|
| cyclic content | 35 datasets: 29 canonical (68,588 sequences) + 6 non-canonical (9,512). "Non-canonical" = peptides with non-canonical monomers **or cyclic structures** (§3). The only cyclic-peptide-specific set is nc-cpp pampa (6,970 PAMPA permeabilities from CycPeptMPDB, a regression property task); others are nc-antibacterial/antifungal/antimicrobial/hemolytic classification sets and **nc-PpI ba, 277 peptide–protein binding affinities** (5–19 residues, "non-natural or modified"; cyclisation not stated). | **Partly.** Cyclic permeability yes; **no cyclic-peptide target-binding benchmark of useful size** (277 points) |
| family-/target-disjoint splits | Canonical single-peptide sets: k-mer + MMseqs2 hybrid split (30 % identity). Non-canonical single-peptide sets: ECFP connected components at Tanimoto 0.95. Peptide–protein sets: **cold-start by protein, proteins clustered at 30 % identity** (§4, §5.1). | **Yes for peptide–protein** (incl. nc-PpI ba, n = 277); **no** family-held-out multi-target design for cyclic binders, no fold balancing by family size, no cluster-based power |
| matched decoys | **BDNegSamp**: negatives drawn from other bioactive peptide classes (excluding related activities), then sampled so length, net charge, hydrophobicity (JS divergence < 0.2 over 30 bins), 1-mer (< 0.05) and 2-mer (< 0.15) composition match the positives (App. D.2; Table 13). For non-canonical sets a generative model converts canonical negatives to modified forms. | **Yes, in spirit, for classification of canonical/non-canonical property sets** — but validated only by marginal JS divergences of shallow features, **not by a trained no-target classifier or any typicality score**. Not used for binding data (their PepPI sets use measured labels). |
| leakage controls | Identifies and removes **k-mer leakage** (Fisher test for representative k-mers; k-mer clusters assigned to one split) and near-duplicate redundancy (MMseqs2 90 %); no redundancy removal for non-canonical sets ("relied on data-splitting"). | **Yes for k-mer/near-duplicate leakage.** **No** no-target validity gate, no control-adjusted scoring, no analog-distance analysis |
| source/lab heterogeneity | IQR outlier removal on regression labels pooled across literature sources (§4). | **No** (no source offsets, no inter-lab ceiling) |
| few-label adaptation | None. "Low-data" remarks only compare fingerprint RF vs PLMs on small datasets (§5.2.1). | **No** |
| stated limitation relevant to us | No structural data (App. A). | – |
Performance statements we can cite: fingerprint RF/XGBoost are the best non-canonical models (avg ROC-AUC 95.9–96.0 on the four non-canonical classification sets); GNN/SMILES models lag; PepPI ROC-AUC 51–61 on the 44,148-pair canonical `ppi` set under cold-start (near chance), nc-PpI ba MAE 1.47–1.74 (§5.2.2–5.2.3, Tables 4–5).
**Position:** PepBenchmark is the closest existing *peptide* benchmark and already establishes (i) the shallow-feature-matched negative idea, (ii) cold-start protein splits, (iii) k-mer leakage. It does **not** cover multi-target cyclic-peptide binding at scale, a classifier-based no-target gate, control-adjusted evaluation, analog-distance strata, few-label adaptation, or family-cluster power. Our gate/M3 result (a matched-decoy cross-target design that remains separable by a no-target classifier even after 18-proxy + typicality matching) is a direct, testable counterpoint to the assumption that JS-divergence-matched negatives are "indistinguishable by shallow features"; we have **not** tested BDNegSamp itself, so that remains a hypothesis.

## 2. PROTAC inter-laboratory ceiling (Klamt, Nejdl, Tang, arXiv 2605.11764, 12 May 2026; preprint)
| our question | what the paper does | covers it? |
|---|---|---|
| cyclic content | PROTACs only (heterobifunctional small molecules; 10,748 measurements, 173 targets). The text contains **no** occurrence of "peptide", "macrocycle" or "cyclic" (searched). | **No** |
| family-/target-disjoint splits | Leave-one-target-out (LOTO, the "C3" cold-target class), 65 eligible folds; leave-one-family-out (LOFO) AUROC 0.616 ± 0.024 (Limitations); SPECTRA-style Tanimoto-ceiling sweep (AUROC 0.898 at s = 0.95 → 0.652 at s = 0.50, Table 3). | **Yes** for target-disjoint and similarity-controlled evaluation (small molecules) |
| matched decoys | None; labels are binarised measured activity (DC50 < 1 µM or Dmax > 50 %). | **No** |
| leakage controls | Random-CV vs LOTO gap decomposed; SMILES-level deduplication of training partitions lowers the plateau (0.668 → ≈ 0.617); multi-seed correction of single-seed HPO winners (Bailey–López de Prado selection bias). | **Partly** (dedup, selection bias); **no** no-target/typicality control |
| source/lab heterogeneity | **Yes, this is the paper's point.** "0.124 AUROC" = within-target cross-laboratory cascade on a 36-target cohort: random-CV 0.802 → cross-lab 0.678 → LOTO 0.653 (paired n = 35); the 0.124 is the random-CV minus cross-lab drop measured on same-compound replicate measurements; target-clustered bootstrap 95 % CI **[0.051, 0.197]**, leave-one-target-out sensitivity [0.113, 0.131] over 12 replicate-bearing targets (§5, App. K). It is described as the operational ceiling "at current dataset scale", not a proven bound. | **Yes** (small molecules). Our lab/assay-offset-with-anchors idea is not in the paper (they quantify, they do not propose anchors) |
| few-label adaptation | **Yes:** per-target retraining on k stratified labelled compounds: k = 5 with ADMET features lifts 65-target LOTO AUROC 0.668 → **0.7050**; 14-target matched cohort 0.7126 (k = 25), 0.743 (k = 50), 0.778 (k = 100); RF beats MAML/ProtoNet; random selection costs ≈ 0.01–0.02 AUROC vs stratified; an RF+Morgan kNN (k = 5) appears as a baseline in the architecture comparison (Fig. 1). | **Partly.** Closest prior to our H4b; **no analog-distance stratification** — whether the gain is neighbour lookup is not separated |
| stated limitations | binary classification only; CRBN-heavy; ESM-2 scope; dedup lowers ceiling. | – |
**Position:** this paper supplies the strongest published framing for "cross-target generalisation gaps are largely measurement variance" and a protocol for measuring it (same-compound cross-lab replicates). It does not cover cyclic content, matched-decoy binding benchmarks, or no-target validity gates. Its few-label gain at k = 5 (+0.037 AUROC with ADMET features) is much smaller than ours (+0.21 AUROC for nearest-neighbour at k = 5), but the tasks differ (binarised degradation activity across labs vs our same-target binder/inactive discrimination within a positive-heavy, series-structured set), so the numbers are not comparable; we should not claim superiority.

## 3. What is, and is not, novel in our benchmark work (after this reading)
Not novel: shallow-feature-matched negatives (PepBenchmark), protein cold-start splits (PepBenchmark), similarity-controlled evaluation (PROTAC paper, SPECTRA-style), few-label per-target retraining beating meta-learning (PROTAC paper), inter-lab ceilings (PROTAC paper; for cyclic peptides we have not measured one — lab/source heterogeneity in CycPeptMPDB is a separate result from the loop study).
Not found in either paper (to be confirmed by a wider search before claiming): (a) a **classifier-based no-target validity gate** (atom-LM typicality + multi-proxy + fingerprint leave-fold-out) that declares benchmarks invalid, with the finding that **similarity-matched, then property-matched cross-target decoys cannot be made clean at this data size (98–100 % of positives lost)**; (b) **control-adjusted scoring** (arm must beat the strongest no-target control); (c) **analog-distance strata** separating transferable from nearest-neighbour few-label gains (running; A9); (d) **family-cluster power** reporting for peptide benchmarks (A9); (e) a **multi-target cyclic-peptide binding set** (17,120 compounds, 851 targets, frozen with hashes). Claim (a) is a finding about our data and construction; it should be framed as an audit result, not as proof that all matched-decoy designs fail.
Related-work citations to add (the synthesis also lists LLMNN, ACNet, Liu et al.): Zhang et al., PepBenchmark, ICLR 2026 (arXiv 2604.10531); Klamt, Nejdl, Tang, arXiv 2605.11764 (preprint; cite as such).
