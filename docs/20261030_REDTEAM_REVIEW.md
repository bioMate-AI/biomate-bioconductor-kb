# Red-team review of the manuscript set (independent; 2026-10-30)

Reviewer stance: a sceptical referee at a computational chemistry / ML venue reading `20261029_MANUSCRIPT_LOOP_SMALL_LLM_DRAFT.md` (main), `manuscript_loop.md` (loop preprint) and the RSI draft. Findings are ranked by severity; each cites the evidence I checked (see `20261030_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md`) and gives a concrete fix. No claim here is an opinion about the authors' honesty: the drafts disclose most of their own limits; the issues below are what a reviewer would still raise.

## Critical
**R1. The paper's central hypothesis has no test in the manuscript.** Title options and the abstract objective frame the study as "IH-LM vs larger pretrained peptide/chemistry LMs"; no IH-LM, no pretrained language model and no larger model has been run through the loop (draft §5.7; ledger "Not available at v1"). What exists is a GBM loop, a GBM replay with controls, a benchmark with failed gates and baselines. A referee will say the title promises a comparison that is absent. *Fix:* retitle and re-abstract as what is shown (an audited evaluation instrument and controlled GBM baselines; the language-model comparison as pre-registered work), or hold submission until the LM arms run. Do not keep an objective sentence whose answer is "not tested".

**R2. No benchmark passes its own validity gate, including the one used as primary.** `validity_gate_*.json`: I (primary) fails the fingerprint control (0.584 [0.533, 0.636]) and two proxy controls; M, M2, M3 and all H4b levels are `VALID = false`. The draft says so (§5.4) but §4.2 still presents "gates a benchmark must pass before any arm is judged" and analysis I as primary. The strongest baseline on I (negated nearest-neighbour similarity to other-family binders, 0.620) is itself a decoy-structure signal: inactives are more similar to other targets' binders than the positives are. *Fix:* state in §4.2 that the gate was applied retrospectively (after M2 failed) and that no analysis passes; restrict zero-shot claims to "not better than the control-adjusted baseline"; do not present any arm result on I as target-specific.

## High
**R3. For the GBM, the loop's benefit is selection yield, not model improvement.** Regime L: loop − one-shot held-out AUROC +0.003 [−0.034, 0.038]; loop − random feedback −0.072 [−0.113, −0.014]; K_phys on − off yield −0.1 [−3.8, 4.8]; the physchem rule ties the loop under scaffold shift (−0.5 [−20.0, 17.2]); regime B primary claim not met. The draft's Discussion reflects this, but every heading and the title still ask whether the loop "improves" a model. *Fix:* separate "yield" from "model improvement" in every table and heading; state the answer for the GBM as no on held-out ranking, yes on yield in-distribution, and make the IH-LM claim conditional on a model-improvement metric defined before the runs.

**R4. The knowledge-shuffle control is mis-specified.** The draft (§5.2) says shuffled knowledge "still raised held-out AUROC … control not passed". From `l7_aggregate.json`, regime A held-out AUROC gain: knowledge-off loop 0.0525 [0.0351, 0.0677], K_phys 0.0509, shuffled-knowledge 0.0555 [0.0404, 0.0703]; regime L: knowledge-off 0.0754, K_phys 0.0915, shuffled-knowledge 0.0812. Every loop arm gains, including the one with no knowledge; the proper contrast is shuffled-knowledge minus knowledge-off (≈ +0.003 in A), not shuffled-knowledge minus zero. The yield contrasts (shuffled-knowledge − loop: A −3.4 [−7.3, 0.5]; L −7.2 [−11.8, −2.2]) show that wrong knowledge costs yield, which is the informative result. *Fix:* report K_phys − shuffled-knowledge and shuffled-knowledge − knowledge-off for AUROC and yield per regime; re-state the control as "knowledge content must matter".

**R5. Statistical inference is conditional on one dataset and ten seeds.** Loop contrasts bootstrap over seeds (10 or 30) on a fixed library of 6,960 peptides in which two labs hold 63 % of peptides and 75 % of hits (52.8 % + 22.3 %). Seeds share the same compounds, so the intervals describe algorithmic variance, not uncertainty about other libraries or other labs; plain-GBM regime B has 2 seeds (inconclusive), LLM arms 4 paired sequences (pre-registered minimum 8). Fifteen to hundreds of contrasts are reported without multiplicity control outside the knowledge family. *Fix:* add lab-cluster (source) resampling or leave-lab-out replicate analysis for every headline contrast; mark all contrasts with fewer than 8 pairs exploratory in the tables; apply a family-wise rule or report the full contrast count.

**R6. Leakage routes not closed.** (a) Regime A pools contain 22.9 % identical-fingerprint and 60.7 % same-stereo-free-scaffold peptides (audit A1); regime C removes flat duplicates only. (b) Pretrained comparison models may contain the evaluation compounds: PeptideCLM-2's authors are reported (corpus survey; not re-opened in this audit) to give 8.06 % structural overlap with CycPeptMPDB; HELM-BERT pretrains on it; our own atom-LM corpus filter removed ≥0.9-Tanimoto neighbours of CycPeptMPDB peptides only, while 58 % / 45 % of benchmark positives are reported present in that corpus (workstream 3 contamination flags; not re-derived here). (c) Within-source standardisation and the hit threshold use all labels (transductive; bounded by Jaccard 0.867 under 30 % subsampling, but not eliminated). (d) K_phys rules are mined from 21 laboratories; the draft does not say whether the loop's knowledge arms in regimes A/B used rules mined from labs whose labels are in the evaluation pool (the leave-one-lab-out test is clean only for regime L). *Fix:* state for each arm which labs the rules were mined from; for A/B re-mine without the pool's labels or label the arm leaky; extend the corpus filter to benchmark positives for every model reported; report all results on the contamination-controlled subset first.

**R7. The database version is undocumented.** The Introduction quotes the paper (7,334 peptides; PAMPA 6,941; Circle 5,115 + Lariat 2,336 = 7,451, which itself disagrees with 7,334); the analysis uses a local file that is v1.0 (7,451 rows) plus v1.2 (1,015 rows) = 8,466 rows, 7,991 unique ids, 7,298 PAMPA values. A reader cannot reproduce the 6,960. *Fix:* state version, download date, file hash and the exact loader filters in Methods; reconcile the paper's own counts in a footnote.

## Medium
**R8. The loop preprint still carries superseded analysis and unsupported interpretation.** Pooled Table 1 (396.7 / 366.7), the "70–83 % of the loop yield" statement, the sentence that the loop is "far better than random … including when the starting peptides share no scaffold", and "fingerprints already carry that information" contradict L5 (B +22.2 [−1.6, 44.1]; rule yields 55 % / 40 % of the loop within source) or have no experiment behind them; the N-methyl 304 [304–304] is a tie-breaking artefact. *Fix:* move the pooled analysis to a supplement under a "superseded" banner and rewrite the Discussion from L5; delete the redundancy claim.

**R9. Two baseline-error analyses report different numbers and one has no committed script.** Random-split Spearman 0.713 / 0.766 (error analysis; scratch script) vs 0.706 / 0.757 (stratification, committed predictions); chirality gain +0.05 vs +0.04; grouped 0.663 / 0.712 vs 0.666 / 0.708. *Fix:* designate the stratification code as canonical, cite one set, commit or drop the scratch script, and reproduce the cited per-source numbers (Faris 0.49, Bhardwaj 0.42) from the canonical code.

**R10. Missing control for "where the gain comes from".** The claim that the in-distribution yield is largely location of the Townsend series rests on pick shares (346 vs 206 of 500). The control that separates series location from learning is absent: random selection restricted to the highest-yield source, and a source-prior baseline (pick proportional to source hit rate). *Fix:* add both arms (T2 acquisition work can supply them); report yield per source for every arm.

**R11. Benchmark baselines make most arms uninterpretable without the nearest-neighbour bar.** Few-label NN reaches 0.753–0.869 and fits-on-revealed-only 0.750–0.861; any LM arm below that is merely an inferior analog lookup. The draft states this; the abstract should state that "better than zero-shot" is not a claim because zero-shot is at 0.539.

**R12. Citation verification is incomplete.** Of 15 spot-checks 11 were re-confirmed; Graber 2025, Jablonka 2024, CycPeptMP 2024 and the PeptideCLM 2024 numbers could not be re-opened (publisher walls, CAPTCHA, HTTP 429/503); Geylan and Liu are already tagged unverified; ChEMBL, CREMP and Landry licences were not re-checked. Two summaries are incomplete as written: the representative-value rule (more data first, assay rank second) and the Amirahmadi GP result (length-focused subset, 5,826 samples, binary label). *Fix:* downgrade the unreachable items to `[LIT-UNVERIFIED]` until re-opened from an accessible mirror; correct the two summaries.

## Lower
**R13. Replays cited as evidence have limits the table states but the Abstract does not.** `replay_permeability.py` has 3 seeds and 3 campaigns, two of which are the dominant labs; `c2_retro_loop` margin comes largely from a fixed cross-target transfer model with paralog leakage (WS7); neither should appear in the abstract or conclusions.

**R14. Reproducibility.** Raw CycPeptMPDB, Stage B inputs and corpus files live in scratch directories or S3 without archived hashes; the audit scripts for the data statistics are not in the repository (only the log); the S3-only `pretrain.json` is cited as an artifact. *Fix:* commit a `data_manifest.json` (file, source URL, version, SHA-256, rebuild script) and the data-statistics script; archive the cited S3 objects' hashes in the repository.

**R15. Terminology drift.** "Small model" appears in H1–H5 and satellites after the IH-LM redefinition (ledger T1–T5); "large LLM" meant general-purpose models in earlier drafts. One term per concept across all documents.

## Contradictions between repository documents (detail in ledger)
Inventory §G still presents the C2 `0.299 / 0.118` log as pending reproduction; the later C2 audit says the margin comes from a fixed transfer model. The KB-rule validation (7 of 8 labs, median 0.10) conflicts with the KB audit (median −0.01, 10 of 20). The citation audit summary ("54 of 57") does not match the artifact tally (45 strict matches).

## What a referee would likely accept as solid
The family-disjoint frozen-list design with hashes (recomputed identical), the stereoisomer-group and flat-structure statistics (recomputed identical), the L5 table (identical to the artifact), the corrected shuffled-label and within-group protocols, the explicit gate failures, and the willingness to publish nulls. The paper is strongest as an evaluation-and-baselines contribution; its weakest point is the absence of the language-model arm its framing promises.

## Resolved since the first review
R2 (gate failure now a finding, applied retrospectively), R4 (knowledge control restated as paired contrasts), R7 (CycPeptMPDB version stated), R8 (pooled analysis moved to Supplement S1; "far better than random" Discussion rewritten), R9 (stratification canonical), R12 (literature tags downgraded to summary/snippet level), R15 (satellite terminology) are fixed in the files I re-read. ## New or still open, ranked
**N1 (high). A key result contradicts itself across documents: K_phys in regime B.** The regenerated aggregate shows, in regime B for the stereo-blind GBM (10 seeds, 99 % boot-mean): K_phys − none yield +4.9 [1.7, 7.8], K_phys − K_shuffled yield +3.2 [0.7, 6.1], held-out AUROC gain K_phys − none +0.0625 [0.024, 0.108], K_phys − K_shuffled +0.0326 [0.011, 0.055], and contradicted knowledge −5.4 [−9.9, −0.1]. These are in the generalisation-relevant regime and agree in direction with L5's descriptor arm (+23.8) and the K_phys arms in regime A. The main draft §5.2, appendix A14, the patch note P4 and KB audit §9a say "no attributable K_phys effect on held-out ranking or yield; 7 of 7 configurations include 0". The 7-configuration audit file has no regime-B plain-GBM entry. The preprint §3.3 reports the B numbers and withholds the claim. *Fix:* one statement everywhere: "positive in regime B on development seeds (stereo-blind GBM), absent in A, L and in the physchem-channel arms; confirmatory seeds 10–29 pending"; add the B arm to the paired audit file and recount (8 configurations).

**N2 (high). "Seed-based interval ⇒ generalisation" is still possible by omission.** The coordinator's rule (no generalisation to new laboratories from seed-based intervals) is met in the Methods and Limitations, but regime L yield contrasts (e.g. loop − random feedback +165.0 [156.2, 175.4]) are seed-based over the same held-out laboratories; calling L "leave-laboratories-out" invites reading them as new-laboratory evidence. *Fix:* state in every L table caption that only the E-metrics use the source-cluster bootstrap and that yield contrasts in L are seed-based on a fixed set of laboratories; report the number of held-out sources next to each L figure.

**N3 (medium). Interval level and reproducibility of the aggregator.** `aggregate_l7.py` uses 99 % intervals for arms with `kphys`/`kn` in their name and 95 % otherwise (line 72), so one bullet mixes levels; the draft labels them all 95 %. The bootstrap uses an unseeded-per-call global generator, so regenerating the aggregate changed published values by up to 0.2 (156.3 → 156.2; 175.6 → 175.4; 68.0 vs 67.9). *Fix:* seed per contrast, print the level in the artifact, regenerate once and freeze with a hash.

**N4 (medium). Arithmetic claim not supported.** Preprint limitation: "about one in four hit labels would change (Jaccard 0.87)". The audit gives 99.7 flips of 6,960 labels (1.4 %) and Jaccard 0.867 on ≈697 hits, i.e. about 7 % of hits replaced (≈1 in 14). *Fix:* state the figures from `l5_label_audit.json` without the paraphrase.

**N5 (medium). Preprint abstract still leads with superseded and withdrawn numbers** (397 vs 50, 367 vs 107, N-methyl rule 276/304, "98 %", "5.7 vs 1.9 N-methyl groups") while Supplement S1 withdraws the concentration figures. *Fix:* delete the pooled sentences from the abstract; keep one sentence that a pooled analysis was confounded and is in S1.

**N6 (medium). Rule-baseline intervals unlabelled in the preprint** (spreads reported as bare ranges; "both intervals above zero" for regime C). *Fix:* add the spread/boot-mean labels as in the main draft.

**N7 (medium). K_nme is described as "indistinguishable from the null" using the median alone.** The same permutation file shows pooled held-out ρ 0.325 (p = 5 × 10⁻⁴) and 7 of 20 laboratories individually significant (1 expected). The per-laboratory median is ≈ 0 while the pooled association is not; the sentence needs both and the reason (heterogeneity, dominance of two laboratories). The matched-pair N-methyl-addition effect (+0.252, I² 0.90) is the same phenomenon.

**N8 (medium). Statements about published work rest on abstracts.** Re-opened: LLMNN, ACNet, PepBenchmark, Klamt et al. (abstract level). Not verifiable from the abstract: PepBenchmark's venue ("ICLR 2026"), Klamt's 0.802 → 0.678 and its CI, and the chair's "no laboratory-held-out splits"; PubMed 40877984 (Liu et al.) blocked. Klamt's abstract also reports few-shot retraining at k = 5 improving leave-one-target-out AUROC (0.668 → 0.705), which sits close to our H4b framing; the draft mentions it, and the novelty paragraph should say so explicitly.

**N9 (low). T1 and anchor results are not yet in the manuscript.** Draft §5.7 lists T1 as "results section empty" and the anchor test as "no result"; §8 lists the lab-offset module as "to be tested". Both have results (fusion: permeability F2 null, F1 fragile at k = 25 only, binding F1 partial and F2 failed; anchors: kill criterion met, coverage-limited, method recovers planted offsets and gains +0.151 with full anchor coverage). They are negative or fragile results for two of the three candidate contributions and belong in §5/§8.

**N10 (low). Committee cost accounting.** Balance 4.339 → 3.961 (0.378) matches the counted reviewer costs plus a 0.008 lag, but the cost log shows the 0.280 drop on the second Opus row; attributing it to the earlier GPT call is an inference. State it as such.
