# All-atom hybrid LLM paper: design (2026-10-31, design phase only; no result, no money spent on models)

Owner request: "I need an all-atom LLM paper, with innovation, hybrid rule, knowledge or feedback." This file is the design, the diagnosis of why the previous attempts failed, the falsifiable claim, the staged stop rules and the harness. Nothing here is a result. Numbers are read from committed files unless tagged [ESTIMATE] (my assumption, to be replaced by a timing probe) or [UNVERIFIED] (prior-art claim whose primary source I did not open). Primary sources I did open (abstract level): PMO benchmark (arXiv 2206.12411), MolLEO (arXiv 2406.16976), Grammar VAE (arXiv 1703.01925), PepINVENT (arXiv 2409.14040), Saturn (arXiv 2405.17066). Augmented Memory and REINVENT-family details are search-result level only.

## 0. Plain answer first
A positive claim is possible only if the LLM brings something a tuned simple optimiser cannot: priors learned from peptide chemistry that matter under verifiers the optimiser did not train on, and in-context use of known series. My honest prior that the hybrid beats the best tuned GA/BO on held-out verifiers at equal oracle budget is about 20% [judgement, not a measurement]; for the retrospective recovery arm about 30-35% [judgement]. The design makes either outcome publishable (a sample-efficiency benchmark with honest negatives, or a positive result), and it spends money in stages that stop early.

## 1. Why every earlier method candidate failed (the diagnosis the design rests on)
1. **The signal was analog lookup.** Where chemical series exist, nearest-neighbour or a Tanimoto-kernel GP captures most of the learnable signal (few-label nearest-neighbour AUROC 0.753/0.822/0.869 at k = 5/10/25; pilot 4: lookup leads from k = 5). A model must beat lookup where lookup has no analog to use (hard stratum), and the lab-shift tests could not detect +0.03 (5-11 usable labs, minimum detectable difference 0.09-0.55).
2. **The generation metric rewarded constraint satisfaction.** In pilot 3 a monomer-library enumerator (S-LIB, Y = 704.6 satisfying unique novel molecules per 1000) beat ChemFM-1B (294.3, 3 seeds), P0 (284.0, 3 of 5 seeds) and PeptideCLM-2 (237.2) because the constraints (macrocycle, N-Me count, MW, TPSA, PAINS) are met by construction. Implication: **an LLM that is only validated on constraint yield cannot win; the rules must be compiled into the decoder so it is not penalised for them, and the comparison must be on an objective the rules do not already solve.**
3. **Generators memorise.** The unified v4 peptide LM's valid samples are 70.8% memorised training peptides (`v4_eval/audit_dpo_result.json`: memorised_frac_of_valid 0.708), and DPO changed the weights by a relative L2 of only 0.00096 (v4_dpo) and 0.0017 (v4_dpo_rc): the feedback loop barely moved the policy.
4. **RL stalled for a study-design reason, not a capability reason.** The unified GRPO run had frac_reward_zero_std about 0.95-1.0 and KL about 0.001 (`docs/20261023_RL_DATAGEN_STUDY_DESIGN_SURVEY.md`): 77% binary reward plus a start policy that is consistent per prompt gives zero within-group variance, so GRPO learns from about 5% of the batch.
5. **Circular verifiers.** Optimising a fitted permeability model and then scoring with the same model measures Goodhart, not design quality. No wet-lab data exists.
6. **Knowledge priors are weak and lab-confounded.** The mined SAR rules (`kb_audit/peptide_sar_rules_v1.json`: tpsa weight -0.29, n_sources 21; hbd -0.42, n_sources 16; logp +0.49, n_sources 21; 6 rules in total) are physchem trends, not strong constraints; fusion with a learned prior was fragile (T1 F2 at k=5 +0.058, fails the Bonferroni t reading).
7. **The one consistent effect is retrieval.** Analog retrieval inside the loop model replicated on new seeds in regime L (+9.5 [6.2, 12.8], Holm p = 0.002) but did not beat GBM/GP. So retrieval is a component to keep, not a result.

**Design principles that follow:** (i) compile hard rules into decoding (remove the S-LIB advantage); (ii) condition on retrieved known series (the only effect that replicated); (iii) pick the feedback algorithm that produces within-group variance (graded reward, rank advantages, variance filtering, pairwise preferences); (iv) separate TRAIN verifiers from HELD-OUT verifiers and add a retrospective recovery arm that needs no verifier; (v) PMO-style fixed oracle budget so "better" means sample-efficient; (vi) compute the minimum detectable effect before looking.

## 2. Central claim (one falsifiable sentence)
**Claim H-AA.** At a fixed budget of B = 3,000 calls to train-time verifiers, an atom-level causal peptide LM with (a) hard rules compiled into its decoder, (b) retrieved known-series context and (c) verifier-feedback preference optimisation attains a higher AUC-top-10 on HELD-OUT verifiers (independent of the train verifiers) than the best tuned simple optimiser (graph/SELFIES GA, edit-operator GA with the same compiled grammar, REINVENT/Augmented-Memory-style RL, Tanimoto-GP Bayesian optimisation, MolLEO-style LLM-as-GA-operator) by at least +0.03 normalised AUC, with the lower bound of the objective-cluster-bootstrap interval above 0 in at least 2 of 3 objective families; and it recovers more held-out known binders in the blind retrospective arm G.
**Falsified if:** any of the gate conditions below fails, or the gain exists on train verifiers but not on held-out verifiers (Goodhart), or any ablation (no rules / no retrieval / no feedback) matches the full model.
**Priors** (judgement): P(primary gate passes) about 20%; P(arm G win) about 30-35%; P(a publishable benchmark-with-negatives outcome) high.

## 3. System design
- **Backbone.** (see section 2A: existing ChemFM-1B + LoRA; nothing is pretrained from scratch.) Causal LM at atom level with chirality: ChemFM-1B (Llama architecture, MIT; pilot 3 measured about 860 s per 1000 samples on 32 vCPU, validity 0.98) for the main arm; a smaller causal model (our atom LM or a causal head on PeptideCLM-2) for fast iteration. PeptideCLM-2 itself is a masked LM (pilot 3: validity 0.85, Y 237.2): used as a comparator and a retrieval/embedding encoder, not as the main generator.
- **Rule compilation (hybrid-rule component).**
  - *Hard rules* (finite-state mask over atom tokens, with prefix-feasibility look-ahead): valid ring closure for a head-to-tail macrocycle, ring size >= 12, N-methyl amide count in [2, 6], additive MW upper bound via a conservative remaining-token bound, no PAINS prefix patterns. Violating tokens get -inf logits, so rule satisfaction is about 1.0 by construction and no oracle call is wasted on invalid or rule-failing molecules.
  - *Soft rules* (log-linear potentials added to logits, product-of-experts): the mined within-source SAR directions (tpsa, hbd, logp, N-Me, D-flip) with weights from `peptide_sar_rules_v1.json`, **mined only from train sources** (the leak guard `assert_priors_clean` already exists in `ihlm/symbolic.py`).
- **Knowledge (retrieval component).** Context = the k nearest known peptides (stereo-aware Tanimoto) with their train-verifier scores or measured values (the M3 mechanism that replicated), plus target pharmacophore rules where the target has them (e.g. RGD for integrins, `acceptance_gate.py`).
- **Feedback (verifier component).** Rounds of: sample N = 100-200 from the compiled decoder, query the train verifiers (budgeted), build preference pairs by graded rank (see section 7), update LoRA weights with a KL-regularised preference or policy-gradient step, update the retrieval store with the new scored molecules. The existing `reward.py` (hard gates dominate; FEP preferred over MM-GBSA) and `score.py` supply the reward structure.

## 5. Baselines and fair tuning
- Graph GA and SELFIES-GA (PMO-style), edit-operator GA (our S-EDIT operators: N-Me add/remove, D-flip, residue swap, ring change) **with the same hard-rule filter**, library enumeration + surrogate ranking (S-LIB with active learning), REINVENT/Augmented-Memory-style RL on the same token set, Tanimoto-GP Bayesian optimisation (the strongest published simple method in the peptide surveys), MolLEO-style LLM-as-operator (no fine-tuning), random search with filter (floor), copy-seed (control).
- **Tuning protocol:** every method gets the same dev budget on DEV objectives (a family and sources disjoint from the test objectives): 20 hyperparameter configurations x 3 seeds, chosen by train-verifier AUC-top-10 on DEV; configs frozen and committed before any test run; all methods then use the same oracle budget B on the test objectives. No method sees held-out verifiers during tuning.

## 6. Controls, seeds, intervals, minimum detectable effect, gate
- **Controls:** shuffled reward (hash-noise verifier: no method should beat random search), random-initialised LM (same architecture), no-rules (post-hoc filter only; reports calls wasted on rule failures), no-retrieval, no-feedback (frozen LM, best-of-B), copy-seed, planted-optimum oracle (all methods must find it; checks the harness and the decoder).
- **Seeds and intervals:** >= 10 seeds per method per objective (20 for the primary comparison if the pilot variance demands it); paired by seed; report the percentile bootstrap CI of the mean, the t-interval and the objective-cluster bootstrap (the unit of generalisation is the objective/target), each labelled; Holm over the primary contrasts.
- **Minimum detectable effect, computed first** from stage-1 pilot variance with `mde_paired(sd_diff, n)`. Illustration only: sd_diff 0.05 and n = 10 gives MDE about 0.044. If the MDE exceeds the +0.03 margin the result is UNDERPOWERED and a miss is worded "no gain detectable above MDE", never "no gain".
- **Gate (pre-registered, `gate_primary`):** mean paired difference >= +0.03 normalised AUC-top-10 vs the BEST baseline, interval lower bound > 0, in >= 2 of 3 objective families (physchem/rule objective, permeability-model objective, structure objective), judged on held-out verifiers; plus arm G.
- **Arm G (blind retrospective recovery):** per target, hold out the top-quartile exact-potency binders as the answer set; context = a few non-answer binders; metric = fraction of answer-set compounds with a generated stereo-aware near neighbour (Tanimoto >= 0.5) among N = 1,000 samples, plus scaffold-level recovery; baselines seeded the same way; document-level blind rule for retrieval and the KB. **Leakage warning:** ChemFM-class models are pretrained on UniChem-scale corpora that contain most ChEMBL binders [UNVERIFIED cut-off]; arm G therefore uses post-cutoff documents only (the data carry document years to 2025) or is reported as contaminated.

## 7. Why RL stalled and the concrete fix
Cause: binary/consistent reward gives zero within-group variance (section 1.4). Fix, all testable cheaply in stage 0/1 as **diagnostics with stop thresholds**: (1) graded reward from the continuous tier-1/2 verifier scores plus a rank-based (not z-score) group advantage; (2) group size >= 16 and sampling temperature chosen so the reward standard deviation within groups is non-trivial; (3) drop zero-variance groups (variance filtering) and report the fraction retained; (4) pairwise preference (DPO-style) from rank-ordered pairs with a margin, which needs no within-group variance; (5) a KL and weight-delta gate: if after the first 3 rounds KL to the reference is < 0.01 or the relative weight change is < 0.005 (v4 DPO was 0.001), raise the learning rate/LoRA rank once, then stop. (6) Memorisation guard: novelty against the training set and the retrieval store is part of the reward and of the evaluation (v4 samples were 70.8% memorised).

## 10. What is and is not new (plain words)
Not new: the PMO-style evaluation, LLMs in evolutionary search, RL on SMILES models, peptide RL generators with ncAA, grammar-constrained generation. Possibly new, to be shown and not assumed: (1) hard cyclic-peptide rules (macrocycle, N-Me count, MW) and lab-invariant within-source SAR potentials **compiled into an atom-level LLM decoder**; (2) the **train-versus-held-out verifier protocol with disjoint fitting sources, plus a blind retrospective recovery arm**, applied to generative loops (this ties to paper 1's evaluation contribution); (3) an honest sample-efficiency comparison for peptides against tuned GA/BO/RL baselines including a MolLEO-style arm. The method novelty is a combination, so the paper should claim a combination and a rigorous test, not a new primitive.

## 14. Erratum (2026-10-29, Track E amendment A4; docs/20261031_ALLATOM_TRACK_E_PREREG.md)
The gate in sections 6 and 8 ('mean paired difference >= +0.03 normalised AUC-top-10 vs the best baseline') is WITHDRAWN. On tuning seeds only, the percentile-against-natural-peptides scale saturated (best tuned baselines 0.979-0.986, random library search 0.886-0.944 at B = 1,000), so +0.03 was unreachable and the metric could not discriminate methods. Replacement: verifier outputs are z-scores against the generation space (random rule-satisfying draws of the library sampler; `track_e/verifiers_e.build_ladder(gen_ref=...)`), the metric is AUC-top-10 in those reference-SD units, and the margin is relative (a fraction of the gap between the best baseline and random library search, fixed with its derivation before the baseline evaluation seeds are read). Anything in this document that says 'normalised AUC-top-10' now means SD-AUC. `pmo_harness.gate_primary` no longer has a default margin.
