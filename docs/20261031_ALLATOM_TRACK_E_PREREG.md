Written 2026-10-29 (CDT) BEFORE any tuning, evaluation or hybrid run. Owner rules in force: no compute on the laptop; EC2 spot only; audit rulebook (docs/committee_raw/20261030c/audit_gates_data_criteria.md section D). Code: `research/guangzhou_all_atom/cyclic_llm/allatom_paper/track_e/` (commits d61d63ff2, 7fa31928e). Nothing below has a result attached; results are appended by amendment A2 (baselines, MDE, P(pass)) and later sections.

## 1. Roles and independence (the three-way split)
Each objective family has a TRAIN verifier (the budgeted oracle), a STOP verifier (used only for Goodhart-gap early stopping, never an oracle) and a HELD-OUT verifier (applied to the final top-10 only). `check_three_way` enforces distinct names, correct roles and disjoint fitting-source sets between the three groups.
Source groups (rule written in `verifiers_e.source_groups`; membership committed in `track_e/source_groups.json` after the EC2 pre-flight): sources with >= 20 labelled PAMPA peptides in the post-source-fix annotation; sort by (-n_peptides, source name); round-robin pattern [0,1,2,2,1,0]; group 0 = A (train fit), 1 = S (stop fit), 2 = B (held-out fit). The rule uses source sizes only, never a score.

| family | TRAIN (oracle) | STOP | HELD-OUT |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 physchem / rule | fixed textbook potential -0.01 TPSA - 0.15 HBD + 0.20 logP - 0.10 max(0, RotB-10); no fitting | RF on sources S (descriptors + 512 fingerprint bits) | ETKDG + MMFF conformers (4), minimum polar SASA (physics), applied to the final top-10 |
| F2 permeability model | HistGradientBoosting on sources A (within-source-z PAMPA; Morgan counts 1024 + 7 descriptors) | RF on sources S (different family from the train model) | Tanimoto-kernel ridge ('GP mean', alpha 1.0) on sources B |

All verifier outputs are percentiles in [0,1] against a reference library of 3,000 randomly chosen labelled peptides (60 for the physics verifier), so normalised AUC-top-10 is comparable across families. The structure family (Boltz/ipTM) is stage 3 and out of scope for stage 1; the gate in this pilot therefore needs BOTH F1 and F2 (min_pass = 2 of 2).

## 2. Protocol
- Budget B = 1,000 train-verifier calls; metric = AUC of the top-10 mean vs calls divided by the budget (`pmo_harness.auc_topk`); duplicates, invalid and rule-failing proposals are free but counted, proposal cap 20 x B. Hard-rule set C = pilot-3 constraint set (ring >= 12, 2-6 N-methyl amides, MW 600-1400, TPSA <= 300, no PAINS) applied identically to every method.
- Seed molecules: 300 rule-satisfying peptides from source group A (deterministic sample).
- Baselines (non-LM, built now): random_lib (floor), lib_surrogate (library enumeration + HGB surrogate ranking), edit_ga (S-EDIT operators), selfies_ga, gp_bo (Tanimoto-kernel GP, UCB kappa 1). Deferred to milestone M3 because they need the Track I generator interface: REINVENT-style RL on a causal LM and the MolLEO-style LM-as-operator arm.
- Tuning: every method is tuned on the OTHER family's train objective (configs for F1 are chosen on F2's train objective and vice versa), seeds 100-102, grids in `baselines.GRIDS` (3-8 configs per method), best mean AUC-top-10 wins; the chosen configs are frozen in `tuned.json` and committed BEFORE the evaluation seeds run. Deviation from the design doc: the design asked for disjoint DEV objectives with 20 configs x 3 seeds; with two families the cross-family tuning keeps tuning and test objectives disjoint but with a smaller grid.
- Evaluation: seeds 0-9 per method per family; held-out verifier applied to the final top-10; stop-verifier score of the final top-10 reported as the Goodhart-gap diagnostic (train minus stop).
- Controls (can fail AND pass): shuffled reward (hash-noise verifier): pass if every method's AUC-top-10 lies inside the random_lib floor interval (paired seed t-interval of the difference contains 0); planted optimum (score = max(0, 1 - (|N-Me - 4| + |ring - 20|/4)/4)): pass if every non-floor method reaches >= 0.9 of the optimum top-10 mean within B; copy-seed: plateau at the best seed score (cannot beat the number of unique seeds). A control that fails is reported with the verdict; the hybrid arm controls (random-init LM, no-rules, no-retrieval, no-feedback) are specified for M3.

## 3. Inference and decision rule (frozen vocabulary)
- Unit of inference: seeds within an objective family; the two families are two clusters, so family-cluster inference is not available (n = 2) and conclusions are explicitly conditional on these two objective constructions. Per-family paired contrasts use the WIDER of the percentile bootstrap CI of the mean and the t-interval (`paired_contrast.ci_wide`).
- Primary contrast: hybrid minus the BEST baseline (selected on the evaluation seeds, which is conservative for the hybrid) in each family, on the HELD-OUT verifier (train-verifier curves reported beside it). Margin = +0.03 normalised AUC-top-10 chosen as the smallest improvement that would change which method we recommend, not from convenience.
- GO: in both families the mean difference >= +0.03 and the lower bound of the wide interval > 0, on held-out verifiers, with controls that can fail having passed. NO-GO-detectable: in at least one family the upper bound of the wide interval is below +0.03 (the margin is excluded). INCONCLUSIVE: otherwise. A compute decision taken from an INCONCLUSIVE screen is a judgement call and is worded as one.
- Stage-1 stop rule (from the design): if the hybrid's point estimate of normalised AUC-top-10 is not above the best baseline's at the pilot budget (both train and held-out), stop and report NO-GO-detectable or INCONCLUSIVE per the interval vs margin.
- MDE first: amendment A2 (committed before any hybrid number) will state, from the baseline paired SD of method differences, the MDE80 for 10 seeds, the effective margin of the 'lower bound > 0' clause (about 0.7 x MDE per the audit), and P(pass) if the true gain is +0.03 and +0.05. If MDE80 > 2 x margin the screen is declared a low-power screen in advance and any miss is worded 'no gain detectable above MDE'.

## 4. Deviations and limits declared in advance
Graph-GA is replaced by SELFIES-GA (one GA on a string representation) plus the S-EDIT GA; the physics held-out uses 4 conformers and a 60-molecule reference; F1's train potential is a hand-set formula (no fitting) and the physics held-out is correlated with it by construction (both reward low polarity), which is the intended 'same quantity, different computation' check but is NOT independent in the statistical sense; the lab-fitted verifiers (F2) inherit CycPeptMPDB's source structure (Townsend and Kelly dominate), so group sizes are reported; the structure family and all wet-lab questions are outside this pilot. Pilot spend cap for Track E: USD 4.

**Why.** Reported from the committed v1 membership file (`track_e/source_groups.json`, 20 sources, 6,784 labelled PAMPA peptides): 2020_Townsend has 2,881 peptides (42.5% of the total) and 2021_Kelly 1,518 (22.4%); together 64.9%. So the stop verifier was essentially a Kelly model, the held-out verifier a CHUGAI/Furukawa model, and the train verifier a Townsend model. The owner judged group A too small and lab-dominated. No result of any kind had been read at the time of this amendment (the v1 pre-flight was terminated before producing numbers).

**Groups v2 (rule).** Sources with >= 20 labelled PAMPA peptides (post-source-fix annotation). The two largest labs, 2020_Townsend and 2021_Kelly, are ALWAYS train (A) and are never used for the stop or held-out verifiers. The remaining 18 sources, sorted by (-n, name), are dealt to S and B by snake draft with pattern S,B,B,S repeated. Sources with < 20 labelled peptides are NOT added to A (deviation from the owner's optional 'may add': within-source z-scores with < 20 peptides are unreliable, and A is already above the lower end of the target share). Consequences computed by hand from the v1 file's sizes (the EC2 job regenerates and commits the exact file `source_groups_v2.json` with the membership sha256 and the annotation hash): A = 2 sources / 4,399 peptides (64.8% of 6,784, inside the 60-68% target); S = 9 sources / about 1,269 peptides; B = 9 sources / about 1,116 peptides. Dominance remains: CHUGAI would be about 69% of S and 2016_Furukawa about 59% of B. The snake draft does not remove this; it is reported, not hidden.
Known caveat: 'lab' is a paper id, so two papers from one lab can sit in different groups (for example 2015_Wang and 2021_Wang, surname only, lab identity unverified); the same-surname pairs across groups are listed in the committed membership file's summary for transparency.

**Consequence for the train verifier.** Train verifier A is now fitted on Townsend + Kelly only (4,399 peptides, two papers), i.e. the F2 train objective is a two-lab permeability model.

**Verifier-quality gate (floors fixed now, run before any baseline).** `track_e/verifier_quality.py` computes (a) leave-one-source-out Spearman of each fitted verifier against the within-source-z labels over its own group's sources with >= 20 peptides, (b) the train verifier's Spearman on every S and B source (descriptive), (c) the physics held-out's Spearman against measured z on a fixed 120-peptide sample of B (seed 2026). Pre-registered floors: STOP and HELD-OUT permeability verifiers mean LOSO Spearman >= 0.15 over >= 5 evaluable sources; physics held-out Spearman >= +0.10. If a floor is not met, that verifier is declared 'too weak to rank' in the report BEFORE anything else runs, its family contributes no stop/held-out evidence, and because the gate needs both families the stage-1 outcome would be INCONCLUSIVE (the baselines on the train objectives still run, cheaply, and are reported descriptively). The floors come from the whole-data leave-labs-out skill we measured earlier (about 0.22 to 0.25 for gradient-boosted models) discounted for the smaller fitting groups; they were not tuned on any v2 number.

**Robustness arm.** The baselines are re-run on groups v1 (`--groups v1`, membership file kept as `source_groups.json`) at the end, evaluation seeds only, same tuned configs; reported as a robustness table beside the v2 primary. Nothing is selected on it.

## Amendment A3 (2026-10-29 CDT, coordinator decision): one time-boxed F1 held-out replacement attempt, and held-out noise in the MDE / P(pass). Committed BEFORE the replacement test ran and before any evaluation number (baseline or replacement) was read.
**Label: POST HOC.** Motivated by the A2 floor failure of the physics held-out (Spearman -0.008). One attempt only; if it fails or cannot be evaluated, F1 stays void and no further replacement is searched.

**A3.1 Replacement verifier (specification fixed now).** `etkdg_bw_polar_sasa`: for each peptide, 60 ETKDGv3 conformers (macrocycle torsions, random coordinates, seed 7, RMS pruning 0.5 A), MMFF94s minimisation in vacuum (<= 500 iterations), Boltzmann weights w_i proportional to exp(-(E_i - E_min)/kT) with T = 298.15 K (kT = 0.5925 kcal/mol; weighting temperature stated in advance), polar SASA per conformer (N and O atoms and hydrogens bonded to them; RDKit FreeSASA classifier radii, probe 1.4 A), score = minus the Boltzmann-weighted polar SASA, converted to a percentile against the same function applied to 120 reference peptides. Per-molecule wall limit 120 s; failures are counted, not imputed.
**A3.2 Floor test.** Evaluated on ALL group-B peptides (1,116 in v2) within a 95-minute time-box, peptides ordered round-robin over the 9 B sources so that partial completion keeps coverage. Metric: Spearman between the verifier score and the measured within-source-z PAMPA label, pooled over B. PASS = point estimate >= +0.10 AND the lower bound of a 95% source-cluster bootstrap interval (9 clusters, 2,000 resamples) > 0. This is the 'uncertainty rule' attached to the A2 floor from now on. Evaluable only if >= 600 peptides covering >= 7 of the 9 B sources finish; otherwise the test is VOID (not FAIL) and F1 stays void. Also reported (not used for the decision): per-source Spearman, the A2 minimum-polar-SASA score on the same peptides, and, as a REFERENCE only (existing peptides, cannot be an oracle on generated molecules), the Spearman of the CycPeptMPDB-4D descriptors (Water_3D_PSA, Hexane_3D_PSA, and their difference; Zenodo record 21274695, CSV md5 e4b957982bd572aba5029963804e0524) against the same labels for the B peptides present in that table.
**A3.3 Consequence.** PASS: the verifier becomes the F1 held-out and the stage-1 gate (2 of 2 families) is evaluable on held-out verifiers. FAIL or VOID: F1 has no admissible held-out verifier; the gate is restated as INCONCLUSIVE-by-design with that stated reason (the registered verdict ceiling is INCONCLUSIVE); F1 train-objective and stop-verifier results are still reported descriptively.
**A3.4 Held-out verifier noise in the MDE and P(pass).** Let lambda_H be the held-out verifier's own reliability against the measured label (leave-one-source-out Spearman from A2: 0.229 registered mean for the F2 held-out, but n-weighted -0.005). A true improvement delta of the latent label (SD units) appears on a held-out score attenuated by approximately lambda_H times delta. Therefore: (a) the observed-scale MDE80 is computed as before from baseline paired SDs, and its true-effect equivalent is MDE_obs / lambda_H; (b) the P(pass) table in the baseline amendment (A4) will have columns for lambda_H in {0.229, 0.10, 0.0}; at lambda_H = 0 the probability of a held-out pass equals the false-positive probability whatever the true gain is; (c) every held-out claim is worded with this attenuation. Because the F2 held-out is essentially uninformative about real permeability on its largest lab, it is used as an independent MODEL check (does a gain transfer to a differently fitted model: an anti-Goodhart check), not as a measure of real permeability.
**A3.5 Finding stated plainly (for the results and the method manuscript).** The held-out verifiers have weak ranking power: the physics held-out failed its floor, the F2 held-out meets the registered mean-based floor only through small sources and does not rank its largest lab; and the TRAIN verifier ranks the B sources better (mean Spearman 0.302) than the held-out verifier ranks its own sources (0.229; n-weighted -0.005). Any held-out claim in this study is therefore limited by held-out verifier noise.
**A3.6 Budget and compute.** Cap USD 2 for this test, EC2 spot only, 36 cores, internal deadline 95 min, instance shutdown at 110 min. Code: `track_e/bw_psa.py`, `track_e/bw_eval.py`.

## Amendment A4 (2026-10-29 CDT): the percentile scale saturates; metric changed to generation-space z-scores; margin made relative; baselines re-tuned. Committed BEFORE any evaluation seed, control run or hybrid run and before any held-out number from a baseline was read.
**What was seen (tuning data only: seeds 100-102, train objectives, no held-out verifier, no evaluation seed).** Under the A1 scale (train verifier output = percentile against 3,000 natural CycPeptMPDB peptides), the best tuned baselines reached top-10 mean percentile 0.979-0.986 at B = 1,000 and even the floor method `random_lib` reached 0.886 (F1 tuning objective) to 0.944 (F2 tuning objective); at B = 500 the best baselines were already at 0.959-0.971 (curves recomputed from the stored tuning curves: B = 100 about 0.81-0.86, B = 200 about 0.90-0.93, B = 300 about 0.93-0.95). **Consequence:** any search of 1,000 draws from a rule-satisfying peptide generator lands in the top percentile of the natural reference, so (i) the metric cannot discriminate methods near the top, and (ii) the registered margin +0.03 normalised AUC-top-10 is unreachable because the best baseline has headroom of at most about 0.02. This is a design defect of the A1 scale, found before evaluation; it is not a result about any method.
**Change (all fixed now).** (1) Scale: every verifier returns a z-score against the GENERATION SPACE, z = (raw - mu_ref) / sd_ref, where mu_ref and sd_ref are computed from 1,500 random rule-satisfying draws of the library sampler (configs drawn uniformly from `baselines.LIB_CFGS`, Python random seed 20261029, same hard-rule set C). No clipping, so scores do not saturate. The metric is AUC-top-10 in these reference-SD units ('SD-AUC'). Stop and held-out verifiers use the same construction with their own raw scores. (2) Margin: relative, defined as 0.10 x (SD-AUC of the best baseline - SD-AUC of random_lib) in each family, computed from the baseline EVALUATION seeds (seeds 0-9) and committed in the baseline-results amendment (A5) before any hybrid run; rationale: a hybrid that closes less than a tenth of the gap that the best baseline closes over random search would not change which method we recommend. The earlier +0.03 is withdrawn. (3) Budget stays B = 1,000. (4) Re-tuning: the tuned configs obtained under the percentile scale were never committed or used (they were selected on differences of about 0.001 at the ceiling); all baselines are re-tuned under the new scale (cross-family, seeds 100-102, same grids), `tuned.json` is committed before evaluation. (5) The edit_ga / selfies_ga stall guards (bug fix, commit after the first tuning attempt; no baseline number had been read) stay.
**Not changed:** groups v2, verifier definitions and fitting sources, floors and their outcomes (A2/A3), the three-way independence rule, the frozen vocabulary, controls, seeds, the cap USD 4 for Track E (revised below).
**Declared consequences.** SD-AUC is not comparable with any percentile AUC reported earlier in this repository; the design document's 'normalised AUC-top-10' now means SD-AUC. The generation space is defined by our own library sampler, so SD units measure improvement over blind sampling from that sampler, not an absolute chemical quality.
**Cost record (estimate from instance launch / terminate times; no cost log; to be replaced by a table in A5).** Pre-flights and quality (two c5.4xlarge, one c5.9xlarge) about 0.6 h; first tuning c5.9xlarge 0.8 h; resumed tuning c5.9xlarge 0.3 h; replacement test c5.12xlarge 0.9 h; total about 2.6 instance-hours, about USD 1.7 at roughly USD 0.65 per hour. Re-tuning adds about 0.5 h. Track E cap USD 4 stands.

### A4b (2026-10-29 CDT, bug-fix disclosure; no baseline evaluation, control or hybrid number had been run or read)
During re-tuning under the A4 scale, four edit_ga runs on the F1 tuning objective did not finish after more than an hour: the first stall guard only fired when a generation produced zero new molecules, but the exhausted single-edit neighbourhood produced about one new molecule per generation (a trickle), so the loop never stalled and never reached the budget. Fixes (committed before the rerun): (1) edit_ga counts a generation as stalled when it yields fewer than 10% new molecules (n_children // 10) and escalates the number of edits per child; 20 consecutive stalled generations stop the run; (2) a UNIFORM wall-clock cap of 1,800 s per run for every method (`pmo_harness.run_one(max_seconds=...)`, new unit test); a run that hits the cap stops, and the curve is padded with its last top-10 value as for any early stop; (3) all edit_ga tuning results (produced under the two earlier guards) are discarded and redone; the other methods' tuning results from the same re-tuning job are kept (none of them reached 1,800 s). The tuned-configuration comparison for edit_ga uses only runs under the final code. This is the third defect found in the same baseline; it was caught by watching wall time, not by reading scores.

### A4c (2026-10-29 CDT, coordinator request on A4b): wall-clock cap policy, truncation reporting, hybrid parity. Committed BEFORE any evaluation seed, control run or hybrid run; no baseline evaluation number had been read.
1. **Budget = oracle calls only.** The comparison budget between all methods (baselines and every hybrid arm) is B = 1,000 train-verifier calls. Wall-clock time is NOT part of the comparison. The wall-clock cap is a safety stop with no scientific role.
2. **Cap values.** Tuning runs: 1,800 s (already applied in the A4/A4b tuning). Evaluation and control runs: 14,400 s (4 h), the same value for baselines and for every hybrid arm, so that it cannot bind first on the slower-per-call hybrid; the cap is raised, not removed, to avoid an unbounded spot instance. If a hybrid run is still truncated at 4 h it is reported as truncated, not hidden.
3. **Recording.** Every run now records `stop_reason` in {budget_spent, wallclock, optimizer_stalled, proposal_cap, optimizer_returned, other}, `budget_fraction_used` and `optimizer_wall_s` (`pmo_harness.run_one`, unit test). For the tuning runs produced before this field existed, a run is classified as truncated if calls < 1,000 and its recorded wall time is >= 1,790 s; a run with calls < 1,000 and a shorter wall time is classified as a self-stop (stall / returned). The baseline-results amendment will contain, per family x method x phase (tuning, evaluation, controls): runs, runs truncated, mean budget fraction used at truncation, runs that self-stopped (stalled / returned) and mean budget fraction used at self-stop.
4. **Rule for truncated and self-stopped runs.** Primary analysis: AUC-top-10 on the calls actually used, padded with the last top-10 value to the budget length exactly as the harness pads any early stop (so a truncated or stalled method is credited with what it found and nothing more). Self-stops (optimizer_stalled) are a method behaviour and stay in the primary analysis. Sensitivity (reported next to the primary numbers): (a) truncated methods excluded; (b) truncated runs re-run with a 4x larger cap on all affected seeds if there are at most 10 of them, otherwise on a random half of them.
5. **Margin derivation (A4/A5) uses untruncated baseline curves only** (`margin_derivation.py` drops runs with stop_reason wallclock or inferred truncation and reports the counts); if no untruncated run exists for a method and config, that config is not eligible for the best-baseline reference.
6. **Hybrid parity.** The hybrid arms get the same 14,400 s cap as the baselines in evaluation; their cost in wall-clock and GPU-hours is reported as a separate efficiency column (not in the gate), because a method that wins only with far more compute per oracle call is a different claim from sample efficiency.

**Margin derivation (rule: the smallest effect that changes a decision).** Margin = f x G with G = SD-AUC(best baseline) - SD-AUC(random_lib) in the evaluation family; the evaluation-seed values of G are computed after the baseline evaluation and committed before any hybrid run (A6). f is fixed NOW at **0.10**. Derivation from the tuning curves (untruncated runs; seeds 100-102): the SD-AUC is a running mean of the top-10 curve, so the margin can be expressed as 'how much of its budget the best baseline spends to gain that much': for the F1 tuning objective G = 1.72, f = 0.10 gives 0.172 SD-AUC, equal to what the best baseline gains between 900 and 1,000 calls (10% of the budget); for the F2 tuning objective G = 3.25, 0.325 SD-AUC equals the best baseline's gain between 715 and 1,000 calls (28.5%). So f = 0.10 means: a hybrid arm must beat the best baseline by at least what that baseline gains from the last 10 to 29 percent of its budget, and with the interval's lower bound above 0. Why this is a decision-relevant size and not a convenience: the hybrid costs a 1B-parameter model, GPU time and engineering per oracle call; unless it saves at least on the order of a tenth of the oracle calls (the cost saved must exceed the added compute) there is no reason to recommend it over a tuned GA. This is a judgement, stated as such; the sensitivity of the verdict to f is reported, not hidden: with f = 0.05 the margin corresponds to 5-17% of the budget, with f = 0.20 to 20-44% (computed values in the derivation file). The P(pass) sensitivity table at f in {0.05, 0.10, 0.20} and lambda_H in {0.229, 0.10, 0.0} (A3.4) is filled in A6 from the evaluation-seed variances; the fraction f = 0.10 does not change after seeing it.
