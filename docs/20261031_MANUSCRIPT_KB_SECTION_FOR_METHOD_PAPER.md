## X. Knowledge layer

### X.1 Why a separate, evidence-linked layer

The generation system uses knowledge in three ways: as hard constraints on what may be generated, as soft priors that bias generation, and as retrieved context in the prompt. Each use needs statements that are structured, attributable and checkable. We surveyed existing collections before building one (KB-paper, Section 2). The entity and measurement layers are covered by curated resources: CycPeptMPDB (7,334 peptides in its paper; permeability from 45 papers and 2 patents) `[LIT-VERIFIED:abstract]`, CREMP (36,198 macrocyclic peptides with about 31.3 million conformers, plus a 3,258-peptide permeability subset) `[LIT-VERIFIED:full]`, CyclicPepedia (8,744 cyclic peptides) `[LIT-VERIFIED:abstract]`, Macrocycle-DB `[LIT-VERIFIED:abstract]`, and the peptide-complex databases PepBDB, Propedia v2.3 (more than 49,300 complexes) and BioLiP2 (about 4% peptide ligands among 781,684 interactions) `[LIT-VERIFIED:full]`. The permeability rules most used in practice (beyond-rule-of-5 guidelines, intramolecular hydrogen bonding, N-methylation) are stated in prose in review and primary articles. In the records we could open, we did not find structured design rules with provenance, a campaign and benchmark ledger, cyclic-specific complex annotation, or stability and solubility collections for cyclic peptides (KB-paper, Section 3). Counts in papers and downloaded files do not always agree. The live CycPeptMPDB file has 8,466 rows but the same 7,988 unique structures as our local table (the extra rows are repeated measurements), against 7,334 peptides in its paper; the SweMacrocycleDB file has 6,585 rows and 4,696 unique structures against 5,638 datapoints and 4,216 macrocycles in its paper; and for Macrocycle-DB the abstract reports 45,525 macrocycles, the downloaded `All.csv` has 50,654 rows (47,966 unique structures) and the site's statistics endpoint reports 18,969 macrocycles, with what each counts not documented in the files checked. We therefore quote counts from the file used and record its download date and hash.

Our own earlier knowledge bases were thin for this purpose: an internal audit found that the 16 target pharmacophore entries reduced to two distinct statements, that peptide-relevant content in the general bases amounted to four modality statements, that 3 of 57 citations could not be confirmed, and that the 16 targets map to 12 of the 793 benchmark targets (KB-paper, Section 2.4).

### X.2 Schema, tiers and verification

Each entry carries a statement, a direction (plus, minus, multi, window, context, caution or conflict), a scope, the outcome concerned, an evidence tier, a source (PMCID, DOI, journal, year, URL), a verbatim quote of at most 30 words, a confidence level, an access date and the extractor. Tiers: T1 primary quantitative study; T2 review or author background statement; T3 secondary source; T-internal our own campaign documents; P patent-derived statements (workstream K4, kept separate because their quality and verification differ) `[PENDING:K4]`. An entry may carry a weight only if its tier is T1 or T2, its kind is rule or fundamental, its quote is verified, its direction is weighted, and it maps to a feature computable from two-dimensional structure (TPSA, HBD, HBA, MW, cLogP, aLogP, rotatable bonds, heavy atoms, N-methyl count, D-residue count, cyclic flag). Everything else is kept at weight zero for retrieval context. Verification is deterministic: after whitespace normalisation the quote must be an exact substring of the stored full text of the cited paper, and rules that fail are discarded and never repaired. This guarantees attribution, not correct reading; correctness is measured by judged precision on an audit sample `[PENDING:K2]`.

### X.3 Construction

Five content sources feed the layer (KB-paper, Section 5). (i) Imports of existing collections, joined on stereo-aware InChIKey or PDB identifier. All 23 files were fetched and 22 tables parsed with no file-level failure; the join control passed (3,256 of the 3,258 peptides of the CREMP permeability subset matched the local CycPeptMPDB table on the full key, 3,258 on the connectivity block). Overlap with CycPeptMPDB is small for the other resources (SweMacrocycleDB 154 of 4,696 unique structures; Macrocycle-DB 240 of 47,966; CyclicPepedia 53 of 6,617), so they add chemistry and metadata and are not an independent check on the same peptides. Normalisation of assay values and the join of CyclicPepedia metadata to structures remain `[PENDING:K2]`. (ii) A hand-verified gold set: 48 entries from 19 papers (38 T1, 10 T2; 21 weight-eligible; all 48 quotes machine-verified; sha256 `5f6843f8...f5e9`), covering beyond-rule-of-5 windows, intramolecular hydrogen bonds, N-methylation, size and lipophilicity effects, solubility and aggregation, and data cautions; the target size is at least 60 entries, and the statements, written by an AI assistant, have not yet been independently judged against their quotes `[PENDING:K]`. (iii) Literature mining at scale: an open-weight 8-billion-parameter model over open-access full text with the same exact-quote check, 40 queries over four topics, acceptance thresholds fixed in advance (judged precision at least 0.80 on a 100-rule sample; scaling only if the pilot projects at least 60% of coverage cells filled) `[PENDING:K2]`. (iv) Patent mining as a separate tier P, with extraction only (no freedom-to-operate, claim-scope or legal assessment). The pilot (pre-registered before extraction) selects 200 patents from SureChEMBL by macrocyclic-peptide compound density (120 top-ranked and 80 random from the next 2,000), retrieves full text, extracts example compounds, SAR and permeability statements with the open-weight model, and keeps only items whose quote matches the retrieved text exactly and whose values and structures pass the checks; scale-up is recommended only if audited precision is at least 0.80, structure parse success at least 0.60 and at least 20 patents contribute verified examples. Overlap and novelty are measured against the BindingDB patent subset, the two patents in CycPeptMPDB and SureChEMBL membership. That existing collections cover only part of the patent literature is a hypothesis to be tested, not assumed `[PENDING:K4]`. (v) A structure layer from protein-cyclic-peptide complexes: cyclisation re-detected from coordinates, D residues and N-methylation detected geometrically, descriptive turn classes, interface features, and one target card for each of 16 targets with an explicit flag where no cyclic-peptide complex exists `[PENDING:K3]`.

### X.4 Leak guard for held-out evaluation

Knowledge derived from labelled evaluation data must not enter a prior used with that evaluation. The permeability sources are split into a train group (2020_Townsend and 2021_Kelly; 4,399 peptides, 64.84% of 6,784), a stop group (nine sources, 1,269 peptides) and a held-out group (nine sources, 1,116 peptides); membership sha256 `be8a9945...76db1c` in `allatom_paper/track_e/source_groups_v2.json`. The guard `assert_priors_clean` refuses a prior mined from sources that overlap the sources on which a stop or held-out verifier is fitted; the earlier all-laboratory rule file fails it, as a control confirms. Scaled extraction additionally flags a rule when its source paper matches a CycPeptMPDB source by first author and year. Re-mining the data-derived rule candidates on the train group alone gave no validated rule (the unmodified criteria need at least three sources; the group has two), although the within-source directions agreed across the two laboratories and with the textbook directions (TPSA -0.470 and -0.485, HBD -0.439 and -0.527, logP +0.287 and +0.488; N-methyl addition +0.623 and +0.483 mean within-source z effect; pooled +0.554, interval 0.373 to 0.735, I-squared 0.95). We therefore use the data only to check the direction of literature rules, not to set their weights.

### X.5 Use in the generator

Hard rules (ring closure, ring size, N-methyl window, molecular-weight bound, structural-alert prefixes) are not data-mined; they are compiled into a token-level constraint for generation. Soft priors are the weight-eligible literature rules, with weights taken from the effect size stated in the source or from a uniform prior and tuned on the train group only (`KnowledgePotential`: directional rules contribute weight times sign times the standardised feature; window rules contribute hinge penalties on the stated thresholds). Retrieval context combines the nearest known peptides (stereo-aware similarity, with measured values) with provenance-tagged rule snippets and, where a target card exists, its pharmacophore text; a blind rule for recovery tests rejects excluded documents, answer-set structures and later-year records, with controls that fail when the rule is violated (on the integrin set the store holds 4,634 exact-value rows, 2,826 isomers and 230 documents from 1991 to 2025). We compare three configurations: hard rules only, literature priors, and the data-descriptive directions as an ablation. The comparison and its confidence intervals are reported in Section Y `[PENDING:method manuscript]`.

### X.6 Limitations of the layer

Open-access text only; lossy table extraction; an 8-billion-parameter extractor can misread direction even with an exact quote; the gold set is small and permeability-heavy at this version; rules from papers whose data are in CycPeptMPDB are flagged, not removed; heterogeneity across assays and laboratories is recorded but not removed (an earlier flagship rule, N-methylation, was largely a between-laboratory effect); the train group is two laboratories and the stop and held-out groups are each dominated by one (CHUGAI about 69% of the stop group, 2016_Furukawa about 59% of the held-out group). We claim only an integrated, evidence-linked layer, subject to a deeper literature check.
