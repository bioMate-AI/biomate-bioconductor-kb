# Knowledge layer

This section summarises the knowledge layer; the full account, with the survey of existing collections and all construction checks, is in the knowledge-base manuscript. The numbers below are the same as in that manuscript.

## X.1 Layer and pipeline

We use an integrated, provenance-linked knowledge layer for cyclic-peptide design that joins structure-property, target-structure and prior-work knowledge with evidence tiers, built by a pipeline in which every extracted statement is verified by exact-quote matching against the retrieved source. The generator uses the layer in three ways: as hard constraints, as soft priors and as retrieved context. A survey of existing collections shows that the entity and measurement layers are covered by curated resources (CycPeptMPDB, CREMP, CyclicPepedia, Macrocycle-DB, and the peptide-complex databases PepBDB, Propedia v2.3 and BioLiP2), while we found no structured design rules with provenance, no campaign and benchmark ledger, no cyclic-specific complex annotation and no stability or solubility collection for cyclic peptides in the records we could open.

Each entry carries a statement, direction, scope, outcome, evidence tier, source, a verbatim quote of at most 30 words, a confidence level, an access date and the extractor. Tiers follow the provenance of the quoted statement: T1 reports a result or method from the source's own experiment, simulation or data analysis; T2 is a review's summary of other work, a restatement of a cited result, a textbook rule, authors' background or a project goal, and the default when the stored text cannot show that a number is the source's own; T-internal is our own campaign documents; P is patent-derived. Verification is deterministic: after whitespace normalisation the quote must be an exact substring of the stored full text, and entries that fail are discarded and never repaired. This establishes attribution and not that a statement is correctly read.

## X.2 Content

The layer has five content sources. (i) Imports: 23 files from existing collections were fetched and 22 tables parsed with no file-level failure; the join control passed (3,256 of the 3,258 peptides of the CREMP permeability subset matched the local CycPeptMPDB table on the full stereo-aware InChIKey, 3,258 on the connectivity block), and overlap with CycPeptMPDB is small for the other resources, so they add chemistry and metadata. (ii) A hand-verified gold set of 107 entries (44 rules, 24 fundamentals, 11 cautions and 28 campaign cards; 60 at T1, 36 at T2 and 11 internal) from 45 external sources and 5 internal documents; all 107 quotes verify verbatim and 32 entries are weight-eligible (14 T1, 18 T2). The 17 external and 11 internal campaign cards record what was designed, the outcome, failure modes and lessons (for example, BindCraft peptides gave no detectable binding for PD-1/PD-L1 while MDM2 gave 7 of 15). (iii) Literature extraction and (iv) patent extraction use an open-weight 8-billion-parameter model with the same exact-quote check; patent-derived statements form tier P, and the patent stage performs knowledge extraction only, with no freedom-to-operate assessment. (v) A structure layer: candidates from the BioLiP2 peptide-ligand class (41,544 lines in 30,644 groups) united with an earlier coordinate-based miner give 30,773 candidates, of which 28,789 parsed; after quality control the library holds 888 cyclic-peptide complexes over 451 targets (disulfide 366, head-to-tail 187, amide-like 140, C-C link 78, thioether 69, ester-like 48; 527 with non-standard residues, 226 with a D residue, 118 with an N-methyl, 134 with a cis amide). Only 218 of the earlier miner's 776 rows survive quality control. Bound-conformation priors computed on 802 complexes over 378 targets are tested by leave-one-target-out: ring spans by closure type, turn presence, position rates and backbone NH fate are stable, N-methylation is about twice as frequent at contact positions (4.7% against 2.2%), and interface-residue enrichment is unstable and exported at weight 0. Target cards for 16 targets plus GRPR show cyclic-peptide complexes for PD-L1 (9; best template 5O45), BAFF, FcRn, IL-17A and TNF-alpha, and none for 11 targets including alphavbeta6 and GRPR.

## X.3 Weights, direction check and leak guard

Only entries of tier T1 or T2, kind rule or fundamental, with a verified quote, a weighted direction and a computable feature may carry a weight. Weights are an untuned uniform prior (0.10 scaled by confidence), and no label from our permeability data creates or weights an entry. The permeability sources are split into a train group (2020_Townsend and 2021_Kelly; 4,399 peptides, 64.84% of 6,784), a stop group (nine sources, 1,269 peptides) and a held-out group (nine sources, 1,116 peptides); anything data-derived is mined on the train group only, and a prior-cleanliness check refuses a prior mined from sources that overlap the sources of a stop or held-out verifier (an earlier all-laboratory rule file fails it, as a control confirms).

The direction of each testable entry was checked against the train group only (within-source Spearman correlation, cluster bootstrap over flat-structure groups, descriptive). Over the 107 entries, 17 agree in direction, 7 are mixed, none disagree and 83 are not testable; of the 32 weight-eligible entries, 15 agree, 7 are mixed and 10 are not testable. N-methyl count (rho +0.50 and +0.53 in the two sources), HBD (-0.44 and -0.53), TPSA (-0.47 and -0.49) and cLogP (+0.29 and +0.49) agree in both sources. Molecular weight changes sign between the sources (-0.12 and +0.48), rotatable bonds are mixed and D-residue count shows no signal, so the size and D-residue terms receive weight zero. Rule candidates mined from our own labels on the train group alone reached no validated status (the criteria need at least three sources and the group has two), so the data check the direction of literature rules and do not set their weights.

## X.4 Use in the generator

Hard rules (ring closure, ring size, N-methyl window, molecular-weight bound, structural-alert prefixes) are not data-mined and are compiled into a token-level constraint. Soft priors are the weight-eligible literature rules whose direction agreed in both train laboratories (15 rules), with the uniform weights above: directional rules contribute weight times sign times the standardised feature, and window rules contribute hinge penalties on their stated thresholds. Retrieval context combines the nearest known peptides (stereo-aware similarity, with measured values) with provenance-tagged rule snippets and, where a target card exists, its pharmacophore text; a blind rule for recovery tests rejects excluded documents, answer-set structures and later-year records (on the integrin set the store holds 4,634 exact-value rows, 2,826 isomers and 230 documents from 1991 to 2025). We compare three configurations: hard rules only, literature priors, and the data-descriptive directions as an ablation.

## X.5 Limitations

The gold-set statements were written by an AI assistant with only the quotes machine-verified; for the 36 T2 entries the studies behind the quoted statements were not opened; the direction check uses two sources, one of which dominates, with no multiplicity control; the train group is two laboratories and the stop and held-out groups are each dominated by one (CHUGAI about 69% of the stop group, 2016_Furukawa about 59% of the held-out group); the structure layer is bounded by BioLiP2's peptide class and the earlier miner, carries no affinity annotations and is dominated by crystallisable, disulfide-rich binders; the literature and patent stages are protocols whose measurements are not reported; and the survey's search was limited, so absence of a record is not proof of absence.
