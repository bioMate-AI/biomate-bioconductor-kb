# Knowledge layer

## X.0 Overview

We introduce an integrated, provenance-linked knowledge layer for cyclic-peptide design that joins structure-property, target-structure and prior-work knowledge with evidence tiers, and we build it with a pipeline in which every extracted statement is verified by exact-quote matching against the retrieved source, including a separate tier for patent-derived statements. The imports, gold set, structure mining, leak guards and loaders described below are parts of this layer and pipeline. The full account, with the survey and the construction checks, is given in the knowledge-base manuscript.

## X.1 Why a separate, evidence-linked layer

The generation system uses knowledge in three ways: as hard constraints on what may be generated, as soft priors that bias generation, and as retrieved context in the prompt. Each use needs statements that are structured, attributable and checkable. A survey of existing collections shows that the entity and measurement layers are covered by curated resources: CycPeptMPDB (7,334 peptides in its paper, with permeability from 45 papers and 2 patents), CREMP (36,198 macrocyclic peptides with about 31.3 million conformers, plus a 3,258-peptide permeability subset), CyclicPepedia (8,744 cyclic peptides), Macrocycle-DB, and the peptide-complex databases PepBDB, Propedia v2.3 (more than 49,300 complexes) and BioLiP2 (peptide ligands about 4% of 781,684 interactions). The permeability rules most used in practice (beyond-rule-of-5 guidelines, intramolecular hydrogen bonding, N-methylation) are stated in prose in review and primary articles. In the records we could open, we found no structured design rules with provenance, no campaign and benchmark ledger, no cyclic-specific complex annotation and no stability or solubility collection for cyclic peptides. Counts in papers and downloaded files do not always agree: the CycPeptMPDB file has 8,466 rows but the same 7,988 unique structures as our local table, because the additional rows are repeated measurements; the SweMacrocycleDB file has 6,585 rows and 4,696 unique structures against 5,638 datapoints and 4,216 macrocycles in its paper; and for Macrocycle-DB the abstract reports 45,525 macrocycles, the downloaded `All.csv` has 50,654 rows (47,966 unique structures) and the site's statistics endpoint reports 18,969 macrocycles. We therefore quote counts from the file used and record its download date and hash.

Our own earlier knowledge bases were thin for this purpose: an internal audit found that the 16 target pharmacophore entries reduced to two distinct statements, that peptide-relevant content in the general bases amounted to four modality statements, that 3 of 57 citations could not be confirmed, and that the 16 targets map to 12 of the 793 benchmark targets.

## X.2 Schema, tiers and verification

Each entry carries a statement, a direction (plus, minus, multi, window, context, caution or conflict), a scope, the outcome concerned, an evidence tier, a source (PMCID, DOI, journal, year, URL), a verbatim quote of at most 30 words, a confidence level, an access date and the extractor. The kinds are rule, fundamental, caution and card. Tiers follow the provenance of the quoted statement and not the article type: T1 the quote reports a result or method from the source's own experiment, simulation, benchmark or data analysis; T2 a review's summary of other work, a restatement of a result the source cites to another paper, a textbook rule, authors' background or a project goal, and the default when the stored text cannot show that a number is the source's own; T3 secondary source; T-internal our own campaign documents; P patent-derived statements, kept separate because their quality and verification differ. An entry may carry a weight only if its tier is T1 or T2, its kind is rule or fundamental, its quote is verified, its direction is weighted, and it maps to a feature computable from two-dimensional structure (TPSA, HBD, HBA, MW, cLogP, aLogP, rotatable bonds, heavy atoms, N-methyl count, D-residue count, cyclic flag). Eligibility does not distinguish T1 from T2, so weighting only T1 statements is a filter on the tier field. Weights are a uniform prior (0.10 scaled by confidence); no label from our permeability data creates or weights an entry. Everything else is kept at weight zero for retrieval context. Verification is deterministic: after whitespace normalisation the quote must be an exact substring of the stored full text of the cited source, and entries that fail are discarded and never repaired. This establishes attribution and not correct reading; correctness is measured by judged precision on an audit sample.

## X.3 Construction

Five content sources feed the layer. (i) Imports of existing collections, joined on stereo-aware InChIKey or PDB identifier. All 23 files were fetched and 22 tables parsed with no file-level failure; the join control passed (3,256 of the 3,258 peptides of the CREMP permeability subset matched the local CycPeptMPDB table on the full key, 3,258 on the connectivity block). Overlap with CycPeptMPDB is small for the other resources (SweMacrocycleDB 154 of 4,696 unique structures; Macrocycle-DB 240 of 47,966; CyclicPepedia 53 of 6,617), so they add chemistry and metadata and are not an independent check on the same peptides. (ii) A hand-verified gold set of 107 entries (44 rules, 24 fundamentals, 11 cautions, 28 campaign cards; 60 at T1, 36 at T2, 11 internal) from 45 external sources (41 Europe PMC open-access articles, 4 arXiv preprints) and 5 internal documents, with all 107 quotes verified verbatim and 32 entries weight-eligible (14 T1, 18 T2). The campaign ledger comprises 17 external cards (for example RFpeptides, AfCycDesign, BindCraft, PepINVENT, PepGLAD, PepTune, romidepsin) and 11 internal cards recording our own earlier campaigns; it makes explicit that hit rates are reported under incompatible definitions, that design papers rarely report non-binders (BindCraft peptides gave no detectable binding for PD-1/PD-L1 while MDM2 gave 7 of 15), and that stability engineering and permeability-raising N-methylation can cost potency or selectivity. (iii) Literature mining at scale: an open-weight 8-billion-parameter model over open-access full text with the same exact-quote check, 40 queries over four topics, and criteria fixed in advance (judged precision of at least 0.80 on a 100-rule sample; scaling only if at least 60% of coverage cells are projected filled). (iv) Patent mining as a separate tier P, with extraction only (no freedom-to-operate, claim-scope or legal assessment). The stage selects 200 patents from SureChEMBL by macrocyclic-peptide compound density (120 top-ranked and 80 random from the next 2,000), retrieves full text, extracts example compounds, SAR and permeability statements with the open-weight model, and keeps only items whose quote matches the retrieved text exactly and whose values and structures pass the checks. Overlap and novelty are measured against the BindingDB patent subset, the two patents in CycPeptMPDB and SureChEMBL membership. (v) A structure layer from protein-cyclic-peptide complexes: cyclisation re-detected from coordinates, D residues and N-methylation detected geometrically, descriptive turn classes, interface features, and one target card for each of 16 targets with an explicit flag where no cyclic-peptide complex exists.

## X.4 Leak guard and direction check

Knowledge derived from labelled evaluation data must not enter a prior used with that evaluation. The permeability sources are split into a train group (2020_Townsend and 2021_Kelly; 4,399 peptides, 64.84% of 6,784), a stop group (nine sources, 1,269 peptides) and a held-out group (nine sources, 1,116 peptides). A prior-cleanliness check refuses a prior mined from sources that overlap the sources on which a stop or held-out verifier is fitted; an earlier all-laboratory rule file fails it, as a control confirms. Scaled extraction additionally flags a rule when its source paper matches a CycPeptMPDB source by first author and year.

The direction of every testable literature entry is checked against the train group only (within-source Spearman correlation of the computable feature with PAMPA, cluster bootstrap over flat-structure groups, descriptive). Of 107 entries, 17 agree in direction, 7 are mixed, none disagree, and 83 are not testable. N-methyl count (rho +0.50 and +0.53 in the two sources), HBD (-0.44 and -0.53), TPSA (-0.47 and -0.49) and cLogP (+0.29 and +0.49) agree in both sources; the lipophilicity peak agrees in both. Molecular weight changes sign between the two sources (-0.12 and +0.48), rotatable bonds are mixed and D-residue count shows no signal, so those rules are not weighted. Rule candidates mined from our own labels on the train group alone reached no validated status (the criteria need at least three sources and the group has two), although their within-source directions were consistent across the two laboratories and with the literature; the data therefore check the direction of literature rules and do not set their weights.

## X.5 Use in the generator

Hard rules (ring closure, ring size, N-methyl window, molecular-weight bound, structural-alert prefixes) are not data-mined; they are compiled into a token-level constraint for generation. Soft priors are the weight-eligible literature rules, with weights taken from the effect size stated in the source or from the uniform prior and tuned on the train group only (directional rules contribute weight times sign times the standardised feature; window rules contribute hinge penalties on the stated thresholds). Retrieval context combines the nearest known peptides (stereo-aware similarity, with measured values) with provenance-tagged rule snippets and, where a target card exists, its pharmacophore text; a blind rule for recovery tests rejects excluded documents, answer-set structures and later-year records, with controls that fail when the rule is violated (on the integrin set the store holds 4,634 exact-value rows, 2,826 isomers and 230 documents from 1991 to 2025). We compare three configurations: hard rules only, literature priors, and the data-descriptive directions as an ablation. The comparison and its confidence intervals are reported in Section Y.

## X.6 Limitations of the layer

Open-access text only; lossy table extraction; an 8-billion-parameter extractor can misread direction even with an exact quote; the gold set is permeability-heavy, and its statements were written by an AI assistant with only the quotes machine-verified, so statement-to-quote fidelity has not been judged by a person; for the 36 T2 entries the studies behind the quoted statements were not opened and each statement is attributed to the source that states it; weights are an untuned uniform prior; the direction check uses two sources, one of which dominates, with a cluster bootstrap by flat structure and no multiplicity control; rules from papers whose data are in CycPeptMPDB are flagged, not removed; heterogeneity across assays and laboratories is recorded but not removed (an earlier flagship rule, N-methylation, was largely a between-laboratory effect); the train group is two laboratories and the stop and held-out groups are each dominated by one (CHUGAI about 69% of the stop group, 2016_Furukawa about 59% of the held-out group); and the survey's search was limited, so absence of a record is not proof of absence. The comparison with other knowledge bases is by content type and counts, not by head-to-head use in a downstream task.
