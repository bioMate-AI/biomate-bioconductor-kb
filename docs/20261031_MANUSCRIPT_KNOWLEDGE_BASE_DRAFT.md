# A provenance-linked knowledge layer for cyclic-peptide design: survey of existing collections, gap analysis and construction

## Abstract

Design systems for cyclic peptides, including language-model-based generators, need machine-usable knowledge: how structure relates to permeability, stability and solubility; how cyclic peptides bind their targets; and what earlier design campaigns achieved. We introduce an integrated, provenance-linked knowledge layer for cyclic-peptide design that joins structure-property, target-structure and prior-work knowledge under one schema, shared join keys and evidence tiers, and a construction pipeline in which every extracted statement is verified by deterministic exact-quote matching against the retrieved source text. Exact-quote matching establishes that a statement is attributable to a passage, not that it is a correct reading of it. The layer contains a hand-verified gold set of 107 entries (44 rules, 24 fundamentals, 11 cautions and 28 campaign cards from 45 external sources and 5 internal documents, every quote verified verbatim), 23 imported files from existing collections joined on a stereo-aware key, and a structure layer of 888 validated cyclic-peptide complexes over 451 targets with bound-conformation priors and target cards. The same verification is specified for literature and patent extraction, with patent-derived statements in a separate evidence tier. The layer carries leak guards for use under held-out evaluation and loaders that turn it into decoding priors and retrieval context. A survey of existing collections shows that curated resources cover the entity and measurement layers (for example a 7,334-peptide permeability database and peptide-protein complex databases with more than 49,300 complexes), while structured design rules with provenance, a campaign and benchmark ledger, cyclic-specific complex annotation, and stability and solubility collections were not found in the records we could open. Checked against two large permeability sources that were not used to create or weight any entry, 17 of the 107 entries agree in direction, 7 are mixed and none disagree (83 make no testable directional claim); N-methyl count, hydrogen-bond donor count, polar surface area and lipophilicity agree in both sources. A join control in which 3,256 of 3,258 peptides matched supports the shared stereo-aware key.

## 1. Introduction

Cyclic peptides and macrocycles occupy a chemical space between small molecules and biologics. Their permeability, stability and binding depend on conformation, intramolecular hydrogen bonding, backbone modification and stereochemistry in ways that two-dimensional descriptors capture only partly. A design system that proposes, filters and ranks candidates therefore needs more than a trained scorer: it needs rules and facts that can be inspected, cited and corrected.

Three kinds of knowledge matter. Structure-property knowledge covers how permeability, solubility, stability and conformation relate to chemistry (type A). Target-structure knowledge covers how cyclic peptides bind their targets, in which conformation and through which contacts (type B). Prior-work knowledge covers what earlier design and optimisation campaigns produced, including hit rates and failures (type C). A generative system uses the same knowledge in three ways: as hard constraints on what may be generated, as soft priors that bias generation, and as retrieved context in a prompt. Each use requires that the knowledge be structured, attributable to a source and checkable against it.

Before building, we surveyed what already exists (Section 2) and used the gaps it exposed to design the layer. We make two contributions.

1. **An integrated, provenance-linked knowledge layer for cyclic-peptide design.** The layer joins structure-property, target-structure and prior-work knowledge in one schema, with join keys (stereo-aware InChIKey, PDB identifier, DOI) that connect structure or sequence, measured property, conformation, target complex and design outcome, and with evidence tiers that determine what may influence a generator. Leak guards that keep knowledge derived from evaluation data out of any prior used with that evaluation, and loaders that turn the layer into decoding priors and retrieval context, are parts of the layer.
2. **A construction pipeline with deterministic exact-quote verification of every extracted statement.** A statement is kept only if its quote is an exact substring of the retrieved source text. We apply this to the 107-entry gold set (all quotes verified), to 23 imported files from existing collections (join control: 3,256 of 3,258 peptides matched) and to 28,789 parsed structure candidates (888 validated cyclic complexes); the same verification is specified for literature and patent extraction, with patent-derived statements in a separate tier (Sections 5.3 and 5.4).

We release the schema, builders, loaders and the verified gold set with hashes and access dates.

### 2.3 Rules and design literature

The permeability rules most used in practice are stated in prose in review and primary articles, read here at abstract level: oral druggable space beyond the rule of 5 and intramolecular hydrogen bonds (Doak et al., Chem Biol 2014, 10.1016/j.chembiol.2014.08.013); conformational determinants of macrocycle permeability across more than 200 compounds (Over et al., Nat Chem Biol 2016, 10.1038/nchembio.2203); lipophilic permeability efficiency (Naylor et al., J Med Chem 2018, 10.1021/acs.jmedchem.8b01259); on-resin N-methylation giving a hexapeptide with 28% rat oral bioavailability (White et al., Nat Chem Biol 2011, 10.1038/nchembio.664); amide-to-ester substitution as an alternative to N-methylation (Hosono et al., Nat Commun 2023, 10.1038/s41467-023-36978-z); and chameleonicity for ranking beyond-rule-of-5 compounds (Rossi Sebastiano et al., J Med Chem 2022, 10.1021/acs.jmedchem.2c00774).

Design and prior-work literature, also read at abstract level: RFpeptides (Rettie et al., Nat Chem Biol 2025, 10.1038/s41589-025-01929-w) reports at most 20 designs per target against four targets, all yielding medium-to-high affinity binders, one with Kd below 10 nM; AfCycDesign (bioRxiv 2023, 10.1101/2023.02.25.529956) predicts 36 of 49 native structures within 1.5 A and reports about 10,000 designs with seven validated within 1.0 A; HighFold (bioRxiv 2023, 10.1101/2023.08.27.554979) adapts AlphaFold for cyclic peptides. Several permeability-prediction papers model CycPeptMPDB with non-comparable splits (for example CPMP, R2 0.67 for PAMPA); the survey found no common benchmark.

### 2.4 Earlier knowledge bases of this project

The project's earlier knowledge bases held 73 distinct statements for 16 targets, of which the 16 per-target pharmacophore entries reduced to two distinct statements, and only four peptide-relevant modality statements in the general base; 3 of 57 citations could not be confirmed by a Crossref check, and the entries carried no ingestion date. A within-source validation found TPSA, HBD, logP and Veber pass consistent in direction within source but the N-methyl count rule inconclusive within source (pooled rho 0.37 falling to a median within-source rho of 0.01, 95% interval -0.13 to 0.28, 9 of 18 sources positive), which is why the schema of Section 4 records the assay and the number of supporting sources.

## 4. The knowledge layer

### 4.1 Schema

Entries are of four kinds: rule (a directional or windowed statement about an outcome), fundamental (a mechanistic statement), caution (a statement about data or method limits) and card (a structured summary of a target or campaign). Each entry carries an identifier, kind, statement, direction (plus, minus, multi, window, context, caution or conflict), scope, the outcome concerned, evidence tier, source (PMCID, DOI, title, journal, year, URL; for internal entries a file path), a verbatim quote of at most 30 words, a confidence level (high, medium, low), access date, extractor, weight and weight-eligibility flag, a quote-verification flag with the offset of the quote in the source text, an applicable target and a structure reference. Optional fields give effect size and units, windows with thresholds, and the computable feature to which the rule maps.

### 4.2 Evidence tiers

| Tier | Definition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | the quoted statement reports a result or method obtained in the source's own experiment, simulation, benchmark or data analysis | may carry weight |
| T2 | a review's summary of other work, a restatement of a result the source cites to another paper, a textbook rule, authors' background or motivation, or a project goal; also the default when the stored text cannot show that a number is the source's own | may carry weight |
| T3 | secondary source (encyclopaedic, vendor, aggregator) | retrieval context only |
| T-internal | our own campaign documents (file path as source) | retrieval context only |
| P | patent-derived statement | retrieval context |

The tier follows the provenance of the quoted statement and not the article type: a review that analyses its own data set yields T1 statements, and a research article that restates cited work yields T2 statements. Patent-derived content is kept in a separate tier because its quality, claim style and level of verification differ from journal content.

### 4.3 Weight eligibility and weights

An entry may carry a nonzero weight only if its tier is T1 or T2, its kind is rule or fundamental, its quote is verified, its direction is plus, minus, multi or window, and at least one computable feature of the molecule (TPSA, HBD, HBA, MW, cLogP, aLogP, rotatable bonds, heavy-atom count, N-methyl count, D-residue count, cyclic flag) is mapped to it. A rule whose natural feature is not computable from two-dimensional structure (for example exposed NH) may map to a proxy feature with its weight halved. Eligibility does not distinguish T1 from T2, so weighting only T1 statements is a filter on the tier field. Weights are an untuned uniform prior: 0.10 scaled by the confidence level (high 1.0, medium 0.5, low 0), and zero for conflicts, cautions and context entries; no label from our permeability data creates or weights an entry. In the literature-prior configuration used downstream, only the weight-eligible entries whose direction agreed in both train laboratories contribute (Section 6.1), and the size and D-residue terms receive weight zero. Eighteen unit tests cover the schema, provenance, quote verification against the stored text, that an unverified entry never carries a weight, the restriction of weights to T1 and T2 rules with computable features, the directional and window behaviour of the potential, the consistency of the tier audit table with the build, and build determinism.

### 4.4 Provenance and citation integrity

Every entry traces to a source that was opened. Full texts are fetched from Europe PMC (open-access XML), arXiv (PDF text) or the repository (internal documents), normalised and stored with a sha256. The builder checks every quote, and every additional quote on cards, as an exact substring of the stored text and sets the verification flag from that match. In the scaled pipeline the rule is the same and stricter in its details: after whitespace normalisation (single spaces, case preserved, nothing else) the quote must be an exact substring of the stored normalised source text, its length must be between 6 and 30 words, any numerical effect size must occur as a digit string in the quote or the passage, and required fields must be present with values in closed vocabularies. An entry failing any check is discarded and never repaired. The controls specified for the scaled pipeline are: 20 synthetic rules with fabricated quotes must all be rejected; 20 quotes with altered words, dropped words or changed numbers must be rejected while 20 quotes differing only in whitespace must be accepted; entries paired with a different paper's text must fail. For the gold set, a unit test plants a quote absent from the source and requires that it be detected. An exact quote establishes attribution and not that the statement is a correct reading of it.

### 4.5 Merge, support and contradiction

Equivalent rules (same property, substructure class and direction, and statement token overlap of at least 0.5 on content words) are merged. Support is the number of distinct DOIs. Opposite directions on the same property, substructure class and scope key from different papers set a contradiction flag; contradictions are flagged and never resolved by the pipeline.

### 4.6 Leak guards for held-out evaluation

Knowledge derived from labelled evaluation data must not enter a prior used with that evaluation. Three guards are in place. First, a prior-cleanliness check refuses a prior whose mining sources overlap the sources on which a stop or held-out verifier is fitted; an earlier all-laboratory rule file (mined from all 21 training sources) fails this check against the stop and held-out groups, and a control confirms this. The literature layer is clean by construction because no label is used to create or weight an entry. Second, scaled extraction sets a `possible_benchmark_source` flag when a source paper matches a CycPeptMPDB source name by first author and year, so such rules can be excluded from priors used with held-out verifiers. Third, the consuming system splits the permeability sources into train, stop and held-out groups (Section 7.1), and anything data-derived is mined on the train group only.

### 4.7 Loaders

The loader provides two outputs. A sequence-level log-linear potential is built from the weight-eligible rules: directional rules contribute weight times sign times the standardised feature, with the mean and spread supplied by the caller from the train group, and window rules contribute hinge penalties on the stated thresholds, with any-of and maximum-violation semantics for the guideline rules; permeability, stability and solubility rules are never summed into one potential. A second function returns short provenance-tagged snippets for retrieval-conditioned prompts, deterministically. Hard constraints (ring closure, ring size, N-methyl window, molecular-weight bound, structural-alert prefixes) are not data-mined and are compiled separately by the generation system.

## 5. Construction

The layer is built from five content sources that share the schema of Section 4.1 and the join keys stereo-aware InChIKey, PDB identifier and DOI.

### 5.1 Imports of existing collections

All 23 files were fetched with HTTP date, size and sha256 recorded, and 22 tables were parsed (the CyclicPepedia structure archive was fetched and not unpacked) with no file-level parse failure. The files are the CycPeptMPDB peptide and monomer tables; the SweMacrocycleDB overall table; the CyclicPepedia spreadsheets (13 tables) and structure archive; the CREMP permeability summary (the conformer archives of 8.3 GB and 11.6 GB are not imported); and the Macrocycle-DB static files (the 377 MB structure archive is not imported). Norine has no confirmed bulk route and is not imported. Each record is parsed into a canonical table (resource, record identifier, SMILES, full and flat stereo-aware InChIKey, assay, value, unit, source reference, DOI); failures are counted and not dropped; assay values are kept raw. Joins use the full 27-character RDKit InChIKey and its first block (connectivity only) against the local CycPeptMPDB annotation table (7,991 rows, 3,214 unique flat keys) and the live CycPeptMPDB file.

Join validation. The internal control is the CREMP permeability subset, which was built from CycPeptMPDB: 3,256 of its 3,258 peptides (99.94%) matched the local table on the full key and 3,258 of 3,258 on the flat key, and the live CycPeptMPDB file matches the local table on 8,466 of 8,466 rows. Structures without a parsable SMILES cannot be joined (CyclicPepedia 1,711 of 8,751 rows, 19.6%; Macrocycle-DB 2 rows); they are counted and kept in the canonical files. Overlap with CycPeptMPDB is small for every other resource, measured on unique full keys: SweMacrocycleDB 154 of 4,696 (3.3%), Macrocycle-DB `All.csv` 240 of 47,966 (0.5%), CyclicPepedia 53 of 6,617 (0.8%). The imports therefore add chemistry and metadata that CycPeptMPDB lacks; they are not an independent permeability check on the same peptides except for the 154 SweMacrocycleDB structures.

### 5.2 Hand-verified gold set and campaign ledger

The gold set holds 107 entries: 44 rules, 24 fundamentals, 11 cautions and 28 cards. By tier there are 60 at T1, 36 at T2 and 11 internal (no T3), resting on 45 external sources (41 Europe PMC open-access articles and 4 arXiv preprints) and 5 internal documents. Of the 36 T2 entries, 18 come from review or commentary sources and 18 from research articles; by the character of the quoted statement, 11 are a review's own wording, 16 restate a primary result cited to another paper, 6 are authors' background, 1 is a textbook rule, 1 a project goal and 1 is uncertain and defaulted to T2. All 107 quotes verify verbatim, with no schema problems. By outcome class, 55 entries concern permeability, 10 stability, 2 solubility and 40 other topics; 32 entries are weight-eligible (14 T1 and 18 T2; 28 permeability, 1 stability, 1 solubility, 2 other). Topic coverage, where an entry can count in several topics: cyclisation, ring size and bridging 34; size, beyond-rule-of-5 and oral guidelines 24; N-methylation 13; intramolecular hydrogen bonds and chameleonicity 12; lipophilicity, solubility and aggregation 12; stability (proteolysis, serum, deamidation) 12; stereochemistry and D-residues 10; campaign cards 28. Quantitative examples include the macrocycle drug window (HBD at most 7 together with MW below 1000 or cLogP above 2.5) and the non-monotonic dependence of permeability on lipophilicity; a data caution records that in one curated macrocycle permeability database 91% of the PAMPA records come from a single publication.

The campaign ledger is the 28 cards. Seventeen external cards record what was designed, the outcome, failure modes and lessons for campaigns and methods including RFpeptides, AfCycDesign, BindCraft (proteins and peptides), PepINVENT, HighFold-MeD, PepGLAD, PepTune, PepMLM, CP-Composer, CycDiff-DPO, two Cyclin A/B macrocycle campaigns, approved and clinical macrocycles, and romidepsin. Eleven internal cards record our own earlier campaigns and are included in the released knowledge file; documents that carry intellectual-property content are not indexed. The ledger makes several lessons explicit, each backed by a verified quote: hit rates are reported per target and under incompatible definitions (relative ipTM, Delta-G below 0, affinity, functional readout); peptide design papers rarely report non-binders; BindCraft peptides gave no detectable binding for PD-1/PD-L1 while MDM2 gave 7 of 15; the AfCycDesign pLDDT above 0.85 filter was right for 80% (44 of 55) of predictions; PepGLAD notes that even ground-truth peptides reach only 36% under AF2-consistency evaluation; removing a reducible disulfide for stability cost romidepsin 10-100-fold potency; and permeability-raising N-methylation cost cellular activity and selectivity in matched pairs.

### 5.3 Patent stage (protocol)

Medicinal-chemistry knowledge on macrocyclic peptides appears in patents as well as in journals. The patent stage extracts knowledge statements from public patent documents: example compounds with sequences or structures and activity values, assay conditions, SAR statements, permeability and ADME data, and binding-mode and design-rationale statements. It performs knowledge extraction only and makes no freedom-to-operate, claim-scope or validity assessment; unpublished or internal material is out of scope. Extracted statements carry the separate tier P (Section 4.2).

Candidate patents are identified from the SureChEMBL bulk snapshot of 2026-05-15 (compound, patent-compound map and patent tables of 3.9 to 5.5 GB) by compounds with molecular weight 500 to 2,600, a ring of at least 12 atoms and at least three amide bonds in or attached to the ring, mapped to patents published from 2000. The BindingDB patent subset and Google Patents pages supply curated SAR, an overlap reference and full text (the patent path permits automated retrieval; requests use a fixed delay and an identifying user agent). Other patent sources (PatentsView and USPTO bulk data, Espacenet, WIPO PATENTSCOPE, Lens.org, BigQuery public patents) need a token or a billed project, or were not reachable by automated access, and are not used. Cooperative Patent Classification codes C07K 7/50 to 7/66 for cyclic peptides were verified against the USPTO scheme page; A61K 38/12 could not be verified and is used only as a query hint. The corpus takes the 120 highest-ranked patents by number of distinct qualifying compounds plus 80 drawn at random (seed 20261031) from the next 2,000 ranked patents, 200 in total, to limit selection bias; patents whose retrieved page lacks a description are dropped and counted.

An open-weight 8-billion-parameter instruction model at temperature 0 processes passages matching example, table and SAR patterns and returns five item kinds (example compound, SAR statement, ADME or permeability, binding mode, design rationale), each with a verbatim quote of 6 to 40 words. A failing item is discarded: the quote must be an exact whitespace-normalised substring of the retrieved patent text; every numeric value and unit given as a field must occur in the quote or the passage; a structure string must occur verbatim and SMILES must parse in RDKit; and patent number, passage offset, kind code and publication date must be present. Examples with measured data are tagged `example` and claim text `claim`, because their reliability differs.

The stage reports patents retrieved, passages processed, items extracted, verified and rejected by reason, structure parse success, and precision from an audit of at least 50 kept items stratified by kind, with the sample file supplied so that the audit can be redrawn independently. Overlap and novelty are the share of kept example compounds, matched by stereo-aware InChIKey, that are absent from the BindingDB patent subset, from the two patent-derived entries in CycPeptMPDB and from SureChEMBL for the same patent. Scale-up requires audit precision of at least 0.80 (Wilson interval reported), structure parse success of at least 0.60 among example compounds with a structure string, and at least 20 patents contributing verified example compounds. Limits are loss of layout in HTML text, no mapping of IUPAC names to structures, English- and US-centric selection, and a selection by compound density that favours patents with large libraries.

### 5.4 Literature stage (protocol)

Europe PMC open-access full text (PMC XML) is retrieved by 40 topic queries covering four topics: SAR rules, structure-property fundamentals, prior-work campaigns, and target-structure statements for 16 targets. An open-weight 8-billion-parameter instruction model on a single 24 GB GPU, at temperature 0, processes passages of about 700 to 900 words after topic-keyword filtering in one pass and returns JSON in the schema fields. A second, independent prompt asks whether the quote supports the stated direction; it is reported as a proxy and not as precision, since it can share the model's blind spots. Tiers follow the source type (research article T1, review T2, other T3), verification follows Section 4.4 and merging follows Section 4.5.

The stage is specified with these criteria: a raw-output quote-match rate below 40% makes it inconclusive for the model; precision is judged on a sample of 100 surviving rules (fixed seed, stratified by topic and tier) and must be at least 0.80; coverage is measured on a property by substructure matrix, where a cell is filled with at least three verified rules from at least three papers including at least one T1 or T2 source, and scaling requires a projection of at least 60% of cells filled; recall against the gold set is reported without a threshold, because few gold papers fall in the corpus.

### 5.5 Structure knowledge layer

Sources and own parsing. Candidate complexes come from the BioLiP2 flat file (41,544 peptide-ligand lines in 30,644 groups; the parse was validated on all 989,058 lines, each having at least 17 columns and a valid PDB identifier) united with the candidate list of our earlier coordinate-based miner (776 rows). The union has 30,773 candidates: 129 from the miner only, 647 in both and 29,997 from BioLiP2 only. Entry metadata come from the RCSB data API and coordinates from the RCSB mmCIF files. The structural annotation is our own parsing and is independent of any resource's label: cyclisation is detected from coordinates by distance tests and classified as disulfide, head-to-tail, amide-like (lactam), thioether, ester-like or C-C link with the ring span in residues; D residues are detected geometrically (sign of the C-alpha chirality volume against the receptor's L-amino-acid majority) and N-methylation geometrically (a backbone-nitrogen carbon with no other heavy neighbour), without assuming chemical component dictionary codes; backbone turns and cis amides are classified with fixed textbook windows as descriptive classes; interface features (contacts, hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, buried surface, hotspots) and the fate of backbone NH groups are computed on the deposited coordinates; and a covalent-link filter removes peptide chains covalently bonded to their receptor chain. PepBDB and Propedia were not imported: both derive from the same PDB source as BioLiP2 and carry no cyclic-specific annotation, which is a scoping decision and not a verified equivalence. Twenty-three unit tests cover surface-area checks, ten hand-checked complexes, determinism, the covalent filter, the priors and exclusion mechanics, the card flags and an end-to-end regression.

Library. Of the 30,773 candidates, 28,789 parsed; the rest have a peptide chain of more than 40 residues, an mmCIF file above 12 MB, fewer than 3 residues or no receptor chain. After quality control and de-duplication (best resolution per target, sequence and closure class) the library holds 888 cyclic-peptide complexes over 451 receptor targets (target = the set of UniProt accessions of the receptor chains), of which 802 have a non-antibody receptor (the primary set) and 86 an antibody-like receptor (epitope-peptide complexes, kept and flagged). Ninety per cent of parsed peptide-ligand rows are linear. Of the earlier miner's 776 rows only 218 (28%) remain; the rest included insulin A chains disulfide-bonded to their B chains, peptides of 3 to 4 residues and chain-broken fragments, and its side-chain-to-backbone label was unreliable (4 of 4 sampled rows showed no closure on re-detection). Bound-conformation priors are computed on the primary set (802 complexes, 378 targets; target-set hash `419f8bf3d8dc7334`), and stability is tested by leave-one-target-out: a prior is stable if it has at least 20 targets, a median deviation of at most 0.05 and a maximum of at most 0.25 (total-variation distance for categorical priors, maximum absolute change for rates; thresholds fixed before the run). Ring-span priors by closure type (disulfide n = 301, head-to-tail 174, amide-like 137, thioether 67), beta-turn presence by closure type, per-position rates (n = 10,443) and the fate of backbone NH groups (n = 8,909) are stable, with median deviation 0.0 to 0.0003 and maximum 0.0778 or less. Interface-residue enrichment (n = 8,494) is not stable (maximum deviation 0.50 log2 pooled over closure types) and is exported at weight 0. Disulfide-closed rings span 8 to 13 residues (interquartile range, median 10), head-to-tail 7 to 11, amide-like 5 to 10 and thioether 9 to 12; 56.5% of disulfide complexes contain a type I beta turn; N-methylation is about twice as frequent at positions in contact with the target (4.7% against 2.2%) while D residues are not enriched at the interface (5.3% against 6.9%); and backbone NH groups are 47% intramolecularly hydrogen-bonded, 10% hydrogen-bonded to the receptor only and 43% free. Interface residues are enriched for isoleucine, tryptophan, phenylalanine, tyrosine and valine and depleted for glutamine, alanine, cysteine, glutamate and lysine, with the unstable status noted above. ## 6. Validation

### 6.1 Direction checks against data, on training sources only

For each entry with a directional claim about permeability, the direction is checked against the PAMPA data of the train group and never against the stop or held-out groups. The train group is 2020_Townsend (n = 2,881) and 2021_Kelly (n = 1,518); rows from every other source are dropped while reading, and the script asserts that only these sources remain. The statistic is the within-source Spearman correlation of the computable feature with the raw PAMPA value (equivalent to using the within-source z score), with intervals from a cluster bootstrap over flat-structure (stereoisomer) groups (B = 300, seed 0); the check is descriptive, with no multiplicity correction. Only entries whose stated outcome is permeability are tested.

Over the 107 entries, 17 agree in direction, 7 are mixed, none disagree, and 83 are not testable (cards, cautions and context entries have no directional claim, and some windows lack a group size). Of the 32 weight-eligible entries, 15 agree, 7 are mixed and 10 are not testable; the 15 form the literature-prior configuration.

The lipophilicity peak (inverted U) agrees in both sources (quadratic coefficient -0.113 in Townsend and -0.120 in Kelly, both intervals excluding 0). The macrocycle drug window (HBD at most 7 with MW below 1000 or cLogP above 2.5) shows inside-window peptides more permeable in Kelly (rho +0.43, 95% interval 0.09 to 0.63; 1,172 inside against 346 outside) and is untestable in Townsend, where all peptides are inside; the polarity window (cLogP below 4) is weakly contradicted in Townsend (rho -0.12, 95% interval -0.24 to 0.04).

N-methyl count, HBD, TPSA and cLogP are therefore the stable, literature-supported directions on the two largest sources. The size and D-residue rules show an unstable sign or no signal, so their terms receive weight zero. Both sources are laboratories known for heavily N-methylated series, so agreement is evidence of consistency and not of causation.

For comparison, rule candidates mined from our own labels on the train group alone reached no validated status: the unmodified mining criteria require at least three sources and the group has two. Their within-source directions were nonetheless consistent across the two laboratories and with the literature (TPSA -0.470 and -0.485, HBD -0.439 and -0.527, logP +0.287 and +0.488; mean within-source z effect of N-methyl addition +0.623 and +0.483; pooled +0.554, 95% interval 0.373 to 0.735, I-squared 0.95). They are descriptive and are not used as weights: priors with weight come from the literature, and the data check their direction.

### 6.2 Verification and coverage of the gold set

All 107 gold-set quotes verify verbatim against the stored full texts, and a planted quote absent from the source is detected by a unit test. The gold set covers the topics listed in Section 5.2. On the structure side, 5 of the 17 target cards have a cyclic-peptide complex and a best template, 1 has only an antibody-epitope complex and 11 have no cyclic-peptide complex in the library (Section 5.5).

## 7. Use in a generative system

### 7.1 Consumption under train, stop and held-out source groups

The layer is consumed by a hybrid generation system, described in a companion manuscript, under a fixed split of the permeability sources into a train group (A), a stop group (S) and a held-out group (B). The split uses the 20 sources with at least 20 labelled PAMPA peptides, which hold 6,784 of the 6,960 PAMPA-labelled peptides in the local table. A is 2020_Townsend plus 2021_Kelly (4,399 peptides, 64.84% of 6,784); S has nine sources (1,269 peptides) and B has nine sources (1,116 peptides). Anything data-derived is mined on A only; the stop group supplies the early-stopping signal and the held-out group the final evaluation. An earlier split (7, 7 and 6 sources) is retained for a robustness arm. The split limits leakage but has two features that matter: group A is two laboratories, and the stop and held-out groups are each dominated by one laboratory (CHUGAI about 69% of S; 2016_Furukawa about 59% of B).

### 7.2 Three uses

Hard rules are compiled into a token-level constraint for generation and are not data-mined. Soft priors are the weight-eligible literature rules with the untuned uniform weights of Section 4.3. Retrieval context combines the nearest known peptides (stereo-aware similarity, with measured values) with provenance-tagged rule snippets and, where a target card exists, its pharmacophore text. The retrieval package includes a blind rule for recovery tests (excluded documents, answer-set structures and a year cutoff are rejected, with controls that fail when violated); on the integrin set its store holds 4,634 exact-value rows, 2,826 isomers and 230 documents spanning 1991 to 2025 for 8 targets. Three configurations are compared in the companion manuscript: hard rules only, literature priors, and the data-descriptive directions of Section 6.1 as an ablation.

## 8. Discussion

Existing collections give a designer measurements and structures; they do not give a statement such as "adding a hydrogen-bond donor and enlarging the ring are expected to lower permeability" together with its source, quote, evidence tier, scope and the feature it maps to. The layer provides that statement-level knowledge and links it to the measurement and structure resources through shared keys, so that a rule can be traced to a passage, checked against data on training sources, and consumed by a generator as a prior or as retrieved context. Three properties distinguish the construction from a literature table: attribution is machine-verified rather than asserted, evidence tier controls what may influence a model, and the leak guards make the layer usable inside a held-out evaluation. The campaign ledger records negative and partial outcomes alongside successes, and our own earlier campaigns sit beside the published ones.

The construction also showed several things. The imports worked on every route and the join control passed, which supports one stereo-aware key across resources; files and papers disagree on counts, so counts must be quoted from the file used. The structure library shows how much of the apparent cyclic-peptide complex data in peptide-ligand tables is linear or mislabelled (90% of parsed rows are linear, and 28% of an earlier miner's rows survive quality control). The direction check on two independent large sources agreed with the literature for the rules that could be tested (17 agree, none disagree), and the stable directions are the ones a generator can safely weight; data-derived rules need several independent sources to reach validated status, so the literature carries the weights and the data check their direction. Quantitative, computable rules are a minority of statements (32 of 107 weight-eligible), which is why retrieval context and hard constraints carry much of the use. Whether the priors improve generation is reported in the companion manuscript.

## 9. Limitations

The survey read 10 of 13 resources at abstract level and searched only Europe PMC titles and a few web pages, so absence of a record is not proof of absence; the degree to which existing collections cover the patent literature was not established. Literature coverage is limited to open-access full text; paywalled primary studies are missed, and target pharmacophore rules, encyclopaedic and database facts, display-campaign hit rates and per-drug properties of approved cyclic peptides are not in the gold set. The literature and patent stages are described as protocols and their measurements are not reported here. Table extraction from XML is lossy, and an 8-billion-parameter extractor can misread direction or comparator even when the quote is exact; exact-match verification guarantees attribution, not correctness.

The statements of the gold set were written by an AI assistant while reading the opened papers; only the quotes are machine-verified, and the fidelity of statement to quote has not been judged by a person. For the 36 T2 entries the studies behind the quoted statements were not opened, and each statement is attributed to the source that states it. The tier audit re-read all T2 entries and the T1 entries flagged by a review-type source, a citation marker or a restatement cue word; T1 entries that were not flagged were not individually re-read for provenance. Several sources are recent 2026 articles that have not been independently reviewed by us. Weights are an untuned uniform prior.

The direction check uses two sources, one of which dominates, descriptors from the stratification annotation (Crippen logP, RDKit TPSA and HBD), a cluster bootstrap by flat structure and not by laboratory, no multiplicity control, and PAMPA only; both sources are known for heavily N-methylated series. Assays and laboratories differ across sources, and an earlier flagship rule (N-methylation) was mostly a between-laboratory effect; the schema records assay and support but cannot remove this heterogeneity. Source papers whose data are in CycPeptMPDB are flagged, not removed, so a literature rule may reflect data that also underlies the evaluation. Group A is two laboratories. Files differ from their papers in several cases (Section 2.2) and may change.

The structure layer is bounded by BioLiP2's peptide class and the earlier miner, so cyclic peptides deposited with non-standard residues as hetero-ligands can be missed (alphavbeta6 is such a case); crystallisable, soluble, disulfide-rich binders dominate (765 of 888 by X-ray diffraction), so the priors describe what is in the PDB and not what binds best; cyclisation, D-residue and N-methyl calls are geometric, depend on model quality and use only the first alternate conformation; the antibody-like receptor flag is a text heuristic; a heterodimer counts as one target; there are no affinity annotations, so no ligand-efficiency proxy; no paper behind a target card was opened; PepBDB and Propedia were not imported; and the structure priors are not fitted to any label and have not been validated as design aids. The comparison with other knowledge bases is by content type and counts, not by head-to-head use in a downstream task.
