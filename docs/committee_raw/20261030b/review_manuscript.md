# Referee report: "Loop / small-LLM" manuscript draft (v1, 2026-10-30)

Reviewer role: independent, computational chemistry / ML venue. Read: main draft (sections 1-9 and appendices A-C skimmed), loop preprint, RSI patch note, owner brief, figures status; spot-checked l5_aggregate.json keys and the figures_paper output list. I did NOT read the claim-evidence ledger, red-team review or committee synthesis (deliberately; so I cannot state agreement or disagreement with them). I did not re-run any analysis; numbers below are checked for internal consistency and against the cited web sources only. Uncertainty is marked [U].

## 0. Verdict
As written this is not a paper a venue can review. Its title, introduction, hypotheses (H1-H5), method description and one third of the abstract frame the study as a test of a small in-house peptide model (IH-LM) against larger pretrained models. That test has no result, and IH-LM's own retrieval module and verifier head fail their own thresholds. What exists is a careful, honest, mostly negative evaluation-instrument and baselines study on one library (6,960 PAMPA peptides) plus one binding benchmark that fails its own validity gate. That is publishable (a negative-results / methods paper) only if reframed and cut to about a third of its present length. The honesty of the tagging is a strength; the volume of tags, caveats and unfinished items is now the main readability defect.

## 1. Are the headline claims supported at the strength stated?
(a) "Loop beats random in-distribution (+186.3)". Supported, but the authors themselves show it is mostly location of one laboratory's series (346/500 picks from 2020_Townsend vs 206 random; 52.8 % of hits). Calling it "series interpolation, not generalisation" is correct. It is not a finding about learning.

(b) "Scaffold-shifted: claim not met (+22.2, spread -1.6 to 44.1) and physicochemical rule ties (-0.5)". The verdict rests on a pre-registered rule that uses the spread of per-seed differences, a prediction-style range that is not a confidence interval and is structurally harder to pass than a CI. The same data give a bootstrap CI of the mean of 17.7 to 26.5. The paper reports both but the abstract leads with "not met by the letter of the rule", which a reader will take as "no effect"; the data say "small positive mean on one fixed library, not distinguishable from a label-free rule". The rule K_z (z(N-methyl) - z(HBD) - z(TPSA)) is a composite of unit-weighted features chosen with prior knowledge of what drives permeability in this very database; the preprint says the rule baseline was added "after the main result". Its equality with the loop is therefore a post hoc, development-set observation. Strength stated ("a label-free physicochemical rule scored equal to the loop") is slightly above what a post hoc, single-library, 30-seed comparison supports; "was not distinguishable from" is accurate.

(c) "Benefit is yield, not model improvement on new laboratories". The supporting contrasts are non-significance results: loop - one-shot +0.003 (-0.034 to 0.037); cluster-level loop - shuffled +0.071 (-0.079 to 0.181) with 11 held-out sources. Absence of evidence with wide intervals and no stated minimum detectable effect is presented in the abstract in the same grammatical register as a positive finding. Table row "N4 consistent" is the same issue. The physicochemical learner does show a feedback effect at cluster level in regime L (+0.155, 0.071 to 0.241), which weakens the blanket sentence; the abstract mentions only the plain GBM.

(d) "No benchmark passes the validity gate; this is a finding". The gate was defined after the M2 failure was seen (disclosed), has MDE about 0.11 AUROC on 26 families, and is applied to constructions the authors designed. The statement that "a clean zero-shot benchmark for cyclic-peptide binders was not obtainable from this data collection" is fair; the drift towards "cyclic-peptide binder benchmarks" in general (Section 4.2: "a finding about cyclic-peptide binder benchmarks") is not. One could also say the gate is simply miscalibrated: on analysis I the zero-shot pretrained model (0.539) scores below the no-target fingerprint control (0.584), which says the task, not the benchmark design, rewards compound-only features. The paper does not discuss that this makes every I-based result (including few-label) dependent on a benchmark it has declared invalid.

(e) "Few-label gain is analog lookup". Reasonably supported (NN 0.753-0.869 vs fit-on-revealed 0.750-0.861). But this is on analysis I, which fails the fingerprint control; so some of "analog lookup" may be series/typicality shortcut rather than within-target similarity. The paper reports the gate failure but then uses I without caveat in the abstract. Also, the registered "transferable" rule requires the very-remote bin (4 % of items at k=5, ≤1 % at k=10/25) to have lower bound > 0, which is nearly untestable; "no gain demonstrated to be transferable" is therefore close to true by construction. The paper half-says this; it should say it in the abstract.

(f) "K_phys exploratory lead". Correctly demoted. But the abstract devotes a long clause to it (items vii) while the evidence is 15 of 54 dependent contrasts, no correction, and the cluster-level table shows it is never better than shuffled knowledge and is worse for the physicochemical learner. A leads-only finding should not be in the abstract.

(g) "Cross-loop pattern: in all three loops, selection beats random and lookup reproduces most of the margin under shift". The three loops are very unequal: C2 (binding, family-cluster CIs, withdrawn original numbers), L5 (permeability, rule tie only in regime B), and a 3-seed replay with no interval that the paper itself calls illustration. The "pattern" is also not uniform: in L5 regime A, loop - K_z is +53.6 and loop - N-methyl rule +105 (loop clearly better than lookup in-distribution); in L5 B only the K_z rule ties. "Stated finding" should be "consistent observation in two analyses plus one illustration".

(h) "Cheap-tier LLMs far behind GBM". Four seeds, below the registered power rule, shared 150-candidate subsets, two cheap models. Labelled exploratory, fine, but see objection 7 about its relation to the paper's title and to Gupta et al.

(i) T1/T1b/anchor-offset nulls and failures: honestly reported; these are the most "publishable" negatives but are scattered. The anchor test is described both as "not supported" and as "not a negative result for the method"; that is defensible (planted control passes), but the kill criterion was met, so the module M2 cannot also be listed as a "candidate contribution".

## 2. Internal contradictions, stale statements, definitions
1. Stale: Limitation 8 still says "The c2_retro_loop numbers await reproduction"; the same draft says the reproduction was done and the original numbers withdrawn (Sec 4.3.1, 5.6).
2. Tag legend in the preamble defines [LIT-SUMMARY] twice with different meanings (primary text read vs model-written summary); the first should be [LIT-VERIFIED]. The reference list tags PeptideCLM (2024) [LIT-UNVERIFIED] yet Section 2 uses its AUROC 0.781 +/- 0.067 and architecture as fact.
3. Numbering is broken throughout: abstract results go (i)-(vi), (viii), (vii); discussion goes (i), (ii), (iii), (vi), (iii-b), (iv), (v); limitations go 1-7, 8a, 8b, 8c, 8e, 8d, 8, 9, 10.
4. Definitions of the key terms drifted. The file name and Appendix C say "small LLM" was replaced by "IH-LM vs larger pretrained peptide/chemistry models". But the comparators are 32M-337M parameter encoders (PeptideCLM-2) and 55M HELM-BERT, while IH-LM is planned at about 94M (71M without cross-attention) parameters (P1 plan). So IH-LM is not smaller than the "larger" models it is meant to be compared with; "small" and "large" have no stable referent, and the cost axis (parameters, GPU-hours) cannot support the framing "small matches large". Meanwhile the only genuine LLMs actually run are the cheap API models, which the paper says are not part of the central comparison. Define "LLM" and "large" once, numerically.
5. "IH-LM" is variously a model, a ladder R0-R4, "design, no result", and the subject of the title question in the Introduction ("does a closed loop ... improve IH-LM"). The Intro question cannot be answered by the paper.
6. Regimes: "primary regimes" are A and B in L5 (C1), but Section 4.3 says "the headline regimes are B and L". Regime L's held-out set varies per seed (6-18 laboratories, 25-34 % of peptides, all 18 eligible laboratories held out in 3 of 10 seeds) so regime L is not one regime, and in those seeds the model trains on only the two big labs and tiny sources. This is disclosed in a caption but conflicts with the 25 % statement in Section 4.3 and with using L as "the" generalisation test.
7. Interval types: handled with unusual care, but the abstract mixes spread, boot-mean, cluster-boot and meta in one paragraph, and the physicochemical-learner table uses cluster-boot over 6-11 sources; the reader cannot track which interval supports which verdict without the labels. Also yield contrasts in regime L use seed bootstraps although laboratories are the unit of inference, which the paper concedes but still uses for the abstract's +165.0.
8. Counts: preprint says label re-standardisation flips 99.7 of 6,960 labels "about 7 % of hits"; Jaccard 0.867 over 697 hits implies roughly 13-14 % of hit set membership changes. One of these is mis-stated [U, arithmetic not re-derived from file].
9. The data licence: draft records CycPeptMPDB as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (internal metadata) and states the database paper "states no separate data licence". A fetched summary of the PMC article says the data are downloadable under CC BY 4.0 (the page-summary tool could be wrong). The licence table, the share-alike plan and the brief's decision item all depend on this; it must be checked at the source.
10. Preprint vs draft: the preprint's text on K_phys - shuffled yield (A +5.8, L +7.1, "helps relative to a no-information channel") and the draft's "largely the cost of shuffled noise" are two readings of the same contrasts; the draft's is the more careful one, so the preprint (if it will circulate) is stale.
11. Dataset size: Section 1 cites 7,334 peptides/6,941 PAMPA measurements for the 2023 release (verified at the PMC page), Section 4.1 uses 7,298 PAMPA values and 6,960 peptides after filters from a later release, but the loop preprint cites only the 2023 paper. Release version and hash are "to be recorded".

## 3. Overclaiming / hedging, novelty, planned vs result
- Planned and results are separated well in Section 5.8, the status box and Appendix A; this is the paper's best feature. They are not separated in the title options (option 2 includes "pre-registered test of a loop-native hybrid model"), the Objective paragraph of the abstract (Aim A occupies half of it), Section 1's contributions and Section 3's H1-H5. A reader skimming gets a model paper.
- Contribution (iii) "a demonstration that leave-one-out evaluation of this task is artifactual" is a mathematical identity (a leave-one-out group-mean predictor has within-group Spearman -1 by construction); presenting it as a demonstration inflates it. Contribution (ii) is "gates a benchmark must pass" while no benchmark passes them.
- The abstract's "Conclusions allowed by the evidence" contain "simple physicochemical priors match it there" (post hoc rule, one regime).
- Hedging excess: nearly every sentence carries three tags. The effect is that strong, clean results (the nearest-neighbour few-label table, the 346/500 concentration, the C2 learn-nn +0.002) are lost in qualifiers.
- Novelty: Section 8 correctly disclaims first-benchmark, tokenizer, retrieval, shrinkage. The remaining "candidate contributions" are explicitly not claimed. Honest, but then the paper has no stated positive contribution beyond an audit; say so in the title/abstract.

## 4. Story, figures, reproducibility, limitations
- Story: there are three papers fused: (1) loop controls on permeability (L5/L7/K_phys), (2) validity-gated binder benchmark (I/M/M2/M3/H4b), (3) technique nulls (T1, T1b, anchors, C2). The connecting thread ("what does a loop learn beyond lookup") is clear in Section 6 only after 40 pages. A reader cannot tell what the contribution is. The abstract is a 1,100-word single paragraph.
- Figures: the draft's Section 5 contains no embedded figures; figures_paper/out has rendered F1, F2, F3, FL5, FH4b, F5(partial), F8, F9, FCV, FK, F11(partial) and six PENDING placeholders (F4, F6, F7, F10, F11b, F12). Central-comparison figures F4/F6/F7/F10/F12 are all pending, i.e. the paper's figure set is mostly figures for the missing result. The draft does not cite figure numbers in the text [U: I grepped for "Fig" and found none in Section 5], so the mapping text-to-figure is undefined.
- Reproducibility: strong on hashes and prereg amendments; weak on availability: "public repository snapshot [PENDING]", CycPeptMPDB file hash "to be recorded", results spread over 30+ internal docs, many numbers [REPORTED] from documents whose data file was not re-read (T1/T1b, strata, power for M2, anchor CIs). Several results come from files "present in the working tree but not committed". For a referee, [REPORTED] numbers are unverifiable.
- Data/licence: the table is thorough; unresolved items (CycPeptMPDB licence, share-alike on atom-LM weights, ND-sourced data) are honestly left open. They block release.
- Limitations: thorough, arguably too numerous and unranked. Missing: (i) hit definition uses all labels of each source (transductive, 1.4 % flip); (ii) K_z and N-methyl rules post hoc; (iii) 10 development seeds reused for many contrasts; (iv) the benchmark gate's applicability to few-label I-based claims; (v) the evaluated learners are only GBMs; the paper's own title/abstract speaks of "LLMs".

## 5. Ten most damaging objections (ranked)
1. Title/aim vs content. Passage: "Aim A (pre-registered, not yet run): test whether a loop-native in-house hybrid ... IH-LM matches or exceeds larger pretrained ..." (Abstract Objective; title options 2; Section 1 question; H1-H5). Damage: the stated question is unanswered, and the model's own modules fail thresholds (M3 gap 0.41 vs 0.70; verifier AUC 0.53). Reviewers will reject for "no model, no experiment". Fix: delete Aim A from title/abstract/intro; move to a short "Future work/planned" paragraph; retitle as an evaluation/negative-results paper.
2. Rule-based verdict vs CI. Passage: "not met by the letter of the rule ... spread in regime B includes 0" while "post hoc bootstrap CI ... excludes 0" (Abstract (i), 5.1). Damage: looks like either a statistical sleight (spread is not a CI) or an unstable conclusion; either way the headline of the study is ambiguous. Fix: state the primary inference once: "+22.2 hits per 500 (boot-mean 17.7 to 26.5, seeds on one library), not shown to exceed a physicochemical rule; the preregistered spread criterion was not met"; drop "by the letter".
3. Post hoc rule baseline ties the loop. Passage: "a label-free physicochemical rule scored equal to the loop (-0.5; spread -20.0 to 17.2)". Damage: K_z built with knowledge of the dataset's drivers, added after the result, compared on development data. Fix: label as post hoc; add a held-out check (apply K_z with weights fixed from literature to regime L; or show equal-weight alternatives).
4. Non-significance read as equivalence. Passage: "sequential selection does not improve held-out ranking beyond the same labels given at once (+0.003, -0.034 to 0.037)"; "N4 consistent". Damage: with 10 seeds/11 sources, power unknown; the physchem learner shows a feedback effect. Fix: report MDE/equivalence bounds or reword to "no effect detected; cannot exclude +/-0.04"; qualify the abstract by learner.
5. Everything rests on one library, one task, GBMs. Passage: Limitations 8e. Damage: "loop", "LLM", "feedback" generalise nowhere; two labs hold two thirds of peptides; regime L varies per seed and includes seeds where all eligible labs are held out. Fix: state in the abstract's first sentence; fix a single regime-L definition (L9 or a fixed list) before claiming anything about unseen labs.
6. Validity gate: retrospective, post hoc, low power, and invalidates the few-label results it still uses. Passage: "No analysis passes ... Analysis I fails ... yet remains the only design on which a zero-shot claim could be made" and the few-label table on I. Damage: "invalid benchmark" and "AUROC 0.869" cannot both be headline. Fix: present few-label results as relative (NN vs fit vs pretrained) and drop the absolute AUROC from the abstract; state the zero-shot model < fingerprint control as the key diagnostic.
7. The LLM story is absent. Passage: "Cheap-tier general-purpose language models ... far behind a gradient-boosted model ... (exploratory; 4 seeds)" and "small LLM"/"large LLM" naming. Damage: the only LLMs run are two cheap API models, 4 seeds, inconclusive per the paper's own power rule; the 32-337 M "larger" comparators are not large; Gupta et al. already show LLM agents ignore feedback. Fix: remove the API arm from the abstract, cite Gupta et al. in Section 2, define "LLM" and "larger" with parameter numbers, and drop "LLM" from file/title unless an LM is run.
8. Many headline numbers are [REPORTED]/uncommitted/unverified. Passage: Appendix A levels D/L; T1/T1b "D"; anchor CIs "D"; strata part B "D". Damage: referees cannot check; some data in working tree uncommitted. Fix: commit and re-derive by script, or move such numbers to supplement labelled unverified; no [REPORTED] number in the abstract.
9. K_phys and 54-contrast fishing. Passage: Abstract (vii); Section 5.2 K_phys bullet; H3 row. Damage: 15/54 dependent contrasts, no correction, cluster-level shows no or negative effect; an abstract mention invites a "forking paths" rejection. Fix: cut from abstract; keep in Results as a pre-specified-confirmation-pending note; report cluster-level result first.
10. Unverified literature and licence base. Passage: tags [LIT-UNVERIFIED] on CycPeptMP, PeptideCLM, Graber, Jablonka, Liu; PeptideCLM-2 sizes; "CycPeptMPDB NC-SA". Damage: the related-work and novelty section is built on unopened sources; CycPeptMPDB licence conflicts with a fetched statement (CC BY 4.0); PeptideCLM-2 title/size/corpus differ from the search listing. Fix: open every cited source or delete the claim; resolve the licence at cycpeptmpdb.com; remove "do not submit with this tag" items.
(Runner-up: abstract length and numbering; stale limitation 8; "demonstration" of the LOO identity.)

## 6. Recommended title
"Controls for retrospective cyclic-peptide feedback loops: laboratory concentration, analog lookup and benchmark validity gates on public permeability and binding data"

## 7. Recommended abstract (states only what is supported)
Closed design-test-learn loops are proposed for cyclic-peptide discovery, but gains from feedback are rarely separated from gains from lookup of analogs or from the concentration of positives in a few laboratories. We built a retrospective replay in which the measured label of a peptide is revealed only when a policy selects it, with controls for random, shuffled-label, frozen and one-shot updates, and applied it to 6,960 PAMPA permeability measurements from CycPeptMPDB, together with a family-held-out binder benchmark from BindingDB/ChEMBL with no-target validity controls. With gradient-boosted regressors on fingerprints, the loop found many more top-decile peptides than random selection when the start was in-distribution (+186.3 of 500 picks; seed-bootstrap CI 181.1 to 191.5), but 346 of its 500 picks came from one laboratory. Under a scaffold-shifted start the mean advantage was +22.2 (seed-bootstrap CI 17.7 to 26.5; the pre-registered criterion, based on the per-seed spread, was not met) and a label-free physicochemical rule was not distinguishable from the loop. With held-out laboratories as the unit (11 sources), none of the plain-GBM contrasts for held-out ranking excluded zero, so the measured benefit is selection yield, not demonstrated improvement of the model on new laboratories. On the binding benchmark no design passed the no-target controls (best-case fingerprint control AUROC 0.584, 95 % family-cluster CI 0.533 to 0.636), and with 5 to 25 revealed labels per target, nearest-neighbour lookup (AUROC 0.753 to 0.869) was as good as fitted models, with no gain shown to transfer beyond close analogs. Two pre-registered attempts to go beyond lookup (evidence-weighted fusion; laboratory-offset calibration) were null or failed. All results are retrospective, from public data, on one permeability library and one binding collection; intervals are labelled by type and seed-based intervals do not describe new laboratories. Comparisons with pretrained peptide or chemistry language models are planned and not reported.
