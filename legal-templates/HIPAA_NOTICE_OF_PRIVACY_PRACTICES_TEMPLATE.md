## What is Protected Health Information (PHI)?

PHI is individually identifiable health information that:
- Relates to past, present, or future physical/mental health
- Relates to provision of healthcare
- Relates to payment for healthcare
- Identifies or could identify the individual

**Examples of PHI:**
- Patient names, dates, addresses
- Medical record numbers
- Genomic or genetic data linked to individuals
- Treatment or diagnosis information
- Lab results, imaging data
- Any of the 18 HIPAA identifiers

**De-identified data is NOT PHI** if all 18 identifiers are removed (Safe Harbor method) or if determined by a qualified expert (Expert Determination method).

## How We May Use and Disclose Your PHI

### Uses and Disclosures We May Make WITHOUT Your Authorization

#### 1. For Treatment

We may use and disclose your PHI for treatment purposes when requested by your healthcare provider.

**Examples:**
- Analyzing genomic data to assist in diagnosis
- Generating bioinformatics workflows for treatment planning
- Providing results to your healthcare team

**We do NOT:**
- Make treatment decisions
- Provide medical advice
- Prescribe medications

#### 2. For Healthcare Operations

We may use PHI for healthcare operations necessary to run our business and support the healthcare system.

**Examples:**
- Quality assurance and improvement
- Training our staff
- Business planning and development
- Customer service
- Internal audits

**We do NOT:**
- Use PHI for marketing without authorization
- Sell PHI
- Use PHI for purposes unrelated to healthcare operations

#### 3. As Required By Law

We will disclose PHI when required by federal, state, or local law.

**Examples:**
- Court orders or subpoenas
- Law enforcement investigations
- Public health reporting
- FDA adverse event reporting

#### 4. Public Health Activities

We may disclose PHI to public health authorities for:
- Disease prevention and control
- Reporting adverse events or product defects
- Tracking disease outbreaks
- Notifying persons of exposures to communicable diseases

#### 5. Health Oversight Activities

We may disclose PHI to health oversight agencies for:
- Audits
- Investigations
- Inspections
- Licensure reviews
- Compliance monitoring

**Example:** HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR) compliance audits

#### 6. Judicial and Administrative Proceedings

We may disclose PHI in response to:
- Court orders
- Subpoenas (with patient authorization or other legal basis)
- Discovery requests in lawsuits

#### 7. Law Enforcement

We may disclose PHI to law enforcement for:
- Reporting crimes
- Responding to lawful requests
- Identifying suspects or victims
- Reporting deaths

#### 8. Coroners, Medical Examiners, Funeral Directors

We may disclose PHI to:
- Coroners to identify deceased persons
- Medical examiners to determine cause of death
- Funeral directors for burial arrangements

#### 9. Research

We may use PHI for research when:
- An Institutional Review Board (IRB) or Privacy Board approves
- We obtain your authorization
- The research uses de-identified data

#### 11. Workers' Compensation

#### 12. Specialized Government Functions

## Your Rights Regarding Your PHI

### 1. Right to Access

You have the right to inspect and obtain a copy of your PHI that we maintain.

**How to request:**
- Submit written request to hipaa-privacy@biomate.ai
- Specify what information you want
- Specify format (paper or electronic)

**Timeframe:** We will respond within 30 days (may extend 30 more days with notice)

**Fees:** We may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee for copying, mailing, or preparing a summary.

**Denials:** We may deny access in certain limited circumstances. You have the right to have denials reviewed.

### 2. Right to Amend

You have the right to request amendment of your PHI if you believe it is incorrect or incomplete.

**How to request:**
- Submit written request to hipaa-privacy@biomate.ai
- Explain why you want the amendment

**Timeframe:** We will respond within 60 days (may extend 30 more days with notice)

**We may deny if:**
- PHI was not created by us
- PHI is not part of our records
- PHI is not available for inspection under HIPAA
- PHI is accurate and complete

If denied, you may submit a statement of disagreement.

### 3. Right to an Accounting of Disclosures

You have the right to receive an accounting of disclosures we made of your PHI (with certain exceptions).

**Accounting includes:**
- Date of disclosure
- Name and address of recipient
- Description of PHI disclosed
- Purpose of disclosure

**Exceptions (not included in accounting):**
- Disclosures for treatment, payment, healthcare operations
- Disclosures to you
- Disclosures pursuant to your authorization
- Disclosures for national security or law enforcement

**How to request:**
- Submit written request to hipaa-privacy@biomate.ai
- Specify time period (up to 6 years prior, but not before April 14, 2003)

**Timeframe:** We will respond within 60 days (may extend 30 more days with notice)

**Fees:** First accounting in 12 months is free. Subsequent requests may incur a reasonable fee.

### 4. Right to Request Restrictions

You have the right to request restrictions on:
- How we use or disclose PHI for treatment, payment, or healthcare operations
- Disclosures to persons involved in your care

**We are NOT required to agree** to your request, except:
- If you pay out-of-pocket in full for a service, you can restrict disclosure to your health plan for payment purposes

**How to request:**
- Submit written request to hipaa-privacy@biomate.ai
- Specify what information you want restricted
- Specify to whom the restriction applies

If we agree, we will comply except in emergency situations.

### 5. Right to Request Confidential Communications

You have the right to request that we communicate with you in a certain way or at a certain location.

**Examples:**
- Email instead of postal mail
- Home address instead of work address
- Alternate phone number

**How to request:**
- Submit written request to hipaa-privacy@biomate.ai
- Specify how and where you want to be contacted

We will accommodate reasonable requests without asking for an explanation.

### 6. Right to a Paper Copy of This Notice

You have the right to a paper copy of this notice, even if you agreed to receive it electronically.

**How to request:**
- Contact hipaa-privacy@biomate.ai
- Or download from biomate.ai/hipaa-notice

### 7. Right to Notification of Breach

You have the right to be notified if a breach of your unsecured PHI occurs.

**Notification includes:**
- Description of the breach
- Types of PHI involved
- Steps you should take
- What we're doing to investigate and mitigate
- Contact information

## Our Responsibilities

### We are required to:

1. **Maintain Privacy**
   - Implement administrative, physical, and technical safeguards
   - Train our workforce on privacy and security
   - Have policies and procedures to protect PHI

2. **Provide This Notice**
   - Make this notice available to you
   - Post it on our website
   - Provide a copy upon request

3. **Follow This Notice**
   - Comply with the terms of this notice
   - Not use or disclose PHI except as described or authorized

4. **Notify of Breaches**
   - Investigate suspected breaches
   - Notify affected individuals within 60 days
   - Report breaches to Covered Entity and HHS

5. **Not Retaliate**
   - We will not retaliate against you for exercising your rights
   - We will not require you to waive your rights

### Safeguards We Implement:

**Administrative:**
- Designated Privacy and Security Officers
- Workforce training
- Risk assessments
- Policies and procedures
- Sanctions for violations

**Physical:**
- Secure facilities (AWS data centers)
- Access controls
- Workstation security
- Device and media controls

**Technical:**
- Encryption (AES-256-GCM at rest, TLS 1.3 in transit)
- Access controls (unique user IDs, MFA)
- Audit logging (6+ year retention)
- Integrity controls (checksums, authentication tags)
- Automatic logoff (15-minute timeout)
