Design & generate

BioMate · Function-module spec 05Status: for reviewLane: Instant → Cloud

De novo molecular design as one pipeline: generate → predict activity → optimise properties → rank candidates. This module was the standing open question in Spec 00 §0.10, and a live brief answered it — a lead-discovery programme whose deliverable is exactly this chain, optimising candidates on druglikeness, target affinity and safety together. Generation is a distinct intent from analysing data or training a predictor, so it earns a module rather than a track.

5.1The four stages

TrackThe user's sentenceProducesLane

Optimise properties"Better potency without losing solubility."A revised series, with the trade-off shown.Cloud

Pipeline rule

The four stages are one run, not four products. A user who asks only to generate still gets predictions and a ranking, because an unranked pile of structures is not an answer. What varies is where the run stops being interesting to them — so every stage's output is inspectable as a step in the run surface (Spec 07), and any stage can be re-run with changed parameters without redoing the others.

5.2Generate

Generation is only useful when constrained, so the constraints are the interface. The composer resolves them from the goal and the project, then shows them as resolved parameters before the run starts.

ConstraintExampleOrigin
TargetA structure, or a pharmacophore inferred from a known series.Project file, or a database lookup.
Scaffold policyStay on this scaffold · hop away from it · grow this fragment.Stated in the goal.
Property envelopeMW, logP, TPSA, rotatable bonds — a drug-like window, not a single cut-off.Project instruction, or a default the run names.
ExclusionsStructural alerts, a moiety the chemists have banned, claimed chemical space.Project instruction; patent exclusions come from Search (Spec 01).
Count500 candidates.Stated, with the compute cost shown before the run.

Chemistry validity is a gate, not a filter

Generated structures pass a validity gate before anything downstream sees them: parseable, valence-correct, stereochemistry defined where it matters, synthetic accessibility above a floor. Rejections are counted and characterised, never silently dropped — "412 of 500 passed; 61 failed synthetic accessibility, 27 hit a structural alert" — because a high rejection rate is the most useful early signal that the constraints are wrong.

5.3Predict activity

Every prediction carries the model, the version and a confidence, and links to the model object (Spec 03 §3.3). Two things this stage refuses to do:

  • No confident numbers outside the applicability region. A candidate unlike anything in the training set is reported as out-of-domain, not as a number with an invisible caveat.
  • No score without a rationale. A predicted affinity comes with what drove it — the interaction, the substructure, the nearest measured analogue — because a chemist cannot act on a scalar.

Where a docking step runs, pose plausibility is gated separately from score: a good score on an implausible pose is a failure, and is shown as one.

5.4Optimise properties

The brief's core axis — druglikeness, target affinity and safety optimised together. The design commitment is that the trade-off is shown rather than collapsed.

No composite score

A single blended number is never shown. It hides the weighting, and the weighting is the scientific judgement — so weights are the user's to set, are stated on every ranking, and changing them re-ranks without re-running the predictions.

Safety is a gate rather than an objective to be traded away: a candidate below the safety floor is rejected and stays visible with its reason, however good its affinity. That is why BM-4519 above, the most potent candidate in the round, is the one that is rejected.

5.5Rank candidates

The output that a programme actually consumes. Criteria are explicit, the shortlist is traceable, and the tail is not hidden.

ElementBehaviour
criteria stripThe objectives, their targets and their weights, editable in place. Every ranking states the criteria that produced it.
shortlist rowsCandidate id, per-objective values with units, confidence per prediction, and the gate verdict.
why?Opens the models and predictions behind that row — model version, nearest measured analogue, assay round if the surrogate has been refitted.
rejected setCollapsed but present. A programme lead must be able to see what was excluded and on which gate.
exportOrder sheet and plate map, pairing platform candidate ids with the lab's registry ids — see §5.7.

5.5.1 Two views of one ranking new

The shortlist renders as a table or as a 2D depiction grid — the same ranking, the same verdicts, switched by a control. The grid is not a decorative alternative: a chemist reads structures, and a ranked list of identifier strings withholds the one thing that makes a shortlist judgeable at a glance. Depictions render from the structures themselves, so the grid cannot disagree with the table.

In both views a rejected candidate keeps its card with the rejecting gate named, and a candidate outside the scoring model's applicability region is marked held — shown, ranked nowhere, and offered no confident number. The evidence-class instances behind these viewers are specified in Spec 03 §3.8.

5.7The wet-lab loop

Design without a return path from the bench is a demo. The loop's mechanics — the round contract, the three intake routes, the mapping template, partial returns and censored values — are specified in Spec 03 §3.7, because the refit is a training run. What belongs here is the design-side consequence:

  • A ranked shortlist leaves the platform as an order, in the format the lab registers from, with identity preserved both ways.
  • Returned measurements re-score the candidate set and are shown against the predictions that preceded them — a visible predicted-versus-measured record per round, which is the only honest measure of whether the design pipeline is working.
  • Proposals that produced no data (not synthesised, insufficient material, assay QC failed) stay in the round with their reasons, so the next round is not designed on survivors only.

5.8States and failure

SituationWhat the UI does
Most generations rejectedReports the rejection profile and points at the constraint most likely responsible, before offering to re-run.
All candidates out-of-domainSays the predictor cannot speak to this chemical space, and offers to adapt the model on the user's own series (Spec 03 §3.2.2) rather than returning numbers anyway.
No candidate clears every gateSays so plainly and shows the closest misses with the gate each fails. Never quietly relaxes a safety floor to fill a shortlist.
Predictions contradict measurementsThe predicted-versus-measured panel flags the divergence and offers a refit; the surrogate is not trusted silently after a bad round.
Claimed chemical spaceCandidates inside a claim are marked, with the claim cited, and excluded from the shortlist by default.
Scoring model is pre-trained, not oursThe prediction still names its model and version, and the model object says its evidence is published rather than held out on our data (Spec 03 §3.2.4).

5.9Copy rules

  1. Never one score. Objectives keep their units and their targets.
  2. Weights are the user's, and are always stated.
  3. Rejections stay visible — with the gate that rejected them.
  4. A prediction names its model, version and confidence, and out-of-domain is a verdict, not a footnote.
  5. Counts of what failed are reported, because the failure profile is the diagnostic.
