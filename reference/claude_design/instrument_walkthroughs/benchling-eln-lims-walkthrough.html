Compounds & assays — from the lab notebook to a safety profile

Turn recorded compounds and assay numbers into a ranked, safety-screened shortlist.

Benchling is the system of record where your team keeps compounds (as SMILES) and the assay numbers measured against them. BioMate's drug-discovery campaign ingests those results, predicts each compound's ADMET / safety properties, ranks them, and keeps the record — so you never re-type a molecule or an IC50.

Benchling is a cloud service, so reading from it means data leaves your organisation — that residency is yours to account for before you connect.

The problem

A list of compounds is not a decision — and potency alone will not tell you what is safe.

Benchling records the molecules and the numbers measured on them, but it cannot tell you which to make next or which are likely to be unsafe. Most candidates fail not because they are weak, but because they behave badly in the body.

1 · RECORD, NOT RANKING

A registry, not a verdict

Benchling keeps compounds and their assay numbers faithfully — but it does not say which molecules are worth pursuing, or which carry risk.

2 · POTENCY ISN’T SAFETY

Most candidates fail on ADMET

Drugs fail on poor absorption, fast clearance, or heart-rhythm risk from hERG — not on potency. A potent molecule can still be unviable.

3 · RE-TYPING LEAKS

Provenance leaks

Copying compounds and IC50s by hand loses the tie to the notebook entry and the record of what was actually measured.

The predictive workflow is a real, established capability. The goal is to feed it your measured data and read the safety flags.

The ideas you need first

Four ideas behind compounds-to-ADMET.

From a Benchling record to a ranked, safety-screened shortlist is a short chain. These four ideas cover all of it.

1 · SMILES

The molecule as text

A SMILES string encodes each compound's structure — the input the ADMET workflow reads for every molecule.

2 · ASSAY RESULT

The measured half

A measured number per compound — e.g. an IC50 in nanomolar (lower means more potent) — tied to a compound and an assay type.

3 · THE CAMPAIGN

A running project

A drug-discovery campaign ingests assays, runs Predict ADMET Properties (AI), and ranks compounds — measured beside predicted.

4 · CLOUD RESIDENCY

Where the data lives

Benchling is a cloud service — data leaves your organisation when BioMate reads from it. For restricted data, that is yours to own.

ADMET = Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, Toxicity — the properties that decide whether a potent molecule can become a safe, usable drug.

Step 1 · What you provide

A real Benchling connection, plus the compounds and assays to screen.

Once Benchling is connected (see Tutorial 6) with a real API key, the campaign ingests your compounds and their measured assay values — so you do not re-type them.

1 · A REAL API KEY

Not mock mode

Connect with an API key / token for your tenant (e.g. yourorg.benchling.com) — not a host/port. Without a real key you see synthetic placeholder compounds, not your molecules.

2 · THE COMPOUNDS

SMILES + identifiers

Each compound arrives with its SMILES, plus a batch number, concentration, and plate position.

3 · THE MEASURED ASSAYS

e.g. IC50 in nM

The measured number per compound (IC50 = potency), tied to an assay type — the measured half of the ranking.

Step 2 · How a run works

Pull from Benchling — or just ask — then confirm.

Open or create a campaign and let it ingest the assay results (lims/poll → ingest-assay), or launch the prediction directly in chat — for example, “Run ADMET screening on these compounds” with the SMILES list.

Campaign ingests assays / plain request→ SMILES feed Predict ADMET Properties (AI)→ Review & confirm→ Runs on cloud compute
  • The workflow is a real named one. Predict ADMET Properties (AI) (predict_admet_properties) — graph-neural-network models (ADMET-AI, Swanson et al. 2023) predicting ~15 endpoints, never “undefined.”
  • You review before anything runs. Settings are shown and editable, and predictions never overwrite your measured Benchling values — they sit alongside them.
  • Then Confirm & Run. BioMate predicts the ~15 ADMET endpoints for every compound on cloud computers; a configured safety gate (e.g. hERG) flags crossing compounds rather than silently ranking them high.

Step 3 · The result — measured beside predicted

One campaign ranking — measured potency beside predicted safety.

The campaign orders compounds by combining their measured assay values with predicted ADMET, and the two-tab report explains the run in plain language.

  • The compound ranking: compounds ordered by combining measured assay values (from Benchling) with predicted ADMET — the top of the list is where the team looks first.
  • ADMET columns per compound: hERG (heart ion-channel risk) is the one to watch; solubility, CYP inhibition, and blood–brain-barrier flag absorption, drug–drug interaction, and brain exposure.
  • The two-tab report (Tutorial 3): Summary — plain-language findings built from the real output files — plus Outputs (the per-compound predictions) and the reproducibility record.
  • Export: a CRO order and, further along, the regulatory IND document — the formal hand-off to a CRO or regulator.

Step 4 · Reading and trusting it

Confirm you’re on real data, read hERG yourself, and account for the cloud.

The ADMET workflow is a core BioMate capability — but a result is only defensible once you know it is your data and you have read the safety numbers yourself.

  • Confirm you are not in mock mode. The Benchling connector falls back to synthetic data without a real API key — generic BNG-0001-style IDs and made-up IC50s. Check a real key is configured before trusting anything.
  • Predictions are estimates, not measurements. The workflow states its own limits: training data is biased toward drug-like small molecules and may not generalise to unusual chemistry. Use them to prioritise, then confirm in the lab.
  • The hERG safety gate is not confirmed to fire on every live run. Read the hERG value yourself; don’t assume the gate caught everything.
  • Cloud residency is yours to own. Reading from Benchling means data leaves your organisation’s own systems; account for that with restricted or patient data.
  • Predictions sit beside measurements, never replace them. Your measured Benchling values remain the ground truth; ADMET is an added layer, kept with parameters and versions as a record.
