Mass spectra — from raw peaks to an answer

Turn LC-MS spectra into proteins or metabolites, and how they differ.

Your mass spectrometer weighs whatever elutes off the column and records millions of (m/z, retention time, intensity) measurements as vendor raw files. BioMate reads what you measured — proteins or small molecules — and how you acquired it, then routes the run to the matching pipeline: nf-core/proteomicslfq, nf-core/diaproteomics, or xcms_lc_ms_preprocessing.

What you do with spectra depends entirely on what you measured and your acquisition mode — and that is exactly what decides which pipeline runs.

Raw / mzML spectra→ Proteins or metabolites · DDA/DIA→ Matched pipeline→ Quant & differential tables
The problem

Raw spectra are not an answer — and what you measured decides the pipeline.

A raw file of millions of peaks means nothing until the spectra are identified, quantified, and compared. And “identified” means something different for proteins, for metabolites, and for DDA versus DIA — feed DIA data to a DDA pipeline and you get a confident wrong or empty answer.

1 · PEAKS, NOT RESULTS

A chain, not a tool

Conversion, identification, feature-based quantification, QC, and statistics must be chained before a single protein or feature exists.

2 · THE MODE DECIDES

Same instrument, different spectra

Proteins → search a FASTA. Small molecules → detect features. DDA → top peaks; DIA → wide windows. Each picks the path.

3 · FILES BY HAND

Provenance leaks

Moving raw files between tools loses the FASTA, the FDR thresholds, and the record of what produced each number.

The pipelines are real, indexed BioMate workflows. The goal is to route to the right one for your mode and run it consistently.

Background — the problem in one line
The ideas you need first

Four ideas behind spectra-to-biology.

From a finished LC-MS run to a readable result is a multi-step but well-trodden chain. These four ideas cover all of it.

1 · m/z & RT

The measurement

Mass-to-charge m/z at a retention time, with intensity — plus MS/MS fragments that help identify each peak.

2 · DDA vs DIA

The hinge

DDA fragments the top precursors; DIA fragments everything in wide windows. They need different pipelines — know yours.

3 · THE PIPELINE

A tested chain

Not one tool: nf-core/proteomicslfq (OpenMS + MSstats), nf-core/diaproteomics (OpenSWATH), or xcms_lc_ms_preprocessing.

4 · FASTA vs FEATURES

What you compare

Proteomics searches peptides against a FASTA database. Metabolomics has no database — it detects features (one m/z at one RT).

The acquisition mode is the hinge: proteins route to a FASTA search, small molecules to feature detection, DDA and DIA to different engines.

Your spectra, plus the one thing your analysis needs.

Once the LC-MS instrument is connected (see Tutorial 6), BioMate surfaces a New data event per finished run with its vendor and experiment-type badge — or you upload a converted .mzML by hand (Tutorial 4).

1 · YOUR SPECTRA

Raw or mzML

Thermo .raw, Waters .raw, Bruker .d, SCIEX .wiff — or a vendor-neutral .mzML, which most pipelines actually read.

2 · THE DATABASE OR POLARITY

One analysis detail

Proteomics: a FASTA protein database for your organism. Metabolomics: the ion polarity (positive / negative).

3 · THE ACQUISITION MODE

If the type is a guess

Step 2 · How a run works

Launch from the event — or just ask — then confirm.

Click Launch on an LC-MS data event, or describe the goal with the acquisition mode — for example, “DDA label-free proteomics on these Thermo raw files, search the human UniProt FASTA, treated vs control,” or “untargeted metabolomics with XCMS on these mzML, positive mode.”

Data event / plain request→ Mode routes the pipeline→ Review & confirm→ Runs on cloud compute
  • The pipeline is chosen for you from the mode. DDA → nf-core/proteomicslfq, DIA → nf-core/diaproteomics, small molecules → xcms_lc_ms_preprocessing — a real named pipeline, never “undefined.”
  • You review before anything runs. Inputs are shown, filled in and editable — attach the spectra (input), the FASTA database, set labeling_reagent or polarity.
  • Then Confirm & Run. Expect a long cloud run — phases move pending → running → completed, and a mid-run QC gate pauses-and-reports; it never silently passes a failed check.
Nothing runs until you confirm
Step 3 · The result — matched to what you measured

One After-run report, shaped by proteins or metabolites.

The run returns a report matched to the data: quantified proteins and differential abundance for proteomics, a feature table for metabolomics.

  • Proteomics: how many proteins identified, and which are differentially abundant by fold change + FDR / adjusted p-value (a common cut is FDR < 0.05).
  • Outputs: the protein and peptide quantification matrices, the differential-abundance table, and QC plots (MultiQC / PTXQC).
  • Metabolomics: a feature table (each row = one m/z × retention-time feature, intensity per sample), plus putative HMDB/KEGG annotations.
  • Downloadable tables and reports for your figures and supplement.
Treat every result as experimental until checked
Step 4 · Reading and trusting it

Real pipelines, QC you must read, and an audit trail — but no E2E validation yet.

The workflows are real, indexed pipelines applied the same way each run. But this is the weakest-evidence instrument of the set — there is no recorded real end-to-end run on the dev site, so what makes a result usable is the QC you read and the record you keep.

  • Read the QC first. Proteomics: a sensible number of IDs for your sample type and clean QC plots. Be suspicious of very few IDs — usually a wrong FASTA, wrong acquisition mode, or a bad .raw → .mzML conversion.
  • A feature or a call can be real or noise. In metabolomics, retention-time alignment should line the same feature up across samples; a feature present in only one of several replicates is usually noise, and a mass-only annotation is putative, not confirmed.
  • Engine licensing is yours to own. DIA-NN, MSFragger, and Spectronaut are not license-clean for commercial use; prefer proteomics_dda_search (Comet/Sage/mokapot) or OpenSWATH-based DIA if that applies.
  • A record you can defend. The FASTA, FDR thresholds, tolerances, software versions, and outputs are kept together as a reproducibility record.
Available — not yet validated end-to-end
The habit at a glance

Launch the LC-MS event → tell BioMate the mode and proteins-vs-metabolites → attach FASTA / mzML + sample table → run → read the quant, treating it as experimental.

The value is simple: raw spectra become proteins or metabolites with one request, through a real indexed pipeline for your mode, on cloud compute, with a record you can defend — as long as you treat the result as experimental until checked against a known dataset.
