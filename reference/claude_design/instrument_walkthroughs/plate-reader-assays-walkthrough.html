The problem

Raw wells are not an answer — and the assay decides the fit.

A grid of absorbance, fluorescence, or luminescence means nothing until a model is fitted to it. And the right model differs: a dilution series wants a dose–response curve, a screened plate wants QC and hit calling, a kinetic read wants a rate — use the wrong one and you get a confident wrong number.

1 · NUMBERS, NOT POTENCY

A fit, not a single well

One well can’t give an IC50 — potency is the shape of how response changes with dose. A model has to be fitted before a number exists.

2 · THE ASSAY DECIDES

Same plate, different fit

Dilution series → IC50/EC50. Whole screened plate → hits + Z′. Kinetic read → rate. The assay type picks the path.

3 · FILES BY HAND

Provenance leaks

Copying plate exports between tools loses the model, the column mapping, and the record of the software version that produced each fit.

The fits are community-standard statistics. The goal is to route to the right one, map your columns, and keep the record.

Background — the problem in one line
The ideas you need first

Four ideas behind a plate-to-potency run.

From a finished plate read to a readable IC50 is a short, well-trodden chain. These four ideas cover all of it.

1 · THE SIGNAL

What the well holds

Absorbance (OD), fluorescence, or luminescence, read once (endpoint) or over time (kinetic). Kinetic reads give rates.

2 · ASSAY TYPE

The hinge

Dose–response, whole-plate screen, or enzyme kinetics. This is what routes the plate to a fit.

3 · THE FIT

A named model

Dose Response fits a 4PL / Hill (LL.4) curve — or LL.5 (5PL) for asymmetric — reporting top, bottom, slope, and IC50.

4 · Z′-FACTOR

Is the plate any good

A 0–1 assay-quality score from your positive/negative controls. It tells you whether to trust the plate at all.

The assay type is the hinge: a dilution series routes to IC50, a screened plate to hits, a kinetic read to a rate.

Background — the vocabulary of plate-reader analysis
Step 1 · What you provide

An exported plate, plus the columns your fit needs.

Once the plate reader is connected (see Tutorial 6), finished plates arrive as New data events — or bring one .csv in by hand (Tutorial 4). Either way, the fit needs to know which column is dose and which is response.

1 · AN EXPORTED PLATE

From Gen5 / MARS / SoftMax Pro

A .csv/.txt/.xlsx grid of wells × values, often with a header naming the wavelength, read type, and plate format.

2 · THE COLUMN MAPPING

Dose and response

A concentration column (your doses) and a response column (the signal). Know your units (µM, nM) and whether the effect is inhibition or activation.

3 · CONTROLS (FOR A SCREEN)

Positive and negative wells

Include your control wells, correctly labelled — the Z′-factor QC needs them. Mislabeled controls give a misleading quality score.

The connector guesses the assay from the file name and header — that tag is a hint, so on Launch say what the assay was.

Step 2 · How a run works

Launch from the data event — or just ask — then confirm.

Click Launch on a plate event, or describe the goal the way you would to a colleague — for example, “fit a 4-parameter dose–response and report the IC50 for this inhibition assay; concentration in dose_uM, signal in pct_inhibition.”

Data event / plain request→ Assay type routes the fit→ Review & confirm→ Runs (fast)
  • The fit is chosen from the assay. Dose–response → Dose Response, a screen → Hts Plate Qc Hit Calling, a kinetic read → Analyze Enzyme Kinetics Assay — a real named workflow, never “undefined.”
  • You review before anything runs. The panel shows concentration_col, response_col, model (LL.4), and response_type (inhibition/activation) — filled in and editable.
  • Then Confirm & Run. This is fast — curve fitting on a small table, not a heavy pipeline — so the result comes back quickly.
Nothing runs until you confirm
Step 3 · The result — matched to the assay

One After-run report, shaped by what you ran.

The run returns a report matched to the assay: a potency for a dose–response, a hit list for a screen, a rate for a kinetic read.

  • Dose–response: the IC50 / EC50 (half-maximal concentration) and Hill slope, plus the fitted 4PL parameters and curve points.
  • Screen: a hit list against your threshold, per-well % activity, and the plate’s Z′-factor (quality).
  • Enzyme kinetics: the reaction rate, and Km / Vmax from the kinetic slope.
  • Downloadable fitted tables and curve points for your figures, filed under the producing step.
Real workflows — not yet validated end-to-end
Step 4 · Reading and trusting it

Check Z′ first, read the curve shape, and verify the first number yourself.

The fits are standard statistics applied the same way each run. What makes an IC50 defensible is the QC you read, the shape of the curve, and — because this path is not yet validated end-to-end — a first check against your usual tool.

  • Read the Z′-factor first (screening). > 0.5 = an excellent, robust assay; 0–0.5 = marginal; < 0 = your positive and negative controls overlap, so don’t trust hits from that plate. It is the single most important QC number in plate screening.
  • The curve shape sets whether the IC50 is real. A trustworthy IC50 sits where you have data on both the top and bottom plateaus. If your highest dose never plateaued, the IC50 is an extrapolation — treat it as “greater than your top dose.” An IC50 from triplicate points is solid; from single points, indicative only.
  • Available, but not yet validated. These are real, indexed workflows, but there is no recorded real end-to-end plate-reader run yet — verify your first IC50 against GraphPad/Prism or your usual tool before trusting it.
  • A record you can defend. The model, the mapped columns, and the software version are kept together as a reproducibility record.

The habit at a glance

Launch the plate event → confirm the right fit → map dose and response columns → run → read the IC50, checking Z′ and curve shape.

The value is simple: a plate of numbers becomes a potency with one request, through the community-standard fit for your assay, with a record you can document — once you’ve read the Z′ and cross-checked the first result.
