Idea · 1 · The task — what ADMET predicts, and why hERG leads Concept

ADMET asks: will the body even tolerate this molecule?

Binding the target is not enough. A molecule must be absorbed, distributed where it should go, not destroyed too fast, cleared cleanly, and poison nothing. Those five questions are ADMET. One toxicity endpoint, hERG, outranks the rest: block that cardiac potassium channel and you risk a fatal arrhythmia — it has killed approved drugs.

Any harm? incl. hERG

A molecule can bind its target beautifully and still fail here — which is why ADMET comes early, before you spend money on anything expensive. The input is the structure (a SMILES string); the output is a number per endpoint.

Idea · 2 · Why it is hard — and what good looks like Concept

The structure–property map is non-linear, the labels are sparse, and new scaffolds break shortcuts.

Drug-like chemical space is estimated at roughly 10⁶⁰ molecules; measured ADMET labels number in the thousands-to-hundreds-of-thousands per endpoint, and the assays carry their own noise. A good model is one that generalizes to chemotypes it has never seen — the hard part — not one that memorizes a training table.

VAST SPACE

~10⁶⁰ molecules

You can never enumerate it. The model must interpolate from a tiny labeled fraction.

SPARSE, NOISY LABELS

Thousands per endpoint

Public measurements are limited and assay-dependent; the same compound can read differently across labs.

THE HARD PART

Chemotype generalization

A novel scaffold far from training data is where estimates are least reliable — exactly where you most want them.

ASYMMETRIC COST

One miss can be fatal

A missed hERG liability is not a rounding error; the method must flag, not silently pass.

"State of the art" here means a model that is data-driven and reports calibrated per-endpoint scores it can defend on held-out chemistry, with its applicability domain made explicit. Performance is reported per endpoint, dataset, and split — not as one headline number.

Idea · 3 · The classical baseline — QSAR on molecular descriptors Concept

First, the method it must beat: hand-engineered descriptors into a shallow model.

Classical QSAR (quantitative structure–activity relationship) turns each molecule into a fixed vector of pre-defined numbers — logP, molecular weight, ring counts, fingerprint bits — then fits a shallow learner such as a random forest to map that vector to the endpoint. BioMate ships this too (episode D1: Qsar Rf Model, herg_cardiac_safety_qsar). It is fast, cheap, and interpretable — and it is a real, strong baseline.

Strengths — and the ceiling

Interpretable (you can read which descriptor drove the call), fast to train, and data-efficient on a single endpoint. But the features are fixed in advance: any structural signal the descriptor set does not encode is invisible, and extrapolation to genuinely new scaffolds is weak.

This is a strong baseline the deep model has to beat — not a strawman.

Idea · 4 · The deep-learning model — a message-passing graph network Concept

Let the molecule be a graph, and let the model learn its own features.

A molecule is a graph: atoms are nodes, bonds are edges. A message-passing neural network (Chemprop's directed MPNN) passes information along the bonds for several rounds, so each atom's representation absorbs its chemical neighbourhood — then pools to a whole-molecule vector and reads off every endpoint. Nothing is hand-engineered; the features are learned from data. This is what BioMate's ADMET-AI workflow runs.

What BioMate actually runs

ADMET-AI (Swanson et al., Bioinformatics 2024) bundles pre-trained Chemprop graph models covering 15 endpoints — hERG, DILI, BBB, Caco-2, the CYP panel, solubility, PPBR, half-life, LD50 and more — trained on 2M+ experimental measurements from ChEMBL and the Therapeutics Data Commons.

On the TDC benchmark it reports AUROC > 0.85 on 20 of 31 classification tasks — strong, uneven across endpoints, and still an estimator. The model is a frozen, published artifact; BioMate runs it, it does not retrain it per request.

Idea · 5 · Classical vs deep — when each wins Concept

Not "deep is always better" — two tools with different trade-offs.

The graph network usually wins on accuracy across diverse chemistry and gives you many endpoints from one model; the descriptor+forest baseline wins on interpretability, speed, and tiny-data regimes. Both are estimators of experimental values, and both need wet-lab confirmation before any decision that matters.

CLASSICAL · QSAR + RANDOM FOREST

Hand-made features, shallow model

+ interpretable, fast, data-efficient on one endpoint, strong when data is scarce.
− fixed features miss unencoded signal; weak on novel scaffolds; one model per endpoint.

DEEP · CHEMPROP GRAPH NETWORK

Learned features from the graph

+ learns its own representation; 15 endpoints at once; better across diverse chemistry.
− needs large training data; less directly interpretable; least reliable off its training distribution.

BioMate keeps both in the catalog and routes to the model that fits the request. For broad ADMET screening on drug-like compounds it defaults to the deep ADMET-AI workflow — the one the rest of this episode runs.

Idea · 7 · How to read the ADMET results Concept

Read the gate and the per-compound scores — not any single number in isolation.

The native hERG output is a blocker-risk probability; the IC50 you see is an estimate derived from that probability (a power law anchored to terfenadine), not a measured value. A low-risk compound's IC50 sits at the estimator's ceiling — a model output, not proof of safety. Here is how to hold these numbers.

ESTIMATES, NOT ASSAYS

Validation required

Every value is a model prediction. Advance nothing on it without wet-lab confirmation (hERG patch-clamp, hepatocyte/ALT-AST panels, Ames).

KNOW THE EDGE

Training-data bias

The network generalizes from ChEMBL/TDC chemistry. The further a scaffold sits from that space, the less you should trust the estimate.

TRUST THE GATE

Flag, never silent-pass

The gate compares a worst-case value to a cited threshold and halts or flags. It discriminated here — silent on safe chemistry, halting on the real liability.

The defensible output is not a single IC50 — it is a per-compound table, a cited gate decision, and a reproducibility record tying both back to the exact model and inputs. Read in that order.
