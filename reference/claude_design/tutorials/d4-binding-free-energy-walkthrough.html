Which analog binds harder? FEP ranks them before you touch the bench.

You have a lead and a handful of close analogs — the same scaffold, one group swapped. The question that governs the whole round is simple and expensive: which one binds the target more tightly? Relative binding free energy — FEP — answers it with physics, in silico, so you synthesize and assay the winners instead of the whole series. The problem this solves

Lead optimization is a ranking problem — and docking scores can't rank this finely.

In a hit-to-lead round you have one scaffold and many small variations. You can't make them all. You need to know, before synthesis, which swap gains potency and which loses it — a difference often smaller than one kcal per mole. That is exactly the regime where fast scores fall short.

The ideas you need first

Four ideas, and the whole method follows.

You don't need the statistical mechanics — just these four concepts, in order.

1 · ΔG

Binding free energy

How favorably a ligand binds the target. More negative means tighter binding. It's the quantity that maps to potency.

2 · ΔΔG

Relative, between two analogs

FEP computes the difference in ΔG between two close ligands — not either absolute value. That difference is what ranks them.

3 · ALCHEMICAL

Morph A into B in silico

A non-physical path that gradually transforms one ligand into the other through intermediate λ states — the trick that makes ΔΔG computable.

4 · THERMO CYCLE

Close the loop

Run the transformation both in the pocket and free in solvent; the cycle isolates the binding part and gives an internal consistency check.

The whole method is: build a map between the analogs, morph one into the other along λ windows in the pocket and in water, and read the ΔΔG from the difference.

The real FEP workflow, set up for you
Inputs · what you bring

Three inputs: the target, a reference ligand, and the analog you're testing.

FEP is comparative, so the inputs are a pair of related ligands in the same binding site. From those, BioMate builds the map that links them — the perturbation network — automatically.

RECEPTOR

The target structure

A protein structure as a PDB file (receptor) with the binding site defined by a bound pose. The starting structure matters — FEP assumes it's right.

LIGAND A → B

The congeneric pair

ligand_a is your reference (known), ligand_b the query analog — each an SDF. They must be close relatives: same scaffold, a local change.

THE NETWORK

Built for you

LOMAP finds the atom-mapping between the two and plans a minimal-spanning ligand network — the edges FEP will actually compute.

Required: receptor, ligand_a, ligand_b. A series is handled as a set of pairwise edges across the network.

The calculation · what it actually simulates

Prepare, map, then sample the transformation across λ windows.

The three stages are real and sequential. Preparation and setup are fast and run on CPU; the sampling itself is the heavy, GPU-bound step that produces the number.

1 · PREP

Clean, 3D ligands

RDKit standardizes both ligands and generates 3D conformers (ETKDG). The protein is prepared inside OpenFE's handling during setup.

2 · RBFE_SETUP

Map + protocol

LOMAP atom-mapping and the ligand network, then the alchemical protocol and its task graph — built but not yet sampled. No ΔΔG here, by design.

3 · OPENFE_ANALYSIS

Sample, then estimate

OpenMM molecular dynamics across the λ windows on GPU, then MBAR gathers the per-edge ΔΔG ± SEM from the overlapping states.

Defaults you can tune: temperature 298.15 K, equilibration 1 ns, production 5 ns per λ window, 11 λ windows, 3 independent repeats (for the SEM), LOMAP max3d 1.0 Å.

Real OpenMM sampling across λ windows → MBAR estimate — not a scoring heuristic
The output · a ranked, defensible number

Per-edge ΔΔG with an uncertainty — and cycle closure shows which edges are solid.

The result is a free-energy difference for each edge, with the Monte-Carlo error that tells you whether to trust it. A negative ΔΔG means the query analog binds more tightly than the reference; a positive one, less. The sign and size are what order your series.

ddg_summary.json

ΔΔG ± SEM, per edge

The per-edge relative binding free energy in kcal/mol with its standard error, plus the network and mapping quality. Negative → ligand_b binds stronger; positive → weaker. Rank the series by these values.

QC GATE

Cycle closure

Across a closed loop of ≥3 edges, the ΔΔG's should sum to zero; the deviation is a built-in convergence check (gate at ~1 kcal/mol), so you can see which edges are solid across the network.

  • The error travels with the number. Each ΔΔG carries an MBAR uncertainty, so a tight, converged edge is distinguishable from a noisy one.
  • Every number ships with its uncertainty. The converged, low-error edges are the ones you rank and act on — a call the loop-closure check confirms across the whole network.

Using the result · decide, then confirm at the bench

Prioritize what to synthesize — and treat the number as a prediction to validate.

FEP earns its cost by reshaping the synthesis queue: make the analogs it ranks as gains, deprioritize the losses. It is a prediction, so the loop closes at the assay, and the workflow carries a built-in way to check the method against known answers.

PRIORITIZE

Reorder the queue

Synthesize the edges predicted to gain affinity; park the ones predicted to lose it. The round's chemistry effort goes where the physics says it will pay.

VALIDATE

Confirm by assay

The measured potency of the compounds you make is the ground truth. FEP narrows the list; the wet lab confirms the winners.

CALIBRATE

Check against knowns

A built-in validate option compares an edge to a published experimental ΔΔG (OpenFE benchmark pairs, e.g. Tyk2 / CDK2) so you can see the method's accuracy on your setup.

As a general expectation, well-converged small-molecule RBFE lands within roughly a kcal/mol of experiment — enough to rank — but that depends on the system, the pose, the force field, and convergence, and must be checked, not assumed.

What you get, precisely

Precise about scope, so you know exactly what you get.

FEP is powerful and precise. Knowing exactly what it gives you is what makes it trustworthy for decisions you'll defend — and work you'll publish.

RELATIVE, NOT ABSOLUTE

Differences between analogs

RBFE gives ΔΔG between congeneric pairs — it ranks a series against a reference. It is not an absolute affinity, and it needs the ligands to be close relatives with a good atom-mapping.

POSE & SAMPLING

Garbage pose, garbage number

It assumes a correct starting structure and binding pose, and enough sampling to converge. A wrong pose or under-sampling gives a confident but wrong ΔΔG; the SEM and cycle-closure checks are there to catch it.

COST & COMPUTE

Hours on a GPU, per edge

Production RBFE is compute-intensive: a GPU and hours of MD per edge. Accuracy depends on the force field and on convergence — this is a deliberate, expensive calculation, not a quick filter.

Specific and honest — a physics tool with stated assumptions, not a black box
