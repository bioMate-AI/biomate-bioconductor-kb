Does it fit the pocket? Docking places the ligand and scores the pose.

You have a target structure and a molecule you hope will bind it. Before any assay, one question comes first: can this ligand sit in the pocket at all, and in what orientation? Molecular docking searches for the ligand's best pose inside the binding site and scores each one, so you can triage a list down to the candidates worth pursuing. The problem this solves

You can't co-crystallize every candidate — you need a fast way to ask "could this bind?"

Early in a campaign you hold a target structure and dozens or thousands of candidate molecules. Solving a structure for each is impossible, and wet assays cost time and material. You need a cheap, physics-aware way to predict whether a molecule can occupy the pocket, and how, so you spend effort on the plausible ones.

The ideas you need first

Four ideas, and the whole method follows.

You don't need the scoring-function mathematics — just these four concepts, in order.

1 · THE POCKET

Where binding happens

A protein has a cavity — the binding site — where a ligand sits. Docking needs to know where to look: a search box centred on that pocket.

2 · THE POSE

A placed orientation

A pose is one concrete way the ligand sits in the pocket: its position, rotation, and rotatable-bond conformation. Docking searches over many.

3 · THE SCORE

How good the fit looks

A scoring function estimates binding affinity in kilocalories per mole. More negative means a predicted tighter fit — it is how poses are ranked.

4 · SEARCH

Explore, then rank

The engine samples many poses, scores each, and returns the best-scoring ones. More search effort means a more thorough exploration.

The whole method is: point the engine at the pocket with a box, let it search for low-energy poses, and read the ranked scores — more negative being the better-looking fit.

Inputs · what you bring

Three things: the ligands, the target, and where the pocket is.

Docking is site-directed by default, so you tell the engine where to look. Supply the molecules, the receptor structure, and a box around the binding site — and, if you want a confidence check, a reference ligand to validate the pose against.

LIGANDS

The molecules to dock

A smiles_list — one or many candidates as SMILES strings. The engine builds 3D conformers and searches poses for each.

RECEPTOR + BOX

The target and the pocket

The target as a receptor_pdb_file, with the pocket defined by box_center and box_size — the search box the engine samples inside.

REFERENCE (OPTIONAL)

A pose to check against

A reference_ligand_smiles switches on validation mode: the docked pose is compared to the known crystal pose and the deviation reported.

Don't know the pocket? Blind docking. Run Autosite detects cavities (pdb_file, spacing default 1.0) to define the box, or Run Diffdock With Smiles — a diffusion model — docks without a predefined box (pdb_path, smiles_string, use_gpu). Site-directed Vina is the default here.

The method · what it actually does

Prepare, search the box, then score and rank the poses.

The steps are concrete and sequential. Vina builds a receptor grid, searches ligand poses inside the box with a stochastic optimizer, and scores each with its empirical affinity function — returning the best few, ranked.

1 · PREP

3D ligand + receptor grid

Each SMILES is turned into a 3D structure; the receptor and the search box define the grid the engine will score poses on.

2 · SEARCH

Sample poses in the box

Vina's optimizer explores positions, rotations, and rotatable bonds inside the box. exhaustiveness (default 8) sets how thorough that search is.

3 · SCORE & RANK

Affinity per pose

Each kept pose gets a Vina binding-affinity score in kcal/mol; n_poses (default 9) are returned, ranked from the most negative — the best-looking fit.

Modes: site-directed uses your box; validation adds a reference_ligand_smiles and reports the docked pose's R-M-S-D to the known crystal pose — a pass is a deviation of ≤ 2.0 Å.

Real pose search with Vina's empirical scoring — poses ranked, not a one-shot guess
The output · ranked poses with scores

Ranked docked poses, each with an affinity score — and an RMSD when you validate.

The result is a set of placed poses for each ligand, ranked by a Vina binding-affinity score in kcal/mol, with the pose coordinates you can inspect. When you supply a reference ligand, the pose also carries its RMSD to the crystal structure, so you can judge whether the geometry is trustworthy before you trust the score.

RANKED POSES

Score + coordinates, per pose

Each docked pose carries a Vina binding-affinity score in kcal/mol — more negative is a predicted tighter fit — plus the 3D coordinates of the placement. Rank your candidates by the best score each achieves.

VALIDATION

Pose R-M-S-D

With a reference_ligand_smiles, the docked pose is compared to the crystal pose and its R-M-S-D reported. A deviation of ≤ 2.0 Å is the conventional pass — the engine recovered the known geometry.

  • The pose travels with the score. You never get a bare number: the coordinates let you see how and where the ligand was placed, which is half the interpretation.
  • Validation mode gives a confidence anchor. When the reference pose is recovered within 2.0 Å, the scoring on related ligands is far more credible than from a blind setup.

Using the result · triage, then confirm

Shortlist what to pursue — and treat the score as a hypothesis, not a measurement.

Docking earns its keep by cutting a long list down to a credible shortlist. Rank by score, inspect the top poses for sensible geometry, and carry the survivors forward — to a more rigorous free-energy calculation, or to the bench. The number is a hypothesis of a pose, so it is a filter, not a verdict.

TRIAGE

Rank and shortlist

Order the candidates by their best Vina score, drop the obvious non-binders, and keep the top poses whose geometry makes chemical sense in the pocket.

ESCALATE

Hand off to FEP/RBFE

To rank close analogs by potency, docking is too coarse — pass the shortlist to the free-energy workflow (the sibling Binding Free Energy tutorial) for the fine ranking.

VALIDATE

Confirm in the lab

The pose and score are predictions. Co-crystallography and binding assays are the ground truth; docking tells you which compounds are worth that cost.

A good practice: run validation mode on a ligand with a known crystal pose first. If Vina recovers it within 2.0 Å, you have evidence the box and setup are sound before you trust scores on new molecules.

A sharper shortlist — escalated to free energy or the bench, not acted on blind
What you get, precisely

Precise about scope, so you know exactly what you get.

Docking is fast and useful, and knowing exactly where it is reliable — and where it is not — is what makes it trustworthy as a triage tool rather than a false oracle.

A GATE, NOT A RULER

Separates, doesn't finely rank

Vina scores separate binders from non-binders well, but are unreliable for the fine affinity ranking of close analogs. For that, use the FEP/RBFE free-energy workflow — the sibling Binding Free Energy tutorial.

RIGID BY DEFAULT

Limited induced-fit

The receptor is treated as rigid by default, so pocket rearrangement on binding — induced fit — is largely not captured. A pocket that reshapes around a ligand can be mis-scored.

A POSE HYPOTHESIS

Predicted, not measured

A good score is a hypothesis of a pose, not a measured affinity. And blind docking — without a known pocket — is lower-confidence than docking into a defined site; validate the geometry before trusting it.

Specific and honest — a fast triage tool with stated assumptions, not a measured affinity
