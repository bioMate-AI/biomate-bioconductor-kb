How long will this formulation hold? Accelerated stability projects it before the shelf clock runs out.

A drug product has to stay potent and clean for its whole shelf-life — but a real-time two-year study takes two years. Accelerated stability borrows a physics shortcut: stress the formulation at higher temperature and humidity, measure how fast it degrades, and project back to the real-time condition. BioMate runs this as the Analyze Accelerated Stability Of Pharmaceutical Formulations workflow — domain drug_discovery — using Arrhenius temperature acceleration plus an empirical humidity factor, following the logic of I-C-H Q1A accelerated-stability study design. The problem this solves

Shelf-life is a waiting problem — and you cannot wait two years on every candidate recipe.

Early in development you may have several candidate formulations of the same active ingredient — different excipient systems, different concentrations. Each needs a shelf-life, and the definitive answer is a real-time study at the label condition, which runs for the product's full claimed life. You cannot afford to wait that long on recipes you may discard, so you need a defensible early estimate of which formulations are robust and which degrade.

The ideas you need first

Four ideas, and the whole method follows.

You don't need the full physical chemistry — just these four concepts, in order.

1 · DEGRADATION

Potency falls over time

The active ingredient breaks down and degradants accumulate. Shelf-life is how long potency stays within spec and degradants stay below limit.

2 · ARRHENIUS

Heat speeds reactions

A temperature-activated reaction runs faster when warmer, by a predictable factor. That factor lets a short hot study stand in for a long cool one.

3 · HUMIDITY

Moisture matters too

Relative humidity — R-H — drives hydrolysis and moisture uptake. An empirical humidity factor adjusts the rate alongside temperature.

4 · PROJECTION

Extrapolate to real time

Combine the two factors to translate the stressed rate back to the label condition, and project potency across the requested time points.

The whole method is: measure how fast it degrades under stress, correct for temperature with Arrhenius and for moisture with the humidity factor, and project the real-time behaviour at the storage condition you care about.

The method · what it actually models

Accelerate with temperature, adjust for humidity, then project across the time points.

For each formulation paired with each condition, the model works through three steps — the same logic that lets a short high-stress study estimate long-term real-time behaviour.

1 · ACCELERATE

Arrhenius temperature factor

Model the degradation-rate increase with temperature via the Arrhenius acceleration factor, so a run at 40°C or 50°C maps back to the rate at 25°C.

2 · ADJUST

Empirical humidity factor

Correct that rate for the condition's relative humidity, capturing the moisture contribution to degradation alongside temperature.

3 · PROJECT

Potency across time

Apply the combined rate to project the active-ingredient potency and degradant level across the requested time points, per formulation per condition.

Standard conditions: 25°C / 60% R-H long-term, 40°C / 75% R-H accelerated, 50°C / 90% R-H stress. Standard time points, in days: [0, 30, 60, 90, 180, 365]. Method basis: Arrhenius acceleration + empirical humidity factor, following I-C-H Q1A logic.

What you get, precisely

Precise about scope, so you know exactly what you get.

Arrhenius projection is a powerful shortcut, but it rests on assumptions. Knowing exactly where it holds is what makes it trustworthy for triage decisions — and keeps it in its proper place next to a real stability study.

ONE PATHWAY ASSUMED

A single dominant mechanism

Arrhenius extrapolation assumes a single dominant, temperature-activated degradation pathway. It breaks for phase changes, non-Arrhenius kinetics, or moisture-driven mechanisms outside the humidity term.

EMPIRICAL HUMIDITY

The moisture term is a model

The humidity factor is empirical, not first-principles. Where moisture drives degradation through a mechanism the term does not capture, the projection can mislead.

ESTIMATE, NOT CLAIM

Triage, not a regulatory substitute

This is an in-silico estimate to triage and rank formulations and design studies — not a regulatory substitute for a real-time I-C-H stability study.

Specific and honest — a kinetics shortcut with stated assumptions, not a shelf-life certificate
