The problem this replaces

Manual literature review is slow — and hard to audit.

Before any experiment, you need to know what is already established: is this target really linked to the disease? Has this side effect been reported? Today that means a scattered, hours-long read — and a conclusion a colleague has to take on trust.

THE COST

Hours, not seconds

Open a database, a search engine, a handful of papers — read, cross-check, and hope you didn't miss the one study that disagrees.

THE FRICTION

Scattered across tabs

Literature, curated databases, and the patent record each live somewhere different, in a different shape, behind a different search box.

THE RISK

No trail left behind

A summary without its sources is a claim you can't check — and can't defend in a lab meeting, a grant, or a review.

The question is never just "what's the answer" — it's "what is the answer, and how do I know it's right."

What a good answer looks like

Every claim carries the source it came from.

A BioMate answer isn't a chatbot paragraph — it's a small sourced report with a fixed shape, where each part earns its place and every factual sentence can be traced back to a real, openable source.

  • Numbered claims. Each factual sentence carries a marker (¹, ²) tying it to a source below. A sentence with no marker is context, written to read that way — never a cited fact in disguise.
  • A real source list. One row per source — the paper's title, its PubMed ID, and a link that opens it at the publisher. Not a bare list of links.
  • Open to verify. Each row opens the source at the publisher for the full text, so you check the answer rather than trust it.
  • How it was searched. The panel shows which databases were drawn on — Europe PMC, OpenAlex, PubMed — with how many results each returned and how many were kept, so the retrieval behind the answer is visible.

How to trust it

It cites real sources — and tells you what it does not cover.

The trust is built into the shape of the answer. BioMate reports what is known and shows its provenance; where the evidence is thin, it says so rather than dressing a weak claim in confident prose.

  • Real citations only. Every claim is tied to a source that actually exists and that you can open. BioMate reports what it found — it does not invent references.
  • It answers "what is known." The answer synthesises the published record and carries its sources with it — so the conclusion and its evidence travel together.
  • The boundary block is always shown. A short note states what the answer is not evidence for — here, that it synthesises published literature and is not a verdict on your own data.
  • Thin is said plainly. One source, or one lab, is reported as exactly that. A hedged answer is the honesty working — a claim you can weigh, not one you have to take on faith.
Provenance on every claim; an honest boundary on every answer
