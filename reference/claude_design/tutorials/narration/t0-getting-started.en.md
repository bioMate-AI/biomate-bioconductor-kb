# T0 Getting-started walkthrough narration (EN)

## Step 1 — Title · one doorway, one loop

BioMate can feel like a lot of features. But everything you'll ever do starts from one doorway and follows one simple loop: type a goal in plain words, check which lane it lands in, review what gets filled in, send it yourself, and read the result. Learn that loop once, and every other tutorial is just a richer version of it.

---

## Step 2 — The idea · what BioMate is

In one honest paragraph: BioMate is a cloud workbench for drug discovery and biology. Instead of installing software and wiring tools together yourself, you describe what you want in everyday language. BioMate then either answers from the scientific literature and databases, or runs a tested analysis on cloud computers — and it hands back the results with a record of how they were produced. Crucially, it always shows you what it's about to do before anything runs or costs money.

---

## Step 3 — The doorway · the cold start

Every new conversation opens on the cold start — your doorway. There's a single intent bar, where you type your goal; four module cards in the middle; and the composer, the message box, at the foot. Think of it as a reception desk: you say what you need, and it points you in the right direction. You can always get back here by starting a new chat from the sidebar.

---

## Step 4 — The four modules · the kinds of work

The four cards name the four kinds of work BioMate does, so you can recognise which one your goal belongs to. Search and evidence answers "what is known" with a cited reply. Data analysis runs a pipeline on your own data. Design and generate proposes new candidate molecules. And Models learns from your lab results to improve the next round. But here's the key point: you do not have to pick a module first. Just type your goal in the intent bar, and BioMate works out which kind of work it is.

---

## Step 5 — The two lanes · Instant vs Cloud

This is the single most useful thing to understand. BioMate sorts every request into one of two lanes, and it tells you which one before you send. Instant gives a cited answer in seconds — it reads the literature and replies in the chat, with no large computation and no pipeline bill. Cloud is a validated workflow that runs on cloud computers over minutes to hours, and it uses paid compute. The lane tells you the time and cost of your request up front — and BioMate will always confirm with you before it runs anything in the Cloud lane.

---

## Step 6 — The safety rule · nothing runs until you send

Here is the safety rule that applies everywhere in the product: nothing runs until you review and send. The module cards and the intent bar only seed — pre-fill — your message box. They never send on their own. You read what was filled in, adjust it, attach any data, and press Send yourself. Only then does anything happen.

---

## Step 7 — Step by step · asking your first question

Let's ask a real one. We'll use an evidence question, because it returns in seconds: what is the evidence linking the protein JAK1 to rheumatoid arthritis, and how strong is it? Type that into the intent bar. As you type, BioMate shows a short restatement — the lane it heard, here the Instant lane, shown as "Answer here, with sources", and a plain reading of the subject. It doesn't list tool names. Your goal lands in the composer; that's your chance to edit it. When it says what you mean, press Send.

---

## Step 8 — Reading it · the answer, and honest limits

An evidence answer is a small, sourced report: the text carries numbered markers tying each claim to a source, the citations open to the publisher, and a boundary block always states what the answer is not evidence for. And be honest about day-one limits. An evidence answer is literature, not a verdict on your own data. If you ask a patent question, the Patents framing is a doorway, not a patent search — BioMate does not search a patent database, so confirm freedom-to-operate with counsel. BioMate is built never to pad a section with invented content: an empty section means nothing to show honestly, not a bug.

---

## Step 9 — Closing · the one loop

So the habit is always the same: type a goal, check the lane, review, send, and read the result with its sources or its record. That's the whole doorway. From here, Tutorial 1 designs molecules, Tutorial 2 learns from lab results, and Tutorial 3 runs a full analysis workflow — but each one is just this same loop, with more at stake. Start smart, and let BioMate show you what it's about to do before anything runs.
