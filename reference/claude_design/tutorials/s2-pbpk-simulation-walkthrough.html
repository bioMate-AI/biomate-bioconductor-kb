1 · Idea · The task — a concentration–time curve Concept

Pharmacokinetics is what the body does to the drug over time.

After a dose, plasma concentration rises as the drug is absorbed, peaks, then falls as it is distributed, metabolised, and excreted. Reading that single curve gives you the numbers every dosing decision rests on: Cmax (the peak), Tmax (when it peaks), AUC (total exposure), and half-life (how fast it clears).

Task predict conc. vs time· Read-outs Cmax · Tmax · AUC · t½· Why dose & safety margin depend on it

Too low a peak and the drug never works; too high and it risks toxicity. The same curve, computed for different tissues (blood, liver, kidney) and different populations (adult, child, impaired), is what turns a molecule into a dosing plan.

2 · Idea · State of the art, and why PBPK is hard Concept

The hard part is the inputs and the equations — not drawing the curve.

A PBPK model is a system of coupled differential equations, one per organ, driven by physiology (blood flows, organ volumes) and by the molecule's own properties. The curve is only as good as those inputs — and most of them are themselves estimates.

INPUT · PHYSCHEM

Molecule properties

MW, logP, pKa — set how the drug partitions into tissue.

INPUT · ADME

Clearance & binding

Intrinsic clearance (CLint), unbound fraction (fu), renal CL — often measured or predicted, rarely exact.

MODEL · ODE

Coupled equations

One mass-balance equation per organ, solved together over time — a stiff ODE system.

RISK · PROPAGATION

Errors compound

A wrong clearance or fu shifts the whole curve — garbage in, garbage out.

This is why PBPK is a specialist discipline: the equations are standard, but choosing and justifying the parameters is the real work. BioMate exposes every input so you can check and override it before the solver runs.

3 · Idea · The baseline — non-compartmental analysis (NCA) Concept

If you already have measured concentrations, you don't need a model.

Non-compartmental analysis (NCA) makes no assumption about organs. Given an observed concentration–time table, it reads the numbers straight off the curve — area by the trapezoidal rule, the peak, the terminal slope. It is the honest, model-free baseline PBPK is measured against. BioMate ships it as Nca Pknca, built on the R PKNCA package.

NCA vs PBPK — a useful contrast

NCA summarizes the data you already have — AUClast, AUCinf, Cmax, Tmax, t½, CL/F, Vd/F, per subject.

PBPK predicts forward to a new dose, a child, or a renal-impaired patient — exactly the scenarios you have no data for.

4 · Idea · Mechanistic PBPK — organ compartments as ODEs Concept

Model the body as linked compartments, one equation per organ.

BioMate's native engine is an 8-compartment Rodgers/Rowland model: gut lumen and wall, blood, liver (with first-pass metabolism), lung, kidney, and lumped peripheral tissue. Blood flow links them; tissue partition coefficients (Kp) and a well-stirred hepatic clearance set how the drug spreads and leaves. The whole system is integrated by a SciPy LSODA ODE solver.

Grounded in published methods

Tissue partitioning: Poulin & Theil (2002) / Rodgers & Rowland (2006). Physiology database: Willmann (2003). Hepatic clearance: the well-stirred model (Wilkinson 2005). These are the same methods behind commercial tools.

5 · Idea · Special populations — the real reason to go mechanistic Concept

Change the physiology, re-run the same equations.

The payoff of a mechanistic model is that a child, a pregnant patient, or a patient with organ impairment is not a new model — it is the same equations with different physiological parameters. BioMate ships dedicated workflows for each, grounded in regulatory guidance.

PEDIATRIC

Enzyme maturation

CYP3A4/2D6/2C9/1A2 maturation Hill functions (Bjorkman 2005, Johnson 2006); age-scaled organ volumes (WHO growth data).

PREGNANCY

Trimester physiology

Plasma-volume expansion, +50% GFR, CYP3A4 induction (T3 ≈ 1.8×) and a fetal compartment (Jeong 2012).

RENAL / HEPATIC

Disease-state submodels

eGFR-staged renal CL (FDA 2010 / ICH E7); Child-Pugh A/B/C hepatic scaling (FDA 2003, Wilkinson well-stirred).

ADULT VARIANTS

Elderly & impairment

The core engine also carries healthy-adult, elderly, and hepatic-impairment physiology presets out of the box.

The disease-state workflow emits AUC and Cmax ratios (impaired / normal), a dose-adjustment suggestion, and draft FDA "Use in Specific Populations" label text for an IND/NDA — a projection to reason with, reviewed by a pharmacologist, never auto-submitted.

7 · In BioMate · Reading the result — with gates on the physics Concept

The PK parameters come with automatic sanity checks.

A PBPK result is only trustworthy if the simulation itself was physically sound. BioMate runs four QC gates on every PBPK run, so an implausible curve is flagged rather than reported as fact.

GATE · MASS BALANCE

Nothing lost

Total drug (compartments + excreted) must recover to the dose, 0.95–1.05 — the ODE conserved mass.

GATE · Tmax FRACTION

Peak in window

Tmax must fall within the simulated window (≤ 0.8 of duration) — not clipped by too-short a run.

GATE · CL FLOOR

Plausible clearance

CL = dose / AUC must clear a physiological floor (≥ 0.01 L/h).

GATE · AUC FOLD-ERROR

vs observed

When you supply an observed AUC, it reports the fold-error and whether it is within 2-fold (FDA 2018).

Read it as a projection

The gates check the internal consistency of the simulation — that the maths held. They do not certify the prediction is correct against a real patient; only an observed-AUC comparison does that.

So read Cmax, AUC, and t½ as a mechanistically-consistent projection to reason with — then anchor it to in-vivo data whenever you have any.

What to take away
  • Inputs rule. Accuracy depends on good physchem and ADME inputs; defaults are estimates, so supply measured values when you have them.
  • Estimates, not guarantees. Absolute Cmax/AUC are projections; the benchmark's within-2-fold target (0.70) shows how hard that is.
