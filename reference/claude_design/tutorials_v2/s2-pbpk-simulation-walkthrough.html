1 · Idea · The task — a concentration–time curve Concept

Pharmacokinetics is what the body does to the drug over time.

One curve — rise, peak, decline — carries every number a dosing decision rests on.

Cmax · Tmax
The peak
How high the drug goes, and when.
AUC
Total exposure
Area under the curve over time.
t½
Half-life
How fast the body clears it.
2 · Idea · State of the art — why PBPK is hard Concept

The hard part is the inputs and the equations — not drawing the curve.

Coupled differential equations, one per organ — only as good as the inputs you feed them.

PHYSCHEM
Molecule properties
MW, logP, pKa — set how the drug enters tissue.
ADME
Clearance & binding
CLint, fu, renal CL — usually estimated, rarely exact.
RISK
Errors compound
A wrong input shifts the whole curve.
3 · Idea · The baseline — non-compartmental analysis (NCA) Concept

If you already measured concentrations, you don't need a model.

NCA reads the numbers straight off observed data; PBPK predicts where you have none.

NCA
Summarize data you have
AUC, Cmax, t½ read off the observed curve — model-free.
PBPK
Predict forward
A new dose, a child, a renal-impaired patient — no data needed.
4 · Idea · Mechanistic PBPK — organ compartments as ODEs Concept

Model the body as linked compartments, one equation per organ.

Gut, liver, lung, kidney and plasma — blood flow links them, one ODE system solves them.

5 · Idea · Special populations — the real reason to go mechanistic Concept

Change the physiology, re-run the same equations.

A child, a pregnancy, an impaired organ is not a new model — just different physiology.

Grounded in regulatory guidance — emits dose-adjustment ratios and draft label text, reviewed by a pharmacologist, never auto-submitted.
