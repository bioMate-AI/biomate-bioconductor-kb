# T0 Getting-started walkthrough narration (EN)

## Step 1 — Title · one doorway, one loop

You don't need to install any software to use BioMate. Describe your goal in plain language, and BioMate takes it from there. Everything follows one simple loop: type a goal, review what gets filled in, send it yourself, and read the result. Nothing runs until you confirm.

---

## Step 2 — One place to start · the four kinds of work

Every new conversation opens in the same place: a message box at the foot, and four cards naming the four kinds of work BioMate does. Search and evidence gives a cited answer to "what is known." Data analysis runs a validated pipeline on your own data. Molecule design and generate proposes new candidate molecules. And Models learns from your lab results to improve the next round. You don't have to pick a card first — just type your goal in the message box, and BioMate routes it to the right kind of work for you.

---

## Step 3 — Step 1 · ask your first question

Let's ask a real one. We'll use an evidence question, because it returns in seconds: "What is the evidence linking JAK1 to rheumatoid arthritis, and how strong is it?" Type that into the message box. BioMate works out how to handle it for you — an evidence question like this is answered from the literature in seconds, with no cloud compute. Read what's in the box, adjust it if you like, and press Send yourself. Nothing is sent until you do.

---

## Step 4 — Step 2 · read the answer

An evidence answer is a concise, sourced report. Three things to notice: numbered markers tie each claim to a source; the citations open to the publisher; and a boundary note states what the answer is not evidence for. It summarises the published literature — it is not a verdict on your own data. For your own data, a validated analysis runs in the cloud and returns the results with a record of how they were produced.

---

## Step 5 — Three things to remember

That is the whole doorway, and the habit is always the same. Three things to remember. First, one place: search, data analysis, molecule design, and model training all begin from this one conversation. Second, your way: describe what you want in plain language, and explore each result however you prefer — view it in the app, download it, or open it in Jupyter or RStudio to take it further in code. And third, everything is traceable: every claim cites its source, and every run keeps a reproducible record. Everything else in BioMate is a richer version of this one loop.
