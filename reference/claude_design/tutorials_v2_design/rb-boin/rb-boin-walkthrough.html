The task · phase-I dose escalation

Which dose next — and which is the maximum tolerated dose?

In a phase-I trial, patients are dosed a few at a time, and each cohort's toxicities decide what happens next: escalate, hold, or step down — and, in the end, which dose is declared the maximum tolerated dose. This is not a judgement call. It is a pre-specified, sequential statistical decision rule, and getting it wrong has a direct cost in patient safety.

Observed toxicities per cohort→ A pre-specified decision rule→ Escalate / stay / de-escalate→ The maximum tolerated dose
BioMate doing it · the published BOIN design

BioMate runs the Bayesian Optimal Interval design, not a guess.

The Bayesian Optimal Interval (BOIN) design derives its escalation and de-escalation boundaries in advance, from the model and the target toxicity rate. BioMate applies those boundaries to the observed data the same way every time — a codified rule, executed, rather than a model talking itself toward an answer.

1 · BOUNDARIES

Set before the trial

From the target toxicity rate, BOIN fixes the escalate-below and de-escalate-above boundaries in advance — the rule is defined before any patient is dosed.

2 · APPLY

To the observed data

At each cohort, the observed toxicity rate is compared to those boundaries to decide the next dose — deterministically, with no re-interpretation.

3 · SELECT MTD

In milligrams

When enrolment ends, the design selects the maximum tolerated dose — reported as a concrete dose level in milligrams, the clinically actionable answer.

Concept view of the published BOIN rule — pre-specified boundaries applied to the trial data. This is decision-support for trial design, not medical advice.
