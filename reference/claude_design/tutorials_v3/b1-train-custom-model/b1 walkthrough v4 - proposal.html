Idea · why held-out matters

The honest score is the one the model never saw.

  1. Train on most, test on the restA model scores well on data it has seen. The held-out set shows how it does on new data.
  2. Cross-validation checks the fitEach fold takes a turn as the test set.
  3. The gap is the signalA big gap between training and held-out scores means overfitting.

· an example
Take a random forest as the example. BioMate routes to other learners, such as gradient boosting or neural networks, depending on your data. Small data overfits, so the held-out score and cross-validation are the numbers to trust.

In BioMate · 2 Workflow

Two phases: train, then evaluate.

  1. Read and hold outThe file is described, and 20 % is set aside for testing.
  2. Fit with cross-validationThe model is fitted on the rest with five folds.
  3. Score the held-out setTraining and held-out scores are reported together.
· the split
80 % to train and tune; 20 % kept untouched until the end.
· folds
Five rounds, each testing on a different fifth of the training data.
· the floor
A quality floor decides whether a model can be registered.

Read it right · before you register a model

Importance is not causation.

  1. Feature importance shows what the model usedlogP carries the most weight here.
  2. It does not show what causes activityTreat it as a clue, not a mechanism.
  3. The record shows the settingsSplit, folds and learner, each with its origin.
  4. Registration is a decisionAn Owner registers a model that clears the floor.
