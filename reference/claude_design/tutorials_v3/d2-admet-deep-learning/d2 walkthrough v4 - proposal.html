The question · episode 2 · ADMET

Is this molecule safe

A deep-learning model predicts absorption, toxicity and cardiac safety from structure alone, before any wet-lab work. Here, BioMate runs it end to end, from two structures to a report.

Idea · what an ADMET screen checks

Two safety questions come first: the heart and the liver.

  1. hERG: the heartA compound that blocks the hERG channel can disturb heart rhythm. Lower IC₅₀ means a stronger block.
  2. DILI: the liverThe model gives a probability of drug-induced liver injury.
  3. Each has a recorded criterionhERG IC₅₀ is safe at 10 µM or more; DILI probability is checked against 0.5.
· the two criteria

· an example
Take ADMET-AI with a Chemprop MPNN as the example: a published deep-learning model, used as released. BioMate offers other ADMET models too. A flag is a finding to follow up, not a verdict on the molecule.

In BioMate · 2 Workflow

One phase, two steps, and the checks beside them.

  1. Infer in shardsThe model predicts all properties for each compound, shard by shard.
  2. Merge and reportShards are merged into the toxicity report and the QC metrics.
  3. Checks run when step 2 finishesEach recorded criterion is checked, and the outcome is reported.

Read it right · before you act on a flag

A flag is a reason to look closer.

  1. The file behind the numberstoxicity_report.json holds every compound and property.
  2. One check is not a verdictPassing DILI says nothing about hERG, and the reverse.
  3. The record shows the settingsModel, shard size and criteria, each with its origin.
  4. Decide what to do nextRe-run with other settings, or take the flag to the bench.
