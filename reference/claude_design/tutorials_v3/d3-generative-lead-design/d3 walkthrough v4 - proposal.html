The question · episode D3 · lead design

Which new molecules
should you make next?

A generator proposes new structures around your scaffold. Filters and a ranking decide which are worth a chemist’s time. Here, BioMate runs it end to end, from a seed to a report.

Idea · how generation works

A generator proposes. The reward decides.

  1. A generator proposes moleculesIt starts from your seed and writes new structures, thousands at a time.
  2. The reward shapes what it proposesEach round, high-scoring molecules make the next round more like them.
  3. Filters decide what survivesDrug-likeness, ease of synthesis and alerts are checked on every candidate.

· an example
Take REINVENT4 with the qed_sa score as the example. BioMate also offers other generators and other scoring, such as docking. Whatever you choose, the ranking is only as good as the scoring function.

In BioMate · 2 Workflow

Two phases: generate, then filter and rank.

  1. Generate, e.g. with REINVENT4Reinforcement learning, 500 steps, batch size 128.
  2. Filter and rankLimits are applied after generation. Rejected candidates stay listed.
  3. Missing models stop the runA missing model stops the run with a clear error. It never falls back to silent enumeration.
· the loop
Propose → score → keep what scores well → propose again. 500 rounds.
· the filters
QED ≥ 0.4 · SA ≤ 5.0 · MW ≤ 500 Da · no PAINS · diverse by scaffold.
· clear errors
A missing model stops the run with a clear error. The list is never quietly replaced.

Read it right · before you make anything

A score is not a measurement.

  1. The score is the reward you setqed_sa comes from descriptors. A docking score would be an in-silico pose, not a measured affinity.
  2. Every filter shows its limitQED ≥ 0.4, SA ≤ 5.0, MW ≤ 500 Da, no PAINS.
  3. The record shows how it was madeParameters, software and QC checks, each with its origin.
  4. Candidates are a hypothesis queueThe lab decides which to make.
