The question · episode 4 · binding free energy

Which analog binds better
than the reference?

Free-energy calculations compare two close ligands by turning one into the other on the computer. Here, BioMate runs it end to end, from a receptor and a ligand series to a report.

Idea · what free energy tells you

Compare close analogs by their difference in binding.

  1. Turn one ligand into the otherAtoms change gradually over 11 steps, in the protein and in water.
  2. The cycle gives the differenceBinding of B minus binding of A, written ΔΔG. Negative means B binds more strongly.
  3. Check that it convergedAround a closed loop of ligands, the differences should add up to about zero.

In BioMate · 2 Workflow

Two phases: prepare, then analyse.

  1. Map the atomsLOMAP finds which atoms correspond between ligands and builds the network.
  2. Run alchemical MDEleven λ windows, three repeats each, on GPU.
  3. Check convergenceAround a loop of ligands, ΔΔG should sum to about zero.
· a network
Each line joins two similar ligands. L1, L2 and L3 form one closed loop.
· λ windows
11 steps from ligand A to ligand B. Each is simulated for 5 ns, 3 times.
· the check
Σ ΔΔG around a loop ≈ 0. A large leftover marks a weak edge.

Read it right · before you commit to synthesis

A ΔΔG is a prediction with error bars.

  1. Negative means B binds more stronglyL1 → L2 is −1.2 ± 0.3 kcal/mol.
  2. A tight edge differs from a noisy oneCompare the SEM across the edges.
  3. The record shows the settingsTemperature, windows and repeats, each with its origin.
  4. Make the winners, not the seriesFree energies decide which analogs earn a synthesis.
