The question · episode 7 · stability

Which recipe will
still be potent in six months?

A short hot study can stand in for a long cool one. Here, BioMate projects it, from your formulations to a ranked report.

Idea · why a hot study can predict a cool one

Heat speeds up the chemistry, so you can see it sooner.

  1. Heat speeds degradationArrhenius maps a rate measured hot onto a rate at 25 °C.
  2. Humidity adds its own effectAn empirical factor adjusts for moisture.
  3. Projection ranks recipesYou get potency and degradant over time, per formulation.
· the ICH conditions
ConditionTemperature / humidityUse

· an example
Take Arrhenius with a humidity factor as the example. It assumes one dominant temperature-activated pathway, so use it for triage and study design, then confirm in a real-time ICH chamber study.

Read it right · before you plan the study

A projection ranks recipes. It does not replace the chamber.

  1. Use it to rank, not to certifyShelf life is confirmed in a real-time study.
  2. Assumptions are statedOne dominant temperature-activated pathway.
  3. The record shows the settingsConditions, time points and model, each with its origin.
  4. Design the formal study from itChoose conditions and recipes worth the chamber time.
