The question · plate reader

How potent is
this compound?

A plate reader gives a grid of numbers. A dose-response fit turns it into one potency, the IC50. BioMate runs the fit and checks the plate.

Idea · potency from a curve

A curve, and the numbers that describe it.

  1. IC50The concentration that gives half the maximum effect.
  2. Hill slopeHow steep the curve is around the IC50.
  3. Top and bottomThe two plateaus. When both have data, the IC50 is read inside the data, not extrapolated.
· what the fit reports
NumberHereReads as
IC500.12 µMhalf-maximal concentration
Hill slope1.05steepness of the curve
Top · Bottom99 % · 2 %the two plateaus
R²0.995how closely the curve follows the points
· a separate check
The Z′-factor scores the plate itself, from the control wells, between 0 and 1. Above 0.5 is excellent.

In BioMate · 2 Workflow

Two phases: fit the curve, then check the plate.

  1. Map columnsDose and response are paired up.
  2. Fit the 4-parameter curveEvery replicate point counts; the fit gives IC50, slope, top and bottom.
  3. Check the controlsThe Z′-factor from the control wells says whether to trust the plate.
· the columns
Names must match the file header.
· the model
LL.4 is the standard four-parameter logistic (Hill) curve.
· the check
A Z′ above 0.5 means the controls separate cleanly.
