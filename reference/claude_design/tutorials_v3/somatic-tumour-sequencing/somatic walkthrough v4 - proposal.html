The question · somatic tumour sequencing

Which mutations
did the tumour acquire?

A tumour and its matched normal are sequenced together. What the tumour has and the normal does not are the somatic mutations. BioMate finds and annotates them.

Idea · germline versus somatic

The normal sample tells you what to ignore.

  1. Inherited variants are everywhereEvery person carries many variants they were born with. They are not what the tumour acquired.
  2. The matched normal removes themA variant seen in both samples is inherited. One seen only in the tumour is somatic.
  3. Then each call is annotatedEach variant gets its gene, its effect and an impact level.
· how to read a call
FieldReads as
Gene and changewhich gene, and what changed, e.g. KRAS p.G12D
VAFthe share of reads carrying the variant
Impacthow strongly it is expected to affect the protein
· two numbers for the whole tumour
TMB counts mutations per megabase; MSI says whether the repeat regions of the genome are stable.

In BioMate · 2 Workflow

Two phases: prepare the reads, then call and annotate.

  1. Align and recalibrateBoth samples are aligned and their quality scores recalibrated.
  2. Call somatic variantsThe normal is subtracted, so only tumour-specific variants remain.
  3. Filter and annotateLow-confidence calls are dropped; the rest get gene, effect and impact.
