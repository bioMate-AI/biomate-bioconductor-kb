The question · episode T1 · design and generate

Which molecules
should exist next?

Screening tests the molecules you already have. Generating proposes brand-new ones, then scores them against your goals. BioMate takes it from one seed to a ranked shortlist.

Idea · why it is hard

Four demands at once, and no single score.

  1. PotencyIt must bind the disease target.
  2. SafetyIt must spare the heart and the liver.
  3. MakeabilityA chemist must be able to build it.
  4. Behaviour in the bodyIt must be absorbed and not cleared too fast.
· the four demands

· what BioMate does about it
Each demand keeps its own number and its own units. They are never blended into one score, so a weak one cannot hide behind a strong one.
In BioMate · 1 Brief

Set the constraints before anything is generated.

  1. ModalitySmall molecule, peptide or protein.
  2. Seed, optionalA known binder from PubChem or ChEMBL steers the search.
  3. Scaffold policyStay, hop or grow: how far from the seed to roam.
  4. Property windowMW, logP and TPSA as a window, not a cut-off.
  5. Generate and rankNothing is generated until you press it.
Idea · scaffold policy and property window

How far to roam, and what a pill looks like.

  1. StaySame core, new decorations.
  2. HopSwap to a new core.
  3. GrowBuild a fragment up into a molecule.
  4. A window, not a cut-offA promising molecule just outside ranks lower. It is never deleted.

In BioMate · 2 Results

One row per candidate. One column per goal.

  1. Every candidate is a rowEight generated, eight scored.
  2. Objectives keep their unitsDrug-likeness (QED) has its own column, never merged.
  3. The score only ranksA mean of normalised objectives, not what the pipeline optimises.
  4. A verdict, with a reasonAccepted or rejected, and a Why? link on every row.

  2. The front stands outThe best compromise is larger; dominated ones stay visible.
  3. Rejections counted by causeFour candidates carry a structural alert.
  4. Rejected ones stay listedEach keeps its row and the reason it was set aside.
Read it right · before you make anything

A score is a ranking key, not a measurement.

  1. The score ranks, nothing morePer-objective scores are normalised and averaged for ordering.
  2. A prediction is not an assayDrug-likeness is computed from structure, not measured on a bench.
  3. Safety is a gateA structural alert rejects a candidate whatever its other numbers.
  4. A hypothesis queueThe lab decides what is made.

· before you order
Nothing here is a measured affinity. Make, then measure: the bench result is what tells you whether the design worked.
