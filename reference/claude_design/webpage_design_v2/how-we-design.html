                How we design · 中文

                How we design
                medicines.

                Designed in silico, tested in the lab, guided by patient data.

                Three loops, not one

                Computer, lab, and clinic —
                three loops that compound.

                Real drug design runs more than one loop — and its most valuable input is patient and clinical data. ① An in-silico loop generates and scores candidates against everything we know — patient and clinical data, known biology and structure, patents and freedom-to-operate, medicinal-chemist expertise, and our predictors — over many fast, low-cost rounds that converge on a small set, before anything is synthesized. ② A wet-lab loop takes over from there — synthesis, in-vitro assays, ADMET and in-vivo are all experimental — and the results recalibrate the computational models, so each in-silico round sharpens while each wet-lab round shrinks. ③ A clinical loop closes around both: patient and clinical data guides which targets to pursue and validates the mechanism, feeding back so the whole system gets better at choosing what to make.

                Before anything is made

                We check what matters —
                not just whether it works.

                Most candidate medicines fail late, for reasons that could have been caught early. We weigh all of them together, from the start.

                The right target

                Designed to act where it should — and leave the rest alone.

                Able to become a medicine

                Stable, deliverable and manufacturable — not just active in a dish.

                New and ownable

                Distinct from what already exists, with clear freedom to develop.

                True to the biology

                Grounded in how each target actually behaves in the body.

                Balanced, not one-sided

                Activity, safety and makeability weighed together — the real trade-off.

                Proven at the bench

                The lab, not the model, has the final say.

                The lab decides

                The wet lab has
                the final say.

                Every design is made and tested for real. Those results feed back into the next round — so the work compounds, and the ideas that advance are the ones that hold up at the bench.
