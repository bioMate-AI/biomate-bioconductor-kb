                Target identification · 中文

                How we identify
                targets

                The hardest decision in a drug program is which target to pursue · we make it from human biology

                Why it decides everything

                Most drugs fail on the target
                not the molecule

                A perfect molecule against the wrong target still fails. So we treat target identification as a first-class discipline, and we anchor it in human biology — what patient data and human genetics say actually drives the disease — rather than in what is already being drugged. We look across diseases, not one at a time, and we work drug-blind: a target has to earn its place on the evidence, not on the fact that someone else is already chasing it.

                From evidence to a shortlist

                Many candidates in
                a few credible targets out

                We start from human-genetic and patient evidence, rank candidates by how causally they drive the disease, and keep only the ones that clear every gate that matters.

                A prioritized target then enters the design engine — see how we design against it →

                What makes a target credible

                Five gates every time

                Causal human genetics

                Human genetic evidence that the gene actually drives the disease — not just correlates with it.

                Expression in disease tissue

                Present in the right cells and the right compartment, where a medicine could act.

                Mechanistic plausibility

                A credible biological path from engaging the target to a benefit for patients.

                Tractable with our modality

                Reachable by a cyclic-peptide / macrocycle — including hard interfaces others set aside.

                Novel & differentiated

                Distinct from crowded, me-too targets, with clear freedom to develop.

                Shared across diseases

                Where a single target reaches more than one immune-mediated disease, its value compounds.
