# Abstract

**Autonomous Parameter Convergence under Regulatory QC Constraints**

Drug discovery iteration cycles in ADMET screening, pharmacokinetics, molecular docking, and drug-drug interaction prediction typically require weeks of manual parameter tuning across coupled QC criteria. Existing parameter-optimization frameworks — design-of-experiments, Bayesian optimization, active learning — converge on objectives without anchoring each adjustment to a citable evidence base, leaving their decisions opaque to regulatory review. We introduce an evidence-anchored auto-loop that pairs autonomous convergence with provenance-graded knowledge bases (GOLD/SILVER/BRONZE) across four drug-development domains, recording the citation backing every threshold and every remediation step in a portable audit trail. On 125 convergent benchmark scenarios (drawn from a 139-scenario total catalog; 112 scenarios triggered at least one remediation and form the per-scenario provenance contingency) spanning GOLD, SILVER, and BRONZE tiers, the auto-loop achieved gate-pass-at-three-iterations of 100% for ADMET (40→100%), CYP-DDI (30→100%), and NCA-PK (50→100%), and 85.7% for docking (20→85.7%), where every gate is BRONZE-tier. Per-domain convergence tracked aggregate provenance with Pearson r=0.977 (descriptive, N=4); the pre-registered per-scenario Fisher's exact contrast on GOLD vs BRONZE did not reach the p<0.05 threshold (p=0.106), and a pre-specified sensitivity analysis excluding adversarial `persistent` scenarios yielded Fisher p=0.0044. The framework combines parameter convergence with regulatory traceability suitable for IND-stage filings. Four domains are benchmarked here; a fifth (PBPK) is newly wired but not yet evaluated under the same protocol.
