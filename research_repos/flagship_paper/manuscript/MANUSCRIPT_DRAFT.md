## Abstract

Autonomous biomedical agents advise, generate code, or close ad-hoc loops, but place validation and reproducibility on the user because the analysis is generated per request. BioMate instead routes a natural-language request to a curated, validated, containerised pipeline and executes it under evidence-graded quality governance, returning an audit-ready result. Across 4,057 executable Nextflow/nf-core workflows in 39 domains it interprets intent, completes parameters, recovers prerequisites, runs on cloud infrastructure, and reports — the intelligence living in the catalog plus deterministic, no-LLM-in-loop retrieval, not a larger model. Its governance layer is, to our knowledge, unmatched among biomedical agents: 117 evidence-graded (GOLD/SILVER/BRONZE) gates and a portable 21 CFR Part 11-aligned audit trail in which every threshold decision carries its citation and evidence grade — a per-decision provenance completeness that standard domain tools do not meet (1.0 vs 0.0; E4). On RealBio — an open cross-system benchmark we release — the deterministic catalog matches a scaffolded agent and far exceeds bare frontier-LLM use at cross-domain routing (0.965 vs 0.345–0.615), at near-zero cost, showing a curated executable catalog is *sufficient* for reliable routing without an in-loop model. BioMate argues that a curated, executable, governed catalog — not a bigger model — is the path to a trustworthy biomedical agent.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The bottleneck: fragmented, expert-dependent computational biology

The methods needed to answer a modern biomedical question are published and, in principle, available — nf-core pipelines, GATK Best Practices, Bioconductor packages, cryo-EM suites, ADMET and PBPK tooling. The bottleneck is not capability but **access and integration**. To run any one of these analyses correctly, a researcher must select the right pipeline from thousands, choose containers and default parameters that suit their sample, satisfy upstream data prerequisites, provision cloud compute, apply quality thresholds that decide which data and which results proceed, and finally interpret and report the output in a form defensible in a paper or a filing. Each of these steps concentrates in a small number of computational specialists, and each is a place where a wet-lab scientist stalls. The problem is fragmentation: the workflow, its configuration, its execution, and its quality control live in different tools maintained by different people.

### 1.2 The agent landscape: advise or generate versus execute a validated pipeline

A first generation of biomedical agents has begun to close this gap, but along a different axis than BioMate. Function-library agents such as Biomni¹ expose a catalog of Python/R functions that a model composes into code; AI-scientist and co-scientist systems²,³ generate research plans and hypotheses; and full-loop reasoners such as FutureHouse's Robin/PaperQA2¹³ do close an end-to-end loop by driving *ad-hoc* analysis and, in some cases, wet-lab experiments. The distinction is therefore not simply "advise versus execute" — several of these systems do execute. The axis that separates BioMate is *what* is executed and *under what governance*: full-loop reasoners execute ad-hoc, per-request analysis code, placing the burden of *validation and reproducibility* on the user. BioMate takes the complementary position: rather than generating a novel program per request, it **routes to a curated, validated, containerised pipeline and executes it** on managed infrastructure, returning a quality-graded, reported, audit-ready result. We do not claim any single one of these capabilities is new — executable-pipeline orchestration, prose→parameter extraction, and closed-loop QC each appear in prior systems (below). What distinguishes BioMate is the *integrated composite*: a curated validated containerised catalog + evidence-graded GOLD/SILVER/BRONZE QC + a portable 21 CFR Part 11-aligned audit trail + deterministic no-LLM-in-loop retrieval, assembled into one governed loop.

**Hypothesis generation versus execution — Google's AI co-scientist³.** The most prominent system in this space is Google's AI co-scientist: a Gemini-based multi-agent system (generation → reflection → ranking → tournament evolution) that produces and self-improves research *hypotheses*, validated on drug repurposing, novel target discovery, and antimicrobial-resistance mechanisms — a genuine advance in automated hypothesis generation, and we credit it as such. It and BioMate are complementary rather than competing, and naturally chained: the AI co-scientist proposes *what to investigate*; BioMate routes to and executes *how the investigation is run* — a validated, containerised pipeline under evidence-graded QC, returning an audit-ready result — so a co-scientist's hypothesis becomes a BioMate execution. As capable LLM-agent systems, either could in principle attempt the other's tasks; we report no head-to-head benchmark only because the AI co-scientist is a trusted-tester system with no public API to run under an identical protocol (not a judgement on its ability). The full nine-dimension capability comparison against it — and against Biomni, Coscientist, ChemCrow, The AI Scientist, Robin/FutureHouse, STELLA, and BioMedAgent — is Supplementary Table S4, with a primary-source citation per cell; a structural overlap/difference analysis against the two nearest open tool-ecosystem agents (Biomni, ToolUniverse) — unit, shared database substrate, and governed execution — is in Suppl. *Overlap & difference*.

**Related workflow-orchestration agents.** BioMate sits in a crowded and fast-moving orchestration class; our own competitive survey flags the honest gap that *route-and-execute, prose→parameter extraction, and closed-loop QC each individually exist in prior art* — so BioMate's difference is on the integrated governance+catalog composite defined above, not on the novelty of any one capability. The nearest analogues are **BioMaster**¹⁸ (RAG-driven route-and-execute across omics modalities), **NEXUS**¹⁹ (closed-loop QC as an LLM-judge with remediation), and the direct "LLM drives an execution engine" baseline **GalaxyMCP**¹⁵ (an off-the-shelf LLM attached to Galaxy, for which we treat the measured direct-LLM dispatch condition of §4.1 as a close proxy). The full orchestration- and AI-scientist-class matrix — including BioMedAgent²⁰, AutoIND²¹ (IND-summary drafting), BioWorkflow²² (prose→parameter extraction), FlowAgent²³, CRISPR-GPT²⁴, and an open-LLM lab-orchestration study²⁵, each capability of which is established in prior art and none of which BioMate claims as new — is the nine-dimension Suppl. Table S4, with a per-cell primary-source citation and the "not documented ≠ absent" honesty rule; we cite ref 25 when framing the §4.1/§6 result that catalog+retrieval is sufficient without a large in-loop model. Against all of these, BioMate's differentiator is that same composite packaged into one governed loop, not any one capability in isolation. Established GxP-capable execution platforms (Nextflow Tower, Galaxy, KNIME Server) are compliance-ready and mature but expert-only — no natural-language routing, no evidence-graded QC; their audit **data models** (which capture run provenance but not a per-decision citation + evidence grade) are the subject of a dedicated comparison in the companion governance paper (C3), not of the E4 experiment here, whose baselines are the domain analysis tools practitioners actually run (Fig. 4b).

### 1.3 Contributions

This paper introduces BioMate as a whole system and makes four contributions.

1. **A general-purpose biomedical agent that executes.** BioMate closes the full loop — interpret → route → parameters + prerequisites → execute on AWS Batch → analyse and report — over 4,057 executable workflows in 39 domains (`galaxy_ai_tools.db` snapshot 2026-08-17), accessible from plain-language chat with no configuration required of the user (§3).
2. **Cross-domain integration under one governed loop, backed by a curated executable catalog and a comprehensive evaluation.** The most defensible axis is that a single governed loop synthesises structurally different domains — omics → ADMET → PBPK → IND — as one end-to-end chain, and it is that integrated cross-domain synthesis chain *as a whole* for which our survey finds no published equivalent as of the survey date (2026-07-18), across the orchestration-agent class enumerated in §1.2 and Suppl. Table S4; individual stages have prior art (IND-summary drafting via AutoIND; orchestration and prose→parameter extraction via BioMaster/BioWorkflow), so the unmatched element is the synthesis, not any single stage. Co-equal to this: on the open cross-system RealBio benchmark, the catalog plus deterministic retrieval matches or exceeds the best direct frontier-LLM use, establishing that a curated catalog plus retrieval is *sufficient* for reliable routing without a large in-loop model (sufficiency only; §4.1). The new element here is the *no-LLM-in-loop determinism plus governance packaging*, not the retrieval idea itself (as positioned in §1.2). We back both claims with a full evaluation across a battery of public and internal benchmarks, organised by capability dimension and reported with every below-target and null result and its cause (§4).
3. **Demonstrated breadth end-to-end.** We map the platform across representative domains — RNA-seq, WGS, cryo-EM, drug discovery/ADMET, PBPK, structure prediction — with a concrete walk-through of each of six canonical use-cases, and position the companion deep-dive papers against this map (§3).
4. **One governance pillar: evidence-graded QC with an audit trail.** BioMate governs threshold-dependent QC decisions through evidence-provenance-graded gates (117 grade-bearing gate entries across 35 graded domains at the submission commit; the gate catalog spans 41 domains, 6 of which are newer and not yet graded), with a portable 21 CFR Part 11-aligned audit trail — condensed here as one pillar and treated in depth in the companion papers (§5).

We describe the system (§2), its breadth (§3), evaluate it comprehensively against the published frontier (§4), summarise the governance pillar (§5), and discuss boundaries and limitations (§6).

## 2. The BioMate system

![Figure 1](../../../docs/figures/c1/fig1_architecture.png)

**Figure 1 | BioMate architecture.** A natural-language request is interpreted, routed across 4,057 Nextflow workflows in 39 domains (`galaxy_ai_tools.db` snapshot 2026-08-17), parameter-filled with prerequisite recovery, and executed on cloud infrastructure. Every threshold-dependent QC decision passes through one evidence-graded governance layer — 117 grade-bearing gate entries across 35 graded domains (the catalog spans 41 domains, 6 newer and not yet graded), each graded GOLD (citation-anchored; e.g. FSC (Fourier Shell Correlation) 0.143, hERG 10 µM), SILVER (method convention) or BRONZE (operational heuristic) — emitting PASS / ADVISORY / HALT into a portable JSONL audit trail designed against 21 CFR Part 11 (measured value · threshold · PASS/ADVISORY/HALT · evidence DOI · grade · active profile · configured-vs-applied overrides).

BioMate is a virtual research workforce reachable entirely through natural language: the user describes an experiment, and the system selects, configures, runs, quality-checks, and reports the analysis without the user standing up a bioinformatics team (Fig. 1). Its stages are cleanly separated, so that adding a workflow or a gate touches only configuration and an evaluator, never the engine, the event bus, or the frontend.

### 2.1 The end-to-end loop

**Interpret.** A natural-language request is parsed for scientific intent — the experiment, not just keywords. BioMate understands scientific language ("K3 movies of a GPCR–Gi complex in MSP nanodisc — determine the structure") and asks at most one or two clarifying questions when a request is genuinely ambiguous (e.g. germline versus somatic variant calling), never re-asking for information already given.

**Route.** The request is embedded and matched against the catalog with a hybrid retrieval system — dense vector embeddings (semantic similarity), domain scoring (biological discipline), and LLM reasoning for ambiguous phrasing. Candidate pipelines are ranked with explicit confidence scores; alternatives are surfaced when the top choice has a known limitation, and genuinely ambiguous requests are refused rather than guessed.

**Parameters and prerequisites.** Required parameters are extracted from the prose with a **rule-first, LLM-fallback** layer: rules handle unit conversions, negations, boolean flags, and canonical alias normalisation deterministically; the LLM handles free-text values and ambiguous enum selection; a validation guardrail drops or normalises values that violate the schema. Optional parameters carry evidence-based defaults with the citation that justified the value. Missing upstream prerequisites — reference genomes, indices, prior-run artefacts — are recovered before submission, and blocked prerequisites (missing inputs, licenses, unconfirmed functional assays) hold the run until resolved.

**Execute.** Selected workflows are implemented in Nextflow DSL2 and executed on AWS Batch with per-process containerised (ECR) images; BioMate selects the execution mode (mock/local/cloud) from data size and resource availability. Each terminal process emits a structured JSON artefact (`qc.json`, `toxicity_report.json`, `pk_summary.json`, …) into the run's S3 output prefix.

**Analyse and report.** Intermediate outputs become available as each step finishes, so the user can inspect and decide mid-run. On completion, BioMate renders interactive results (3D structure and density-map viewers, FSC curves, sortable tables, volcano plots), an AI findings interpretation, and an audit-ready methods report — for drug-discovery workflows including ICH CTD Module 2.6.1 (the pharmacology written summary of an IND/NDA submission; §2.6.1 hereafter) narrative sections in DOCX for IND submissions.

### 2.2 The curated executable catalog

The routing intelligence is the catalog itself: **4,057 executable workflows across 39 biological domains** in the `galaxy_ai_tools.db` snapshot dated 2026-08-17 (transcriptomics ≈1,693, genomics ≈750, epigenomics ≈262, proteomics ≈182, variant_calling ≈181, drug_discovery ≈122, single_cell ≈95, and others; per-domain counts from that snapshot, released via Data and code availability — the database is not a git-committed artefact and is distributed as a dated snapshot, so the live catalog may be larger at read time), each hand-curated with correct containers, correct default parameters, literature-grounded QC thresholds, and prerequisite validation. Each `workflow_ga` object returned by the router carries `name`, `domain`, `description`, `required_params`, `optional_params`, `steps`, and `nextflow_path`. The catalog also fronts live access to 60+ biological databases (protein/structure, genomics/variant, transcriptomics/expression, chemistry/pharmacology, pathways/interactions, and literature) queried at analysis time rather than from cached snapshots. A full quantitative anatomy of the catalog — step and tool composition, semantic-phase structure, code volume, and per-tool enrichment coverage — is given in Suppl. *BioMate catalog anatomy*.

### 2.3 The chat + workflow-panel interface

The user-facing surface is a chat panel (left) paired with a workflow panel (right). After a request, BioMate replies with a structured pre-execution summary and a **workflow card** showing the pipeline name, domain, pre-filled parameters, input slots, prerequisites, and expected outputs; parameters are editable in the card or by chat instruction. The workflow panel reflects live process state via server-sent events (SSE) — pre-run, running (per-phase spinners), completed, or failed — and exposes after-run Outputs, Findings, and Parameters tabs, a step timeline with per-step file previews, and a download panel (individual files, full ZIP, or one-command S3 sync). Beyond the web UI, the same capabilities are reachable via an MCP server (10 typed tools), and through Slack and WeChat Work bots for messaging-native access.

### 2.4 A concrete end-to-end journey

One journey illustrates the §2.1 loop end-to-end. In RNA-seq, FASTQ paths route to nf-core/rnaseq → DESeq2/edgeR on AWS Batch; steps update live via SSE; and a findings panel and methods report are produced (volcano plot, DE table, GO enrichment) — one pass through interpret → route → parameters + prerequisites → execute → govern → report, with no configuration required of the user. The same loop drives every other domain the platform covers; §3 and Table 1 give the full breadth map (ADMET screening, WGS/variant calling, cryo-EM, CRO compliance, drug-discovery IND, and more), each a variation on this single journey rather than a separate architecture.

## 4. Comprehensive evaluation

Evaluating an agent that routes, executes, and governs cannot rest on a single number. The battery below is designed to test the *scope* of the thesis, not to maximise a headline: BioMate should excel where a curated, validated, governed catalog is the right instrument — cross-domain routing, prerequisite recovery, governed threshold-QC, deterministic pharmacology — and should underperform where it deliberately does not target — open-ended scientific ideation and free-form per-request code generation. The win/loss pattern that follows is meant to be read in that light (developed in §6): the sub-target scores delimit the thesis's scope rather than undercut it. We therefore evaluate BioMate across a battery of open and internal benchmarks (18 rows spanning 1,539 unique tasks in Suppl. Table S3 — a row-count total of 1,545 minus the n = 6 Test-1 re-scoring row, which is a subset of the 18 pharmacology cases, not additional tasks (§4.2); the row-collapsing rules are in Methods and the per-row provenance in Suppl. Table S3), organised by the capability *dimension* each measures: **governance** (D1: correct routing, prerequisite detection, and gating), **scientific accuracy** (D2: numerical correctness of computational outputs), and **knowledge & reasoning** (D3: expert question answering). The battery also includes a data-analysis benchmark (BixBench) and a run-to-run reproducibility measurement, reported directly in §4.5. The headline open result is RealBio (Fig. 2a), and every below-target and null score is carried with its cause. Each benchmark is run under its own protocol; the priced portion of the battery costs ≈$2.44 in LLM spend (mean ≈$0.0034/priced query; the twelve-row Σ(N × Cost/q) derivation and row-by-row accounting are in Methods). Figure 3 consolidates every score; the subsections give the reading. *Most BioMate scores are drawn from the 2026-04-29 complete benchmark matrix (the canonical internal record); the BiomniEval1 aggregate, the pharmacology error figures, and the license-gating number are traced individually to the committed files named in the relevant subsection, because they are not rows in that matrix.*

**Which model powers BioMate — a bounded answer.** BioMate's results separate into two classes by whether a language model is exercised on the scored decision. The governance and pharmacology results — cross-domain routing, parameter prefill, protocol-threshold QC, and the 18 pharmacology cases — are produced by a deterministic retrieval-and-scoring path with no model in the loop (zero recorded LLM latency and cost on these paths; §4.5); they are therefore *invariant to the backend model*, and the routing figure (0.965) does not change when the configured model changes. The knowledge-and-reasoning results (BiomniEval1, BixBench, SGI-Bench) do exercise a generation model and were produced with Claude Sonnet 4.6 for generation and a Claude Haiku model as the hallucination-audit judge (Methods; Suppl. Table S2). The practical consequence is that BioMate's central claims rest on figures for which *no* model is in the loop, while its knowledge scores are Sonnet-4.6-dependent and would move with a stronger or weaker generator. The model-invariance analysis (Suppl. Table S5) proves the engine-free results invariant by construction (no model call on the scored path) and reports the Sonnet-4.6 values for the model-dependent knowledge tasks.

### 4.1 Governance (D1): routing, prerequisites, and gating

**RealBio routing — the catalog is the intelligence (open, cross-system).** To evaluate routing under a *single open protocol* rather than a self-reported internal number, we report BioMate's result on **RealBio**, an open cross-system benchmark we release (8 objective-ground-truth tasks over 660 items; full task set, open scorer, and fairness-by-construction protocol in the companion RealBio report [26]). On cross-domain routing (n = 200), BioMate's **engine-free deterministic retrieval scores 0.965 [95% CI 0.930, 0.983]** — far above the same eight frontier models used directly, as a practitioner would call them (0.345–0.615; Claude Opus 5 0.615, Gemini 3.1 Pro 0.580, GPT-5.6 0.530, Kimi K3 0.450, DeepSeek V4 0.415, GLM-5.2 0.400, GPT-5.6-luna 0.355, Qwen3.8-Max 0.345; the released RealBio leaderboard/predictions). The best direct-LLM model, Claude Opus 5 at 0.615 [95% Wilson CI 0.546, 0.680], does not overlap BioMate's interval [0.930, 0.983] at n = 200, so BioMate's deterministic retrieval over the curated catalog *matches or exceeds* the best direct frontier-LLM use at near-zero LLM cost. This establishes *sufficiency* only — that the catalog plus retrieval is enough for reliable routing without a large in-loop model — and not that a large model could not reach the same score, because the direct-LLM condition has no catalog access and so the design does not isolate catalog-vs-model. Notably, a *scaffolded* agent narrows the gap sharply: a scaffolded Biomni agent reaches parity (0.950, 38/40, n = 40 pilot; not statistically separable from BioMate, Methods) and leads on autofix (0.950) on the same task set (released RealBio leaderboard/predictions). The honest reading is that BioMate's differentiation over a competent agent scaffold is *not* a routing-accuracy gap but the executable-catalog + governance + validated-execution stack: BioMate reaches near-identical routing with *no LLM in the loop*, at near-zero cost, and returns a governed, audit-ready, executed result rather than a routing decision alone. The full protocol and cross-system leaderboard are in the companion RealBio report [26]; RealBio is a companion paper by the same authors, so this is a released-benchmark result, not independent third-party validation. The direct-LLM condition is the bare "attach an off-the-shelf LLM to an execution engine" setup (RealBio's deployment-default condition), so the non-overlap above is against a deployable comparator, not an estimate. An earlier *internal* cross-domain routing benchmark (n = 120) scored **94.6%** (113.5/120 weighted; the hybrid BM25 + BGE + LLM search beats BM25-alone ≈35%); it predates RealBio and is superseded by the open score.†ᵃ A parameter-prefill metric of 84.8% is likewise superseded and self-reported; note that BioMate's param-prefill (n = 115) and protocol-QC (n = 20) figures are cross-harness against the direct-LLM runs at *non-matched n* (LLM param-prefill n = 170, protocol-QC n = 100), so they are **not** part of the re-scorable-now claim (routing 0.965 is re-scorable now from committed predictions via the standalone scorer, and pharmacology is re-runnable now via its committed runner; Data and code availability) and must not be read as matched head-to-head columns. (An earlier internal routing run had scored 61.9%; we omit it from the main comparison as it is superseded by the open RealBio score and we do not hold a committed per-query diff establishing the cause.)

> †ᵃ The often-cited "raw-LLM dispatch ≈45%" figure is *estimated, not measured*: the competitive moat briefing (Suppl. Table S2) gives "<50% raw LLM" / "<40% prerequisite", and 45% is merely the midpoint of that "<50%" bound. We do not lean on it — the measured direct-LLM condition (0.345–0.615, n = 200) is the stronger, deployable baseline and is what carries the non-overlap claim.

**The rest of the governance surface** is measured on purpose-built internal benchmarks that stress the parts of orchestration RealBio does not:

- **Prerequisite recovery — 97.5% (78/80).** Given task queries missing 1–3 required parameters, BioMate detects the gap and asks for the *specific* missing parameter rather than a generic prompt. The two misses inferred plausible defaults (e.g. a CryoSPARC pixel-size default) instead of asking — reasonable behaviour the rubric marks incorrect. This is the n = 80 v2 run (78/80) from the 2026-04-29 canonical matrix, on which the Wilson CI [91.3, 99.3] and any power half-width are computed; an earlier n = 40 run gave the identical 97.5% point estimate (39/40).
- **License gating — 98.1% (53/54).** On queries touching licensed, paywalled, or GPLv3-incompatible tools, BioMate correctly blocks or warns per the user's tier. The one miss was a GROMACS free-vs-commercial case whose license entry had not yet propagated to the workflow DB. (This score is not a row in the 2026-04-29 matrix; it is traced to two committed records that both record 98.1% (53/54) — Suppl. Table S2.)
- **ADMET routing — 96.3% (n = 60), above the 70% target.** SMILES-list and property-panel queries route to the correct ADMET workflow (hERG, DILI, Ames, BBB, bioavailability) with the right endpoints selected; the few misses are ambiguous single-property asks. This governance/routing score is distinct from the ADMET *model-quality* numbers reported in §4.2 — routing selects the right pipeline; it does not itself claim leaderboard-competitive prediction accuracy.
- **Protocol-threshold QC — 92.5% (weighted 18.5/20 partial-credit, n = 20), point estimate above the 80% target, though the 95% Wilson CI [73.1, 98.2] includes it at this n.** Twenty workflows (HTS plate QC, allometric scaling, ICH S5/S7 toxicology, druggability, PK/PD, BOIN, LNP, antibody developability, and more) run end-to-end with live gate logging; the gates fire correctly and the evidence-grade payload is emitted at the right threshold.
- **BOIN dose-finding — 80.0% (48/60), point estimate above the 70% target, though the 95% Wilson CI [68.2, 88.2] includes it at this n.** Phase-I dose-escalation queries in three classes (complete / incomplete / negative control); failures were generic "please provide more details" asks and partial routing on negative controls. (We cite the exact-count 80.0% (48/60) from the canonical 2026-04-29 matrix; a weighted partial-credit variant of 80.5% (48.3/60) is recorded separately (Suppl. Table S2).)
- **Docking triage — 78.9% (35.5/45), point estimate above the 75% target, though the 95% Wilson CI [65.0, 88.3] includes it at this n.** Failures concentrate in the partial (missing-PDB) case, where BioMate sometimes initiates structure prediction instead of asking for the target ID.
- **PBPK routing — 75.6% (34/45), *above* the 60% target.** An earlier scoring bug penalised the "no-SMILES" cases (they scored 0 even when the necessary physicochemical parameters were present); fixing the scorer and clarifying the `pbpk_simulation` workflow description to declare intrinsic clearance (CLint) as a prerequisite lifted the score from 50.0% to 75.6%. The residual gap to the 80% stretch target is a proactive-ask improvement (CLint / fu_plasma), not a capability ceiling.

### 4.2 Scientific accuracy (D2): deterministic numerical correctness

**Pharmacology validation — a curated known-answer set (n = 18), not a large benchmark: 18/18 across three deterministic tests, no LLM in the evaluation loop.** We present this as a *validation* that the deterministic pharmacology workflows reproduce published values on hand-checked cases, not as a powered accuracy benchmark; at n = 18 the 100% carries only a one-sided Wilson lower bound of 86.9%. The suite comprises three tests of six cases each (18 unique cases total): **Test 1** — PBPK disease-state renal/hepatic AUC ratios (n = 6, FDA-guidance gold standard; mean absolute error 10.6% on this 6-drug set; e.g. gabapentin 3.57 vs 3.6, midazolam 1.24 vs 1.24); **Test 2** — BRIM-3 biomarker enrichment (BRAF ranked #1 at ER = 33.2×; 6/6 checks); and **Test 3** — polymorph risk stratification against the CSD (6/6, e.g. ritonavir HIGH). At exact numeric tolerance all 18 cases pass (100%, 95% one-sided Wilson lower bound 86.9%; Suppl. Table S2). The six Test-1 PBPK impairment pairs are additionally re-scored under a looser ≤35% clinical tolerance, where the same six pairs also all pass (6/6, mean absolute error 10.6%) — this is a re-scoring of the Test-1 subset, not six additional cases, so the unique-case count remains 18.† All three tested workflows are Level-1 validated (independently checked against published data).

### 4.5 Data analysis, reproducibility, and statistical power

- **Data analysis (BixBench / BiomniBench Verified-50).** The Verified-50 score (BioMate 92.0%, 46/50, vs Biomni Lab 88.7%) and the withdrawn full-set 92.7% figure are reported in full in §4.3. The Verified-50 is the human-verified, trace-based subset (NOT FutureHouse's open-answer BixBench, whose ≈17% ceiling is a different protocol), measured through BioMate's analysis path (execution handlers); the new element for §4.5 is that a held-out generic-analysis slice — to characterise generalisation beyond the covered handlers — is planned as strengthening future work.
- **Cross-run reproducibility.** BioMate's retrieval-scoring layer yields **zero flips across 24 workflows × 3 independent repeats (flip-rate 0.0; `evaluation/results/stability/routing_stability.json`)** — the primary reproducibility metric — reproduced on BiomniEval1 (n = 45 × 3; `evaluation/results/stability/stability_summary.json`). We are precise about what this measures and what it does not: the run was executed under the Sonnet-4.6 configuration (`model = biomate_claude_sonnet_4_6`), but the measured path was the deterministic retrieval-scoring layer (zero recorded latency and zero recorded cost in `evaluation/results/stability/routing_stability.json`), so the LLM was configured rather than exercised on the scored decision; the agreement reflects the retrieval-scoring layer's *outputs* across repeats, not their correctness (the run did not evaluate `mean_score`). Because the scoring path is deterministic the run-to-run agreement table is degenerate (all agreement), so the accompanying Cohen's Kappa = 1.0 is reported as a formality, not a chance-corrected effect estimate — we do not lead with Kappa as an informative inferential statistic. The deterministic scoring path is what makes identical output across independent repeats expected — reproducibility-by-design of the retrieval layer, not stochastic luck — but this metric is an agreement measure, not an accuracy measure. A full adversarial-perturbation suite (BioAgentBench¹⁷ — corrupted inputs, decoy files, prompt bloat, missing intermediates, on which the published finding is that frontier closed-source models fail ≥3 of 4 perturbation types) is planned future work.

**Dataset-size insufficiency is the dominant threat to validity** — for the field and for this study. Most benchmarks above are small-n, falling far short of the n ≈ 385 needed for a ±5-pp binomial CI (see Methods; Suppl. Figure S1). The **adequately-powered set** (defined here once, and referenced as "the adequately-powered set (§4.5)" in the Suppl. Figure S1 caption and §6.1) is **RealBio routing (n = 200), BiomniEval1 (n = 223), and SGI-Bench (n = 369)**; the per-benchmark worst-case Wilson half-widths (±6.9, ±6.5, ±5.1 pp respectively) are in Methods. Two further benchmarks reach the power threshold by n but are not live results on that basis: the internal Routing run (n = 120) is *superseded and self-reported* (retained only to characterise n, not as a live result — §4.1), and BixBench's full-set local-execution n = 205 is *withdrawn* (§4.3), so neither carries a current powered claim. Even where n is adequate it is *necessary but not sufficient* (power ≠ reliability): SGI-Bench's ≈40% is a provisional end-of-day estimate, and the BixBench / BiomniBench Verified-50 row (92.0%, n = 50) is small-n (±13.4 pp; §4.3 — see the power list below). The one directional claim we back with actually-measured non-overlapping CIs is **RealBio routing: BioMate 0.965 [0.930, 0.983] vs the best direct-LLM model (Claude Opus 5) 0.615 [0.546, 0.680]**, non-overlapping at n = 200. Against the *agent-scaffold* condition, by contrast, we claim parity, not superiority (Methods). All small-n point estimates should be read as provisional; the per-benchmark half-widths, the four target-clearance CIs, and the partial-credit-interval treatment are given in Methods (§ Statistical power of the evaluation battery).

## 5. Evidence-graded QC governance (one pillar)

Quality-control governance is **one pillar of the system**, condensed here and treated in depth by the companion papers C3 (governance architecture), C4 (cryo-EM), and C6 (auto-loop remediation). BioMate governs every threshold-dependent QC decision through a single evidence-graded gate layer — unlike QC steps that emit a pass/fail without recording the threshold, its provenance, or an override path (established orchestration platforms — Nextflow Tower, Galaxy, and KNIME Server — do not carry these fields in their data models; a dedicated data-model comparison is in companion paper C3, while the E4 experiment here (Fig. 4b) contrasts BioMate against the domain analysis tools).

### 5.1 The evidence-graded gate catalog

A single in-process hook (`qc_trace_monitor`) intercepts each step's structured output and routes its metrics through a domain-specific gate evaluator. On threshold breach the pipeline surfaces the failing metric alongside the threshold, its citation, and the **evidence grade** of that citation: **GOLD** (verbatim in a peer-reviewed paper or regulatory document; cannot be silently overridden), **SILVER** (community convention attributed to a source; an override is recorded in the override audit log), or **BRONZE** (operational heuristic, explicitly disclaimed). The catalog contains **117 grade-bearing gate entries across 35 graded domains — 56 GOLD, 45 SILVER, 9 BRONZE, and 7 hybrid "GOLD-metric / SILVER-tier" entries** (parsed directly from the released qc_evidence.yaml gate catalog at the submission commit; the largest single domain is `bioconductor_qc` with 23 entries, followed by `rare_disease` 9 and `single_cell` 7), keyed in the released qc_evidence.yaml gate catalog as the single source of truth — a developer cannot add a gate without an evidence record, nor grade it GOLD without quoting the source verbatim. (The released qc_evidence.yaml gate catalog, a derived per-grade/per-domain count table, and the parser that produces it are committed now — `docs/figures/c1/qc_gate_counts.csv` (per-entry), `docs/figures/c1/qc_gate_summary.csv` (totals), and `docs/figures/c1/parse_qc_evidence.py` — so the 117 / 56 / 45 / 9 / 7 split is reviewer-reproducible from the released catalog. The parse rule counts only entries carrying a provenance concept: GOLD/SILVER/BRONZE where the provenance field equals that token (56/45/9), and 7 hybrid entries that carry a provenance key with an empty/composite value; a naïve token count of the YAML gives 76/56/10 because it also counts prose mentions such as "GOLD-anchored", which the field-scoped rule excludes. The gate catalog spans 41 top-level domains, but 6 are newer and carry no provenance grade yet — `fda_protein_ligand_affinity`, `immuneapp_hla_screen`, `scgwas_immune_cell`, `scupa_polarization`, `torchgwas_udip_phenotype_scan`, `turep` — and are excluded from the 117-entry / 35-graded-domain counts above.) Example: the ADMET evaluator compares `herg_ic50_uM` to the 10 µM threshold and, on failure, emits the evidence payload from Redfern 2003 (DOI 10.1016/S0008-6363(02)00846-5, GOLD, cross-confirmed by Bowes 2012). The nine BRONZE gates are retained frankly rather than promoted (e.g. the −7 kcal/mol docking "hit" is community heuristic, *not* prescribed by Trott & Olson 2010).

### 5.2 Advisory remediation and the audit trail

For five domains (ADMET, CYP-DDI, NCA-PK, docking, PBPK) the gate signal also feeds a rule-based engine that **proposes** a parameter change with a mechanistic rationale, rendered as a was→now remediation card. In production this engine is **advisory by default**: it emits suggestions but does not autonomously re-run the workflow; whether to apply a suggestion is an operator decision, and the resulting override is recorded in the audit. Every gate evaluation is appended to a per-run JSONL stream carrying the measured value, threshold, gate identifier, PASS/ADVISORY/HALT action, evidence grade, and citation DOI; each override is logged separately with timestamp, run UUID, event type, the active domain profile, and the configured-vs-applied threshold values — designed against 21 CFR Part 11 and aligned to the FDA 2025 draft guidance on the use of artificial intelligence to support regulatory decision-making for drug and biological products. *The current schema records the override and its profile, not a named-approver electronic signature; a Part 11-conformant e-signature field is a documented gap, not a claimed capability (§ Limitations).*

### 5.3 Governance verified at scale

The governance architecture is a deployed, verified layer, not a curated demonstration. A code-generation Bioconductor QC harness instruments analysis workflows with domain-appropriate gates and re-executes each under R to confirm the gate fires: **659 workflows assembled via 11 narrow domain filters, with 658/658 dynamic Rscript checks passing** (Suppl. Table S2; 396 → 659 via 11 narrow domain filters; the 659-vs-658 difference is one workflow that carries no dynamic Rscript gate; the per-domain-group breakdown and the harness log — per-workflow pass/fail and the identity of that one workflow — are provided as a named supplementary file (Data and code availability) with the tagged release and referenced against the C3 dataset). A separate protocol-threshold benchmark ran 20 workflows (HTS plate QC, allometric scaling, ICH S5/S7 toxicology, druggability, PK/PD, BOIN, LNP, antibody developability, and more) end-to-end with live gate logging, scoring **92.5% (n = 20)** with gates firing correctly and the evidence-grade payload emitted at the right threshold. On the false-rejection stress test (E1) (Fig. 4a), default profiles reject 100% of biology-valid marginal samples (HTS Z′∈[0.40,0.49]; LNP elevated-PDI within published ranges) while protocol-aware profiles recover all of them without changing rejection on `truly_bad` samples — the metric values are synthetic-but-literature-anchored, a stress test of the failure mode, not a population estimate. On regulatory audit completeness (E4) (Fig. 4b), we score, for each run, the fraction of threshold decisions whose log carries a full scientific anchor — `paper_ref` + citation DOI + GOLD/SILVER/BRONZE provenance grade — at 0 / 0.33 / 0.67 / 1.0 per decision, averaged per run. BioMate scores **1.0** (every gate decision fully provenance-anchored) versus **0.0** for a pool of standard domain tools — CryoSPARC Live, RELION 4, DataWarrior, PK-Sim/GastroPlus, TIBCO Spotfire, and the SAS BOIN macro — across n = 10 runs per group (five domains × two). We are precise about the criterion: these tools *do* record a metric and a pass/fail flag, and several embed a bare numeric threshold, so the 0.0 is not a claim that they log nothing — it is specifically that they do not carry a per-decision scientific anchor (citation + evidence grade) in the run log. ## 6. Discussion and limitations

The result with the widest practical reach is that reliable routing over a curated catalog is achievable with *no model in the loop* (§4.1): if a deterministic catalog can match a frontier model on the routing that dominates real analytical work, deploying trustworthy biomedical agents at lab scale need not be gated on the non-determinism (and non-reproducibility) that an in-loop LLM introduces. Whether a scaffolded model **with** catalog access would exceed deterministic retrieval is untested here — the direct-LLM condition had no catalog — so we register it as the key open experiment rather than a settled result; the pending step (re-scoring BioMate's exported task sets through the identical released RealBio scorer (score.py) against the released RealBio leaderboard/predictions) is the test. Governance is one pillar of that trustworthiness, not the system's identity: the cryo-EM validation, the endotyping/antigen translational lanes, and the auto-loop remediation each merit — and receive — a dedicated companion paper.

The win/loss pattern across §4 is itself evidence for the thesis rather than an unexplained scatter. BioMate is strong exactly where a curated, validated, governed catalog should be strong — cross-domain routing of validated pipelines (RealBio 0.965), prerequisite recovery (97.5%), governed threshold QC, and deterministic numerical pharmacology (18/18) — and weak exactly where it does not target: open-ended scientific ideation (SGI-Bench ≈40%), single-answer direct-API question answering framed as a workflow launch (CompBioBench 29.6%), and knowledge subtasks needing publication-specific facts (BiomniEval1 screen-gene 42%). The sub-target scores therefore trace to capabilities BioMate deliberately does not optimise — per-request code generation and free-form ideation — not to failures of the route-execute-govern loop it does. Read this way, the low scores delimit the thesis's scope rather than undercut it: a governed executable catalog is the right instrument for routine analytical work, and a poor instrument for open-ended discovery, which is what the pattern shows.

This paper is the system-overview umbrella and carries the comprehensive evaluation in its own body (§4). The released companion is **RealBio** [26] — the open, cross-system routing/orchestration benchmark dataset and scorer (8 frontier LLMs + BioMate + a Biomni scaffold across 8 objective tasks / 660 items, open scorer), a paper by the same authors that sources the cross-system leaderboard summarised in §4.1. The domain and governance deep-dives (**C3** governance architecture — gate catalog, provenance grading, audit schema; **C4** cryo-EM known-structure reproduction and protocol-discrimination on membrane proteins; **C6** the auto-loop advisory remediation engine and its convergence analysis; and the translational **M1–M7** series — M1 endotyping/integration, M4 antigen resolution, and the target-discovery/biomarker/trial-design lanes, with C5/C7 as additional deep-dives) provide depth against this flagship's breadth.

### 6.1 Limitations

- **Autonomous remediation is advisory.** We make no autonomous-convergence performance claim; the production engine emits suggestions only, and an efficient autonomous search is unvalidated future work.
- **Translational components are method-verified, not cohort-validated.** Per the capability whitepaper, several translational workflows (target scorecard, integration/endotyping, antigen resolution) are verified on idealized/known-answer inputs (🟡), not on real, noisy patient cohorts; moving 🟡→🟢 on real data is current priority.
- **ADMET/heuristic predictions are not on official splits.** BioMate's heuristic ADMET models are not evaluated on the official TDC ADMET-Group scaffold split; no leaderboard-comparable ADMET claim is made here.
- **BixBench / BiomniBench Verified-50: BioMate 92.0% vs Biomni Lab 88.7%; the full-set 92.7% is withdrawn.** On the Phylo / BiomniBench Verified-50 subset (n = 50) — the human-verified trace-based subset, distinct from FutureHouse's open-answer BixBench (≈17% ceiling) — BioMate scores 92.0% (46/50) versus a Biomni Lab comparator of 88.7% on the same subset (§4.3/§4.5). BioMate's 92.0% is measured through its analysis path (execution handlers), and the Biomni Lab 88.7% comparator's primary source is to be pinned at submission (author-asserted). The earlier full-set 92.7% (n = 205) local-execution figure remains withdrawn because it conflated BioMate handlers with reference notebooks run without BioMate. This is an in-distribution measurement; a held-out generic-analysis evaluation to characterise generalisation is planned future work.
- **Reproducibility is measured as agreement, not correctness; the adversarial-perturbation suite is not run.** The stability metric is run-to-run *agreement* of the retrieval-scoring layer (flip-rate 0.0 over 24 workflows × 3 repeats; §4.5), measured under the Sonnet-4.6 configuration but on the deterministic retrieval-scoring path (zero recorded latency and cost), so the LLM was configured rather than exercised on the scored decision, and correctness was not evaluated (`mean_score` was not scored in that run); the reported Cohen's Kappa = 1.0 is a formality on a degenerate all-agreement table, not a chance-corrected effect. It covers only run-to-run repeat of the deterministic scoring path; a full adversarial-perturbation suite (BioAgentBench¹⁷ — corrupted inputs, decoy files, prompt bloat, missing intermediates) is planned future work (§4.5). The reported battery does not imply completeness on that axis.
- **Several scores are small-n / underpowered.** Most benchmarks fall below the n ≈ 385 needed for a ±5-pp CI (Suppl. Figure S1); only the adequately-powered set (§4.5) reaches or approaches the ±10-pp line — the internal Routing (n = 120) and the withdrawn full-set BixBench (n = 205) points reach the n threshold but are superseded and withdrawn respectively (§4.1, §4.3), so neither carries a powered claim (the current BixBench / BiomniBench Verified-50 row, n = 50, is small-n; §4.3). Even SGI-Bench's ≈40% is a provisional end-of-day estimate, so adequate n is necessary but not sufficient (power ≠ reliability). Small-n point estimates (e.g. LAB-Bench n = 33, TDC-ref n = 20) are provisional. CompBioBench 29.6% (8/27) is a routing/format artefact (§4.3), not a knowledge ceiling.
- **Some internal metrics are self-reported.** Routing 94.6% and parameter prefill 84.8% are self-reported and are superseded by the open RealBio scores; an earlier internal routing 61.9% dip is omitted from the main comparison for lack of a committed per-query diff.
- **E1/E4 are stress tests, not population estimates.** E1 uses literature-anchored synthetic metrics; E4's 1.0-vs-0.0 separation is structural and small-n (n = 10 per group).
- **The audit trail is Part 11-*designed*, not Part 11-*conformant*.** The committed schema records each gate decision (measured value, threshold, action, evidence DOI, grade) and each override (timestamp, run UUID, event type, active profile, configured-vs-applied values), but it does **not** yet carry a named-approver electronic-signature field, which 21 CFR Part 11 requires for a compliant record. We therefore claim "designed against / aligned to Part 11," not certified conformance; the e-signature field and formal certification are documented gaps, consistent with certification following the deployed system rather than preceding it.
- **Network capability is early.** BioMate is strong at the single-lab loop; multi-lab identity, data-sharing, and workflow-marketplace primitives are product-stage roadmap, not claimed here.

## Methods

### Routing and retrieval

Beyond the interpret-stage behaviour described in §2.1, the routing implementation is as follows. The request is embedded and matched against the curated catalog with a hybrid retrieval system: dense vector embeddings (semantic similarity, BGE), a BM25 lexical component, and domain scoring (biological discipline), with LLM reasoning invoked only for genuinely ambiguous phrasing. The scoring path is deterministic, with no LLM in the loop; candidate pipelines are ranked with explicit confidence scores, alternatives are surfaced when the top choice has a known limitation, and genuinely ambiguous requests are refused rather than guessed. The routing intelligence is the catalog itself — 4,057 executable workflows across 39 biological domains in the `galaxy_ai_tools.db` snapshot dated 2026-08-17 (transcriptomics ≈1,693, genomics ≈750, epigenomics ≈262, proteomics ≈182, variant_calling ≈181, drug_discovery ≈122, single_cell ≈95, and others), each hand-curated with correct containers, correct default parameters, literature-grounded QC thresholds, and prerequisite validation. Each `workflow_ga` object returned by the router carries `name`, `domain`, `description`, `required_params`, `optional_params`, `steps`, and `nextflow_path`. The catalog also fronts live access to 60+ biological databases queried at analysis time rather than from cached snapshots. On the open RealBio protocol this engine-free deterministic retrieval was evaluated on cross-domain routing (n = 200) using the released RealBio scorer (score.py, exact-match, CC-BY-4.0) against the same eight frontier models used directly; the hybrid BM25 + BGE + LLM search was compared against a BM25-alone condition on the earlier internal benchmark (see §4.1).

### Parameter extraction and prerequisite recovery

Required parameters are extracted from the prose with a rule-first, LLM-fallback layer: rules handle unit conversions, negations, boolean flags, and canonical alias normalisation deterministically; the LLM handles free-text values and ambiguous enum selection; and a validation guardrail drops or normalises values that violate the schema. Optional parameters carry evidence-based defaults with the citation that justified the value. Missing upstream prerequisites — reference genomes, indices, prior-run artefacts — are recovered before submission, and blocked prerequisites (missing inputs, licenses, unconfirmed functional assays) hold the run until resolved. Prerequisite detection was evaluated on task queries missing 1–3 required parameters, scoring the system's ability to ask for the *specific* missing parameter rather than a generic prompt (§4.1).

### Execution

Selected workflows are implemented in Nextflow DSL2 and executed on AWS Batch with per-process containerised (ECR) images. BioMate selects the execution mode — mock, local, or cloud — from data size and resource availability. Each terminal process emits a structured JSON artefact (`qc.json`, `toxicity_report.json`, `pk_summary.json`, …) into the run's S3 output prefix; intermediate outputs become available as each step finishes. On completion BioMate renders interactive results, an AI findings interpretation, and an audit-ready methods report — for drug-discovery workflows including §2.6.1 pharmacology narrative sections in DOCX for IND submissions.

### Evidence-graded QC governance

A single in-process hook (`qc_trace_monitor`) intercepts each step's structured output and routes its metrics through a domain-specific gate evaluator. On threshold breach the pipeline surfaces the failing metric alongside the threshold, its citation, and the evidence grade of that citation: **GOLD** (verbatim in a peer-reviewed paper or regulatory document; cannot be silently overridden), **SILVER** (community convention attributed to a source; an override is recorded in the override audit log), or **BRONZE** (operational heuristic, explicitly disclaimed). Grades are keyed in the released qc_evidence.yaml gate catalog as the single source of truth — a developer cannot add a gate without an evidence record, nor grade it GOLD without quoting the source verbatim. Gate evaluation emits PASS, ADVISORY, or HALT. For five domains (ADMET, CYP-DDI, NCA-PK, docking, PBPK) the gate signal also feeds a rule-based engine that proposes a parameter change with a mechanistic rationale, rendered as a was→now remediation card; in production this engine is advisory by default. Every gate evaluation is appended to a per-run JSONL audit stream carrying the measured value, threshold, gate identifier, PASS/ADVISORY/HALT action, evidence grade, and citation DOI; overrides are logged separately (`qc_overrides_audit.jsonl`) with timestamp, run UUID, event type, the active domain profile, and the configured-vs-applied threshold values — designed against 21 CFR Part 11 and aligned to the FDA 2025 draft guidance on the use of artificial intelligence to support regulatory decision-making for drug and biological products. The schema records the override and its profile but not a named-approver electronic signature; a Part 11-conformant e-signature field is a documented gap (§ Limitations), not a claimed capability.

### Benchmark protocols and scoring

Each benchmark is run under its own protocol. **RealBio** is scored with its released open scorer across BioMate, eight frontier LLMs used directly, and a Biomni agent-scaffold pilot; its full task set, scorer, condition definitions, and fairness controls are specified in the companion RealBio report [26] and are not restated here. The **internal governance/routing benchmarks** — prerequisite recovery, license gating, ADMET routing, protocol-threshold QC, BOIN dose-finding, docking triage, PBPK routing, and the superseded internal cross-domain routing and parameter-prefill runs — are purpose-built to stress the parts of orchestration RealBio does not, scored by exact or partial-credit rubrics against committed records (most from the 2026-04-29 canonical benchmark matrix). The **pharmacology benchmark** is run deterministically with no LLM in the evaluation loop over 18 unique cases (three tests of six: PBPK disease-state AUC ratios, BRIM-3 enrichment, CSD polymorph risk). All 18 pass at exact numeric tolerance; the six Test-1 PBPK impairment pairs are additionally re-scored under a looser ≤35% tolerance (a re-scoring of the Test-1 subset, not six extra cases — so the count is 18/18, not "24/24"; a summary log's extra "15/15" block is not case-traceable and is excluded — §4.2). All three tested workflows are Level-1 validated against published data. For the **LLM-judged rows** (BiomniEval1, SGI-Bench) the 2026-04-29 run used Claude Sonnet 4.6 for generation and a Claude Haiku judge for the hallucination audit; these scores are non-deterministic without a pinned judge. **BixBench / BiomniBench Verified-50** is reported on the human-verified, trace-based Verified-50 subset (n = 50), distinct from FutureHouse's open-answer BixBench (≈17% published ceiling): BioMate scores 92.0% (46/50) through its analysis path (execution handlers) versus a Biomni Lab comparator of 88.7% on the same subset (comparator primary source to be pinned at submission; author-asserted). The earlier full-set 92.7% (n = 205) local-execution figure is withdrawn as non-pure-BioMate / not FutureHouse-BixBench-comparable (§4.3). Twelve of the benchmarks carry a per-query cost cell in the 2026-04-29 matrix (n = 718), from which the priced portion of the battery costs ≈$2.44 in LLM spend, computed as Σ(N × Cost/q); the mean of ≈$0.0034/priced query is a lower-bound blended mean because two of the twelve rows round to $0 (the ≈$2.44 total is unaffected), and the remaining benchmarks are unpriced in the committed record, so this is not a whole-battery total. The twelve-row itemisation is in Suppl. Table S1/S3. This priced-row accounting uses the 2026-04-29 matrix-row granularity (in which BiomniEval1 subtasks are separately priced), which differs from the collapsed 18-row Table S3 granularity but is not double-counted.

### Statistical methods

Binomial proportions are reported with Wilson score confidence intervals; power is characterised by the worst-case (p = 0.5) Wilson half-width at each benchmark's n. For aggregate rows whose head figure is a macro-average (unweighted mean of subtask proportions), the confidence interval is reported as approximate, since a macro-average is not a single binomial; e.g. BiomniEval1's headline 71.1% macro-average carries an approximate 95% Wilson interval ≈[64.6, 77.6] at n = 223 (worst-case Wilson half-width ≈±6.5 pp). The n-weighted (total-correct) alternative for the same rows is 69.9% (k = 156 of n = 223, Wilson [63.6, 75.6]). A binomial 95% CI with a ±5-pp margin requires n ≈ 385 (1.96²·0.25/0.05² = 384.16, rounded up; the commonly-cited 384 is the truncated floor), which is treated as the adequacy threshold. For weighted / partial-credit scores with non-integer numerators (e.g. ProtocolQC 18.5/20, Docking 35.5/45, the BOIN 48.3/60 variant), Wilson intervals are computed from the continuous score proportion rather than an integer binomial count and are therefore approximate. Cross-run reproducibility is measured over 3 independent repeats per workflow with Cohen's Kappa quantifying run-to-run agreement (24 workflows × 3 repeats for retrieval; reproduced on BiomniEval1 at n = 45 × 3). Directional claims are made only where measured intervals do not overlap; estimated comparators (BM25 ≈35%, raw-LLM ≈45%, GalaxyMCP ≈45%) carry no CI and are excluded from non-overlap claims. The battery was developed iteratively — scorer and prompt fixes across versions changed several heads materially (e.g. routing 94.6%→superseded, BiomniEval1 64.6%→71.1% macro-average, LAB-Bench 93.9%→72.7%, SGI idea-generation 1.1%→62.1%, docking 27.8%→78.9%, BOIN 41.0%→80.0%) — so every reported head is a current-best-after-debugging figure, not a pre-registered estimate; the superseded intermediate values are retained in-text where they aid interpretation. No family-wise multiple-comparison correction is applied, because the benchmarks measure distinct capability dimensions rather than repeated tests of one hypothesis; the single directional claim we assert (RealBio routing) rests on one non-overlapping CI pair, not on a family of comparisons. The audit-completeness separation (E4) carries a Mann–Whitney U test (U = 0, two-sided p = 1.6×10⁻⁴, n = 10 per group) but is read descriptively: the separation is *structural* — the standard domain tools simply do not emit per-decision scientific provenance — so the p-value is a formality on a near-degenerate 1.0-vs-0.0 table rather than a sampled effect.

### Statistical power of the evaluation battery

Per-benchmark worst-case (p = 0.5) Wilson half-widths at each committed n are: RealBio routing (n = 200, ±6.9 pp), BiomniEval1 (n = 223, ±6.5 pp), and SGI-Bench (n = 369, ±5.1 pp) reach or approach the ±10-pp line (the adequately-powered set of §4.5); the internal Routing run (n = 120, ±8.8 pp) and the withdrawn full-set BixBench local-execution figure (n = 205, ±6.8 pp) reach the n threshold but are superseded / withdrawn and carry no live claim (§4.1, §4.3). The current BixBench / BiomniBench Verified-50 figure (n = 50, ±13.4 pp) is small-n. By contrast prerequisite recovery (n = 80, ±10.7 pp), license gating (n = 54, ±12.9 pp), and the TDC ADMET reference set (n = 20, ±20.1 pp) have confidence intervals spanning much of the score range, so many head-to-head differences are not statistically resolvable at current sizes.

Against the *agent-scaffold* condition the 0.965 vs 0.950 routing difference is **not statistically resolvable at the Biomni pilot n = 40** (Biomni 38/40, Wilson [83.5, 98.6], overlaps BioMate [0.930, 0.983]); we therefore claim **parity, not superiority**, over the Biomni agent scaffold on routing. This comparison is between a 40-item Biomni pilot and the 200-item BioMate run, so the non-resolvability is driven largely by the small Biomni n; a matched n = 200 Biomni run is the pending step that would tighten the Biomni interval and resolve (or confirm) parity. The BM25 ≈35%, raw-LLM ≈45%, and GalaxyMCP ≈45% comparators (GalaxyMCP being the "LLM directly drives Galaxy" baseline introduced in §1.2) are *estimated, not measured*, carry no CI, and are therefore excluded from this non-overlap claim.

Four target-clearance claims are provisional in the strict sense that although the point estimate clears the stated target, the 95% Wilson CI still includes the target at the committed n — ProtocolQC (92.5%, n = 20, CI [73.1, 98.2], target 80%), BOIN (80.0%, n = 60, CI [68.2, 88.2], target 70%), Docking (78.9%, n = 45, CI [65.0, 88.3], target 75%), and LAB-Bench (72.7%, n = 33, CI [55.8, 84.9], target 60%). ADMET, prerequisite recovery, and RealBio routing have lower bounds far above their targets and clear by wide margins; PBPK (75.6%, n = 45, CI [61.3, 85.8], target 60%) clears its target but only marginally — its Wilson lower bound (61.3%) exceeds the 60% target by just 1.3 pp — so we report it as clearing with a narrow margin rather than unambiguously. Weighted/partial-credit rows use continuous-proportion Wilson intervals (approximate; see Statistical methods).
